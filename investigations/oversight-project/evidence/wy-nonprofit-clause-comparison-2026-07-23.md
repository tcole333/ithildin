# Wyoming nonprofit formation-clause comparison

Date checked: 2026-07-23

Scope: Lead #73725, global thread 178. This is a clause-level comparison of
official Wyoming law and forms against already-held primary Wyoming filings. It
tests whether The Oversight Project's mutual-benefit, no-member, registered-agent,
and dissolution provisions are unique to that entity. It does not infer motive.

## Official Wyoming requirements and defaults

Sources:

- Wyoming Secretary of State, `Nonprofit Corporation Articles of Incorporation`,
  revised June 2021:
  <https://sos.wyo.gov/Forms/Business/NP/NP-ArticlesIncorporation.pdf>
- Wyoming Statutes, Title 17, current through July 1, 2026:
  <https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title17.pdf>

The official articles form expressly requires or provides fields for:

- a choice among `Religious`, `Public Benefit`, and `Mutual Benefit`;
- whether the corporation `will have` or `will not have members`;
- a Wyoming registered agent;
- mailing and principal-office addresses; and
- provisions for distribution of assets on dissolution.

W.S. 17-19-202(a) requires the articles to state one of the three corporation
classes, whether the corporation will have members, and a dissolution
distribution provision. W.S. 17-19-603 states, `A corporation is not required to
have members.` W.S. 17-19-501 requires every nonprofit corporation to maintain a
Wyoming registered agent. The statutory definition of principal office permits
an office within or outside Wyoming.

If a mutual-benefit corporation has no dissolution provision and no members,
W.S. 17-19-1406(a)(vii) defaults remaining assets to the persons the corporation
holds itself out as benefiting or serving. The articles may replace that
default. W.S. 17-19-1804(a)(v) makes mutual-benefit status the residual
classification for corporations outside the religious and public-benefit
categories.

## Clause-level result

Facts:

- Mutual-benefit classification, membership status, and dissolution distribution
  are required public charter fields under Wyoming law, not omitted information.
- The no-member choice and commercial registered agent recur in both
  mutual-benefit IRC 501(c)(4)-purpose filings and the public-benefit IRC
  501(c)(3)-purpose State Leadership Foundation filing.
- The Oversight Project's dissolution language is substantively the same as the
  Frontier Foundation clause and textually the same as the Blueprint for America
  Coalition clause: directors select one or more IRC 501(c)(3) or 501(c)(4)
  recipients. No named recipient appears.
- The mutual-benefit/no-member/C T Corporation/IRC 501(c)(4) package therefore
  appears in at least three held Klimon Wyoming filings. The entity-specific
  text is principally the mission statement and operating address.
- The held non-Klimon New Founding filing uses a different commercial
  incorporator and registered agent, but it is a profit corporation and cannot
  serve as a controlled comparison for nonprofit class, membership, or
  dissolution provisions.

Bounded conclusion:

The primary records do not support describing the repeated formation package as
entity-specific opacity engineering. They show standard required form choices
combined with recurring Klimon tax/governance clauses. Choice of Wyoming,
mutual-benefit status, a no-member board structure, a commercial agent, and the
38-day pre-announcement interval remain documentable facts; the reviewed records
do not establish a concealment motive.
