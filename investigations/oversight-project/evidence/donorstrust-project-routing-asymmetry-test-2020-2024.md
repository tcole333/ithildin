# DonorsTrust project-routing and direct-grant asymmetry test, 2020-2024

Date reviewed: 2026-07-23  
Profile: `oversight-project`  
Lead: `74535`  
Investigation thread: `181`

## Result

The serial project-routing premise is supported, but the claimed “never direct”
graduation asymmetry is refuted by the current filings.

DonorsTrust reported a recurring set of project- or entity-designated grants to
Conservative Partnership Institute (CPI) and Heritage Foundation from 2020
through 2024. But the FY2024 filings also contain direct grants to organizations
that the hypothesis predicted would remain behind an intermediary:

- DonorsTrust paid State Freedom Caucus Foundation $240,000 for general
  operations.
- Bradley Impact Fund paid FAIR Elections Fund $25,000 for general operating
  support.

Those rows falsify the exact zero-direct-grant predictions in the lead. Other
FY2024 direct rows—DonorsTrust's $90,000 to Green Dragon Society and $50,000 to
Michigan Fair Elections Institute—also disconfirm a categorical rule that
election-named or election-network organizations must remain one hop behind a
hub. They do not, by themselves, prove a common program, donor, or intent.

## Classification and inference limits

- FAIR Elections Fund is an election-program organization based on its documented
  name and the existing primary-return record identifying CPI's mission/program
  support to the Foundation for Accountability Integrity & Research in Elections
  Fund (finding #14204).
- State Freedom Caucus Foundation is a state-policy/legislative organization, not
  classified as an election organization merely because the hunch placed it in an
  “election lobe.” Its direct row still falsifies the lead's organization-specific
  zero-grant prediction.
- Green Dragon Society is kept in the Datwyler-network comparison because primary
  filings document that relationship (findings #14198-#14200); its program is not
  reclassified as election work from adjacency alone.
- A missing direct row for Election Integrity Action or State Leadership
  Foundation remains a bounded filing fact. It does not establish deliberate
  opacity, earmarking intent, or a hidden donor.

## Conclusion

The primary records support a narrower fact: DonorsTrust repeatedly used
project-specific purpose strings on grants to CPI and Heritage. They do not
support the stronger claim that election-related organizations “never graduate”
to direct grants, because current filings contain direct FAIR and SFCF rows.

The evidence also cannot establish deliberate one-hop opacity. Grant timing,
recipient eligibility, sponsor choice, donor preference, and program maturity
remain alternatives. Intent would require grant-recommendation records,
agreements, correspondence, or internal approval material, none of which appears
in Schedule I.

## Future test

When FY2025 filings become public, repeat exact-name and EIN searches across all
three grantors. Record the result as a new observation; do not treat the current
unavailability of FY2025 as confirmation.
