# American Ideas / The American Conservative: distress-rescue and governance-capture test

**Lead:** 74541  
**Profile:** oversight-project  
**Reviewed:** 2026-07-23  
**Subject:** The American Conservative / American Ideas Institute, EIN 27-0311492, and Wyoming entity 2025-001661772

## Bottom line

The financial-distress premise is supported, but the alleged rescue-and-capture chain is not.

EIN 27-0311492 reported a $343,127 deficit in TY2022 and a $1,244,221 deficit in TY2023. By the end of TY2023 its net assets had fallen to $306,405. That is a real and material contraction.

The remaining causal steps fail or remain unproved:

1. Wyoming ID 2025-001661772 was newly incorporated on April 21, 2025 as **The American Ideas Foundation**. Its reviewed articles are not a domestication, conversion, merger, or continuation filing for the older California corporation that uses EIN 27-0311492. The Wyoming entity changed its name to **The American Ideas Institute** in May 2026. Shared directors establish affiliation, not legal succession.
2. The alleged $485,000 Carolina Partnership for Reform recapitalization went to **Center for American Ideas, EIN 86-3675838**, not to EIN 27-0311492.
3. The named grantor record does not show a new CPI/Heritage/Bradley/DonorsTrust funding bloc replacing legacy donors. The largest disclosed intermediary remained National Philanthropic Trust (NPT), which had funded the publisher since at least 2017. NPT reported $275,000 for the fiscal year ending June 2024 and $500,000 for the fiscal year ending June 2025.
4. Four directors of the Wyoming corporation—Dominique Watkins, Rachel Bovard, William Ruger, and Scott McConnell—already served the older publisher by TY2021, before the TY2023 deficit and the 2025 Wyoming incorporation. Six of the eight people on the publication's current first-party board page also appeared on the TY2023 return.

The evidence therefore does **not** identify a recapitalizer, a transfer of the magazine, or governance capture. It also does not establish an ordinary re-domestication: the reviewed records instead show a distinct, affiliated Wyoming corporation with an unresolved relationship to the older California publisher.

### Contrary first-party characterization

On October 9, 2023, board chair Dominique Watkins wrote that the magazine was in "a firmer position today both financially and editorially than it has been in many years." The article's subtitle described it as being in an "unprecedently strong position financially and operationally." This is a management characterization, not audited evidence, and it sits in tension with the subsequently filed TY2023 return. It is preserved as disconfirmation rather than accepted as a financial fact.

| Proposition | Assessment | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| The publisher was financially distressed by TY2023 | **Supported** | $1.244 million deficit; $306,405 net assets |
| The 2025 Wyoming filing re-chartered or re-domiciled the old publisher | **Contradicted by reviewed formation records** | New 2025 Wyoming corporation; old EIN remained a 2009 California entity |
| $485,000 of new Carolina money recapitalized TAC | **False** | The grant went to a different recipient and EIN |
| New CPI/Heritage/Bradley/DonorsTrust money replaced legacy support | **Not supported in direct grant rows** | No target row in the newest reviewed filings |
| DAF support was new | **Contradicted at the named-intermediary level** | NPT and Fidelity support predated the distress period |
| The same ultimate NPT donor continued | **Unknown** | NPT does not disclose the recommending donor |
| CPI captured the board after the deficit | **Not supported** | Bovard and the other Wyoming directors were already on the board by TY2021; six of eight current publication directors are TY2023 holdovers |
| The Wyoming affiliate obtained TAC assets or control rights | **Unknown** | No transfer agreement, merger filing, audited note, or other instrument was located |

### Verdict

The lead's combined rescue-and-governance-capture hypothesis is **refuted as framed**. It joined a verified distress event to three unsupported premises: a same-entity Wyoming re-charter, a misattributed $485,000 grant, and post-distress board capture.

A narrower possibility remains: an undisclosed donor could have recommended NPT's FYE 2025 $500,000 payment, and the distinct Wyoming affiliate may later have entered an unpublicized operational or asset arrangement with the old publisher. The public record reviewed here cannot attribute such a transaction, identify its funder, or infer control.

## 6. Evidence limits and precise follow-up

- No TY2024 or TY2025 return for EIN 27-0311492 appears in the current IRS 2025 or 2026 XML indexes reviewed on 2026-07-23; the latest target return remains TY2023.
- The publication links to an "audited financial report and 990," but the linked Google attachment redirects to an authenticated sign-in rather than a public document.
- Grantor Schedule I rows do not disclose the ultimate donor behind NPT or Fidelity Charitable recommendations.
- State articles alone cannot rule out a separate, non-filed asset or services agreement.

The proportionate follow-up is document-triggered: when EIN 27-0311492's next Form 990 becomes public, compare Part I, Part VII, Schedule D, Schedule I, Schedule L, Schedule O, and any related-organization disclosures against the TY2023 baseline; separately acquire any public audited statement or transaction instrument that names the Wyoming corporation. Do not infer a rescue or transfer from the shared name, shared directors, mailing address, or the NPT payment alone.
