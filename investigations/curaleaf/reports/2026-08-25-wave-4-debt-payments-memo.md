# Curaleaf: wave-four debt and payments memo

**Date:** August 25, 2026  
**Status:** Evidence-audited working memo; not publication copy  
**Questions:** Resolve the reported US$300 million refinancing and determine whether Curaleaf or an independently established Abramovich-linked vehicle owns or financed cannabis-payment infrastructure

## Executive assessment

The remembered **US$300 million** headline is real but old. It is Curaleaf's January 15, 2020 announcement that it had upsized and closed a 13%, 48-month senior secured term facility from US$275 million to US$300 million. It is not the current refinancing.

Curaleaf's February 18, 2026 transaction was **US$500 million face value**, but only US$357.8 million was cash-funded; US$142.2 million was a noncash exchange of old notes. Curaleaf's stated principal repayments and financing fees leave about US$7.8 million by financing-classification arithmetic. Including US$6.5 million of old-note accrued interest, separately classified in operating cash flow, leaves roughly US$1.3 million. Neither number is a disclosed retained-cash balance. The central point is narrower: the offering was overwhelmingly a debt rollover and payoff, not US$500 million of deployable new capital.

The new notes also increased annual stated coupon cost by about US$21 million while extending the old maturity by roughly 26 months. Curaleaf named only one purchaser: Boris Jordan's wholly owned Medtech International Group subscribed for US$1 million. Five registered funds later disclosed US$59.046 million of positions, but those snapshots do not reconstruct the closing book and roughly 88% of the face amount remains publicly unnamed.

On payments, no reviewed primary record shows **Curaleaf itself owning a payment processor**. The stronger finding is a related-person vendor relationship. Curaleaf reports that Boris Jordan-controlled Measure 8 held 6.19% and a board seat in High Tech Holdings, parent of Sweed. Sweed became Curaleaf's chain-wide retail operating/POS/payment-orchestration platform in June 2026. Curaleaf's live storefronts separately expose third-party rails including Aeropay and CanPay. Thus the documented equity is in the orchestration vendor's parent, not in the identified funds-moving companies.

A second, independent payment lead comes from T1 Payments' bankruptcy. T1's filed creditor schedule places Curaleaf KY Inc. at the exact address of Curaleaf's Northern Ireland online-retail subsidiary. A deterministic, non-fuzzy join found that 142 of the 220 dissolved online-retail companies formed at that address also appear by exact normalized name stem in T1's schedule. Curaleaf's Northern Ireland company was incorporated one day before Curaleaf announced online hemp sales. This strongly identifies a shared formation/onboarding ecosystem, but it does **not** yet prove T1 or Payvision processed Curaleaf transactions.

No direct Abramovich investment in a cannabis payment processor was established. The closest primary-grounded candidate is Ervington's approximately US$200 million investment in diversified parent Yandex in 2020. Yandex had already contracted to sell Yandex.Money, while Yandex Pay, Yandex Bank and Split launched later. That is indirect parent-company exposure, not a targeted payment investment. The former Dutch parent divested the Russian fintech perimeter in July 2024.

### Economics, security and holders

- The old US$456.815 million balance at 8% carried roughly US$36.55 million of annual stated coupon; US$500 million at 11.5% carries US$57.5 million. The arithmetic increase is about **US$20.95 million, or 57.3% per year**.
- The new maturity is about 796 days beyond the old December 15, 2026 maturity. This is costly refinancing, but the innocent commercial explanation is avoidance of a near-term maturity wall in a federally constrained cannabis credit market.
- The new notes have an 11.5% stated and 12.16% Q2 effective rate. Needham's revolving rate also stepped from 7.99% to 8.99% when the notes closed.
- Curaleaf temporarily repaid US$21.9 million of Needham draws, then reported US$52 million drawn by June 30, 2026.
- The notes are junior to Needham-type credit-facility debt on shared collateral. Bank lenders control enforcement and receive collateral proceeds first; noteholders generally face a 180-day standstill that can continue while bank lenders pursue remedies.
- Curaleaf named Medtech, wholly owned by Boris Jordan, as a US$1 million subscriber. Five registered funds later disclosed US$59.046 million of positions. These period-end holdings identify at least US$60.046 million but do not reveal the original allocation, exchanges or secondary purchases.
- Seaport appears as placement agent in both 2020 and 2026 and is under common control with private-credit managers. No reviewed filing shows a Seaport affiliate bought or warehoused Curaleaf notes.

The decisive records are the February 17, 2026 offering memorandum, purchaser agreements, allocation book, CDS/Odyssey holder and exchange schedules, closing funds-flow, old-note payoff ledger, placement-agent fee/conflict letter, and executed security/intercreditor reaffirmations. Findings `15164–15169` and `15175`; leads `91746`, `91748`, and `91752`.

## 2. Curaleaf's payment stack: ownership versus use

### What Curaleaf appears to own

No Curaleaf payment processor, bank, wallet, ACH operator or payment-company investment appeared in the reviewed 2020–August 2026 SEC filings, acquisition and investment notes, related-party disclosures, significant-subsidiary list, candidate-company filings, registries, or IP records.

That is a bounded disclosure conclusion. Exhibit 21 lists only significant subsidiaries, private-company cap tables are incomplete, and a small differently named minority interest could remain undisclosed.

### What a Jordan-controlled entity owned

Curaleaf's [Q1 2025 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000175677025000016/q125-exx992xfsandmda.htm) says Measure 8, controlled by Curaleaf chairman/CEO/control person Boris Jordan, held **6.19% of High Tech Holdings and a board seat**. High Tech then parented Sweed, a Curaleaf vendor. Earlier filings reported 6.8% and 5.86%; the issuer did not explain the percentage changes, and they should not be converted into inferred purchases or sales.

High Tech previously also owned Leaf Trade. Curaleaf says it began using Leaf Trade in 2018, praised its Leaf Pay ACH product, and in 2022 planned a rollout from 11 states toward all 19 U.S. wholesale markets. LeafLink acquired Leaf Trade in November 2024; Curaleaf's March 2025 filing says High Tech and Measure 8 no longer held Leaf Trade equity, while Measure 8 retained its High Tech/Sweed position.

Sweed says it expanded an existing two-market Curaleaf deployment to 165 stores across 14 states in June 2026. Apple metadata places a Sweed-linked Curaleaf Jacksonville app in market by March 2024. A current Curaleaf Jacksonville storefront exposes a production Aeropay configuration while displaying Aeropay, CanPay, debit and ATM options. Numeric merchant identifiers were deliberately omitted from the investigation memo because they add no reporting value.

The architecture is therefore:

| Layer | Company/system | Evidenced role | Ownership conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retail OS, POS and payment orchestration | Sweed / SD Technologies / High Tech | Chain-wide Curaleaf platform; configures and reconciles payments | Measure 8 minority stake and board seat; no Curaleaf corporate stake found |
| Consumer pay-by-bank | CanPay / Trusted Debit | Curaleaf partner since 2018; vendor claimed all-store coverage in 2024 | No Curaleaf or Abramovich investment found; CanPay described itself as bootstrapped through May 2022 |
| Consumer pay-by-bank | Aeropay | Production configuration at a current Curaleaf store and labels in sampled states | Use/integration only; no Curaleaf stake found |
| Wholesale ordering and ACH | Leaf Trade / Leaf Pay | Curaleaf wholesale relationship and planned national rollout | Historical indirect Measure 8 ownership through High Tech; LeafLink acquired Leaf Trade in 2024 |
| Banking/compliance | Green Check | Displays Curaleaf as customer; hosted Curaleaf technology executive | Module, bank and private cap table unresolved; no ownership evidence |
| Historical retail workaround | Cashless ATM | Curaleaf offered it by 2020; reported roughly one-third of transactions before December 2022 network shutdown | Processor unidentified; no Paybotic attribution supported |

Sweed advertises integrated processing, but the public material points to an orchestration layer using third-party services. It does not identify Sweed or High Tech as the licensed money transmitter, originating bank, ACH operator, acquirer, settlement custodian or merchant of record.

### Fees and governance gaps

Curaleaf's annual related-party tables report aggregate platform fees of US$2.069 million for 2023, US$3.274 million for 2024 and US$0.388 million for 2025. The footnotes combine High Tech-related platforms with Fyllo, so the totals cannot be assigned wholly to Sweed or Measure 8.

A Q1 2025 table separately reports US$698,000 for the quarter, more than the later US$388,000 full-year figure. Both are described as platform-fee transactions/expenses, not cash-payment totals. The annual filing supplies no reconciliation, credit, reversal, vendor-scope change or reclassification explanation.

The board designee is not publicly named. Curaleaf provides generic audit-committee oversight language but no deal-specific approval, recusal or arm's-length finding. A 2022 Leaf Trade page identifies Robert Sciarrone as a Measure 8 principal; Curaleaf now identifies him as chief revenue officer and Jordan's son-in-law. No primary evidence shows he was the High Tech designee or influenced procurement.

The relationship also has strong innocent evidence: Curaleaf used Leaf Trade from 2018 and planned the Leaf Pay expansion in 2022, before the reported 2023 Measure 8 stake. In the registered ACH comparison, ordinary vendor migration is presently least inconsistent; related-party influence remains a testable theory, not the leading conclusion.

A cross-MSO comparator makes the cap-table question more interesting. Verano's 2025 10-K says a CEO-controlled entity held less than 1% of Sweed while Verano paid Sweed US$3.395 million in 2025 and US$1.382 million in 2024. That supports an industry-wide inquiry into whether cannabis executives or their vehicles invested in a shared vendor used by their operating companies. Finding `15186`.

Findings `15176–15183`; connections `6769–6777`.

## 3. Curaleaf KY and the T1 Payments formation cluster

### Locked primary facts

T1 Payments filed voluntary Chapter 7 in Nevada on January 30, 2023; no conversion appeared in the reviewed docket, and the case closed June 10, 2025. Its [Schedule E/F attachment](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/66768712/12/1/t1-payments-llc/) lists:

- `Curaleaf KY Inc.`;
- 1A Erskine Street, Newry, Co. Down BT35 6BX;
- a literal amount of US$0.00;
- no contingent, unliquidated or disputed box checked;
- offset marked `No`;
- blank basis, debt date and account digits.

Curaleaf does not appear in Schedule G's executory-contract list. The filing proves only that T1 placed the Curaleaf entity on the potential nonpriority-creditor/notice side of its schedules at zero.

The address is the exact registered office of [CURALEAF KY LIMITED, NI657238](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/NI657238). Its incorporation packet identifies Curaleaf KY Inc. of Salem, New Hampshire as sole initial holder of one GBP 1 share, Jonathan Faucher as director, and Mind Your Business (NI) Ltd as formation/authorising agent.

The Curaleaf company was:

- received for filing November 19, 2018;
- incorporated November 20, 2018;
- assigned online/mail-order SIC 47910;
- followed on November 21 by Curaleaf's [announcement of online Curaleaf Hemp sales](https://webfiles.thecse.com/PR-21nov2018-launchCBD.pdf);
- voluntarily struck off and dissolved March 3, 2020.

### Deterministic cluster result

Companies House returned 220 companies with `1a Erskine Street` exactly in address line 1. All were dissolved and all carried SIC 47910. Every one of T1's 1,590 nonpriority names was parsed and compared using case/punctuation normalization, `&` to `AND`, removal of `(N.I.)`, and terminal corporate-suffix removal. No fuzzy matching was used.

**142 of 220 companies, or 64.55%, matched T1 schedule names by exact normalized stem.** All 142 T1 entries printed US$0.00; 130 used U.S.-style `Inc.`, `LLC`, or `Corp.` counterparts. Visual review of four incorporation packets—Curaleaf, Neurogan, Medori and Savage Enterprises—confirmed the matching U.S. entity as sole initial GBP 1 shareholder and Mind Your Business (NI) as agent. The other 138 matches are exact-name matches, not independently verified parent determinations.

The cluster-wide coincidence explanation is weak, but two substantive hypotheses remain tied:

1. the Northern Ireland companies formed part of T1/TGlobal/Payvision merchant onboarding or processing; or
2. T1 imported an overinclusive formation-agent, prospect, former-customer or notice list, including companies that never activated processing.

The 2018 Curaleaf Hemp archive identifies Shopify, CartHook, Visa and Mastercard, but no T1, Payvision, ISO/MSP, acquirer, MID or merchant descriptor. T1 litigation establishes that it processed many merchants and contains allegations by other merchants concerning UK/EU companies and Payvision routing, but those allegations were not adjudicated as Curaleaf facts.

The decisive evidence is a Curaleaf–T1 merchant agreement or application, TGlobal/Payvision MID or submerchant table, settlement and reserve ledger, Shopify/CartHook gateway export, card descriptor, formation invoice/onboarding correspondence, or T1's native prospect/customer database. Findings `15184–15185`; leads `91968` and `91970`.

## 4. Abramovich-linked vehicles and payments

No public primary record supports a direct Abramovich investment in CanPay, Aeropay, Sweed, High Tech, Leaf Trade, LeafLink, Green Check, Hypur or another identified cannabis-payment company.

The primary positive candidate is indirect:

- On June 29, 2020, Ervington Investments Limited, expressly described by Yandex as an Abramovich-beneficiary company, bought 4,060,913 Yandex shares at US$49.25: **US$199,999,965.25**.
- Yandex had signed the sale of its remaining Yandex.Money stake six days earlier and closed it in July 2020.
- Yandex Pay, the Acropol/Yandex Bank acquisition, and Split launched in 2021. No Ervington right was payment-specific.
- The Dutch parent completed its Russian-business divestment July 12, 2024 and retained no interest in the divested fintech operations. Ervington's own public exit date remains unknown.

Primary sources: [Yandex placement closing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465920078951/a20-22737_7ex99d1.htm), [Ervington investor agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465920078951/a20-22737_7ex10d4.htm), and [Yandex 2020 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000151384521000016/yandex-20201231x20f.htm).

CanPay is a useful bounded negative. Its own December 2020 page said it was not raising, and its CEO said in May 2022 that the company was fully bootstrapped and revenue-funded. A private cap table or later financing remains unobservable.

The most relevant Curaleaf-adjacent ownership gap is Measure 8, not a proved Abramovich vehicle. Its [March 2025 Form ADV](https://reports.adviserinfo.sec.gov/reports/ADV/300902/PDF/300902.pdf) says Measure 8 Ventures LP had US$110.53 million gross assets, approximately 18 beneficial owners, 41% adviser/related-person ownership and 96% non-U.S. ownership. The form names Boris Jordan as sole adviser owner/CIO but does not name limited partners or any Abramovich-linked vehicle. Foreign ownership is not a proxy for Abramovich ownership.

The ACH comparison disfavors the direct-payment-investment theory. Indirect diversified-parent exposure and no presently supportable direct investment are tied as the least-inconsistent explanations. Findings `15170–15174`; leads `91804`, `91806`, and `91831`.

## 5. Recommended reporting sequence

1. **High Tech/Sweed procurement and ownership file.** Obtain the 2023 merger and Measure 8 subscription documents, cap tables, board designation, investor-rights agreement, Curaleaf RFP/bid scoring, Sweed MSA/SOWs, audit-committee records, recusals, pricing, invoices, credits and revenue-share terms.
2. **T1/Curaleaf merchant bridge.** Seek Curaleaf and T1 merchant applications/contracts, TGlobal/Payvision Appendix 1 and MID tables, merchant/reserve/settlement ledgers, 2018 Shopify/CartHook gateway export, formation invoice and the source database used to create T1's creditor schedule.
3. **Leaf Trade sale economics.** Obtain the LeafLink acquisition agreement, seller waterfall, Measure 8/High Tech proceeds, retained payment IP and liabilities, earnouts, and post-sale High Tech/Sweed cap table.
4. **2026 note allocation.** Obtain the offering memorandum, purchaser and exchange agreements, final allocation book, Odyssey/CDS holder schedule, old-note payoff ledger, agent conflict/fee letter and affiliate allocation records.
5. **Payment regulatory roles.** Identify the Aeropay and CanPay sponsor/ODFI banks, Sweed and Leaf Trade third-party-sender roles, settlement custodians, merchant-of-record status, chargeback and fraud allocation, and any transaction-linked revenue to High Tech or Measure 8.
6. **Cross-MSO comparison.** Obtain Sweed cap tables and Curaleaf/Verano/customer contracts to test whether executive-linked strategic stakes are common commercial alignment or preferential vendor governance.
7. **Measure 8 LP records only through lawful public routes.** Court exhibits, audited statements, sanctions/custody proceedings, UCC records or transaction diligence may identify material investors. Do not infer an LP from nationality percentages or personal associations.

## 6. Hypothesis status and publication guardrails

| Question | Least-inconsistent result | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Did Measure 8 influence Curaleaf's Sweed expansion? | Ordinary vendor migration presently has less inconsistent evidence because the Leaf Trade relationship and rollout predated the stake | Stake, board seat and later expansion justify records requests, not a self-dealing allegation |
| Did T1/Payvision process Curaleaf Hemp? | Processing and overinclusive-list explanations are tied; current evidence is non-diagnostic | Say Curaleaf appears in T1's formation/creditor ecosystem, not that processing occurred |
| Did Abramovich invest in a cannabis payment processor? | Direct strategic investment is least supported; indirect parent exposure or no public direct investment remain viable | Do not look through fund-manager portfolios or unidentified non-U.S. LP percentages |
| Did Curaleaf retain substantial growth cash from the 2026 notes? | Stated uses consume nearly all cash proceeds | US$7.8m and US$1.3m are calculations, never reported balances |

Do not say Curaleaf owns Sweed, CanPay or Aeropay; that Sciarrone held the High Tech board seat; that T1 processed Curaleaf; that the 1A Erskine cluster proves laundering or evasion; that an Abramovich vehicle financed Measure 8; or that unnamed noteholders rolled or exited. None is established.
