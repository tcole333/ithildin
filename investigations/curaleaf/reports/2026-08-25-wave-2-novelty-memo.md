# Curaleaf: wave-two novelty memo

**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** Evidence-audited working memo; not publication copy  
**Purpose:** Identify genuinely additive stories beyond the two Newsground articles, test innocent explanations, and retire interpretations that did not survive primary-record review

## Executive assessment

The strongest new story is a clean securities-disclosure finding in Curaleaf's own 2025 management circular. The circular says each multiple-voting share carried 15 votes, but every combined-vote percentage in its class and principal-holder tables was calculated as if each carried 10. Correcting the arithmetic puts the MVS class at 67.9969% of combined votes and Boris Jordan at 70.5192%, rather than the reported 58.6% and 61.9%. Both correct figures cross two-thirds. The original Odyssey Trust PDF contains the same numbers, the MVS right had been 15 votes since 2018, Curaleaf's Q1 filing ten days earlier used 15-vote arithmetic, and the actual annual-meeting vote total could not have occurred under a 10-vote denominator. No altered vote outcome was identified because the 2025 agenda used simple-majority resolutions. Findings `15076`, `15112`, and `15115`.

The fuller record substantially corrects the initial Cetus lead. The US$90.795 million December 31, 2019 balance reproduced by Newsground rounds exactly to the balance Curaleaf had already published in its audited financial statements. Those statements say January 2020 facility proceeds satisfied the old obligation. The real inconsistency is narrower: the annual information form says the Cetus debt was repaid “as of December 20, 2019,” while the audited year-end liability and subsequent-event note place payoff in January. A second, potentially additive accounting question is why Curaleaf's disclosed early-repayment fee rose from US$9.500 million in interim reports to US$9.920 million in audited reporting without an explanation located in the notes. Findings `15091`, `15092`, and `15095`.

The offshore records do contain other underreported facts, but none presently proves a coordinated hidden treasury or sanctions violation. The most useful are: Cetus's expressly unaudited 2018 statement attributes its entire US$278.872 million long-term funding line to Sonora Capital Investments and Omega Credit Opportunities Master Fund; Gociter's audited-account copy describes a BCS-to-Sputnik debt assignment followed by an exact US$24.862 million setoff; and Tangela's parent receivable more than doubled to US$130.417 million while three annual cash-flow adjustments do not fully bridge the corresponding balance movements. Each is a document-driven extension with important limits.

## 1. The 2025 voting-control arithmetic

The [2025 circular](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000175677025000018/curaleaf-circular.htm) reports 663,418,006 subordinate voting shares, 93,970,705 multiple-voting shares, and 15 votes for each MVS. The correct combined denominator is:

`663,418,006 + (15 × 93,970,705) = 2,072,978,581 votes`.

| 2025 record-date item | Circular figure | Correct at 15 votes | Exact result at 10 votes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MVS class | 58.6% | **67.9969%** | 58.6172% |
| Boris Jordan: 52,286,410 SVS plus all MVS | 61.9% | **70.5192%** | 61.8789% |
| Andrey Blokh: 122,896,211 SVS | 7.7% | **5.9285%** | 7.6660% |

Four checks distinguish this from a benign parsing artifact:

1. The original Odyssey-hosted PDF visibly reproduces the same share counts, 15-vote term and percentages.
2. The [Q1 2025 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000175677025000016/q125-exx992xfsandmda.htm), furnished ten days earlier, reports the MVS block at 68.0%, consistent with 15 votes.
3. The [2025 annual-meeting result](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000175677025000021/pressreleasevotingresults2.htm) reports 1,722,745,691 votes cast—119,620,635 more than could exist under the entire 10-vote theoretical denominator. The meeting therefore used 15 votes.
4. The contemporaneous [2018 CSE listing statement](https://webfiles.thecse.com/CSEListingStatement_0.pdf) created the structure at 15 votes. Reviewed amendments changed sunset terms, not the multiplier. The [2026 circular](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000175677026000047/curaleaf-2026annualandsp.htm) resumes correct 15-vote arithmetic.

The error matters because the correct class and Jordan totals cross two-thirds while the published totals do not. That does not by itself show a legal consequence. The reviewed 2025 resolutions used simple-majority voting, and no changed result was identified. Other transactions can use class votes, disinterested-shareholder exclusions, votes cast rather than outstanding votes, or different statutory thresholds.

Two related disclosure inconsistencies strengthen the case for asking about controls rather than treating 2025 as an isolated typo:

- the 2023 circular's Jordan cell is 72.82%, while its own 15-vote counts yield 71.8165%; the class and Blokh cells in that circular are correct; and
- the 2019 and 2020 audited share-capital notes printed a 50% MVS sunset threshold where the governing 2018 disclosure said 5%; 2021 reporting returned to 5%.

The decisive follow-up is the 2025 circular workpaper, Odyssey register and denominator, board/audit-committee review trail, counsel sign-off, exchange correspondence, and any correction analysis. The question is not “did Jordan control Curaleaf?”—that was disclosed—but why the formal percentages systematically used the wrong multiplier and whether any threshold analysis relied on them.

## 2. Cetus: what the documents actually show

The [2019 audited financial statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000104746920005007/a2242299zex-4_2.htm) report US$90.795 million of principal and accrued interest at December 31, 2019. That is the rounded form of the US$90,795,144.42 balance shown in the Newsground image. The same audited filing says Curaleaf closed a US$300 million senior facility in January and used its proceeds to satisfy the old facility. The [2019 annual information form](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000104746920005007/a2242299zex-4_1.htm) gives January 15 as the replacement closing date.

A January 30 confirmation of a December 31 balance is normal timing. The article image therefore does not establish a concealed post-payoff debt. Its amount is independently corroborated, but the visible image contains no Cetus reply or auditor return evidence.

The filing package still contains two questions worth reporting:

- the AIF says the Cetus debt had been repaid “as of December 20, 2019,” even though the audited balance sheet carries it at December 31 and Note 20 describes January satisfaction; December 20 was also the replacement-facility commitment-announcement date; and
- 2020 interim reports disclose a US$9.500 million early-repayment fee, while audited and later comparative statements use US$9.920 million and also record a US$2.269 million modification gain. No reviewed note explains the US$420,000 increase.

The likely innocent explanations are commitment-date/closing-date conflation and a year-end fee true-up or classification change. A payoff letter, funds-flow memorandum, Cetus-signed confirmation, wire, fee calculation and lien-release package would decide them.

## 3. Cetus's upstream creditors

The public but no-index [Cetus 2018 statement](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/23510500-cetus-fs-2018/) is expressly **unaudited**, director-prepared and subject to IFRS 9 change. It nevertheless provides a useful balance-sheet map:

| Cetus long-term liability | 2018 balance | Share of long-term funding line |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sonora Capital Investments Ltd | US$263,252,409 | 94.3989% |
| Omega Credit Opportunities Master Fund LP | US$15,619,957 | 5.6011% |
| **Total** | **US$278,872,366** | **100%** |

The same statement reports a US$86,334,557 Curaleaf loan, which rounds exactly to Curaleaf's audited US$86.335 million 2018 balance. The page does not earmark Sonora or Omega money to that asset, disclose creditor terms, or prove beneficial ownership.

Primary identifiers now resolved are:

- Sonora: BVI company `1713956`, LEI `254900HAGCJCRO3HSL18`, c/o Osiris. GLEIF names no parent. Journalism based on Cyprus Confidential materials attributes Sonora to Roman Abramovich, but a public primary beneficial-ownership record was not located in this pass.
- Omega: Cayman master fund `661894`, SEC private-fund ID `805-6100946944`, with Omega Associates LLC as general partner and Omega Advisors/Omega Associates as managers. The [2018 Form ADV](https://reports.adviserinfo.sec.gov/reports/ADV/106867/PDF/106867.pdf) describes a pooled fund with 54 beneficial owners, an important ordinary-fund baseline. It was later renamed OCO Opportunities Master Fund LP under the same regulatory identity.

This supports an upstream-funding architecture story, not a claim that Sonora or Omega funded a particular Curaleaf draw. The next decisive records are the two creditor agreements, Cetus bank ledger, draw notices, collateral schedules and a certified BVI ownership record for Sonora. Lead `90188` preserves the last item.

## 4. Other offshore financing extensions

### Gociter, BCS and Sputnik

Gociter's audited-account copy says it lent Sputnik Group up to US$35 million on July 3, 2019 and borrowed US$35 million from BCS Prime Brokerage the next day at 6%, secured by Curaleaf shares held with BrokerCreditService (Cyprus). BCS assigned the Gociter debt to Sputnik Trading on March 12, 2020. On March 23, Sputnik Trading assumed US$24,861,839.87 of Sputnik Group's debt to Gociter, and the two equal obligations were set off. The account says a US$10,569,666.98 residual subordinate note was settled that month. Gociter then disclosed a US$26 million Sputnik Group loan on March 24 and a US$20 million Renaissance Life loan on March 30. Findings `15070`, `15071`, and `15089`.

The page's printed assigned-debt total and components do not arithmetically reconcile even though the two setoff legs do. The executed assignment, setoff, residual note, wires and source of the next US$46 million are absent. The timing is near Tangela's March 2020 Sova/CBoM share transaction, but no reviewed document links the two sequences. Systemic comparison favored ordinary bilateral treasury/refinancing over a common hidden treasury.

### Universal Growth Capital and Tangela

[GLEIF](https://api.gleif.org/api/v1/lei-records/213800VQE49SSGY33A23) establishes that Universal Growth Capital Fund Ltd and Universal Growth Capital Ltd are successive names of the same BVI company, number `371766`, LEI `213800VQE49SSGY33A23`. The [BVI Financial Services Commission](https://www.bvifsc.vg/regulated-entities/universal-growth-capital-ltd) lists the current-name company as a former regulated private fund but gives no cancellation date. The name sequence is therefore not evidence of a parent substitution.

Tangela's account copies show the amount due from Universal rising from US$53.854 million in 2019 to US$130.417 million in 2023. Comparing the annual receivable movement with the cash-flow working-capital adjustment produces unexplained residuals of minus US$4.000 million in 2019, plus US$1.693 million in 2021, and minus US$4.620 million in 2022; 2020 and 2023 reconcile exactly. Those residuals are not proof of third-party transfers because the cash-flow line is not a transaction schedule and company-wide interest and FX lines cannot be allocated to Universal. The story is repeated maturity extensions and incomplete public roll-forwards, pending the parent loan ledger and current ownership record. Findings `15127–15129`.

### Meliastove and Portiagate

Three records strongly fingerprint Curaleaf as the unnamed borrower of Meliastove's US$120 million 2020 facility: Meliastove's account reports a 3% discount and 13% rate; a Curaleaf amendment copy allocates US$120 million to Meliastove at a 97% issue price within a US$300 million schedule; and Curaleaf's filing says its 13% facility reached US$300 million and closed in January 2020. The account omits the borrower, the amendment has a blank effective date, and the schedule is subject to lender confirmation, so this remains synthesis rather than conclusive identification. Meliastove, Cyprus `HE201121`, is now in members' voluntary winding up; the public result does not identify the liquidator or cause. Finding `15133`.

Portiagate is a separate BVI company, number `506040`, LEI `549300AJ27DVJXE0DI09`. Tangela's account and Curaleaf's [2025 financial filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000175677026000019/curlf-20251231_d2.htm) align Tangela and Portiagate on a different US$7.5 million, 15% Q4-2020 ATGII/BHH acquisition note, settled October 1, 2025. No reviewed record ties this note to Meliastove or proves common control. Meliastove's November 2023 individual note buyer remains unidentified. Findings `15130–15133`.

## 5. CBoM: the remaining question is opacity, not an established violation

The account language supports an ordinary repo close-out model: CBoM already held title to the 15.5 million repo shares, realized “the repo shares,” and an affiliate surety covered the remaining obligation. The accounts separately describe another 10.2 million shares as a pledge; they do not say that block was enforced. Treating all 25.7 million shares as realized would be unsupported.

For Tangela, Cyprus creates a direct EU-sanctions nexus. However, public law supplied potential compliant paths:

- EU Article 6b(2c) allowed a competent authority to authorize termination of a pre-listing CBoM contract through June 17, 2023; and
- Article 7 permitted pre-listing-contract payments into a frozen account if the additions remained frozen.

Those paths do not prove authorization, and Article 7 does not plainly authorize a December share disposition. But they prevent the timing alone from establishing sanctions evasion. Cyprus reported hundreds of sanctions applications and approvals in aggregate, while no public Tangela-specific decision was found; such decisions can be nonpublic.

The high-value unanswered questions remain the surety's identity, the GMRA close-out calculation, the share lot and realized value, the custody movement, the payment rail, any authorization, and the downstream holder. The most discriminating record set is the GMRA close-out statement paired with custody and cash-account ledgers. Findings `15119–15126`.

Those are two collection-scoped negatives only. Known DocumentCloud records are themselves marked `noindex`, demonstrating why global-search absence is unsafe.

DDoSecrets' [MeritServus and MeritKapital](https://ddosecrets.org/article/meritservus-and-meritkapital) collection remains Limited Distribution and was not anonymously searchable through Library of Leaks. No archive was downloaded and no access control was bypassed. An authorized, narrowly scoped request through the [DDoSecrets ticket portal](https://tickets.ddosecrets.org/open.php) remains appropriate, especially for Meliastove/Leiston, Sonora, MeritServus correspondence, assignment records and beneficial-ownership leads. Until independently corroborated, any leak-derived ownership fact remains lead evidence rather than confirmed fact. Lead `89156` records this route.

## Recommended reporting sequence

1. **Report and seek comment on the 2025 voting arithmetic first.** Send Curaleaf the three-row recomputation, ask which denominator its circular preparers used, whether the board/audit committee, Odyssey, counsel, TSX or securities regulators were notified, and whether any governance or threshold analysis relied on the published figures.
2. **Prepare a correction/explainer on Cetus.** State that the US$90.795 million year-end balance was publicly audited, then focus on the AIF's contradictory December 20 sentence and unexplained US$420,000 fee revision. Request the closing binder rather than implying hidden debt.
3. **Build the upstream-financing package only with its limitations attached.** The Sonora/Omega composition, BCS/Sputnik setoff, Universal receivable and Meliastove fingerprint can show how the financing network worked without asserting common control or traced flows.
4. **Keep the CBoM story in document-request posture.** The surety, close-out statement, custody ledger and sanctions authorization are outcome-determinative.
5. **Pursue authorized DDoSecrets access narrowly.** Search exact legal names, company numbers, LEIs, dates, amounts and known providers; minimize incidental personal data and independently corroborate every candidate fact.

## Audit and reproducibility

Wave two added findings `15065–15133` across issuer filings, account copies, registries, sanctions instruments, licensing records and structured synthesis. The final profile audit scanned 159 findings and 346 evidence records and reported zero evidence issues; 64 findings carried the separate `verified` workflow status at the time of this memo. The principal analysis runs were `143` (hunch generation) and `144` (systemic competing-hypothesis analysis). The most important open leads are `90407` (voting workpapers), `90409` (Cetus recourse/upstream terms), `90188` (Sonora ownership), `90382` (BCS/Sputnik funds flow), `88935` (Universal receivable), `88947` (Meliastove buyer), `88931`/`88933` (CBoM surety and holder), and `89156` (authorized DDoSecrets access).

Publication guardrails remain unchanged: distinguish audited, unaudited and leak-derived records; do not infer a beneficial owner from a creditor name or provider; do not turn accounting residuals into transfers; do not describe FD-302 statements as FBI conclusions; and do not allege sanctions evasion, nominee ownership, money laundering or corruption without transaction-specific evidence.
