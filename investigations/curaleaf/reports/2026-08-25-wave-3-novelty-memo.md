# Curaleaf: wave-three novelty memo

**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** Evidence-audited working memo; not publication copy  
**Purpose:** Identify new, independently testable Curaleaf stories beyond the released Newsground, Forensic News, Barron's/Cyprus Confidential, and 420Intel reporting

## Executive assessment

The strongest new public-record story is a Nevada regulatory chain involving Andrey Muraviev. Cannabis Compliance Board records say Muraviev was the sole owner of Inter Reserve, that he and related companies sold Curaleaf shares on October 16, 2019 without prior Nevada approval, and that related companies loaned or contributed about US$13 million to BB Marketing/BBMC. Regulators said the debt could confer influence and later directed staff and the Attorney General's office to require a lender-suitability application. The process did not end in a public suitability adjudication: BBMC surrendered every Nevada cannabis license and the Board declared the transfer application moot in March 2023. DOJ separately charged Muraviev in 2022 over alleged illegal political contributions and said he remained at large. These records make Muraviev a serious lead for the teased “future fugitive” component, but they do not identify anyone intended by an unreleased article.

An earlier public DocumentCloud agreement creates a second, strictly qualified junction. Its 2017 Cetus–Andrey Blokh project schedule includes a US$619,000 BBMC/VSLV working-capital item in the arithmetic of a contemplated 60% Cetus participation. The same agreement gave Cetus continuing profit, consent, and transfer rights in four approved projects. Two of those projects—Naturex II/Blackjack and Las Vegas Natural Caregivers/House of Herbs—later entered Curaleaf, with Blokh receiving 722,577 Curaleaf shares in 2021. No public waiver, assignment, exercise, payoff, release, or allocation of those shares to Cetus was found. The available contract copy is not fully executed by Blokh, however, and no utilization request or wire authenticates actual Cetus funding. The question is what happened to the rights, not an allegation that Curaleaf acquired encumbered assets or that Cetus funded Muraviev.

The strongest independent financial extensions concern Meliastove's Curaleaf notes. The reported November 27, 2023 sale price included US$184,109.55 of accrued interest—almost exactly 14 days on US$60 million at 8% under ACT/365, rather than the roughly 165 days since the ordinary June coupon date. Separately, Curaleaf's FY2024 and FY2025 audited filings twice called amounts “accrued interest” even though the figures track future coupons avoided through maturity far more closely than the stated past accrual windows. A third point changes how the original financing should be described: Meliastove's US$60 million subscription form says `Delivery Type: Set-off`, and Curaleaf treated carryover-lender participation as debt modification. The public record therefore does not demonstrate a fresh US$60 million cash injection in December 2021.

The no-index DocumentCloud theory did not survive review. The thirteen no-index files in the uploader's public portfolio form two packages already used in published stories: five Cetus/Blokh/Lafayette records uploaded on December 22, 2022 for that day's Forensic News article, and eight Tangela/Gociter records uploaded August 16–17, 2026 for Newsground Part II. No later uploader document appeared through August 25. That does not reveal or rule out material held elsewhere, but the public upload metadata is not evidence of an unreleased Part III package.

## 1. Muraviev–Curaleaf–BBMC: the strongest public-record extension

The [December 14, 2021 Nevada CCB minutes](https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/CCB-Meeting-Minutes-12.14.2021.pdf) state that staff identified:

- an October 16, 2019 Curaleaf Holdings share sale by Muraviev and Muraviev-related companies without prior Nevada approval;
- Muraviev as the sole owner of Inter Reserve; and
- approximately US$13 million loaned or contributed by Muraviev-related companies to BB Marketing and BBMC for minority buyouts, buildout, and operations.

The Board record says the outstanding financing could allow Muraviev to influence BBMC. Counsel responded that the contemplated ownership acquisition had been terminated, that Muraviev did not assume operational control, and that the parties were trying to unwind the loans. Those responses belong in any account.

The [July 26, 2022 minutes](https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/CCB-Meeting-Minutes-07.26.2022.pdf) record that the roughly US$13 million remained outstanding; requested financial statements had not been supplied; and Muraviev had not complied with interview or appearance requests on advice of criminal counsel. The Board referred TOI 19076 back to staff and directed staff and the Attorney General's office to require a lender application. The [March 28, 2023 minutes](https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/CCB-Meeting-Minutes-03.28.2023.pdf) say BBMC had surrendered its medical cultivation licenses in June 2022 and its remaining adult-use cultivation, production, and distribution licenses in December 2022. Staff then declared the transfer matter moot and removed it with no further action.

“Moot” is not “cleared.” The public endpoint does not establish whether the notes were repaid, discounted, assigned, forgiven, or remained outstanding; whether Muraviev filed a lender application; or whether a suitability conclusion was reached. A bounded review of 110 later CCB-linked minutes through August 2026 found no subsequent public occurrence of the relevant Muraviev/BBMC/Inter Reserve terms.

The [March 2022 DOJ release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/russian-oligarch-charged-making-illegal-political-contributions) supplies separate criminal-case context, not proof that the charged political-contribution venture was BBMC or Curaleaf. Muraviev was charged, not convicted in the cited release, and the allegations must remain allegations.

The October 2019 share sale and the BBMC financing are official regulator statements. The relationship between them is not yet reconstructed. The immediate acquisition target is the complete Nevada file: TOIs 19037, 19037A, and 19076; the agency's later reference to “19706”; the October 2019 seller/buyer schedule; the two transferee companies mentioned but not named in the April 2021 minutes; source-of-funds and lender schedules; interview notices; the lender-suitability letter and response; and every note unwind or payoff document. Findings `15147` and `15153`; lead `91104`.

## 2. Cetus rights in Curaleaf-acquired projects

The public [Cetus–Blokh agreement](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/23510233-blokh-loan-12m_redacted/) contemplated Cetus advancing 60% of approved project costs. It gave Cetus continuing 60% net-profit rights after repayment, required prior written consent for disposal of Blokh's project investments, and allowed Cetus to demand transfer of 60% of a project interest in exchange for debt cancellation.

The available schedule identifies five project buckets. Cetus directors signed approval blocks for the first four: Nova/New Apothecary, Nova/Huntington Park, Naturex II/Blackjack through VSLV, and LVNC/House of Herbs. The fifth bucket names BBMC, BB Marketing, Naturex/Zen Leaf, and a US$619,000 VSLV working-capital loan. That amount is arithmetically embedded in the US$4,492,486 aggregate the document says Cetus would advance after execution, but project five lacks a separate approval block. Blokh's main execution line and the utilization form are blank in the public copy.

Curaleaf's filings independently establish the later asset lifecycle. The [2019 audited statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000104746920005356/a2242536zex-4_2.htm) describe the related-party VSLV/Naturex II acquisition and subsequent control increases. The [August 2021 Form 9](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000110465921108157/tm2125610d1_ex99-1.htm) and [closing letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000110465921108157/tm2125610d1_ex99-3.htm) say the Naturex II and LVNC interests were acquired and title passed. Blokh received 286,246 Curaleaf shares for the Blackjack/VSLV transaction and 436,331 for House of Herbs—722,577 in total.

No reviewed issuer filing, Nevada record, court record, registry entry, or available UCC material disclosed a Cetus consent, waiver, assignment, option exercise, payoff, release, arbitration, profit accounting, or allocation of the Curaleaf shares. That absence defines the records request; it does not prove the rights remained live. Funding may never have occurred, the contract may have been amended, the projects may have produced no net profit, or Cetus may have been privately repaid or settled.

The highest-value records are the completed utilization request and wire, Blokh/VSLV and Cetus ledgers, every consent or waiver, the 2017/2019 purchase agreements, the August 2021 closing binder, lien searches, title representations, LLC consents and amendments, escrow instructions, and allocation of the 286,246 and 436,331 share lots. Findings `15143–15146`, `15154–15156`; leads `91104` and `91301`.

Two subsidiary leads should remain separate:

- Nevada's 2026 corporate record still lists Blokh as an LVNC managing member after Curaleaf's disclosed 2021 minority buyout. “Managing member” may be a governance title or stale filing; the LLC amendment and CCB ownership schedule should decide it.
- Nova Trade Consulting remains active in California, with Vadim Shapiro as CEO, secretary, CFO, sole director, and agent in both 2025 and 2026. Public statements do not disclose shareholders and therefore do not resolve Blokh's reported 2017 interest or any Cetus exit.

## 3. Meliastove: transaction arithmetic and fresh-cash reconstruction

### Fourteen days hidden in the November 2023 sale figure

The published Meliastove account excerpt says it agreed on November 27, 2023 to sell US$60 million of Curaleaf notes for US$51.3 million plus US$184,109.55 of accrued interest. The disclosed figure equals almost exactly:

`US$60,000,000 × 8% × 14 / 365 = US$184,109.589`.

Ordinary coupon dates were June 15 and December 15. June 15 to November 27 is roughly 165 days and about US$2.17 million, not US$184,109.55. The figure therefore fingerprints an economic cut-off around November 13. An interim remittance, separate seller allocation, clean-price convention, or drafting error could explain it. The decisive records are the complete SPA, settlement statement, coupon ledger, and Odyssey/CDS remittance history. Finding `15152`; lead `91165`.

### Two years of “accrued interest” wording that tracks future coupons

Curaleaf's [FY2024 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000175677025000008/curlf-20241231_d2.htm) says its April 30, 2024 cancellation of US$15 million face reduced US$3.2 million of interest accruing since December 15, 2023. The stated past window produces only about US$0.45 million at 8%; future interest from cancellation through the December 2026 maturity is about US$3.15 million.

The [FY2025 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000175677026000019/curlf-20251231_d2.htm) repeats the pattern. Thirty-seven days of past interest on the US$3.2 million cancelled July 22, 2025 is about US$0.026 million, while the filing says US$0.4 million; future coupon expense through maturity is about US$0.358 million.

The leading innocent explanation is that the prose labels future interest expense avoided by cancellation as already accrued. The present record does not show a wrong payment or liability. Repetition in two audited filings makes the drafting and control trail worth requesting: settlement schedules, auditor support, regulator comments, and any correction analysis. Finding `15150`; lead `91167`.

### The December 2021 US$60 million was not demonstrably fresh cash

The signed Meliastove form labels the subscription `Delivery Type: Set-off`; its Curaleaf acceptance is blank. Curaleaf's audited FY2021 accounts say participation by lenders in both the old facilities and the 2026 notes was treated as a non-substantial debt modification. Meliastove had previously been scheduled for US$120 million in the January 2020 facility, making the later US$60 million exactly half that face amount.

These records support calling the December 2021 position a rollover/set-off candidate, not assuming a new US$60 million wire. At the entire facility level, the US$475 million face amount exceeded US$360 million of disclosed legacy principal by US$115 million; after disclosed prepayment penalties and fees, roughly US$86.29 million remains on a crude face-value bridge. Only the closing funds-flow memorandum, bank cashbook, lender payoff/assignment schedule, and Meliastove ledger can allocate actual new cash. Finding `15148`; lead `90728`.

### The 2026 payoff and Meliastove liquidation timing

The later Q2 filing says the February 18, 2026 refinancing comprised US$314.6 million of cash repayment and US$142.2 million of non-cash exchanges into 2029 notes. Curaleaf announced the closing on February 19. The official Cyprus registry dates Meliastove's members' voluntary winding up to February 25—seven days after the transaction and six after the announcement.

That proximity is not an ownership bridge. The global-note mechanics do not name beneficial holders, and reporting says Meliastove sold its position in 2023. The leading null is ordinary cleanup after an asset sale or refinancing. The liquidation resolution, liquidator appointment, asset statement, final account, and holder election/settlement schedule are required before drawing a connection. Findings `15151` and `15162`; lead `91163`.

## 4. Barry T. Mehlman and transaction-team continuity

The exact identity is Barry T. Mehlman, not “Mehiman.” The 2016 and 2017 Lafayette–Cetus notes place “Barry Mehlman, Esq.” only in Lafayette's `With a copy to` notice block. He did not sign the notes, the firm line is redacted, and the documents do not specify his client or work.

Loeb & Loeb's first-party pages separately list Mehlman on Curaleaf's [US$300 million senior facility](https://www.loeb.com/en/experience/represented-curaleaf-holdings-in-a-300-million-senior-secured-term-loan-facility), Select acquisition, and Grassroots acquisition. Curaleaf's AIF and the facility amendment establish that the Loeb matter was the same January 2020 facility whose schedule allocated US$120 million to Meliastove, subject to lender confirmation, and whose proceeds refinanced existing Cetus senior debt.

This establishes repeat Curaleaf-side representation and a possible concentration-of-knowledge question. It does not show that Mehlman represented Cetus or Meliastove, knew either beneficial owner, performed lender KYC, or acted improperly. Repeat counsel is ordinary, and a notice-copy recipient may have a narrow role. The useful targets are the closing binder, lender-confirmation package, legal opinions, engagement scope, conflicts records, and any public litigation or regulatory exhibit defining who received beneficial-owner information. Findings `15142` and `15163`.

## 5. Sonora and Omega: what survived the deep dives

### Sonora / Daccor

Public records still do not independently establish Sonora's beneficial owner. GLEIF identifies Sonora as BVI company 1713956 but its parent exceptions concern accounting consolidation, not ownership.

Daccor's final audited financing roll-forward is the strongest new money-flow target. It shows a negative US$131.34 million movement under `assigned loans & accrued interest`, equal to 94.3% of opening financing liabilities, without naming the assignee. Daccor ceased trading and later dissolved. The movement could be an ordinary intra-group cleanup, forgiveness, or transfer; only the deed and creditor ledger can identify the recipient.

Separately, actual Companies House forms show a coordinated April 12–15, 2023 transition across six Daccor/Anisimov-network companies: Ionics Secretaries and Cyprus-resident directors exited, two Scotland-resident directors entered, all six moved to one Manchester serviced-office address, and all were dissolved by February 2025. The first effective changes overlap the publication of UK/U.S. actions concerning Christodoulos Vassiliades. Ionics Secretaries HE134023 is legally distinct from designated Ionics Nominees HE134010, however, and the records do not establish sanctions causation, instruction, asset movement, or a Sonora/Curaleaf bridge. Findings `15134–15137` and `15161`; leads `90976`, `90997`, and `91002`.

### Omega / OCO

The same Cayman fund continued from Omega to OCO under stable CIMA, LEI, and patent-assignment identifiers, yet OCO reported a new SEC private-fund ID despite the SEC's reuse instruction. OCO's 2019 and 2020 Form ADV schedules called the fund Delaware while contemporaneous brochures called it Cayman. Eight Ocwen/CURO ownership filings repeated the Delaware description through February 2024; a January 2023 13D correctly said Cayman eleven days after a Delaware filing signed by the same managing member.

This appears to be an unreported disclosure-control problem, but it is not evidence of concealment or a Russian connection. The Cetus balance was about 5.08% of the fund's time-aligned gross assets and smaller than another disclosed Ditech credit position, which cuts against a size-anomaly story. Findings `15138–15141`.

## Recommended reporting sequence

1. **Lead with the Nevada public-record request.** Seek TOIs 19037, 19037A, 19076, 19071, 19090, and 19091; the “19706” reference; October 2019 share-transfer schedule; transferee identities; lender/source-of-funds files; suitability correspondence; note disposition; and license-surrender records. This can produce a discrete Muraviev–Curaleaf story without depending on inaccessible leaks.
2. **Run the Cetus-rights request in parallel.** Obtain the executed ELOC/utilization and wire, Blokh/VSLV ledgers, consents/waivers/releases, and the Naturex II/LVNC closing binder. Keep the BBMC schedule and the two Curaleaf acquisitions as separate tests until a document bridges them.
3. **Acquire the Meliastove sale and cancellation mechanics.** Prioritize the November 2023 SPA and settlement statement, Odyssey/CDS coupon ledger, 2024/2025 cancellation schedules, and the Cyprus liquidation resolution/asset statement. Ask why the sale interest equals 14 days and why two audited filings label future-looking amounts as accrued.
4. **Build a lender-by-lender cash bridge.** Reconstruct commitment, cash draw, OID, fees, rollover, set-off, assumption, and repayment for the 2020, 2021, and 2026 facilities. Do not use face value as a proxy for fresh cash.
5. **Use Mehlman as a file map, not a target.** The neutral question is which deal team held which records and lender confirmations. Public records do not justify misconduct or knowledge attribution.
6. **Keep Sonora/Daccor and Omega/OCO as separate side investigations.** The Daccor deed could reconnect Sonora; the OCO filing inconsistencies could support a standalone fund-governance story. Neither should be used as current proof of a Curaleaf–Russia flow.
7. **Continue an authorized, narrow DDoSecrets request.** Search exact entity numbers, dates, amounts, agreements, and settlement fields. Treat the leak as lead generation, minimize incidental personal data, and independently corroborate all material claims.

## Audit and reproducibility

Wave three added findings `15134–15163`, all formally verified after evidence review. The final profile evidence audit reported **zero issues across 189 findings**. Principal analysis runs were `145` (novel hunch generation) and `146` (Meliastove transaction timeline). The post-wave cross-reference scanned 603 items and generated 53 automatic leads. The 47 new leads in the final address/officer/entity batch were manually triaged: 34 were closed as duplicates, false positives, or low-value cross-references, and 13 distinct follow-ups remain open. Automatic leads are scheduling prompts, not findings.

Key durable leads are `91104` (Nevada TOI/Cetus–Muraviev bridge), `91301` (Nova/New Apothecary ownership), `91165` (Meliastove 14-day interest), `91167` (audited accrued-interest wording), `91163` (Meliastove liquidation/refinancing), `90728` (fresh-cash reconstruction), `91002` (Daccor assignee), and `90724` (663,900-share certificate provenance).

Publication guardrails: distinguish direct regulator statements from cross-document synthesis; do not infer the identities behind a teaser; do not treat a contemplated or partially executed agreement as funded; do not turn a status date or exact arithmetic into causation; do not impute knowledge across lawyers or matters; and preserve allegation, charge, settlement, and conviction language exactly.
