# Curaleaf, T1 Payments, and the Newry merchant-onboarding system

**Evidence cutoff:** August 26, 2026  
**Profile/thread:** `curaleaf` / 204  
**Editorial status:** strong original records story; direct Curaleaf processing remains unproved

## Executive conclusion

The investigation has moved beyond a coincidental creditor-list hit. Public court and registry records now establish that multiple T1 merchant families used a repeatable Northern Ireland company-formation mechanism at or near the time they entered T1 payment-processing relationships. Curaleaf formed a near-template-twin company through the same formation agent, at the same Newry address cluster, one day before announcing its online hemp launch. A Curaleaf group name later appeared in T1 Payments' bankruptcy Schedule E/F at that Northern Ireland address.

The responsible formulation is:

> Curaleaf created an adjacent, near-template-twin Northern Ireland e-commerce company through the same formation system used contemporaneously by documented T1 merchants, and a Curaleaf group name later appeared in T1's bankruptcy data. Public records do not yet prove that Curaleaf processed payments through T1, TGlobal, Payvision, or NMI.

That is already a newsworthy and apparently novel records question. It is not a basis to state that Curaleaf was a T1 merchant without a Curaleaf contract, merchant ID, descriptor, gateway/account record, settlement statement, or processor ledger.

## The recurring merchant-formation pattern

| Merchant family | Public T1 evidence | Northern Ireland company timing | Exact limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onyx & Rose | Federal court says its T1 card-processing relationship began September 12, 2018 | MYB filing received September 12; NI655676 incorporated September 13 under the U.S.-parent/GBP1/SIC-47910/Elmfield/no-PSC template | Public opinion names the U.S. LLC, not the NI affiliate, as contracting plaintiff |
| New U Life | Federal order identifies T1 agreements signed October 26 and 30, 2018 | NI656748 incorporated October 30 at 1B Erskine under the same core template | Court uses a composite family definition; it does not prove the NI affiliate alone was merchant |
| Gaia / Mitragaia | Signed T1 application, merchant terms, CPPA and transaction ledger | NI666273 formed December 3, 2019 through MYB; renamed August 4-5, 2020; parties signed August 5/12 | The one examined case where the signed T1 file directly names the NI affiliate as merchant |
| Curaleaf KY | No public T1 merchant record | NI657238 incorporated November 20, 2018 at 1A Erskine under the same core template; Curaleaf announced online hemp sales the next day | Strong circumstantial comparator only |

Curaleaf's November 21, 2018 first-party release said the products were available for online purchase. The earliest archived homepage, December 1, shows Shopify, an installed CartHook checkout replacement, and Visa/Mastercard icons. The launch announcement was 29 days before enactment of the 2018 Farm Bill. The timing explains why payment onboarding could have been sensitive, but it does not establish illegality; product source and the prior 2014 pilot framework would require separate analysis.

A T1-promoted CBD merchant independently used the same visible Shopify/CartHook layer while the underlying processor remained absent from its archived checkout shell. That makes Curaleaf's architecture compatible with T1 but nonspecific. Conversely, the captured Curaleaf products lacked the CartHook-routing tag, no Curaleaf checkout shell survives, and 42 preserved Curaleaf pages displayed a U.S. Curaleaf identity rather than the Northern Ireland identity pattern Gaia alleges T1 required. These are meaningful limits, not proof that Curaleaf never used T1.

## T1's bankruptcy list is a hybrid source, not a clean creditor ledger

T1's filed Schedule E/F contains 1,590 names. Of those, 1,574 print `$0.00`; only five carry positive amounts. Repeated entities such as American Express, Hartford, and U.S. Bank appear once at zero and again as positive liabilities. Address strings frequently combine U.K. formation addresses with U.S. city/state/ZIP fields.

The strongest model is a cross-border corporate-formation/onboarding entity master merged with actual liabilities for bankruptcy notice purposes:

- 142 of 220 companies in the exact 1A Erskine universe match Schedule names directly.
- Another 29 match through the NI company's sole initial shareholder, producing a conservative 171/220 family-level lower bound.
- In a fixed-seed random sample outside the known 1A sets, 10/10 zero-dollar names resolved to contemporaneous U.K./Northern Ireland formations; 9/10 packets named Mind Your Business (NI) and one named BMS Fiduciary.
- Nine of those ten named the scheduled entity as initial shareholder; the tenth used the exact child-company name.
- The public signed Mitragaia merchant file names the NI subsidiary, but the Schedule lists U.S. parent Gaia Ethnobotanical at `$0`. This proves that a real merchant family can appear as a zero-dollar parent row.

The list therefore has real commercial provenance, but `$0` cannot distinguish active merchant, former merchant, prospect, referral, formation-only client, or another contact class. Curaleaf's row proves entry into that data ecosystem, not merchant activation.

## What Gaia Document 78 alleges, and what is independently supported

The Third Amended Complaint in *Gaia Ethnobotanical, LLC v. T1 Payments, LLC*, No. 2:22-cv-01046-CDS-NJK, is the best roadmap to the alleged system. It is a party pleading, not an adjudication.

| Alleged step | Independent support now | Missing proof / contradiction |
|---|---|---|
| T1 cold-called Gaia, met its manager, and required a U.K. company | Signed T1 relationship; official MYB formation; tight rename/signature timing | Calls, meetings, instructions, and who commissioned MYB remain unproved |
| Website changed to display the foreign merchant identity | Complaint allegation; the signed merchant is Mitragaia | Native website-change emails and complete archived footer/config are missing |
| U.S. sales were aggregated through TGlobal's Payvision master-merchant structure | Separate New U record confirms a TGlobal-Payvision agreement existed | Gaia MID/submerchant/account join and the actual contract appendix are missing |
| Funds moved Payvision to Payvision Risk to TGlobal Nevada to Actum and then merchants/T1 | Complaint flowchart and referenced bank exhibits | The cited Chase/Wells Fargo statements are not public; no end-to-end Gaia account join exists |
| Payvision termination was concealed while reserves grew | Complaint cites December letters; signed T1 agreement contains broad reserve rights | Letters, reserve statements, and amount reconciliation are missing |
| Business, merchants, and money migrated to Pixxles | T1 namespace carried Pixxles branding before incorporation; officers overlap | Customer export, bank transfers, contracts, and assignments are missing; Pixxles reports no 2021 revenue and a 2022 soft launch |

The public Gaia merchant package establishes NMI gateway use, a $1 million approval, 6% Visa/Mastercard pricing, a 5% rolling reserve for 180 days, and broad reserve-retention discretion. It does not name Payvision, an acquiring bank, merchant ID, descriptor, or settlement bank.

Gaia sought $351,735.73 plus interest on a breach-of-contract default theory. On August 4, 2026, the court denied default judgment without prejudice because the filing package violated local rules and did not analyze the required merits/sufficiency/amount factors. No monetary judgment was entered. Earlier, in March 2024, the court dismissed Gaia's prior complaint without prejudice because its pleaded theory did not account for the contract's post-termination reserve provisions. Neither order validates the broader fraud or fund-flow allegations.

Document 78 also contains correctable problems:

- it dates Nevada Pixxles LLC's formation one year early;
- its alleged GBP1.415 million and GBP1.737 million Pixxles transfers exactly match cumulative share-capital totals on Companies House forms;
- the actual new cash allotments were GBP400,000 and GBP322,211;
- the forms do not identify payer, originating bank, or source of funds.

The bank-flow theory remains testable, but the Companies House totals cannot prove those alleged transfers.

## Pixxles: incubation is supported; succession is not

Passive certificate-transparency and archive records add a novel operational bridge:

- `pixxles.t1payments.com` and `www.pixxles.t1payments.com` certificates began September 24-25, 2018, nine to ten days before Pixxles Ltd was incorporated.
- `payist.t1payments.com` followed in December 2018.
- PXLS LLC, managed by Amber Fairchild, filed the PAYIST mark for merchant/payment processing in January 2019.

This is specific evidence that T1's namespace incubated Pixxles/PAYIST branding. It does not prove a transfer of customers, money, code, contracts, or regulatory permission.

Material counterevidence comes from Pixxles itself: filed accounts report no 2021 revenue, a still-developing product, and a 2022 soft launch with newly onboarded merchants. Its 2023 accounts disclose GBP1.276 million paid into a receivable from an unnamed related company and GBP618,765 of intermediary fees to an unnamed related company, but do not identify the counterparty or connect either amount to T1.

## T1 bankruptcy and Kasdon

Donald Kasdon paid $36,000 to buy estate-owned claim bundles that included claims against himself and related parties, as well as affirmative claims involving Payvision, New U, Bullion, and prepetition law firms. The entered order preserved creditors' personal and particularized claims; it did not contain an express blanket release or Section 363(m) good-faith finding on its face.

The estate's only receipts were Kasdon's $36,000. All went to administration; `$0` went to approximately $9.83 million in allowed priority and general unsecured claims. An unidentified competing offer existed for one bundle, and the final price rose by $6,000, but the bidder, amount, and oral valuation record remain missing. The evidence supports an insider transaction and zero creditor recovery; it does not yet prove that value was suppressed or claims were neutralized.
