**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** Working investigative memo; not publication copy  
**Scope:** The two Newsground articles, their underlying records, adjacent corporate/financial/licensing records, and public Cyprus-leak search coverage

**Subsequent correction:** Wave-two testing superseded the initial Cetus interpretation below. ## Executive assessment

The investigation has produced several credible follow-up tracks, but it does **not** presently support an allegation that Curaleaf, Boris Jordan, Mary Louise Ferrier, Tangela, or another identified person committed bribery, money laundering, sanctions evasion, securities fraud, or nominee ownership.

The two Newsground articles are grounded in real records, but both headlines compress important distinctions:

- The released FBI file establishes SDNY and EDPA grand-jury activity and witness interviews touching Curaleaf-related licensing and financing questions. It does **not** publicly identify Curaleaf, PalliaTech, Cetus, Boris Jordan, Nicholas Jordan, or another named person as a formal FBI target or subject. The only person expressly indexed as a “main subject” is redacted. The FD-302 memoranda contain uncertainty and denials, not FBI findings. No public disposition was located in the bounded searches. The starting article is [“FBI Investigated Curaleaf for Corruption and Money Laundering”](https://thenewsground.com/fbi-investigated-curaleaf-for-corruption-and-money-laundering/); the released file is preserved as [FOIA 1579784-000](https://archive.org/details/Curaleaf_FBI).
- Tangela’s account copies support the reported 15.5-million-share Credit Bank of Moscow transaction and the later statement that the bank “realized the repo shares.” But CBoM acquired legal title under a 2020 repurchase transaction, subject to Tangela’s repurchase obligation. “Took” is therefore a shorthand for repo close-out mechanics, not evidence of seizure. Later payments and settlement occurred after UK, EU, and U.S. sanctions dates, but the reviewed material does not establish a prohibited nexus, unlicensed transaction, or other violation. The starting article is [“Sanctioned Russian Bank Took Millions of Curaleaf Shares…”](https://thenewsground.com/sanctioned-russian-bank-took-millions-of-curaleaf-shares-from-offshore-company-tied-to-ceo-boris-jordan/); OFAC designated CBoM on [2023-02-24](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1296).

The strongest new primary-record story now is a 2025 voting-disclosure error: Curaleaf's circular states that each multiple-voting share carried 15 votes, while all of its combined-vote percentages calculate at 10 votes. Correct 15-vote arithmetic puts both the MVS class and Boris Jordan above two-thirds of combined voting power; the circular reported both below it. The actual annual-meeting vote total confirms that 15 votes were used, and no changed meeting outcome was identified. The still-unexplained Tangela/CBoM surety and settlement remain a high-value records lead. The Cetus year-end balance, by contrast, is substantially reconciled by ordinary January closing mechanics; only the contradictory AIF repayment sentence and missing closing records remain.

## Article verification

### 1. Federal investigation and licensing records

The 64-page FOIA release documents:

- SDNY file `272B-NY-2225719-GJ`, including October 2018 and January 2019 subpoenas connected to an alleged 18 U.S.C. § 1956 violation;
- a cross-reference to `281H-NY-3007826`;
- EDPA file `194A-PH-3006435-GJ`, Grand Jury `19-07`, and a February 2019 subpoena; and
- an April 2019 instruction to index a redacted individual as a main subject.

The released subpoena recipients, record riders, and subject identity are redacted. The FBI’s 2023 FOIA log labels request 1579784-000 “Cetus Investments Limited,” but that is request metadata, not investigative target status.

The two witness memoranda materially temper the article’s framing. One former contractor recalled there “might have been” an instruction to omit a shareholder but could not remember the person, whether it occurred, or whether an application was submitted that way. A second witness said she was never told to omit a shareholder and did not know that an omission occurred. The same witness denied knowledge of Curaleaf bribes or campaign-finance violations. The memoranda state that they contain neither FBI recommendations nor conclusions.

Pennsylvania’s reproduced ownership rule generally required disclosure of at least 5% beneficial ownership or board-election voting power for a public company, and any security ownership for a private entity. Public applicant summaries changed Curaleaf’s clinical-registrant identifier and officer list between 2018, 2019, and 2020, but they are not the full applications or ownership schedules. They cannot establish who was disclosed, whether a person met the applicable threshold, or whether Pennsylvania knew of the federal activity.

The strongest official counterweight is the Massachusetts Cannabis Control Commission’s 2023 investigation. Its financial team and outside accountant reviewed loan documents, statements, promissory notes, and prior disclosures, concluded the Abramovich relationship was lender–lendee only under Massachusetts ownership/control definitions, and closed `INV-2023-0000001307`. See the [August 2023 CCC meeting packet](https://masscannabiscontrol.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Meeting-Book-Cannabis-Control-Commission-August-Monthly-Public-Meeting-Packet.pdf). This does not resolve the FBI or Pennsylvania questions, but it must accompany any broader ownership/control allegation.

### 2. Tangela, CBoM, and the share-repo chain

The Tangela account copies trace the following sequence:

- a December 2019 CBoM transaction involving 2.175 million shares and an additional 8.325-million-share pledge;
- a March 2020 transaction involving 29,810,391 shares, which the accounts say also settled Sova obligations;
- a September 2020 transaction for 15.5 million Curaleaf subordinate voting shares at US$77 million, secured by 10,232,500 additional shares;
- later terms restating a US$95 million repurchase obligation and extending it to December 2023 in RUB-equivalent form, at the Russian central-bank key rate plus four percentage points;
- an unidentified affiliate surety and a disclosed RUB 3,168,500,000 partial repayment; and
- a 2023 account statement that a “third-party bank” realized repo shares and the surety settled or procured settlement of the balance.

The records do not name the surety, quantify the share block ultimately realized, identify the downstream holder, state the realized value, or allocate the final settlement between Tangela and the surety. These omissions are the most consequential unresolved facts in the bank-share story.

The same account series reports Tangela receivables from its parent rising from US$53.854 million in 2019 to US$130.417 million in 2023. The 2021 figure is US$85.145 million. GLEIF resolves `Universal Growth Capital Fund Ltd` and `Universal Growth Capital Ltd` as successive names of the same BVI company, number `371766`; the BVI Financial Services Commission now lists the company as a former regulated private fund. Three of five annual cash-flow adjustments do not fully bridge the corresponding receivable movements, but the accounts do not identify the residual components and do not support inferring a third-party transfer.

### 3. Lifebrook and the U.S. voting-control architecture

The December 2023 SEC-filed package establishes a formal split: Curaleaf Holdings owns 999 Class B non-voting Curaleaf, Inc. shares, while Lifebrook owns the sole Class A voting share. The Class A share receives no dividends and has liquidation rights capped at US$0.0001. The filed agreements nevertheless give Curaleaf Holdings extensive power: two of four board designations, approval of Lifebrook’s nominee, selection of a chair with a casting vote, an irrevocable proxy enforcing board composition, and prior-consent rights over major corporate, financing, operating, licensing, litigation, and insolvency actions. See the [December 2023 SEC package](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000110465923127214/0001104659-23-127214.txt).

SEC staff asked substantive questions about the arrangement in August 2024. Curaleaf identified Lifebrook as the third-party investor and argued that the contractual rights and economics made Curaleaf Holdings the accounting primary beneficiary. The SEC later closed the review while expressly declining to validate the disclosure. See the [SEC comment letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000000000024009734/filename1.pdf), [Curaleaf response](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000110465924100982/0001104659-24-100982.txt), and [closure letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1756770/000000000024011800/filename1.pdf).

UK Companies House identifies Lifebrook as company 15130253, incorporated 2023-09-11, with Mary Louise Ferrier as sole listed director and person with significant control. Separate primary records document a longstanding Ferrier–Jordan business relationship: a 2021 Renaissance Insurance offering memorandum named Ferrier and Boris Jordan as 31.57% and 42.61% beneficial owners of Sputnik Group, while [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/officers/3xTa_bX301VB9HPad0ao97Jl96U/appointments) records Ferrier as Renaissance Capital’s secretary from 1995 to 1999. These records weaken a colloquial description of the parties as strangers; they do not establish that Ferrier is a nominee or lacks independent ownership.

Utah applicant materials consistently allocate 100% voting rights to Lifebrook and 100% economic rights to Curaleaf Holdings. Nevada regulators approved adding Lifebrook as an owner for the TSX move after staff reported no concern. Those records are compatible with the SEC structure and are not evidence that Lifebrook holds participatory economics.

## DDoSecrets and Cyprus-leak coverage

The public [Library of Leaks](https://search.libraryofleaks.org) interface exposes two anonymously searchable Cyprus collections relevant to this pass: DJC Accountants and Kallias and Associates. Collection-scoped exact searches returned no results for Tangela, `HE376379`, Ferrier, Universal Growth Capital, Cetus, Meliastove, Gociter, Lifebrook, Portiagate, Boris Jordan, or Nicholas Jordan. Broader CBoM, Sova, Sputnik, and Abramovich hits were inspected and were unrelated to this network. Those are two-dataset negatives only.

DDoSecrets’ [MeritServus and MeritKapital](https://ddosecrets.org/article/meritservus-and-meritkapital) collection is labeled **Limited Distribution** and was not anonymously searchable in Library of Leaks. It contains more than 400,000 files and 72,000 emails according to DDoSecrets. This is an access gap, not a zero result. No archive was downloaded. An open lead records the legitimate route through the [DDoSecrets ticket portal](https://tickets.ddosecrets.org/open.php) for authorized, scoped searching.

## Competing-hypothesis assessment

- **Cetus payoff:** ordinary timing now fits best. The public audited statements carry the liability at December 31 and say January facility proceeds satisfied it. The AIF's December 20 sentence remains internally inconsistent and the complete confirmation/payoff package would resolve whether it is a drafting or milestone-date error.
- **Lifebrook independence:** the evidence is mixed. State approval and Curaleaf’s third-party description support the lawful TSX-eligibility explanation; Ferrier’s documented Sputnik/Renaissance relationship with Jordan weighs against a broad “unaffiliated stranger” narrative. None of it proves nominee status.
- **FBI scope:** the public record supports investigative activity but supplies no affirmative evidence that Curaleaf or a named associate was a formal target. Redactions prevent a conclusive finding.
- **Pennsylvania omission:** equally balanced and unresolved. One tentative recollection and one denial cannot replace the actual applications.
- **CBoM settlement:** the ordinary close-out and material-opacity explanations remain tied. Sanctions dates create a legal due-diligence question, not proof of a violation.

## Publication guardrails

- Do not identify the FBI-redacted main subject from biographical clues.
- Do not describe FD-302 witness statements as FBI conclusions.
- Do not characterize a repo transfer as a seizure without the operative agreement and enforcement record.
- Do not allege a sanctions violation without date, jurisdiction, ownership/control, counterparty, authorization, and transaction analysis.
- Do not call Ferrier a nominee or say Lifebrook lacks independence on the present record.
- Do not call the Gociter pledge a reporting omission until the insider, control, related-financial-instrument, and recourse tests are established.
- Include the Massachusetts closure, Nevada approval, witness denials, company position, and unresolved redactions in any balanced account.
