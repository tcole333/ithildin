# Eastport Cinemas / Eastport Shopping Center: Initial Investigation

Date: 2026-08-04  
Profile: `eastport-cinemas`

## Executive assessment

Eastport Plaza LLC is the supported record-title owner of the two Eastport
Shopping Center accounts examined. The accessible primary record does not yet
identify the LLC's members, managers, or ultimate beneficial owners. L & L
Investments LLC is documented as a care-of, permit-owner-field, employment, and
management-associated entity, but those roles do not establish equity or
control.

The former cinema area is physically dormant, but the shopping center and its
owner entity are not wholly inactive. Eastport Plaza LLC appeared as a landlord
in a February 2026 Maryland court filing; City records also show unrelated
utility and tenant work around the center. A bounded permit sweep found no
indexed evidence that The Lofts at Eastport Landing has begun demolition,
grading, building, trade, or occupancy permitting after its 2025 extension.

The best-supported explanation is cumulative delay, not a single proven cause:
an aging two-screen cinema facing multiplex competition; an ambitious
redevelopment concept that was revised over density and design; years of
adequacy-of-public-facilities appeals and judicial review; binding police,
traffic, and stormwater mitigation; and environmental construction controls on
part of the property. Financing, the complete title/encumbrance chain, the
owner's internal decision-making, and the cinema lease-termination record
remain unknown.

The current 98-unit site-design approval is not documented as dead. The City
granted a one-year extension in October 2025 and displays an expiration date of
October 16, 2026. As of this investigation date, the public record places the
project at an entitlement/preconstruction boundary rather than in active
construction.

## Cinema closure

Secondary sources consistently place the closure in May 2004. Contemporary Bay
Weekly coverage described an aging facility in need of renovation, including
old seating and physical-condition complaints, and later described strong
competition from the larger Annapolis Mall multiplex. Those facts support an
operating-feasibility hypothesis; they do not establish the actual closure
decision, lease termination, repair estimate, or operator rationale. See
findings 14696 and 14698.

## Redevelopment history and delay

1. In 2014, reporting described a roughly $45 million concept with about 200
   apartments, ground-floor retail, and structured parking. The developer said
   it was working on equity and had not begun shopping debt; no evidence was
   found that this concept obtained final approval or financing. See finding
   14699.
2. A later 127-unit planned-development proposal did not proceed as filed. The
   City's July 2017 density finding rejected the applicant's methodology and
   identified unresolved parking, landscaping, stormwater, scale, and massing
   issues. See finding 14715.
3. The applicants then advanced a different 98-unit mixed-use site plan. The
   City approved SDP2018-006 in November 2018. See finding 14709.
4. The Board of Appeals vacated and remanded the initial adequacy-of-public-
   facilities certification in 2020. A 2022 Circuit Court decision left
   stormwater, police, and automobile-transportation deficiencies for further
   action. See findings 14710-14711.
5. The City reissued certification in June 2023 after binding police, traffic,
   and stormwater mitigation. The visually checked official mitigation plan
   states an initial police payment of $51,336; the investigation database was
   corrected to that amount with an audit trail. See finding 14712.
6. An MDE covenant recorded in 2020 imposes soil-management, groundwater-use,
   construction, and vapor-mitigation controls on a 0.458-acre former dry-
   cleaner/gas-station area. It adds cost and procedure but does not by itself
   prohibit compliant development. See finding 14714.
7. In October 2025, the City granted a one-year extension after the applicant
   documented continued drawing and permit work. The project page displays
   `APPROVED` and an October 16, 2026 expiration date. See finding 14713.

## Current permit signal

The official public indexes searched through August 4, 2026 did not surface a
Lofts-linked construction permit after the extension. The post-extension hits
were an issued BGE right-of-way permit to replace an existing wireless antenna
at 919 Chesapeake and a void Palate Pleasers tenant-alteration application at
1023 Bay Ridge. Legacy records show a finalized $400,000 exterior-alteration
permit in 2022 and temporary mobile-office electrical service in 2021, neither
explicitly linked to Lofts construction. This is a bounded no-indexed-result,
not proof that no misindexed, nonpublic, or newly entered record exists. See
findings 14722-14726.
