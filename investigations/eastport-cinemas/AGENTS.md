# Eastport Cinemas Investigation Notes

## Scope

The user supplied the target name **Eastport Cinemas** and address
**5215 Chesapeake Avenue, Annapolis, MD 21403**, described as a long-closed
cinema in the Eastport Shopping Center. Treat the name, address, parcel, and
shopping-center relationship as identifiers to verify, not as established
facts.

The central questions are:

1. Which exact parcel or parcels comprise the former cinema site?
2. Who holds legal title, who controls each ownership entity, and who receives
   the economic benefit?
3. When and why did cinema operations cease, and what happened to the lease?
4. Which reopening, reuse, sale, or redevelopment proposals reached a concrete
   stage, and what primary records document their outcome?
5. Which title, financing, zoning, access, parking, environmental, structural,
   market, political, or litigation constraints best explain continued
   dormancy?

## Evidence Discipline

- Resolve the situs address against SDAT, parcel GIS, plats, and deeds before
  assuming that a record belongs to the cinema or the whole shopping center.
- Keep fee ownership, leasehold interests, management authority, guaranties,
  and beneficial/economic ownership separate.
- A Maryland resident agent, organizer, lawyer, or deed signatory is not by
  itself a beneficial owner.
- Do not infer that a proposal “died” from silence alone. Seek a withdrawal,
  denial, expiration, superseding application, sale, litigation record, or a
  dated statement from a responsible participant.
- Distinguish City of Annapolis jurisdiction from Anne Arundel County
  jurisdiction using authoritative boundary and parcel records.
- Treat reporting, business directories, cinema-history sites, and real-estate
  marketing pages as leads until corroborated by primary records.
- Do not treat valuable land or long vacancy as evidence of misconduct or
  intentional land banking without records establishing control, incentives,
  and conduct.
