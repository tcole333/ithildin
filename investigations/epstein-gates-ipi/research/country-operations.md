# Gates–IPI Four-Country Operations and Deliverables Ledger

Updated: 2026-07-15

This ledger separates planned activities, documented fieldwork, IPI's own outcome
claims, recipient records, and grant-specific costs. It uses a claim ladder rather
than making a direct CIA tasking or DNA-collection link the prerequisite for
analysis: documented methods and routing come first; strong inferences about
information position and leverage come second; deliberate tasking, weaponization,
or off-books allocation remain higher-risk hypotheses requiring additional proof.
An IPI claim that its intervention produced an operational outcome is recorded as
a claim unless an independent record confirms causation.

## Claim ladder and current assessment

### Documented facts

- Gates personnel sought political-security analysis, access mapping, influential
  contacts, convening, and locally granular action in Pakistan, Afghanistan,
  Nigeria, and Somalia (**EFTA00717832**; **EFTA00969185–EFTA00969193**).
- IPI proposed and used low-attribution methods: local interviewers, alternative
  entry points, discreet outreach, consultations presented as training or
  workshops, confidential interviews, oral elaboration, and—in Somalia—no Gates
  attribution (**EFTA01087635**; **EFTA00615247**; **EFTA00615279–EFTA00615280**).
- IPI repeatedly routed Gates-related proposals, agreements, and sensitive field
  products to Epstein, including the complete four-country package.
- Gates later testified that, after the initial introduction, he did not know
  Epstein remained connected to IPI. Asked why Epstein received IPI's reporting,
  Gates said it “doesn't make any sense” and that he did not understand why
  Rød-Larsen forwarded it because Epstein had no expertise in the subject
  ([official House transcript](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Bill-Gates-Transcript.pdf),
  p. 112; finding **#13360**).

### Strong reportable inference

Epstein occupied an undisclosed, or at minimum donor-unaware, parallel
recipient-side information position around Gates-funded political-security work.
The same legitimate practices that made the work feasible in dangerous settings—
confidentiality, low attribution, local intermediaries, non-state access, and oral
briefings—also made the complete context unusually valuable and vulnerable to
selective disclosure. That supports a theory of latent informational, relational,
and reputational leverage without requiring proof that Gates knowingly funded an
intelligence operation or that Epstein issued an explicit threat.

### Higher-risk hypotheses to keep testing

Epstein may have stayed in the reporting loop deliberately to acquire leverage;
used different role framings with Gates and IPI to control context; sought to
shield Gates attribution; or recognized that IPI's access maps, source networks,
and political-security reporting had broader governmental or private intelligence
utility. The current record does not establish tasking, later weaponization, or a
specific off-books allocation, but those are open branches—not prerequisites for
the documented information-governance and leverage story.

## Program-wide operating model

The originating chronology begins with the IPI presentation to Gates in Strasbourg
on **March 27, 2013**, not April 23. On April 3, Epstein reviewed and rejected or
edited IPI's draft follow-up (**EFTA01896150**). Robert Rosen's April 23 email then
described the earlier Strasbourg conversation as “invaluable” and said Gates
planned to formalize an IPI engagement (**EFTA02001601**). The sequence is thus:
March 27 introduction, April 3 Epstein-involved follow-up drafting, April 23 Gates
commitment signal.

The recovered June 2014 Nexus Centre package describes a political-security
support program rather than a conventional medical-delivery grant. IPI said its
work for the Gates Foundation and the Polio Eradication Initiative included:

- public-opinion research on health and security;
- analysis of militant groups opposing vaccination;
- mapping accessible and inaccessible areas;
- socio-political analysis of barriers to eradication;
- operational recommendations and implementation strategies; and
- access to political and religious decision-makers to facilitate vaccination
  rounds (**EFTA00615201–EFTA00615202**).

The package said confidential information was available on request. On June 7,
2014, Rød-Larsen forwarded Epstein the 83-page Nexus paper containing the Nigeria
and Somalia assessments and separate Pakistan and Afghanistan briefs
(**EFTA00990921**). This establishes a common analytical package received by
Epstein. It does not establish that he commissioned the research or that Gates did
not receive it.

### Low-attribution access model

The September 2013 one-year proposal described operational choices designed to
reduce visible Western involvement. IPI proposed local interviewers to limit the
appearance of Western intrusion; female interlocutors and “alternative entry
points”; consultations that could be presented as training or workshops; and
discreet, sensitive outreach to particular groups (**EFTA01087635**). These
methods have a straightforward safety-and-access rationale in areas where the
CIA's fake vaccination operation had intensified distrust. They also produced a
field architecture whose sources, access channels, and complete context could be
valuable beyond the public-health purpose and reputationally damaging if
selectively framed.

## Pakistan

### Fieldwork and access

- **EFTA00642394** — Before the executed grants, Hassan planned a low-profile
  April–May 2013 mission involving FATA representatives, tribal contacts, fixers,
  health officials, a possible Peshawar Corps Commander meeting, and private
  meetings in Islamabad. Her estimated cash budget was approximately $13,000,
  excluding hotels, and included fixer fees, per diems, and local cash outlays.
- **EFTA00587691** — The resulting May 2013 mission report describes tribal and
  government contacts, local access constraints, Taliban dynamics, and distrust
  aggravated by the CIA's fake vaccination operation.
- **EFTA01204624** — IPI's August 2014 update says it contacted senior Army
  officials during November–December 2013 and March–April 2014 field visits and
  made discreet, confidential requests. IPI claimed those efforts led to Army
  protection for vaccination teams and access to displaced children. That is an
  attributable IPI outcome claim, not independent proof of causation.
- **EFTA01922175**, **EFTA00864113**, and **EFTA00670737** — Hassan produced
  continuing incident, militant, and security analysis for Gates. Rød-Larsen or
  Pfanzelter also forwarded these products to Epstein.

### Money status

The September 2013 expense schedule (**EFTA00602793**) assigns $40,146 to an
Afghanistan/Pakistan consultant and $19,150 to a Pakistan mission, including
$11,890 for local communications, logistics, and representation. IPI's returns
later report $168,445 paid to Hassan in 2017 and $229,834 in 2018. Her January
2019 farewell says her research/reporting served BMGF during an active IPI grant
that ended in December 2018 (**EFTA01026864**). This establishes her project role
and grant-period service but not the exact Gates-funded share of her compensation.

## Afghanistan

**EFTA00615279–EFTA00615280** is labeled “Background Briefs for BMGF Security and
Access Strategy Session: Afghanistan” and “Confidential—Do Not Circulate.” It
describes Taliban conditions for vaccination access, alleged misreporting and poor
accounting, risks around the Afghan election and NATO/ISAF drawdown, and
cross-border vaccination recommendations. It says asterisked information could be
elaborated in oral IPI briefings.

This is clear evidence of sensitive political-security advice to Gates and of an
oral layer beyond the circulated document. The current file set contains no
Afghanistan travel claim, local-partner agreement, interview roster, consultant
contract, or grant-specific country actuals. Whether the information had broader
intelligence utility or recipients is a distinct research question; proving direct
CIA tasking or biological collection is not necessary to assess Epstein's private
information position.

## Nigeria

The April 2014 document **EFTA00586330** is marked “Confidential—Not for
distribution or circulation” and “Prepared for The Bill & Melinda Gates
Foundation.” Its methodology records six weeks of fieldwork across Borno, Yobe,
Bauchi, Jigawa, Kano, Katsina, Kaduna, Zamfara, Sokoto, and Kebbi
(**EFTA00586336**) and more than sixty interviews (**EFTA00586360**). Local
journalists and interlocutors conducted the work using semi-structured interviews
with a broad set of community sources.

The report recommended local, real-time political and security information
networks. It also warned that police and army channels had their own agendas and
that visibly linking health providers to security forces could make them targets.
Its stated model was civilian field research designed to avoid militarizing
vaccination, but it still generated granular source and security information whose
distribution and secondary use are independently important.

## Somalia

The Somalia assessment, embedded in **EFTA00615196**, records an October
2013–January 2014 rapid assessment and eight weeks of field research in Somalia
and Kenya (**EFTA00615246**). Regions included Bari, Bay, Middle Shabelle, Lower
Shabelle, and Banaadir. Interviewees included community and religious leaders,
businesspeople, health professionals, international organizations, Somali
officials and journalists, and independent researchers. The report states that all
interviews were confidential and that the Gates Foundation was not identified
(**EFTA00615247**; finding **#13363**). This is not merely a proposed tactic: it
documents completed field research prepared for Gates and conducted through a
no-attribution interview model. The exact grant allocation remains unresolved.

Its recommendations included public-opinion monitoring, radio and mobile-health
communications, direct engagement with clan and religious leaders, continued NGO
work in Al-Shabaab territory, and engagement with conservative clerics. Those are
sensitive access and influence strategies. The recovered text does not specify DNA
or medical-sample collection or CIA direction; the broader leverage hypothesis
rests on the documented confidential reporting, no-attribution methods, and hidden
parallel routing rather than requiring either claim.

## Recipient and control map

- Gates staff defined the desired service as political analysis, identification of
  influential contacts, convening, and action at a local or “granular” level
  (**EFTA00717832**; **EFTA00969185**).
- In May 2015, Pfanzelter reported that Gates global-development president Chris
  Elias considered Hassan's Pakistan/Afghanistan security information uniquely
  useful and was aware and appreciative of the Nigeria and Somalia work
  (**EFTA00856351**). This is IPI's account of Elias's reaction, but it is strong
  evidence that the foundation knew the character of the products.
- IPI generated country reports and continuing Pakistan incident analysis.
- Hassan and other IPI personnel delivered or prepared material for Gates.
- Rød-Larsen and Pfanzelter repeatedly routed the same or related products to
  Epstein, including the complete four-country package.
- In his June 2026 House testimony, Gates said he did not know Epstein remained
  connected to IPI after the March 27, 2013 introduction and could not explain why
  Rød-Larsen sent him the reports (official transcript p. 112).
- Current evidence therefore supports more than mere inclusion in a sensitive
  information loop: it establishes a donor-unaware parallel routing relationship.
  It does not yet show a particular product withheld from Gates, Epstein retasking
  field researchers, or later use of the information against Gates.
