# Gates–IPI Grant and Accounting Ledger

Updated: 2026-07-15

This ledger separates proposed budgets, executed commitments, reported payments,
IPI-wide audited accounts, and grant-specific actual expenditures. Those categories
are not interchangeable.

## Status definitions

- **Proposal**: a funding request or working budget; not proof of approval or spend.
- **Executed commitment**: an agreement signed by the funder and grantee.
- **Reported payment**: a grant row in the funder's Form 990-PF; evidence that the
  funder reported a payment, but not an IPI expense classification.
- **Organization-wide audited amount**: an IPI total covering all donors and
  programs; not attributable to Gates without a restricted-fund schedule.
- **Grant-specific actual**: an invoice, expense report, ledger, or budget-to-actual
  schedule tied to the grant. This is the principal missing layer.

## Public-funder comparison and accounting path

A 2013 Norad-commissioned Scanteam review supplies the clearest public description
of IPI's underlying accounting system. It found that IPI tracked revenue by donor
and preferred program or subprogram, and maintained genuine activity-based expense
records with more than thirty categories in one example. Travel could be separated
by ground transport, hotel, and meals; staff versus guests; and program area. But
the review also said detailed expenditures generally could not be traced uniquely
to a donor because funding was linked to programs rather than subcomponents.

This points to a three-level reconstruction: donor to IPI program, program to
detailed activity or expense, and only where the records permit, expense to a
single donor. It also means IPI's program ledgers and donor reports likely existed,
even if shared costs cannot always be uniquely assigned. The [Scanteam review,
pp. 21–22](https://www.norad.no/contentassets/93219c07286c4c86b5c2222de43bfbc2/cooperation-between-ipi-and-norway-current-and-proposed-framework-agreements/)
is thus a records map, not a Gates allocation.

Norway paid IPI NOK 47.708 million during 2013–2018: NOK 8 million in 2013,
12.554 million in 2014, 11.854 million in 2015, 5.8 million in 2016, 5 million in
2017, and 4.5 million in 2018. Its stated purposes included UN reform, peace
operations, Women, Peace and Security, and conflicts in the Middle East and Sahel.
Carnegie's filed rows separately show $573,700 during 2014–2017 for peacebuilding
in Africa, and the UN Foundation reported $150,000 in 2015 for “UN STRNGTHNG.”
These are not substitutes for the Gates project, but they are substantial competing
sources for shared institutional costs. Sources: [Norwegian MFA payment
summary](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/fakta-om-norsk-stotte-til-international-peace-institute-ipi/id3149627/)
and [Carnegie grantee record](https://carnegie.org/grantee/international-peace-institute-inc/).

The 2014 return describes Puri's $161,670 payment as **“consulting for India
exploration.”** IPI's 2014 annual report identifies him as an IPI vice president
and secretary-general of the separately described Independent Commission on
Multilateralism (ICM). The recovered Gates proposals and budgets name neither
Puri nor an India workstream. The corpus nevertheless shows a parallel access
role: Rød-Larsen sent Epstein Puri's contact card in June 2014
(**EFTA01918286**), and Epstein later introduced Puri to Reid Hoffman as “your
man in India” (**EFTA00669578**) and advised him to propose an India trip focused
on science, technology, and social-networking contacts (**EFTA00997875**).
Accordingly, the payment is established but its donor and grant allocation remain
unknown; the present evidence does not classify it as Gates-project spending.

Nasra Hassan's appearance is more directly connected to the Gates work. IPI
introduced her to Gates in July 2013 as a specialist consultant with on-the-ground
experience and contacts (**EFTA00717832**), and the September 2013 Seattle agenda
called her IPI's “Director, Pakistan Project” (**EFTA02725118**). Her reports were
later marked for Gates or produced at BMGF's request (**EFTA01922175**;
**EFTA00670737**). Most importantly, in January 2019 Hassan told Gates personnel
that “the IPI current grant ended at the end of 2018” and that she had continued
research and reporting beyond the contract period (**EFTA01026864**). IPI's 2017
and 2018 returns report $168,445 and $229,834 in compensation to her,
respectively—$398,279 in total. This makes the Gates polio project a strong
functional explanation for at least part of those payments, but the returns do
not name a grant or allocate her compensation. The ledger therefore records
**payee and project role established; grant-specific amount not established**.
The 990s also reveal nothing about contractors paid below the disclosure
threshold.

  recovered invoice, contract, wire, or ledger places either name beside the
  amount, so neither is an invoice-level identification.
- The $11,890 Pakistan “local communications, logistics, and representation” line
  likely aggregated multiple cash payees and categories. **EFTA00642394** proposed
  $1,050 for a main fixer, $550 for a deputy fixer, $1,000 for an appointments
  contact, roughly $5,000 for tribal-representative honoraria and per diems, and
  $2,000 for car, driver, petrol, and small cash. The proposal does not reconcile
  these planned figures to the later actual line or identify recipients.
- **EFTA01113710** generally barred reimbursement of costs incurred before the
  October 23, 2013 start date, but expressly excepted costs noted in an attached
  document titled “IPI Support for Polio Eradication.” **EFTA00602793**, the
  $81,545 expense schedule, has that exact title. The attachment is missing from
  the recovered agreement and no bank-level reimbursement record has been found;
  the documents therefore establish authorization language consistent with
  reimbursement, not proof that Gates funds reimbursed these exact expenses.
- No later invoice set, consultant contract, payroll allocation, project ledger,
  bank receipt schedule, or budget-to-actual report tied to OPP1096058 has yet been
  recovered.

## Current evidentiary conclusion

The record establishes $8 million in Gates payments during 2013–2018 and another
$1,913,936 during 2019–2024. It also shows that IPI's travel and consulting
expenses expanded during the original program's launch and that the institution
maintained more granular activity accounting than its public audits reveal. The
present file does not yet assign a particular contractor, country activity, or
information-gathering operation to Gates funds. The missing amendment,
grant-specific actuals, and public-funder files are the best path to making or
excluding that attribution.
