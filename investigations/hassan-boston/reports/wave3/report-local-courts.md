## Family agreement and business-record evidence

The original December 14, 2000 agreement remains the central missing ownership document. Paragraph 23 of the official complaint quotes the alleged five-percent provision, but the ten-page paper 1 image ended with the complaint and did not contain its stated Exhibit A. The 2022 stipulation contains no economic or ownership terms. An earlier observation about a 23-page paper 11 is not used to establish document absence because that packet was not preserved or reviewed page by page.

Paper 65 is a 40-page official exhibit packet. An independent full-page visual and OCR audit documented its boundaries in `reports/wave3/review-paper65-exhibits.md`: Exhibits A–H are 2021–22 discovery orders, deadline motions, and record-production correspondence; Exhibit I is Hicham's affidavit at pages 36–40. The packet does **not** contain the executed December 14, 2000 agreement.

Hicham's signed affidavit states on page 37, **“My name is Hicham Ali Hassan. I go by ‘Sam.’”** This confirms that this person used Sam, but it does not make every Sam Hassan record his or create a context-free alias rule. Pages 38–39 state that commercial rent checks from buildings he said he owned and controlled through trusts were endorsed by an assistant and deposited into his business accounts. Those are sworn statements, not bank records, beneficial schedules, or independent tracing of cash flows. Page 40 carries the signature under penalties of perjury; the exact February 2022 day is blank, while the court filing date is February 22, 2022. Findings **15657, 15658**.

An accountant email at paper 65 page 34 says the recipient then had all Hassan real-estate-entity bookkeeping workpapers held on the accountant's laptop, that earlier-year workpapers and files for Eighteen Brimmer or Hassan Residential Properties were not held, and that no audits had been performed. This establishes what the filed email says, not the actual universe or absence of records. Finding **15671**.
