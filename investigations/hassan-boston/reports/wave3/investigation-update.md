# Ownership, financing and court-record update

September 5, 2026 · Investigation `hassan-boston` · Third research wave

This wave added **53 sourced findings**, including explicitly labeled identity
candidates, bringing the investigation to **177 findings**. The updated
timeline contains **506 event observations across 75 property/context groups**.
Groups include leased premises and unresolved address references; they are not
a count of properties owned. The [93-row spelling register](name-variants.csv)
keeps observed names, candidate variants and matching requirements separate.

The new records extend the property chronology and resolve several important
court outcomes. They also establish that Hicham pledged property to secure Abdul Rahman's
note, and document the legal mechanism of a 2018 Concepts merger. Neither
development establishes kinship, the subjects' aggregate wealth or the origin
of all acquisition funds.

## Identity evidence

Hicham's signed affidavit in the Tarek v. Hicham case directly says, “My name is Hicham
Ali Hassan. I go by 'Sam.'” This establishes his use of that alias, while each
separate Sam record still needs context to identify the person. The affidavit
also describes commercial rents going into his business accounts and buildings
held through trusts he controls. Those are his sworn statements about how business affairs were handled. They
do not by themselves establish property-specific cash flows, beneficiary
percentages or the origin of acquisition capital.

## Trustee changes: the dates matter

Two original Plymouth instruments identify the trustee changes behind
**400 Boylston Street Realty Trust**. In **28008/229**, Hicham invokes
beneficiary directions to remove Abdul Rahman. In **28008/230**, Zouhair
resigns. Both were executed and acknowledged **December 14, 2000**, then
recorded **April 21, 2004**. They cross-reference the Suffolk **17988/306** and
Plymouth **11586/224** declarations already linked in the prior wave.

The removal describes Hicham as sole trustee. Yet Hicham, Abdul Rahman and
Zouhair all signed Hull deed **19629/323**, executed **March 30, 2001**, as
trustees. The reviewed records do not explain that later signature set or the
recording delay. The timeline preserves both dates and the conflicting capacity
statements; it does not infer an uninterrupted Hicham-only interval beginning
in 2000 or decide the instruments' legal effect.

December 14, 2000 is also the date of the agreement described in Tarek's later
complaint. The original trustee instruments do not identify that agreement,
its consideration, or beneficial allocations. Matching dates supply a lead,
not proof that a particular buyout occurred.

**Hassull Realty Trust** has a distinct capacity issue. The original 1987
sixteen-page declaration permits a single trustee to execute instruments and
makes property decisions subject to beneficiary directions. Both Gregory
Sullivan and Hicham signed the 2002 and 2004 certificates as trustees; Gregory's
single signature on the accompanying 2004 deed therefore does not establish
that Hicham had left office. An August 14, 2017 certificate calls Gregory the
current trustee and Hicham the conditional successor, while the August 30 deed
names and is signed by both as trustees. No intervening appointment or
resignation was found. These papers disclose capacities and powers, not
beneficiary identities or percentages. [Plymouth source report](report-plymouth.md).

## Middlesex: ownership, lending and address evidence diverge

A fully reviewed registered-land deed, **document 1354544**, establishes
**Tarek Ali Hassan's Lexington acquisition** in 2004, with **$795,000
deed-stated consideration**. Transfer certificate **232457** confirms fee-simple ownership at issuance, but
the viewer's cancellation field supplies no verified successor. An exact
certificate search returned seven entries, ending with two November 2004
discharges of the seller's mortgages, and no later deed or successor entry.
That bounded result does not establish present title. The town-linked
**FY2026 assessor record** separately lists **HASSAN TAREK ALI** as owner of
record and repeats the 2004 deed locator and sale information. It assesses the
parcel at **$1.385 million**; this is an assessment, not net equity, market-sale
proceeds or certified current title. The full name
and the North Reading address also used in other investigated records provide
the identity bridge. They do not alone establish a family relationship.

The bounded North Reading address search returned a different owner's
indexed chain. An address appearing in Tarek's and Zouhair's documents is
therefore retained as an identity/context clue; it is not counted as a Hassan
property simply because they used it.

The complete six-page Groton mortgage **83538/298** shows **Houssam Ali-Hassan
as mortgagee**, securing two notes totaling **$562,000**. He was the creditor;
the instrument does not give him property title. The three-page **84057/291**,
recorded May 6, 2025 and indexed as a discharge, states that payoff shall be
satisfied by $562,000 with no interest or daily interest. The wording is a
payoff statement, not independent proof of payment. The index's blanket party
labels are superseded by the original instruments' actual capacities.

## Court outcomes now distinguish allegations, judgments and payment

**Tarek v. Hicham, 2084CV00531, settled.** The public court docket records a
with-prejudice dismissal filed August 17 and entered August 18, 2022, with
costs and appeal rights waived. The original two-page stipulation was read in
full. It discloses no payment or ownership terms. The inconsistent Open header
does not override the operative settlement and dismissal entries.

The original ten-page complaint alleges that the December 14, 2000 agreement
reserved Tarek five-percent beneficial interests in three trusts and minority
corporate interests. The executed agreement has not been identified in the reviewed documents.
The public complaint image omits its referenced Exhibit A, and an independent
visual review of all forty pages of paper 65 confirmed that the later
discovery packet does not contain it. The [page-by-page audit](review-paper65-exhibits.md)
records that check; the complete signed affidavit is on pages 37–40. The pleading's
quoted language remains an allegation about an agreement, and does not
establish current ownership or the terms of the 2022 settlement.

**The Brattle Street judgment was satisfied.** The December 4, 2018 opinion in
**1781CV02053** establishes the lease and guaranty context and allows summary
judgment. The later public court docket supplies successive judgment versions,
culminating in a corrected **$557,359.11** judgment against **Hicham and Paloma
Tannery LLC**. On **February 18, 2020**, it records voluntary dismissal of the
appeal and **satisfaction of judgment in full**. Earlier awards and attachment
limits overlap with this obligation and are not additional current debts.
The opinion also identifies **ALAIA LLC** as an unnamed nephew's replacement
tenant; it does not establish which nephew or that company's exact registry
identity.

**Tivoli has a judgment against the LLC; appeals were entered.** In
**2184CV00205**, the March 18, 2026 amended judgment awards Tivoli
**$420,026.84 against 216–218 Newbury Street Realty LLC**. A June 29 attachment
cap of **$405,000** accounts for an unspecified credited payment and interest;
it is not an additional award or a precise current balance. The docket lists
appeals **2026-J-0711** and **2026-P-1139**, the latter entered August 5, 2026.
The reviewed trial docket does not show satisfaction, an appellate disposition,
or a personal judgment against Hicham. The appellate dockets were not
independently checked for their latest status.

**Rainbows Pottery remains pending.** In **2484CV00348**, Count VII's stipulated
dismissal and the April 8, 2026 partial summary-judgment ruling do not dispose
of the whole case. The latter allowed the motion only for Count VIII insofar
as it sought relief under G.L. c. 12, § 11H, and otherwise denied it. A final
trial conference was scheduled for October 6, 2027 when checked. No final
liability or damages conclusion is drawn.

**Earlier Brimmer cases have distinct outcomes.** In **0484CV01773**, the
June 2007 jury result left the contractor plaintiff with no recovery and the
trust with no recovery on its counterclaim; both were dismissed on the merits.
In **0684CV00302**, the December 30, 2009 amended judgment established a
**$22,373 mechanics lien**, plus post-judgment interest and costs, against the
Eighteen Brimmer trust's property interest. The appeal was withdrawn in 2010.
No satisfaction was identified, which does not establish a present balance or
current enforceability. The separate Sugar Heaven Superior matter was dismissed
without prejudice for lack of prosecution. Its legacy small-claims reference
resolves to **BMC Central 0301SC007620**, naming **Zouhair** as defendant. That
docket is closed but contains both a June 2005 dismissal entry and later
judgment/execution proceedings, including execution issued November 30. The
reviewed display supplies no judgment amount or satisfaction, so closure is
not characterized as payment. Another BMC Central matter, **0401CV002935**,
naming Hicham and Zouhair, was dismissed without prejudice under Rule 4(j)
in August 2005; that is not a merits determination. Related summary-process
cases record a 2004 defense win and a 2008 judgment in Hicham's favor totaling
**$58,214.91**. After partial collection, a March 2009 execution stated a
then-unpaid **$50,395.84**. The reviewed docket does not establish that remainder
as a current balance.

These outcomes come from the Massachusetts Trial Court's public case-access
service and the specifically identified original documents. The service warns
that its display is not the official court record and may omit recent
activity. [Civil-court report](report-local-courts.md) and
[detailed primary-source docket readings](../../evidence/wave3/local-courts/masscourts-official-dockets-2026-09-05.md).

## Concepts: a documented merger, undisclosed economics

A registry-certified French packet includes Concepts International LLC's
October 2, 2014 agreement identifying **Tarek as sole member**. All sixteen
pages were reviewed. That is a dated ownership point, not proof that sole
ownership continued through subsequent transactions.

Original Massachusetts filing **201826378650** establishes that **JORDAN
MERGERSUB, LLC**, formed in Delaware on July 17, 2018 and listing an office
**c/o Amazon.com, Inc.**, merged **into Concepts** effective **August 9, 2018**.
Concepts survived. Tarek signed as duly authorized. A September amendment adds
Tarek as manager; the November report lists him alongside Arun Rajan and Scott
Schaefer. These management and signing powers do not disclose equity shares.

The merger certificate refers to an adopted Agreement and Plan of Merger,
but does not include it. It supplies neither the purchase price, Jordan's
ultimate owner, post-closing percentages, recipients of proceeds nor a flow
into real estate. The Amazon address is an explicit documentary connection;
it does not alone establish ultimate ownership.

A separate USPTO index sequence records Paloma's 2015 CNCPTS trademark transfer
to Concepts, then Concepts' transfer of five marks to Delaware **CNCPTS LLC**
on July 31, 2025, recorded October 20. The 2025 assignment pages failed to load;
these are index-level observations with no stated consideration. They are not
evidence of the 2018 deal price.

Free-copy checks for the missing Alana and Nader bankruptcy schedules,
amendments, sale papers and final accounts found metadata but not the needed
filing PDFs. Precise missing docket entries remain listed in the
[capital report](report-capital.md).

## Probate, divorce and remaining coverage

The approved CAPTCHA was completed and the public MassCourts search became
available. The civil-court track preserved **169 submissions**: person and
business-prefix searches across Suffolk, Middlesex, Norfolk and Plymouth
Superior Courts and statewide Land Court, plus the six canonical names in
BMC Central, four Housing divisions and three District Courts. These are
explicit search scopes, not a claim to have searched every court or spelling.
Four Plymouth Superior cases bearing Hicham's full name remain identity
candidates because the reviewed records did not connect them to the known
property businesses. The [civil search matrix](../../evidence/wave3/local-courts/coverage.csv)
retains unsearched entity cells and divisions.

The probate/family track made **54 submissions**: all **40 priority
county/name cells**, **13 additional Plymouth transliterations**, and one
Suffolk diagnostic. No case was sufficiently identity-resolved to add an
ownership or kinship finding. Two Hicham name candidates remain explicitly
unresolved; neither supplies a property bridge. No minor's details or unrelated
family narrative was extracted.

The matrix preserves **39 unexecuted additional transliteration cells**. Public
remote coverage reaches all Probate and Family divisions from 2000, with some
older records; original-image coverage is narrower. These scoped results do
not exclude older, inaccessible, differently indexed or differently spelled
cases. [Search-by-search coverage](report-probate-family.md).

Remaining work includes the executed 2000 family agreement; public evidence
of the 2022 settlement economics; the 2018 merger plan and consideration;
missing bankruptcy filings; remaining original instruments and later title
continuations; and the explicitly unsearched court variants. These are tracked
as gaps, not silently converted into negative findings. Recorded trustee
capacity, assessed owner, registered title, beneficiary interest and creditor
status remain separate throughout the data.
