## Money, instruments and legal records

**Three named trustees in a 1993 acquisition.** The original deed at [Suffolk 17988/312](https://www.masslandrecords.com/SUFFOLK/D/Default.aspx) names Hicham Ali Hassan, Abdul Rahman Ali Hassan and Zouhair Ali Hassan as trustees of 400 Boylston Street Realty Trust. Boylston Boston Corporation is the grantor; the first page describes 392/394 and 396–398 Boylston parcels. The reviewed page contains a corrected consideration of $1,925,000. This establishes a shared trustee transaction and a financing question, not equal beneficiary shares or the origin of the purchase funds. Finding #15578 records the original. The deed resolves the full-name expansion of “HASSAN ABDUL R” for this instrument; other abbreviated entries require their own context.

**Brimmer acquisition and later transfer.** A December 1997 foreclosure deed at [Suffolk 21956/120](https://www.masslandrecords.com/SUFFOLK/D/Default.aspx) conveys 18 Brimmer from Institutional Asset LLC to Zouhair as trustee of Eighteen Brimmer Street Realty Trust. It states $325,000 paid but expressly leaves the property subject to a June 1994 mortgage at 19143/176 and other encumbrances. In August 2016, the deed at 56617/279 names Hicham as trustee conveying Brimmer to Hassan Residential Properties LLC for less than $100. The original trust is referenced at 21956/113. The records do not establish full acquisition economics, the source of money, beneficiary continuity or why the trustee changed. Findings #15572, #15569 and synthesis #15577 preserve those distinctions.

**Newbury title and a recent attachment.** An August 2016 deed at [Suffolk 56617/267](https://www.masslandrecords.com/SUFFOLK/D/Default.aspx) transfers two Newbury parcels from Hicham as trustee of 216-218 Newbury Street Realty Trust to the corresponding LLC for less than $100. Its predecessor trust is at 20592/119 and prior deed at 20630/164. In July 2026, an attachment recorded at 72957/242 names $405,000 and attaches the LLC's interest in the Newbury property in the Tivoli Audio litigation. This is a dated recorded attachment, not a verified outstanding balance or Hicham's personal debt. Findings #15570 and #15573.

The underlying Tivoli opinions granted the tenant summary judgment in 2022; the July 2023 order required return of a $58,548 deposit with specified interest and awarded $241,620.30 in fees/costs; a July 2025 order denied relief and added $100,000 in fees. The orders and later attachment overlap and must not be summed as independent debts. [2023 monetary order](https://www.nutter.com/assets/htmldocuments/newbury%20vs.%20tivoli.pdf), [2025 order](https://masslawyersweekly.com/files/2025/08/09-111-25.pdf), findings #15574–15576.

**A documented borrowing link.** Suffolk Book 19679/Page 1 records a 1995 mortgage naming Hicham and Zouhair as trustees of 376 Boylston Street Realty Trust, securing a $1 million Berkshire Life Insurance Company note on 372–378 Boylston. The index references a 2001 release at 27659/170; release scope must be read before any current-balance conclusion. This supports a joint fiduciary/business role, not a conclusion about kinship or equal ownership. Finding #15539 and the [deed/finance report](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/investigations/hassan-boston/reports/report-agent-d.md) preserve the original image.

**A family ownership agreement to retrieve.** The July 2020 Tarek/Hicham opinion describes pleaded allegations concerning a December 14, 2000 settlement, a family-interest buyout, Tarek's asserted 5% beneficial interests in three trusts, and positions in two corporations. The judge assumed those allegations for pleading motions; the opinion did not adjudicate ownership. The original agreement, exhibits and later disposition are essential next records. Two trust names appear anomalously in the printed opinion and have not been silently corrected into later LLC names. [Court opinion](https://masslawyersweekly.com/files/2020/08/Rule-12c.SOL_.hassan.pdf), findings #15505–15506.

The later status is a stipulated dismissal: [a Trellis reproduction of the filed instrument](https://trellis.law/doc/270321173/party-s-file-stipulation-dismissal-filed-8-17-22-as-to-plff-vs-defts-with-prejudice-without-costs-with-all-rights-appeal-waived-judgment-entered-on-docket-pursuant-to-mass-r-civ-p-58-a-as-amended-notice-sent-to-parties-pursuant-to-mass) states dismissal of claims and counterclaims with prejudice in August 2022, with costs and appeal rights waived; its docket reproduction records the case as settled. The official docket/original PDF and settlement terms were not obtained. This later disposition does not adjudicate the asserted ownership shares. Finding #15579 and the [disposition supplement](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/investigations/hassan-boston/reports/report-family-case-outcome.md) preserve the access and provenance limits.

**Acquisition funding still unresolved.** In Fienberg v. Hassan, the Appeals Court described a $4.5 million 2008 Boylston offer with a $500,000 deposit and in 2010 directed conveyance. It supplies dates and amounts to match to a deed, mortgage and closing evidence; it does not identify the source of the deposit or final purchase funds. [Opinion](https://www.masscasesarchive.com/masscases.com/cases/app/77/77massappct901.html), finding #15508.

**Operating-company liabilities are not family net worth.** Nader's initial July 10, 2017 schedules reported $0 assets and $2,500,035.02 liabilities, including $986,207.26 in priority IRS claims. Hicham's signed statement reported a 100% interest in Nader. Other schedules identify overlapping Alana/Paloma obligations and possible personal guarantees. The case later reopened and had amendment-related activity, so the initial figures are not final allowed claims or current debts. Shared obligations cannot be counted repeatedly across companies. [Original schedules](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mab.476804/gov.uscourts.mab.476804.9.0.pdf), findings #15509–15512, #15540–15541.

**The article's pending AG case has an outcome.** The August 2021 consent judgment imposed a defined Massachusetts public-accommodation operating injunction and a $220,000 obligation, half suspended for compliance. It contains nonadjudication/nonadmission terms. It does not prove that $220,000 was paid or establish a trial verdict. [Signed consent judgment](https://www.mass.gov/doc/tannery-final-judgment/download), finding #15504.

**Potential capital sources remain attributed leads.** Reported early Frye/Reebok supplier credit and the reported 2018 Zappos investment in Concepts are useful routes to agreements and financial exhibits. Neither establishes funds invested in property, and no verified amount or equity percentage was obtained for the Concepts transaction. [Initiating article](https://www.bostonmagazine.com/news/2019/01/16/the-tannery-sam-hassan/), [2018 investment reporting](https://hypebeast.com/2018/8/concepts-amazon-zappos-partnership-investment-streetwear-industry), findings #15496/#15499. Missing origin-of-funds evidence is an unresolved question, not evidence of illicit money.
