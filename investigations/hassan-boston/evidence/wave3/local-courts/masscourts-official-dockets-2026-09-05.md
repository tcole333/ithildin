> Party(s) file Stipulation of Dismissal (filed 8/17/22) as to plff vs defts with prejudice and without costs and with all rights of appeal waived ... Applies To: Hassan, Tarek Ali (Plaintiff); Hassan, Hicham Ali (Defendant); 216-218 Newbury Street Corporation Inc (Defendant); 419 Boylston Street Corporation, Inc. (Defendant)

The October 7, 2022 event entry says that the October 13 Rule 56 hearing was canceled for the reason “Case Settled.” The disposition and operative entry therefore control over the inconsistent Open header for this review.

The official two-page image of docket paper 78, filed August 17, 2022, was read in full. It is titled **Stipulation of Dismissal with Prejudice** and states that, under Mass. R. Civ. P. 41(a)(1)(ii), “all claims, counterclaims and cross-claims that were or could have been asserted in the above-captioned action by, among and/or between Plaintiff and the Defendants be dismissed with prejudice and without costs, and with all rights of appeal waived.” It is dated August 17, 2022 and bears signature blocks for Hicham Ali Hassan and Tarek Ali Hassan through counsel. It contains no payment, ownership-transfer, release-valuation or other economic settlement term.

### Original pleading and missing agreement

The official image link for docket paper 1 served a ten-page complaint. All ten pages were visually inspected. Paragraphs 18–24 are allegations in Tarek's pleading, not adjudicated facts. They allege that Hicham instructed attorney Craig Gilmartin of Gilmartin Magence to draft an agreement around December 2000; that Tarek and Hicham entered a document captioned Settlement Agreement on December 14, 2000 at the lawyer's office; and that Zouhair Ali Hassan, Houssam Ali Hassan, Talal Ali Hassan and Tarek would sell various interests to Hicham. Paragraph 22 alleges that Hicham would pay money to Zouhair, Houssam and Talal while Tarek would retain a five-percent beneficial interest in the trusts and become a director and minority shareholder in the corporations.

Paragraph 23 quotes the alleged agreement as follows:

> Notwithstanding any language herein to the contrary, it is understood and agreed that Tarek will hold a five per cent (5%) beneficial interest in the 376 Boylston Street Realty Trust, in the 400 Boylston Street Realty Trust, and in the Eighteen Brimmer Street Realty Trust. Furthermore, Tarek will be a Director and minority stockholder in the 419 Boylston Street Corporation, Inc., and in the 216-218 Newbury Street Corporation, Inc. Tarek's beneficial interests in the Trusts noted above and his holding of stock and acting as a Director of the Corporations noted above, shall not require Tarek to pay any compensation, adjustment or setoff to Zouhair, Houssam, Talal or Hicham.

Paragraph 24 alleges that all parties executed the agreement. The complaint says a copy is attached as Exhibit A, but the official image served for paper 1 ends after ten complaint pages and contains no exhibit. The official image for the amended complaint, docket paper 24, identifies an eleven-page document; it likewise is too short to contain the pleading plus the multi-page agreement and correspondence referenced as exhibits. No separately titled December 14, 2000 agreement appears in the public docket list. The executed agreement itself therefore remains unreviewed. The quoted clause is evidence of what the filed pleading alleged the agreement said; it is not an independently authenticated copy of the agreement and does not establish that the claimed ownership survived the 2022 settlement.

## 1781CV02053 — Stearns Brattle Street, LLC et al. v. Hassan, Hicham Ali et al.

Canonical reference: `MASSCOURTS:1781CV02053`. Middlesex Superior Court. Filed July 10, 2017. Plaintiffs are Stearns Brattle Street, LLC, Marshall W. Stearns Trust, LLC and Brattle Square Properties LLC. Defendants are Hicham Ali Hassan and Paloma Tannery, LLC; four banks appear as trustee defendants.

The official docket confirms the November 7, 2018 Rule 56 hearing and the December 4, 2018 memorandum allowing plaintiffs' motion for summary judgment. It then supplies the quantified judgment and later outcome absent from the eight-page liability opinion:

- April 11, 2019, docketed April 17: the court clarified its order and awarded the plaintiffs USD 267,356.86 in rent damages against Hicham Ali Hassan and Paloma Tannery, LLC **jointly and severally**, plus costs and interest.
- July 19, 2019, docketed July 23 and corrected: plaintiffs were awarded USD 184,012.52 in reasonable attorney fees and costs.
- August 15, 2019: judgment listed USD 267,356.86 damages, USD 370 filing fees and USD 184,012.52 court-ordered fees, with a total of USD 519,117.05.
- October 7, 2019: an amended judgment listed USD 267,356.86 damages, USD 370 filing fees and USD 227,634.25 court-ordered fees, with a total of USD 546,380.02.
- November 8, 2019, entered as of October 7: a corrected judgment listed USD 329,354.86 in the damages field, USD 370 filing fees and USD 227,634.25 court-ordered fees, with a total of **USD 557,359.11**, against Hicham Ali Hassan and Paloma Tannery, LLC.
- December 6, 2019: defendants filed a third notice of appeal from the corrected judgment and related rulings.
- February 18, 2020: the docket records a voluntary dismissal of the appeal under Mass. R. App. P. 29(a), followed by “SATISFACTION OF JUDGMENT IN FULL.”

The successive judgment totals and component figures are overlapping revisions of one obligation and must not be added together. The corrected USD 557,359.11 total is the last judgment figure shown. Although the page header still displays Open, the disposition table says Disposed by Court Finding as of August 15, 2019, and the later docket expressly records satisfaction in full. The reviewed docket therefore does not support treating any of these judgment figures as a current unpaid debt.

The earlier July 19, 2017 trustee-process ruling allowed attachment of defendants' Eastern Bank account or accounts up to USD 270,000 and denied the motion in other respects. This was a prejudgment attachment cap, not a separate damages award. The same docket later records the judgment as fully satisfied.
