## Bottom line

The public record supports one current, direct carrier edge with high specificity: **Global Crossing Airlines (GlobalX) says its contract with CSI Aviation for U.S.-government work runs through the end of 2026.** GlobalX's filed 2025 Form 10-K separately establishes that it is a Part 121 Airbus A320-family operator and that its ACMI model supplies aircraft, crew, maintenance, and insurance. Those records make GlobalX the strongest confirmed current operator in CSI's government carrier layer.

The final GlobalX-to-ICE step remains composite rather than a single clean federal record. A May 2025 House Homeland Security letter, expressly relying on media reporting, called GlobalX a CSI subcontractor and said it operated 74 percent of ICE removal flights over the preceding year. A January 2026 Massachusetts governor's release identified both GlobalX and Eastern Air Express as airlines providing ICE flights. Taken with the independently established CSI–ICE prime chain in lead 57836, this produces a high-confidence GlobalX–ICE assessment, but not a primary federal award or unredacted teaming agreement naming GlobalX.

**Eastern Air Express is currently connected to ICE by an official state-government release, but no reviewed primary record connects Eastern to CSI.** Its DOT authority and 2025 BTS fleet record prove that it is a certificated carrier with compatible aircraft; they do not prove which Eastern aircraft, if any, flew for ICE or CSI.

**Avelo is historical, not current.** Its DHS-charter participation began in May 2025 and the company said it would end that work and close its Mesa base on January 27, 2026. Public reporting connected that work to ICE and CSI, but this review found no primary CSI teaming document. Avelo is therefore excluded from the current roster.

**CSI's January 2026 acquisition of Air Wisconsin is confirmed, but no ICE assignment is.** SEC filings establish CSI's acquisition of 100 percent of Air Wisconsin Airlines LLC and 13 CRJ200 aircraft, with the sale closing January 9 after DOT approval. The acquired carrier and aircraft are current CSI assets/capabilities, not proven ICE Air resources.

No aircraft tail can be responsibly marked as a current CSI/ICE Air aircraft from the reviewed public primary record. That zero is a result, not a gap to be filled by inference.

## Contract structure relevant to the roster

Lead 57836 established the upstream chain:

`GSA MAS -> ICE BPA 70CDCR24A00000001 -> CSI Aviation prime -> subcontracted operational layers`

The BPA statement of work makes the prime responsible for the performance of teaming partners and subcontractors. It requires at least 12 daily-schedule large aircraft for exclusive availability, each capable of carrying at least 148 adults, plus aircraft, pilots, flight crews, security guards, medical personnel, and related operational support. It also requires the contractor to give the contracting officer's representative an aircraft registration number, crew identification, and crew qualifications no later than 12 hours before departure. Thus, the authoritative tail and operator roster should exist inside ICE/CSI operational records even though it is not public in the reviewed files.

The Classic Air protest supplies the clearest explanation for the public-record break after iAero. The redacted amended complaint says ICE told offerors that iAero's documents were no longer useful after iAero ceased normal operations on April 7, 2024. It says CSI then submitted new letters of commitment, airworthiness certificates, operations specifications, and teaming agreements in the October 2024 Round 4 discussions. Those are precisely the records that should name the replacement carrier(s) and authorized aircraft, but the public version masks the relevant names and aircraft information. This is a litigant's pleading quoting the administrative record, not a merits holding.

## Carrier findings

### 1. GlobalX — confirmed direct CSI/U.S.-government relationship through 2026

GlobalX's March 5, 2026 results transcript, furnished to the SEC, states:

> “We also extended our CSI Aviation contract with the U.S. government through the end of 2026 …”

This is the strongest current direct carrier evidence. It identifies the carrier, CSI, the U.S.-government customer class, and the extension's end date in one primary company statement. It does **not** name ICE, the BPA, call `70CDCR26FC0000001`, a route, a tail, or an individual crew member.

GlobalX's 2025 10-K describes the operational role. It says GlobalX operates a U.S. Part 121 domestic flag and supplemental airline using Airbus A320-family aircraft; under ACMI it provides aircraft, crew, maintenance, and insurance, while under full-service charter it provides passenger aircraft and most operating inputs. The distinction matters: GlobalX is evidence of an actual aircraft/crew operator in the chain, not merely another intermediary.

DOT Order 2021-9-10 reissued Global Crossing certificates authorizing interstate and foreign charter air transportation of persons, property, and mail and registered `GlobalX` as its trade name. This establishes regulatory authority, not a government-customer or ICE assignment.

**Assessment:**

- GlobalX ↔ CSI: **direct/current/confirmed**, through 2026-12-31, based on GlobalX's own SEC-furnished statement.
- GlobalX ↔ U.S. government: **direct/current/confirmed** within the same company statement.
- GlobalX ↔ ICE: **composite/high confidence**, supported by the House letter's reporting-derived assertion, Massachusetts's official release, and the separately established CSI–ICE prime chain.
- Any GlobalX tail ↔ CSI/ICE: **not established**.

### 2. Eastern Air Express — official ICE edge; CSI edge unresolved

On January 8, 2026, Massachusetts's governor issued a release and letters aimed at the executives of GlobalX and Eastern Air Express, characterizing both as private airlines providing ICE flights. The source is an official state statement, but it is not an ICE procurement record and supplies no contract number, subcontract, flight leg, or aircraft identifier.

DOT Order 2024-10-1 authorizes Eastern Air Express to engage in foreign charter air transportation of persons, property, and mail. FAA materials also treat Eastern as the regulated employer/operator of pilots, flight attendants, and maintenance personnel; a July 2026 FAA proposed-penalty notice is an allegation and is used here only to confirm the operator/employer context, not to infer ICE work.

The official 2025 BTS Form 41 Schedule B-43 file lists 18 Eastern aircraft, 15 with operating status `Y`. Three operating 737-700s have exactly 148 reported seats: `N660CP`, `N668CP`, and `N959BP`. Those aircraft match the SOW's minimum seating threshold, but capacity compatibility and presence in the operator's fleet do not establish assignment to ICE or CSI. None is promoted to the ICE/CSI roster.

**Assessment:**

- Eastern ↔ ICE: **official-state-source/high confidence**.
- Eastern ↔ CSI: **unresolved**; reporting names the relationship, but no primary teaming or award record reviewed here does.
- Eastern tail ↔ ICE/CSI: **zero confirmed**.

### 3. Avelo — historical only

Contemporaneous public accounts and an Avelo spokesperson's later statement place Avelo's DHS-charter operation at Mesa from May 12, 2025 through January 27, 2026. In January, the company said it would close the base and conclude all participation in the DHS charter program. The August 2025 Associated Press monitoring report put Avelo with GlobalX and Eastern among the three carriers accounting for about 80 percent of tracked deportation-related flights at that point. That is monitoring-derived secondary evidence, not an ICE vendor disclosure.

The 2025 BTS B-43 record lists 22 Avelo aircraft with operating status `Y`, including 189-seat 737-800s `N801XT`, `N802XT`, and `N803XT`. Those tails have been associated with the Mesa operation in reporting, but the BTS file itself proves only Avelo's fleet. This review found no primary ICE/CSI operational record assigning those tails.

**Assessment:** historical operator through 2026-01-27; excluded from the current roster; no direct primary CSI edge and zero confirmed ICE/CSI tails in this analysis.

### 4. Air Wisconsin — acquired capability, not an ICE assignment

Harbor Diversified's December 2025 8-K states that CSI agreed to acquire 100 percent of Air Wisconsin Airlines LLC and 13 Bombardier CL-600-2B19 CRJ200 aircraft. A January 2026 8-K confirms the sale closed January 9 after DOT approval.

The acquisition gives CSI a direct ownership relationship with a Part 121 carrier and aircraft assets. It does not show that Air Wisconsin or the 13 CRJ200s were placed on the ICE BPA. The CRJ200 type also does not satisfy the BPA's 148-adult DSLA minimum in its ordinary passenger configuration. It may have relevance to small-aircraft or other government missions, but assigning that role would be inference without a task order or operations record.

**Assessment:** CSI ownership/current/confirmed; ICE assignment not established.

### 5. Other reported carrier names

Recent reporting has named Bighorn Airways, Key Lime Air, Omni Air, Journey Aviation, World Atlantic, Gryphon Air, and others in ICE Air or CSI-related contexts. This review did not locate a current primary ICE, CSI, SEC, DOT, or court record that both names one of those companies and assigns it to the current CSI ICE Air BPA/call. They remain reporting-derived leads and are not part of the confirmed roster.

## Aircraft and crew result

### Aircraft

The number of confirmed current CSI/ICE aircraft tails is **zero**.

The BTS file is an operator fleet inventory. DOT certificates are operating authority. FAA certificates and OpSpecs establish what an operator is authorized to fly. None alone proves that an aircraft was dedicated to the CSI BPA or used on a particular ICE flight. The redacted Classic Air administrative-record references show that an authoritative aircraft list, airworthiness certificates, and OpSpecs existed during award evaluation, but the public pleading withholds the identifying content.

### Crew

GlobalX's 10-K confirms that GlobalX supplies crews as part of ACMI work. FAA's Eastern notice confirms Eastern employs regulated pilots, flight attendants, and maintenance personnel. The ICE SOW requires flight crews and makes crew qualification information part of preflight reporting. No public primary source reviewed here assigns a named individual crew member to the CSI/ICE work. The investigation should seek operator- and certificate-level records, not personal identities.

### GEO Transport / GEO Group

The established GTI edge is security/air-operations support beneath CSI, not Part 121 aircraft or flight-crew operation. Nothing in this lead supports recasting GEO Transport or another GEO entity as an airline, aircraft owner, or flight-crew employer.

## What would resolve the roster

The decisive records are narrow and known:

1. Classic Air administrative-record Tabs 91, 92b, 94, and 101, especially CSI's Round 4 aircraft list, letters of commitment, airworthiness certificates, OpSpecs, and teaming agreements.
2. CSI's response, if any, to the May 28, 2025 House Homeland Security letter requesting all subcontract agreements and monthly flight counts by subcontractor.
3. ICE Air call `70CDCR26FC0000001` flight-leg/preflight records showing date, operating carrier certificate, aircraft registration number, and mission type.
4. COR approvals for rotating or adding different aircraft models to the dedicated-service fleet.
