# GEO CY2025 ICE revenue bridge: what reconciles, what does not

**Lead:** 57844  
**Profile:** `geo-group`  
**Research cutoff:** 2026-07-14  
**Period:** calendar year 2025 unless otherwise labeled  
**Question:** How do direct ICE obligations to GEO's federal recipient identities relate to GEO's ICE-attributed corporate revenue, including B.I./ISAP, IGSAs, transportation, ownership, rent, outlays, and timing?

## Bottom line

GEO's 2025 Form 10-K says ICE accounted for **47.6%** of its **$2.631549 billion** consolidated revenue. Multiplying the reported values gives a **$1,252,617,324 point estimate** of CY2025 ICE-attributed revenue. It is not an exact dollar disclosure: the percentage is rounded to one decimal, and the same filing paragraph contains an internal 0.5-percentage-point inconsistency. It says BOP, ICE, and USMS collectively accounted for 66.6%, while the printed agency shares—2.6%, 47.6%, and 15.9%—sum to 66.1%. The filing's separate concentration table rounds the federal-agency total to 67%. This report therefore preserves the printed 47.6% ICE share but does not silently force it to reconcile to the 66.6% aggregate.

The official USAspending-derived action ledger records **$699,338,118.97** of CY2025 direct ICE obligations across GEO's verified 14-UEI universe. All 124 actions went to only two recipient registrations:

| Legal recipient | UEI | CY2025 ICE actions | Action-date obligations |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| The GEO Group, Inc. | `JMLKZZ1NL2Z6` | 115 | **$527,827,186.25** |
| B.I. Incorporated | `PKK6L9KLMYR5` | 9 | **$171,510,932.72** |
| Other 12 verified GEO UEIs | — | 0 | **$0.00** |
| **Total** | — | **124** | **$699,338,118.97** |

Dividing direct obligations by the ICE-revenue point estimate gives **55.83%**. This is a **different-metric context ratio**, not “coverage.” The **$553,279,205.03 arithmetic difference** is unallocatable and cannot be called IGSA revenue, hidden obligations, undercounted spending, or unexplained revenue. Federal obligations are legal commitments recorded on action dates; SEC revenue is recognized as services are performed; IGSA cash may pass first through a public prime; and the two measures can reflect different service periods, deobligations, accruals, invoices, receivables, and payment timing.

The defensible conclusion is narrower: structured direct-recipient obligations expose a large but incomplete view of the ICE-attributed business, and the missing bridge is material. Open lead **#62481** now seeks a contract-to-customer revenue schedule that could falsify or close that visibility gap. Existing lead **#57974** already seeks the federal and local payment records for the six GEO-linked ICE IGSA chains.

## Measurement rules

| Measure | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Federal obligation | Legal commitment recorded on an award action date | Cash paid, service-period expense, or GEO revenue |
| Federal outlay | Federal cash disbursement when reported | Revenue-recognition timing or a local prime's downstream payment |
| Local receipt/payment | Cash received or remitted by an IGSA/public prime | Full earned revenue when invoices or remittances lag |
| Contract rate/ceiling | Pricing architecture or maximum authority | Amount obligated, outlaid, paid, or earned |
| SEC revenue | Accounting recognition as GEO performs services | Which federal action, invoice, or local remittance funded each dollar |
| Facility capacity | Physical beds disclosed for a site | Guaranteed, occupied, billed, or paid bed-days |

GEO expressly says revenue recognition may differ from invoicing. Secure-services revenue is recognized continually as services are performed and is generally paid at a per-day or fixed monthly rate; electronic-monitoring and case-management revenue is recognized as performed and generally paid per occurrence plus a monthly management fee. This timing policy makes a direct action-to-revenue reconciliation invalid without invoice/service-period and accounts-receivable detail.

The ISAP point estimate is 73.80% of the Electronic Monitoring and Supervision segment total. GEO says that segment revenue fell $11.9 million primarily because average ISAP participant counts decreased.

The narrow CY2025 B.I. ISAP obligation total of **$159,536,432.72** is 67.36% of the **$236,839,410** ISAP revenue point estimate. Adding skip-tracing actions brings B.I.'s direct ICE obligations to **$171,510,932.72**, or 72.42% of that ISAP point estimate—but the latter comparison is deliberately broader than ISAP revenue and should not be treated as a program reconciliation. Both ratios remain obligations-to-revenue context ratios.

Subtracting the 9% ISAP point estimate from the 47.6% ICE point estimate produces **$1,015,777,914**, but this is only a residual of two rounded SEC percentages. It must not be labeled “ICE detention revenue”: it may include secure services, other B.I. work, transportation, IGSA-attributed revenue, and rounding/attribution effects.

### Secure-services ownership and management

GEO disaggregates 2025 U.S. Secure Services revenue as follows:

| Corporate revenue source | CY2025 revenue | ICE-specific? |
|---|---:|---|
| Owned and leased secure services | **$1,388,316,000** | No; all customers in the source category |
| Managed-only secure services | **$438,684,000** | No; all customers in the source category |
| **U.S. Secure Services total** | **$1,827,000,000** | No; includes ICE and other government customers |

These categories describe the operator/property relationship, not a separate rent stream. GEO says managed-only revenue is recognized similarly to owned/leased secure services, except that the customer owns the facility. Separately, GEO reported **$15.1 million** of rental income from nine leased facilities. The filing does not assign that rental income to ICE, so it is not added to ICE revenue or the obligation bridge.

GEO also reported a **$152.4 million aggregate net revenue increase** from a bundled group: company-owned Delaney Hall, North Lake, and D. Ray James; managed-only North Florida; and new transportation contracts. The bundle cannot be allocated to ICE or to facilities:

- Delaney Hall and North Lake were direct ICE activations.
- D. Ray James was activated for ICE under the existing Charlton/Folkston IGSA.
- North Florida is a state-owned, managed-only facility under a Florida relationship, not a direct ICE contract disclosed in this filing.
- GEO's separately identified June 2025 transportation activation was a U.S. Marshals Service contract.

The same bundle had a $93.4 million operating-expense increase. The simple $59.0 million spread is neither ICE profit nor facility-level margin because both revenue and expense disclosures combine different customers, channels, and sites.

## IGSA and local-prime channel

The current facility crosswalk identifies **nine GEO facility rows across six independent ICE → public-prime → GEO chains**, totaling **11,361 physical beds**. Capacity is the only compatible roll-up. The acquired records do not support a six-chain CY2025 federal-obligation total or downstream GEO-receipt total.

| Clearfield/Moshannon | $4,687,231 | 2024 county account actual | Sign/payment direction and service allocation remain unresolved |
| Evangeline/South Louisiana | $39,454,943 | FY2024 audited fund additions and equal reductions | No payer/payee attribution sufficient to identify GEO receipts |

The IGSA records prove the legal and operational chains. They do not provide a compatible, same-period downstream cash total that can be joined to GEO's 47.6% ICE revenue disclosure. Lead #57974 remains the falsifiable path: funded task orders, outlays, local receipts, invoices, checks/EFTs, administrative fees, and GEO remittances for all six chains.

## North Florida and other DHS/state-mediated cash

Florida recorded five FY2025-26 payments totaling **$28,897,523.51** to GEO|CDR BAKER, LLC for North Florida operations. Their check dates were in January–June 2026, so they are neither CY2025 cash nor automatically CY2025 recognized revenue. FEMA award EMW-2025-DS-05000 reported a **$58,292,145 award-wide outlay**, but no recovered public allocation joins that federal outlay to North Florida, PO-010844, or the GEO vendor payments. The two amounts remain separate evidence rows and are not added to the ICE bridge.

## Outlay gap

The current DHS action ledger contains obligations and cumulative award values but no transaction-level outlay column. USAspending award-detail outlays, where available, are cumulative/current award values rather than a clean calendar-year service-period ledger. This pass therefore makes no CY2025 outlay-to-revenue ratio. A defensible outlay bridge requires dated federal disbursements joined to invoices/service periods and, for IGSAs, the public prime's downstream remittance.

## What this bridge supports

1. GEO's printed 47.6% ICE share implies a **$1.253 billion CY2025 revenue point estimate**, subject to rounding and an explicit internal filing inconsistency.
2. Direct ICE action-date obligations across the verified 14-UEI universe were **$699.338 million**, split between the GEO parent and B.I.; the direct-obligation/ICE-revenue context ratio is **55.83%**.
3. The **$553.279 million arithmetic difference is not an attributable residual**. No evidence permits assigning it to IGSAs, transport, prior obligations, or any facility.
4. ISAP provides the narrowest program comparison: a **$236.839 million revenue point estimate** versus **$159.536 million** of CY2025 action-date obligations, still on incompatible accounting bases.
5. Owned/leased, managed-only, rental, and activation-bundle disclosures do not isolate ICE facility economics.
6. Six IGSA chains and North Florida expose material local/state payment channels, but no complete same-period downstream GEO-receipt ledger exists in the acquired public records.

## What this bridge does not support

- Adding obligations, outlays, local cash, contract ceilings, rates, or revenue.
- Calling the obligations/revenue difference an undercount, hidden spending, IGSA revenue, or missing money.
- Treating a GEO parent obligation as GEO Transport affiliate revenue.
- Treating the full $152.4 million activation bundle as ICE revenue or the $59.0 million revenue/expense spread as ICE profit.
- Treating nine-facility physical capacity as guaranteed or billed ICE bed-days.
- Treating North Florida's 2026 state payments or FEMA's award-wide outlay as CY2025 ICE revenue.
- Silently correcting the 10-K's inconsistent 66.6% aggregate and 66.1% printed component sum.

A complete schedule that reconciles to the 10-K closes the corporate side of the gap.
