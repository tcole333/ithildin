## Bottom line

Finding #13015 materially improves the access record but does not change the broader causation assessment. It establishes that GEO sent a voluntary-work-program litigation letter directly to then-ICE Director Thomas Homan and that OPLA leadership planned to meet GEO counsel. Separate Homan correspondence says ICE was tracking the litigation and working with DOJ to determine an appropriate path. Those records prove executive-level agency awareness and a planned legal engagement. They do not prove that Homan intervened in a contracting decision, that DOJ assumed the defense, that GEO received reimbursement, or that the contact was political rather than ordinary contractor-agency engagement.

The later outcome is more causally proximate. On June 21, 2018, ICE contracting officers denied the Aurora, Northwest/Tacoma, and Adelanto requests in full. The three requests totaled $2,662,654.03. The July 9 Edge response confirmed all three denials and referred GEO to DOJ points of contact; later ICE counsel described the denial letters and Edge response as final and sent. Contract clauses independently supplied ordinary written-change, disputes, fixed-price, and litigation-cost explanations.

The record therefore changes one dimension and not the others:

- **Access:** upgraded from adjacency to direct, matter-specific GEO-to-ICE leadership access.
- **Influence:** not established. No record shows direction to a contracting officer, a changed merits analysis, a payment route, or a political actor in the decision chain.
- **Outcome:** adverse to GEO on the documented requests.
- **Network bridge:** none to Guidepost, B.I., the donor ledger, lobbying, a procurement selection, an award, an option, or a policy decision.

This episode is discriminating counterevidence against the proposition that direct access produced favorable treatment on these requests. It is not proof that influence was absent in other matters.

## The decision path

The evidence should not be collapsed into a single Homan-to-denial chain without preserving record boundaries.

1. A February 16, 2018 ICE email says GEO had sent a voluntary-work-program litigation letter to Director Homan and that OPLA leadership planned to meet GEO counsel.
2. A separate undated Homan response to Representative Barry Loudermilk's March 7 letter said ICE was tracking the cases and working with DOJ on an appropriate path. That congressional-response record is not the GEO letter and does not establish DOJ representation.
3. On May 30, 2018, GEO CEO George Zoley separately asked Peter T. Edge for DOJ defense and reimbursement and forecast $15-$20 million in continued defense costs.
4. On June 21, three contracting-officer letters denied the Aurora, Northwest/Tacoma, and Adelanto REAs in full.
5. On July 9, Edge confirmed the three denials and stated that ICE had supplied DOJ points of contact. The referral is not a merits grant, payment, or takeover.

The direct access and planned OPLA meeting are therefore real, but the observed administrative result is denial rather than accommodation.

## Dependency and independence

The three denial letters concern three distinct contract vehicles and three contract-level requests. That cross-vehicle recurrence increases confidence that the adverse result was not a single-facility anomaly. It does not create three independent causal votes: the letters arose from one GEO litigation-cost campaign, the same agency, the same date, substantially similar theories, and a coordinated response episode. The ACH counts one episode with three contract-level decisions.

The contract clauses are explanatory controls, not separate political contexts:

- Northwest assigns GEO responsibility for litigation and its cost and requires contracting-officer-approved written changes.
- Aurora incorporates fixed-price changes and disputes clauses; the located indemnification clause is medical-liability-specific.
- Adelanto requires written changes, provides a disputes route, and makes ordinary rate adjustments prospective rather than retroactive.

These provisions do not prove that no official exercised discretion. They do provide facially ordinary grounds for the denials and reduce the need to infer a political explanation for the observed result.

## Bounded conclusion

The delta makes the access-versus-outcome distinction sharper. GEO reached ICE's director and was scheduled for legal engagement, but the acquired decision record ends in three full denials grounded in ordinary contract-administration rationales. That sequence supports the ordinary-access explanation for this episode and is counterevidence to favorable treatment on these requests. It creates no demonstrated bridge to Guidepost, donations, procurement or award decisions, policy influence, or favorable treatment elsewhere.
