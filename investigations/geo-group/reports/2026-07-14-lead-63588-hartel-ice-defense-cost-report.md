## Bottom line

ICE records authenticate a three-vehicle request-and-denial sequence, not a reimbursement sequence. GEO's May 30, 2018 letter asked ICE/DOJ to absorb litigation costs. On June 21, 2018, three ICE contracting officers denied requests totaling **$2,662,654.03** through March 31, 2018: Aurora **$1,928,433.38**, Northwest/Tacoma **$595,160.69**, and Adelanto **$139,059.96**. ICE said the private suits were defenses of GEO's contract performance, no constructive change had occurred, legal fees were not cognizable, and the requests lacked adequate quantum support. Peter T. Edge's July 9 reply confirmed all three denials and said ICE had provided DOJ points of contact; it did not promise representation or payment. A July 23 ICE counsel email identifies the attached denial letters and Edge letter as final and sent.

No acquired source establishes a payment, compensating modification, reconsideration grant, Contract Disputes Act appeal on these requests, voucher, or DOJ takeover. That is a bounded negative result, not proof that no later nonpublic record exists.

## Reconstructed sequence

### February–May 2018: GEO requests and escalation

The ICE packet contains a February 16 internal email stating that GEO had sent Director Homan a letter about the voluntary-work-program class actions and that OPLA leadership planned to meet GEO counsel. The actual February 14 request and its attachments were not acquired in this pass.

The packet separately preserves Thomas Homan's response to Representative Barry Loudermilk's March 7, 2018 letter. Homan wrote that ICE was “tracking closely” the litigation and “working with the Department of Justice to determine the appropriate path forward.” A May 17 ICE email described that Homan response as the limited information leadership had authorized for appropriations staff. This is contemporaneous evidence of awareness, executive/congressional access, and planned engagement only—not evidence of a favorable decision, reimbursement, or DOJ representation. Finding 13015 records the internal-email and congressional-response passages as separate Bates references and refines finding 12511 in political-influence thread 112; the much later Homan/GEO Care disclosure findings (including 12505) remain a separate temporal and evidentiary record.

The three denials say ICE received updated, vehicle-specific REAs dated April 18, 2018. They describe the requested amounts, invoice vendors, legal matters, and the theory of a constructive change caused by incomplete performance specifications.

In the court-filed May 30 letter, George Zoley wrote: “We are deeply alarmed at the rapidly increasing costs in defending these lawsuits without reimbursement from ICE, or assistance in their defense by the Department of Justice (DOJ).” He estimated continued defense at $15–20 million and wrote that GEO “urgently implore[d] DOJ to take over the defense of these lawsuits and reimburse GEO” for its costs. The February/April/May totals on the filed copy are redacted. These are GEO statements and forecasts.

An ICE lawyer's June 8 email says an AUSA had reported a coming GEO/ICE meeting “to discuss ‘the issues in this litigation.’” This proves agency awareness and routing, not a commitment to reimburse or represent GEO.

### June 21: three final denials

**Aurora — HSCEDM-11-D-00003.** ICE described a **$1,928,433.38** request for Menocal defense fees through March 31, 2018. It identified Norton Rose Fulbright, Precision Discovery, Burns Figa & Will, Holland & Knight, and Vaughan & DeMuro invoice entries. ICE said no constructive change occurred; FAR 52.243-1 Alternate I did not create entitlement without a change; the firm-fixed-price performance-based vehicle placed performance risk on GEO; the specifications were not incomplete; and the legal fees were not cognizable under the contract or FAR 31.205-47. It denied the REA in its entirety and pointed to FAR 52.233-1 and the CDA.

**Northwest/Tacoma — HSCEDM-15-D-00015.** ICE described a **$595,160.69** request: **$113,123.35** for Chen and **$482,037.44** for State of Washington. It applied the same no-change/no-defect/no-cognizable-cost rationale, cited G.1.1, the PWS/PBNDS and CLIN structure, and denied the REA in its entirety under FAR 52.233-1/CDA.

**Adelanto — EROIGSA-11-0003.** ICE described a **$139,059.96** request for Novoa defense fees. It rejected GEO's Article X.B constructive-change theory, said no change to scope or terms had occurred, characterized the IGSA as firm-fixed-price/performance-based, and said the fees were not cognizable under the IGSA or FAR 31.205-47. It denied the REA in its entirety and pointed to Article X.C/CDA.

The **$2,662,654.03** figure is arithmetic across the three amounts ICE described; it is not a payment figure.

### July 9–23: response and finality

Peter T. Edge's July 9 letter states: “On June 21, 2018, the respective ICE contracting officers responded and denied all three of GEO's requests for equitable adjustment.” On DOJ, Edge wrote that ICE had provided “the appropriate Department of Justice points of contact” and that GEO was “free to reach out to them and discuss your concerns.” This is a referral/contact statement only.

On July 23, an ICE Associate Legal Advisor circulated “the final REA response letters that were sent to GEO and the final signed letter from Pete Edge which was also sent out.” This authenticates the final/sent status of the acquired denials and Edge response.

## Contract-clause comparison

The companion CSV/JSON contains the full matrix. The main pattern is consistent across vehicles: changes required contracting-officer action and a disputes route; fixed-price or fixed-rate structures did not make litigation expenses automatically reimbursable.

- **Aurora:** contract Section I incorporates FAR 52.243-1 Changes—Fixed-Price Alternate I and FAR 52.233-1 Disputes. FAR 52.237-7 is medical-liability insurance, not a general government promise to defend GEO. The filed exhibit has no located general litigation-cost indemnity clause.
- **Northwest:** G.1.1 says only the contracting officer may approve changes and no adjustment follows an unauthorized change. The PWS Hold Harmless provision is explicit: “The Contractor shall be responsible for all litigation, including the cost of litigation, brought against it, its employees or agents for alleged acts or omissions.” Government-lawyer cooperation is tied to requests and does not promise a defense. G.3 limits invoice treatment to supported contract items; H.5 runs indemnity from GEO to the Government.
- **Adelanto:** Article X requires written contracting-officer modifications, permits equitable adjustment only for a written change order within scope, and provides a written disputes route. Article XI makes ordinary bed-day rate adjustments prospective and says there are no retroactive adjustments. Article III.C lists certain legal fees as unallowable, including prosecution of claims against the Federal Government and detainees' legal expenses. The filed DE 193-2 skips the first portion of contract page 44 containing Article XIV; no inference is drawn from that missing page.

The USAspending transaction-description query returned 55 IDV actions for Aurora and 51 for Northwest. The post-June-2018 descriptions include routine detention/transportation actions and later REAs expressly tied to wage determinations, CBAs, or MOUs. None describes the 2018 private-litigation defense-cost REAs, a settlement, or legal-fee reimbursement. Every IDV action row reports zero at the IDV level; orders carry funding, so this dataset cannot establish invoice-level disbursement. No exact EROIGSA-11-0003 transaction row was returned.

The CBCA published CDA decision index contained one “The GEO Group, Inc.” decision, CBCA 7168, but it concerns a Department of Justice/Bureau of Prisons dispute, not any of these ICE vehicles. Bounded CourtListener/web searches did not locate a reconsideration or appeal matching the three PIIDs and June 21 denials.
