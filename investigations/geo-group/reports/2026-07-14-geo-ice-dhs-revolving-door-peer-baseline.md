# GEO / ICE-DHS revolving-door peer baseline

Date: 2026-07-14  
Lead: #58137  
Analysis run: #96  
Peers: GEO Group, CoreCivic, Management & Training Corporation (MTC), LaSalle Corrections

## Bottom line

GEO has a higher and distinct ICE-specific concentration in this two-public-company snapshot: three people in GEO's SEC-disclosed 21-person directors/director-nominees/executive-officers group (Matthew Albence, Daniel Ragsdale, and Julie Myers Wood), versus zero people with prior ICE or DHS service in CoreCivic's SEC-disclosed 18-person current directors/executive-officers group. Private-peer disclosure gaps prevent a sector-wide unusualness claim.

That comparison does **not** establish influence, coordination, or statistical significance. The broader baseline cuts the other way: CoreCivic has two former senior federal detention/corrections officials (Harley Lappin and Stacia Hylton), MTC has a former BOP human-resources chief in corrections management (Dan Joslin), and LaSalle's recent executive director came from the Texas Department of Criminal Justice (Rodney Cooper). Hiring former corrections officials is therefore a peer practice; GEO's narrower ICE-specific concentration is the distinguishing feature.

No reviewed source identified any named match as the contracting officer, source-selection official, or matter-specific decision maker for a contract awarded to the company that later employed them. The strongest procurement-relevance record is Lappin's program-level oversight of BOP private contract facilities and community-corrections contracts. That is material professional adjacency, but it is not evidence that he made a specific CoreCivic award decision.

## Comparison rules

The analysis used three nested, non-interchangeable role categories:

1. **ICE/DHS-specific service** — service at ICE or another DHS component.
2. **Other detention/corrections service** — BOP, USMS/Federal Detention Trustee, or state corrections.
3. **Acquisition/procurement authority** — documented contracting-officer, source-selection, award-approval, or matter-specific procurement responsibility.

Company work, lobbying, and consulting were recorded separately from government service. Agency leadership was not treated as proof of procurement authority. A company title mentioning contracts or client relations was not treated as evidence of influence.

For the public companies, the denominator is the explicit group count in each 2026 proxy. GEO's proxy says its ownership group comprises 21 directors, director nominees, and executive officers. Its printed current-officer roster appears smaller than that group count; the disclosed 21 is retained without reconstructing omitted people. CoreCivic's proxy gives an internally reconcilable 18-person group.

MTC and LaSalle are private and do not publish comparable SEC rosters. Their results are public-record inventories only, not percentage rankings. MTC's Florida registration names three legal officers, while separate public records identify a corrections vice president. LaSalle's 2025 PREA audit leaves the agency CEO fields blank, and a 2025 federal court order stresses that the various LaSalle-branded LLCs may have distinct and incompletely documented leadership.

## Ethics and recusal check

Exact-name public-domain searches were run for the identified people across OGE, DHS, DOJ/BOP, and related government indexes. No person-specific ethics agreement, waiver, recusal, screening memorandum, or post-employment advice was located for Albence, Ragsdale, Wood, Lappin, Hylton, Joslin, or Cooper.

This is a bounded negative result, not proof that no ethics advice existed. Career officials may not have public nominee ethics agreements; agencies may withhold legal advice or publish it only after a records request. General post-employment restrictions such as 18 U.S.C. § 207 are not evidence that any individual complied with or violated a restriction. The novel follow-up is lead #62429, focused on BOP/DOJ ethics and procurement-screening records for Lappin and Joslin.

## Contract-timing interpretation

The timeline does not supply a clean “hire, then award” pattern across peers:

- MTC's immigration-detention business began in 2006, well before Joslin's reported 2016 move.
- GEO's Guidepost/BI consulting relationship existed before Wood joined the board.
- CoreCivic and BOP used private contract facilities during Lappin's government tenure, but the reviewed record does not tie him to a particular CoreCivic selection.
- GEO's major FY2025 ICE expansion occurred years after Albence joined, and existing procurement analysis found a broader, heterogeneous sector expansion affecting CoreCivic and MTC as well.
- Ragsdale's 2017 transition is temporally close and role-relevant, but timing plus a contract-compliance title is not evidence of an agency-side procurement act.

The strongest justified conclusion is “GEO assembled more ICE-specific senior expertise than the disclosed CoreCivic peer roster.” The evidence does not answer whether that expertise affected any procurement result.

## Hypothesis test

### Hypothesis #337 — distributed ICE expertise channel

The 3/21 versus 0/18 ICE-specific comparison is **consistent** with a GEO-specific expertise concentration. It is not proof of a coordinated channel. Peer evidence is otherwise neutral: CoreCivic, MTC, and LaSalle show the broader former-corrections pattern, but not an ICE/DHS-specific cluster in the publicly visible rosters. No matter-specific procurement intervention, shared plan, or coordinated timing was found.

### Hypothesis #338 — ordinary regulated-sector practice

The broader peer evidence is **consistent** with the null: CoreCivic hired former BOP/USMS leaders, MTC hired a former BOP human-resources official, and LaSalle hired a former state corrections leader. The narrow CoreCivic absence of ICE/DHS alumni is one inconsistent datum for a simple “all peers look alike” prediction, but it is a small two-public-company comparison and does not overcome the broader evidence of ordinary former-official hiring.

After adding findings #12855–#12859, the ACH competition remains substantially more favorable to #338: 42 evaluations, 1 inconsistent (ratio 0.02), versus #337 with 42 evaluations, 11 inconsistent (ratio 0.26). This is an evidence-consistency score, not a probability.

- Search-engine and website non-results are coverage statements, not proof of absence.
