## Bottom line

The corrected DHS action ledger does not show a dramatic increase in the *frequency* of GEO actions under Trump II once unequal administration lengths are normalized. Trump II annualizes to 124.370 actions, only 7.3% above Trump I's 115.921 and 5.9% below Biden's 132.159. Net obligation pace is different: Trump II annualizes to USD 871.607 million, 106.8% above Trump I and 20.3% above Biden. Average net obligation per action increased from USD 3.636 million in Trump I to USD 7.008 million in Trump II. The temporal change is therefore chiefly action size and mix, not action frequency.

Three timing patterns warrant bounded follow-up, but none currently establishes coordination, advance knowledge, procurement influence, or misconduct:

1. OGE reports 15 Trump-household GEO transactions from January 30, 2025 through March 4, 2026. All fall within 14 days of at least one GEO DHS action, but that proximity is non-discriminating in a ledger with 184 actions over 540 Trump-II days, many of them incumbent modifications. The public forms do not identify the covered household owner or account decision-maker.
2. Checkmate's 265-day GEO engagement overlapped greater net obligations than the equal prior window, but fewer actions and the same number of base actions. Two August 2025 base actions preceded the engagement. The best current explanation is general detention-capacity advocacy rather than identifiable award-specific work.
3. David Venturella first appeared on ICE's archived leadership page 472 days after his GEO proposal-support consultancy ended and 429 days after scheduled performance-share vesting. ICE publicly assigns acquisition and procurement to Management and Administration, which cuts against assuming Venturella held operational procurement authority. His exact appointment, financial disclosure, ethics screen, and GEO-matter participation remain unresolved.

ACH does not select a causal theory. The household-trading pair is tied on inconsistency and remains untested. The innocent/null explanation is least inconsistent for Checkmate and Venturella. “Least inconsistent” is not proof.

## Key-period chronology

### 2016–July 2017: political money, transition, and Ragsdale

Rebuilding America Now reported GEO Corrections Holdings receipts of USD 100,000 on August 19, 2016 and USD 125,000 on November 1. The inaugural committee reported USD 250,000 on December 10, 41 days before inauguration. These are distinct corporate super-PAC and inaugural streams, not GEO PAC or individual giving.

The official ICE transition packet identifies Ragsdale as approving official and Homan and Albence as agency subject-matter experts. Ragsdale became acting director January 20; Homan's acting-director appointment followed January 30, a ten-day interval. GEO reports only that Ragsdale joined in July, placing the move 162–192 days after January 20 depending on the unknown July day. The month-only precision cannot support a more exact interval. This personnel pattern is already covered by hypotheses #337/#338 and was not duplicated.

### September 2019: Albence and Ballard reporting

The September 27 White House record identifies Albence as performing ICE director duties. Ballard's Q3 GEO LDA filing dated September 30 named Pamela Bondi and Justin Sayfie and listed DHS and the White House Office. The three-day separation is a reporting-calendar artifact unless underlying contacts are dated; the filing covers a quarter and does not say who contacted whom. No new timing hypothesis was created.

### September 2022: Albence joins GEO

GEO places Albence's start on September 1, 2022 and reports USD 137,531 paid to his consulting firm for pre-employment 2022 work. The records do not date individual services or connect them to an ICE action. This is strong revolving-door chronology but duplicates the distributed-expertise hypothesis set #337/#338.

### January–March 2025: appointment, trades, and activations

Homan's Trump-II appointment was January 20. The first five reported household GEO purchases occurred January 30 and February 3, 4, 5, and 24—10, 14, 15, 16, and 35 days after appointment. Homan's new-entrant disclosure was signed February 10, 11 days after the first purchase, but it concerns his separate pre-appointment consulting sources and supplies no connection to the household account.

The Delaney Hall announcement followed the February 24 purchase by three days; North Lake followed by 24 days. The action ledger also contains frequent January–March modifications. Different actors, household-member anonymity, public company news, and procurement lead times prevent treating this sequence as coordinated.

### August 2025–May 2026: Checkmate

Checkmate's registration was effective August 13. Base task orders dated August 1 and August 12 preceded it by 12 and one day; another followed August 28. The ISAP V solicitation followed by two days and award by 48 days, but the LDA issue description says detention-center contracts and names neither ICE, B.I., ISAP V, a PIID, nor a contracting official. The ISAP inference is therefore filtered out.

During the 265-day engagement, the ledger contains 85 actions, USD 749,167,436.85 net obligations, and 11 base actions. The equal prior 265 days contain 94 actions, USD 441,722,835.78, and 11 base actions. Higher dollars with fewer actions and identical base-action counts is consistent with action mix and larger modifications; it does not establish lobbying effect. This produced finding #12544.

### 2025–March 2026 household trade sequence

The 2025 annual disclosure reports ten purchases and four sales; the March 4, 2026 278-T reports another purchase. All 15 dates have at least one ledger action within plus or minus 14 days. This sounds striking but is not diagnostic in a 184-action/540-day background, and several nearest actions are modifications or deobligations. Finding #12543 preserves that boundary.

Reported-transaction gaps of 31–100 days are not operational silence. They show only that no other GEO transaction appears in the reviewed disclosure series during those intervals.

### March–May 2026: purchase, disclosure, Checkmate end, and Venturella

The March 4 purchase occurred one day after a USD 2.085 million modification and one day before a USD 10.192 million modification. Nine actions within plus or minus 14 days net to USD 49.693 million; the first base action in that window was March 18, 14 days after the purchase. Procurement initiation dates are unknown.

The 278-T was dated May 8, 65 days after the purchase, and marked notification received more than 30 days later. Checkmate's engagement ended May 4, four days before the report. Venturella first appeared on the archived ICE leadership page May 18—75 days after the purchase, ten days after the report, and 14 days after Checkmate ended. These are three separate records with no documented communication or common decision-maker.

Venturella's consultancy ended January 31, 2025, 472 days before the May 18 appearance. Scheduled performance-share vesting was March 15, 2025, 429 days before. The separation and ICE's public placement of procurement under Management and Administration cut against an immediate procurement-control theory, while missing ethics and participation records preserve a narrower follow-up question. This produced finding #12545.

## Silence periods

The full DHS ledger has only two gaps of at least 30 days between unique action dates: 36 days from June 5 to July 11, 2023, and 30 days from September 30 to October 30, 2025. Neither is evidence of inactivity, destruction, or concealment. CBP gaps are much longer because CBP accounts for only 54 actions, making silence comparisons structurally sparse. Annual disclosures and quarterly lobbying reports likewise create artificial gaps.

## Falsification plans

Test owner identity, investment discretion, same-day portfolio context, and exact procurement initiation/evaluation/selection milestones. Seek public LDA amendments, calendars, visitor logs, agency correspondence, work descriptions, and procurement milestones. H1 fails if records show only general policy, budget, appropriations, or capacity advocacy.
