# Homan consulting sources, Guidepost–B.I. payments, and the unresolved ethics record

**Lead:** 59112  
**Profile / thread:** `geo-group` / 112  
**Record cutoff:** 2026-07-14  
**Method:** Public and already-released records only. No subject contact, private-data collection, live HigherGov use, or FOIA submission occurred.

## Bottom line

The records-only refresh did not recover a client contract, statement of work, invoice, receivable schedule, payment ledger, last-service certification, recusal, screening arrangement, or authorization that resolves Thomas D. Homan's disclosed consulting relationships with Guidepost Solutions or “GEO Care.” The official disclosure and the corporate payment record remain two strictly separate ledgers:

1. **Homan disclosure ledger.** Homan personally provided consulting services associated with Guidepost and GEO Care, and each source crossed the OGE Part 4 threshold in at least one covered calendar year. The public form does not report the exact amount, year, service date, contract, project, invoice, or whether payment went to Homan or to an employer for his services.
2. **GEO–Guidepost/B.I. corporate ledger.** GEO reported that B.I. Incorporated paid Guidepost under a consulting agreement: $180,000 in fiscal 2023, $180,000 in fiscal 2024, and $420,000 in fiscal 2025. The latest reported retainer was $35,000 per month, and the agreement ended on December 31, 2025. No reviewed primary record names Homan as personnel on that engagement or shows that any B.I. payment flowed to him.

The common Guidepost name is therefore a valid records lead, but it is not a payment bridge.

## Ledger 1: Homan's disclosed consulting sources

### What the official form establishes

Homan's White House-hosted OGE Form 278e identifies him as a non-career DHS Border Czar appointee effective January 20, 2025. He signed the form on February 10, 2025. The form records DHS ethics review by Ashley Lam on April 10 and certification by Michael O'Connor on May 8, 2025, after nine listed data-revision dates.

Part 4 lists:

- `Guidepost Solutions | Washington, District of Columbia | Consulting Services`; and
- `GEO Care | Boca Raton, Florida | Consulting Services`.

The form's own instructions state:

> The filer discloses a source even if the source made its payment to the filer's employer and not to the filer.

They also state that the filer does not report an employer's client unless the filer personally provided the services for which the client paid. Applied with the OGE reporting-period guide, the entries establish personal service and a payment of more than $5,000 by each source in at least one calendar year from January 1, 2023 through the February 10, 2025 filing date. They do not establish direct payment.

The same form separately reports that Homan served as president of Homeland Strategic Consulting, LLC through December 2024 and received $360,000 in salary. That salary is an employer-level amount, not a client-by-client allocation. An official FEC filing shows Homeland Strategic Consulting as a campaign strategy-consulting payee in 2021, outside the Part 4 period. It confirms that the named company functioned as a consulting payee, but it does not show that GEO Care or Guidepost paid that company.

### What remains missing

The public record does not identify:

- which covered year crossed the $5,000 threshold for either client;
- Homan's last date of service for either client;
- the exact gross fee, Homan's share, or an invoice or payment date;
- whether Guidepost or GEO Care paid Homan, Homeland Strategic Consulting, or another employer/intermediary;
- whether any fee remained unpaid on January 20, 2025;
- the exact legal entity behind the suffix-free “GEO Care” label;
- any project, deliverable, personnel assignment, ICE/ISAP work, or B.I. engagement; or
- any client-specific recusal, screening arrangement, covered-matter list, waiver, or authorization.

The form's `Homeland Strategic Consulting` versus `Homeland Security Consulting` name inconsistency also remains unresolved. It is not safe to merge the dormancy line into the identified legal-name node without an official correction or registry record.

An all-form EDGAR exact-phrase sweep for `Guidepost Solutions` and `B.I. Separate 2023–2026 searches combining `Thomas Homan` with `Guidepost Solutions` or `GEO Care` returned zero SEC hits.

GEO's proxies say its Audit and Finance Committee reviewed and approved the disclosed related-party transactions. The public 2024 committee charter authorizes the committee to review contracts or transactions above the Item 404 threshold and to develop approval procedures. Neither the charter nor the proxies disclose meeting minutes, votes, Wood's participation or abstention, a recusal, the agreement text, or the basis for approving the Guidepost transaction.

### Separation controls

- **Julie Myers Wood's GEO board compensation is separate.** GEO's proxies identify Wood as a GEO director and separately report her non-employee director compensation. The reported totals were $267,498 for 2023, $287,497 for 2024, and $303,759 for 2025. Those personal board-compensation rows are not the B.I.-to-Guidepost payments.
- **Homan is not identified as a GEO director.** His OGE entry characterizes the GEO Care work as consulting services. No reviewed GEO filing calls it board or director compensation.
- **Other Guidepost personnel are not Homan.** The only two Homan-bearing URLs in Guidepost's current post sitemap are November 2024 and February 2025 media pages attributed to Guidepost executive John Torres. The current Guidepost people sitemap has no Homan URL. Those pages document Torres's commentary about Homan, not a Homan employment, personnel, or project assignment.
- **Corporate committee approval is not a federal ethics determination.** GEO's Audit and Finance Committee approval concerns the corporate related-party transaction. It does not establish a DHS recusal, screen, waiver, or authorization for Homan.

## Ethics-screen test

OGE's general consulting-employment guidance says a former consulting client remains a covered relationship for one year after the employee last provided services and while an outstanding fee remains unpaid. When the former client is or represents a party to a particular matter and the impartiality test is met, the employee should not participate without informing the agency designee and receiving authorization.

The controlling dates and payment status are absent here. Homan's form does not disclose a last-service date or outstanding receivable for Guidepost or GEO Care, so the one-year periods cannot be calculated. Its certification says that, based on the information in the report, the filer complied with applicable laws and regulations. That is not a client-specific determination that Homan recused, was screened, received authorization, or participated in a GEO, B.I., or Guidepost matter.

The OGE PAS index returned no Homan row as of the cutoff. That is not evidence that the official form is invalid or that no other agency record exists: Homan is identified as a non-career, non-PAS appointee, and the operative disclosure is hosted by the White House. Official-domain searches of OGE, White House, DHS, and ICE public pages found the disclosure but no separate client-specific ethics record.

## Bounded conclusion

No searched public record supplies the discriminator needed to join the two ledgers. The established links are:

- Homan personally provided reportable consulting services associated with Guidepost;
- Homan personally provided reportable consulting services associated with the suffix-free source `GEO Care`;
- B.I. had a separately disclosed paid consulting agreement with Guidepost; and
- GEO's Audit and Finance Committee reviewed and approved the disclosed related-party transaction.

The missing links are:

- Guidepost or GEO Care → an identified Homan payee account or employer;
- any client-specific exact amount, invoice, or service date;
- Homan → the Guidepost–B.I. engagement, ICE, or ISAP;
- any B.I.-to-Guidepost payment → Homan;
- Wood's committee participation, abstention, or recusal; and
- a DHS/White House matter-level ethics screen or authorization.

The fastest discriminator is a DHS ethics-file record that supplies Homan's client-specific last-service and receivable fields together with any covered-matter screen. The second is an agency-held or later-filed Guidepost–B.I. agreement/invoice/personnel record naming Homan. Two narrowly scoped human actions preserve those routes without contacting the subjects.
