# CoreCivic FY2025 direct-ICE acceleration and California City acquisition audit

**Lead:** #58082  
**Profile:** `geo-group`  
**Prepared:** 2026-07-14  
**Evidence cutoff:** USAspending actions through 2026-07-14

## Bottom line

CoreCivic's direct U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) prime-contract obligations increased by exactly **$126,323,664.15**, from **$179,444,877.48 in FY2024** to **$305,768,541.63 in FY2025**. This is an action-obligation calculation, not a sum of award ceilings.

Two new, noncompeted activation families explain **$74,943,682.25**, or **59.33%**, of the increase:

- California City: **+$49,152,682.25**
- Midwest Regional: **+$25,791,000.00**

The remaining **$51,379,981.90** is the net increase across legacy Otay Mesa, Houston, T. Don Hutto, and Central Arizona Florence task families plus a $250 emergency-vehicle minimum. Deobligations did not create the acceleration: FY2025 had **$4,177,911.74** of negative actions versus **$1,936,255.98** in FY2024, so the change in deobligations reduced the gross increase by **$2,241,655.76**.

California City's base IDV, `70CDCR25D00000010`, is coded by USAspending/FPDS as a **letter contract**, **not competed**, **only one source**, **one offer**, and **urgency (FAR 6.302-2)**. It began with a **$31,165,372.80** potential value and a 2025-09-30 ordering-period end. Modification `P00003`, dated 2025-09-26, says the action definitized the letter contract under FAR 52.216-25 and extended the ordering period through 2027-08-31; it raised potential value to **$261,980,434.71**. The IDV itself carries no obligations; the task orders do.

The public award fields establish the procurement route, but they do not expose the signed justification and approval (J&A), market research, alternatives analysis, price-negotiation memorandum, CLIN schedule, or any exceptional-circumstances determination supporting a period beyond one year. Their absence from the sources searched is a bounded document gap, not evidence that ICE failed to create them.

### Sole-source and urgency basis: established facts versus missing file evidence

Established by the primary award record:

- `extent_competed`: **NOT COMPETED**
- `solicitation_procedures`: **ONLY ONE SOURCE**
- `number_of_offers_received`: **1**
- `other_than_full_and_open_competition`: **URGENCY (FAR 6.302-2)**
- `undefinitized_action`: **LETTER CONTRACT** at base; **NO** after definitization
- `solicitation_identifier`: blank
- Federal Business Opportunities status: **NOT APPLICABLE**
- Pricing: **FIRM FIXED PRICE**

FAR 6.302-2 allows limits on sources for unusual and compelling urgency, but it also calls for a written justification and approval and requests to as many potential sources as practicable. Its period-of-performance rule generally limits urgency awards to the time needed to meet the urgent requirement and compete a successor, and to no more than one year absent a documented exceptional-circumstances determination. Because this IDV remained coded to urgency when it was definitized for a two-year ordering period, the acquisition file should be obtained to test:

1. What serious injury ICE identified if award were delayed.
2. What market research and alternative facilities ICE evaluated.
3. Why one source and one offer were practicable.
4. How ICE benchmarked the price and negotiated the $230.8 million/$262.0 million values.
5. Whether the file contains the written J&A and an exceptional-circumstances determination for the period beyond one year.
6. What definitization schedule, proposal, cost/pricing data, and negotiation record supported `P00003`.

No public J&A, market-research memorandum, acquisition plan, alternatives analysis, price-negotiation memorandum, or rate schedule was found in the USAspending award/transaction records, HigherGov's award and opportunity results, the local SAM entity result, ICE/DHS public web searches, or CoreCivic's SEC filings. That search does **not** establish nonexistence.

**Human action #53** requests the complete acquisition and pricing file. Its `related_lead_id` is intentionally NULL; the lead reference appears only in the action notes.

## Capacity, activation, revenue, and rate normalization

California City was existing, idled capacity rather than a newly built facility, but it was not immediately operational. CoreCivic says the facility was idle beginning 2024-04-01, activation began under the letter agreement on 2025-04-01, and first detainee intake occurred in August 2025. At 2025-12-31, its reported population was **1,436**, or **56.1% of 2,560 design beds**.

CoreCivic reported:

- Calendar-2025 California City facility revenue: **$39.2 million**
- Expected stabilized annual revenue: **$130.0 million**
- Expected stabilized occupancy: first quarter 2026

For comparison only, $130 million divided by 2,560 design beds equals **$50,781 per design bed-year**, or **$139.13 per design bed-day**. This is a normalization of company guidance, **not** the contract's per diem. The actual CLIN structure, guaranteed minimum, fixed monthly component, per diem, transportation rates, medical rates, and startup pricing were not available in the public file reviewed.

The federal FY2025 obligation amount of $49.15 million must not be subtracted from calendar-2025 facility revenue to infer profit. Obligations are funding actions; revenue follows company accrual accounting; task `...0122` included service through 2025-12-31, crossing into federal FY2026.

## Fiscal timing test

The transaction chronology supports hypothesis #345's timing prong but does not fully prove that timing alone explains the acceleration.

- **$80,338,431.54** of CoreCivic's FY2025 direct ICE obligations landed from 2025-09-26 through 2025-09-30.
- That equals **26.27%** of all FY2025 direct obligations and **63.60%** of the year-over-year increase.
- California City plus Midwest accounted for **$51,235,282.25** of those five-day actions.
- Several late actions funded periods extending into federal FY2026, including California City through 2025-12-31 and Otay Mesa through 2025-12-19.

Re-cut by calendar action date, CoreCivic direct ICE obligations were **$179,216,891.33 in calendar 2024** and **$266,391,160.57 in calendar 2025**, an increase of **48.64%**. The lower calendar-year rate versus the 70.4% fiscal-year rate is consistent with boundary effects, but calendar action totals still are not service-period-aligned expenses or outlays.

USAspending does not provide transaction-level outlays in these FPDS action rows. It provides a current overall-award outlay snapshot repeated on each modification. As of extraction, the two California City task families showed **$48,474,853.63** in cumulative outlays against **$108,378,766.24** in lifetime obligations. Those snapshots cannot be allocated back to FY2025 without historical account/outlay data.

## Hypothesis assessment

- **#343 — supported on concentration, still open on capacity adjustment.** California City and Midwest alone explain 59.33% of the direct-obligation increase. This satisfies the predicted concentration threshold. It does not establish favoritism, and no capacity-, geography-, or service-adjusted GEO/MTC comparison was completed here.
- **#344 (H0) — neither accepted nor falsified.** CoreCivic's 10-K documents sector-wide demand and multiple ICE channels, including direct awards and IGSAs. California City's existing 2,560-bed asset and four-month activation path are plausible operational explanations. The missing acquisition file prevents testing ICE's alternatives and comparative ready-bed analysis.
- **#345 — supported but not fully resolved.** Late-year actions and the 48.64% calendar-action growth rate show material fiscal-boundary effects. Historical FY outlays and a defensible allocation of obligations to service months were unavailable, so the hypothesis remains investigating rather than confirmed.

## Scope and channel boundary

The reconstructed totals cover only direct ICE prime-contract actions where CoreCivic's UEI `HJGMJN1JKL46` is the federal recipient. They exclude IGSAs in which ICE obligates a state or local government and CoreCivic receives downstream operating revenue. Therefore, Dilley, West Tennessee, Farmville, and other IGSA activations appear in CoreCivic's company revenue and capacity disclosures but not necessarily in the direct-prime obligation ledger used for the $126.32 million calculation.
