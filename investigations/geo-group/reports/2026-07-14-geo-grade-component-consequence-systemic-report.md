## Bottom line

The complete linked denominator supports a descriptive finding: an ICE inspection's aggregate grade does not summarize all of its component-level content. Forty-two of 58 events in the Meets Standards / Meets Standard / Acceptable family separately report deficient components. Twelve of the 58 explicitly report zero deficient components, and four leave component status unstated or ambiguous. Those 42 divergence events span 13 facilities from 2018 through 2022, and 12 events across eight facilities contain repeat-deficiency language.

That is not yet evidence that the grade caused a weaker contract consequence. The component findings remained visible in the inspection records, the denominator contains substantial compliant and zero-deficiency controls, and the public consequence records are mixed. Folkston and Northwest are the strongest consequence-gap sequences. Golden State and South Texas have documented recommendation closures; Folkston also records vendor-paid overtime; Adelanto later records a no-findings inspection; ISAP QASP records authorize deductions; and non-GEO Torrance shows that ICE can disclose a billing penalty and guaranteed-minimum reduction.

After adding 12 explicit ACH assessments for findings #12846–#12851, null hypothesis #336 is currently least inconsistent: 5 inconsistent assessments among 37 evaluated findings (0.1351), versus 7 among 37 (0.1892) for working hypothesis #335. Least inconsistent is not confirmation. The decisive QASP, invoice, CPARS, discrepancy, UCAP-closeout, and option-decision records remain unavailable or incomplete.

## System and evidence classes

The system under review is not a single grade. It is a sequence with distinct records and decision owners:

`inspection grade → component findings/repeats → action plan or UCAP → OIG/ODO recommendation status → contracting-officer penalty/invoice/CPARS decision → option, extension, or capacity action`

This analysis keeps every link separate. A component deficiency is not a Does Not Meet standard; a completed action plan is not durable correction; an OIG closure is not necessarily a price change; an award modification is not a performance remedy unless its record says so; and a death review is not an inspection or a causal finding.

The comparison group comprises the GEO-linked Adelanto, Denver, Folkston, Golden State, Mesa Verde, Northwest, South Texas, and Karnes sequences, with B.I./ISAP contract controls and non-GEO Torrance as limited remedy comparators. The comparison is systemic across facilities and years, but no full non-GEO grade-distribution denominator exists.

## H1: what survives

Three observations remain important for hypothesis #335:

1. **A grade-only summary loses component variation.** Seventy-two percent of the 58 compliant-grade-family events (42/58) separately list deficient components. A reader using only the overall grade would miss those components.
2. **Repeat language is not rare within this bounded set.** Twelve events across eight facilities explicitly identify repeat deficiencies. Northwest's 2024 ODO follow-up separately says a completed UCAP may not have prevented two repeats.
3. **Two consequence sequences remain troubling.** Folkston has a direct statement that attempted penalties were not enforced and full funding continued. Northwest combines an unresolved minimum recommendation and repeat deficiencies after UCAP with later extension/added funding whose public description does not explain performance treatment.

These observations justify keeping #335 proposed and continuing the record requests. They do not establish that the grade caused non-enforcement, renewal, expansion, or payment.

## H0: why it is now least inconsistent

The full denominator adds counterevidence that a narrow adverse sample could not supply:

- Twelve compliant-grade events expressly state zero deficient components; 16 Karnes monthly reports state no area of noncompliance; and one deep-dive record lists no issue.
- Four compliant-grade rows leave component status unknown and remain blank rather than being recoded as either adverse or zero.
- Golden State and Desert View were rated `Not Selected` pre-occupancy and conditioned compliance on action plans, procedures, and policy revisions.
- Karnes expressly labeled one Personal Hygiene noncompliance and prescribed mitigation.
- Inspection documents reveal component deficiencies even when the aggregate grade is compliant. That is aggregation, but not concealment.
- Golden State and South Texas recommendations are publicly closed; Folkston records vendor-paid overtime; Adelanto later reports no findings.
- ISAP contract/QASP records authorize deductions or credits, while Torrance demonstrates a publicly described 25% billing penalty assessment and minimum reduction outside GEO.

These controls fit an ordinary, hierarchical grading and correction system better than a universal insulation mechanism. H0 remains incomplete because closure packages, actual deductions, price effects, and option treatment are largely not public.

## Novelty and commissioning decision

The grade/component measurement result passes the 3+ context, surprise, descriptive-support, and falsifiability gates. It fails the distinctness and causal-mechanism gates for a new hypothesis:

- #335/#336 already test the performance-consequence gap.
- The new denominator sharpens the upstream measurement question but does not supply the missing grade-to-remedy decision record.
- No full non-GEO grade/component base rate exists.
- Human actions #38, #60, and #61 and infrastructure request #150 already seek the decisive contract-administration records.

No new hypothesis or lead was created. The existing competition was updated through ACH. Verified synthesis finding #12861 records the denominator result only; it does not claim that grade compression caused weak consequences. The machine-readable gate decision is in [the novelty artifact](./2026-07-14-geo-grade-component-consequence-novelty.json).

## Falsification and next evidence

The mechanism becomes supportable only if matched records link inspection/component severity to administration outcomes. For at least Adelanto, Denver, Folkston, Northwest, South Texas, and Mesa Verde, the needed fields are:

1. QASP scoring and monthly surveillance records.
2. Discrepancy, cure, or show-cause notices and contracting-officer dispositions.
3. UCAP submission, acceptance, closure, and recurrence analysis.
4. Invoice deductions, withholds, credits, offsets, or award-fee records.
5. Releasable performance-evaluation material and option/renewal decision memoranda.
6. Occupancy/readiness forecasts and signed minimum/rate modifications.
7. A matched non-GEO inspection-grade/component denominator, not only a single remedy comparator.

Routine, severity-proportionate application across these records would falsify H1. Repeated absent/nominal consequences after controlling for operational need and contract type would falsify H0.
