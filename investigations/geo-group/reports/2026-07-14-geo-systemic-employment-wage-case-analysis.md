## Bottom line

CourtListener and RECAP were covered in two layers: the 458-docket lead-59509 inventory was re-screened, then targeted RECAP searches were used to recover older or incompletely indexed wage matters. The strongest employee-payroll record is not a single adverse merits judgment. It is a pattern of collective and class allegations followed by procedural certification or settlements:

1. A five-case California consolidation produced a **$3.69 million non-reversionary settlement** for non-exempt GEO Secure Services employees employed in California from April 11, 2020 through September 30, 2023. The agreement resolved *Hendrickson*, *Bell*, *Lopez*, *Ortiz*, and *Castillo*, conditionally certified the class only for settlement, and expressly disclaimed an admission of liability.
2. *Lewis* conditionally certified three FLSA collectives—correctional-facility instructors, lieutenants, and case managers—after plaintiffs submitted evidence of common corporate job descriptions and alleged Kronos programming that did not compensate case managers for pre-shift work and allegedly failed to approve post-shift overtime. This was an initial notice-stage ruling, not a liability finding. The case later settled and was dismissed with prejudice, but the agreement and amount are unavailable in RECAP.
3. *Mayes* alleged a rounding policy affecting correctional officers at 49 facilities in 17 states. The docket shows that the court accepted **1,446 consent filers as party plaintiffs** before approving a collective settlement and dismissing with prejudice. The complaint proves the allegation was made; it does not prove the policy existed or violated the FLSA.
4. The separate *Perez* litigation alleged that cash-in-lieu benefit payments were omitted from the regular rate used to calculate overtime and California premiums. The federal case was dismissed without prejudice, while an official *Mazzei* order judicially noticed final approval of the related state class/collective settlement on October 6, 2023.

The balanced result matters. *Garcia* began as a proposed nationwide collective but ended before certification in a $17,000 individual settlement with an express no-admission clause. *Mazzei* likewise ended in an individual settlement; the court dismissed the individual's claims with prejudice and the uncertified putative-class claims without prejudice. *Burch* ended without prejudice. Those matters should not be reported as class recoveries.

No additional government-plaintiff employee-wage case emerged beyond the previously completed EEOC and Arizona appellate sample. The Washington Attorney General detainee-labor judgment is a major government-plaintiff matter, but it belongs to the distinct detainee-work-program cluster and should not be mixed with employee payroll litigation.

## 1. California five-case settlement: $3.69 million, five actions, settlement-only certification

The strongest monetary record is an executed November 2023 agreement between GEO Secure Services and five named plaintiffs. It states that the settlement resolves:

- *Tezsanique Hendrickson v. GEO Secure Services*, San Bernardino County No. CIVSB2118376;
- *Samdeeshia Bell v. GEO Secure Services*, C.D. Cal. No. 5:21-cv-01927;
- *Priscilla Lopez v. GEO Secure Services*, Imperial County No. ECU002060;
- *Nickolas Ortiz v. The GEO Group / GEO Secure Services*, Kern County No. BCV-21-102469; and
- *Julie Castillo v. GEO Secure Services*, S.D. Cal. No. 3:22-cv-00445.

The agreement says the five complaints collectively encompassed overtime, minimum-wage, meal- and rest-period, waiting-time, wage-statement, expense-reimbursement, accrued-vacation, unfair-competition, and PAGA claims. It stipulates, for settlement only, to a class of all people employed by GEO Secure Services as non-exempt employees in California from April 11, 2020 through September 30, 2023.

The exact payment term is:

> “Defendant agrees to pay the ‘Gross Settlement Amount’ of Three Million Six Hundred and Ninety-Thousand Dollars and Zero Cents ($3,690,000.00).”

The gross amount included up to $1.23 million in fees, up to $50,000 in costs, up to $10,000 for each of five representatives, and $150,000 allocated to PAGA penalties. It was non-reversionary, aside from separately paid employer payroll taxes. The agreement also says:

> “Nothing in this Agreement is intended or will be construed as an admission by Defendant that the claims in the Lawsuit of Plaintiffs or the Settlement Class have merit or that Defendant bears any liability.”

The Imperial County court entered preliminary approval on January 22, 2024. That order conditionally certified the same class and found the settlement made in good faith. A later federal *Mazzei* order describes the Lopez matter as finally settled. The state final-approval order itself was not recovered in this wave, so the executed agreement, entered preliminary order, and later judicial description should be cited together.

This five-case settlement is separate from *Daniel Perez*. The agreement required the five plaintiffs to withdraw objections or motions to intervene in the Perez settlement; it did not purport to resolve Perez itself.

## 2. *Lewis*: corporate classifications and alleged Kronos off-clock configuration

In *Lewis v. GEO Group*, W.D. Okla. No. 5:08-cv-00881, plaintiffs sought FLSA collectives for correctional-facility instructors, lieutenants, and case managers. The November 9, 2009 order records evidence that GEO's corporate office generated generic job descriptions and made exemption decisions for instructors and lieutenants. For hourly case managers, the court summarized plaintiffs' evidence that GEO programmed Kronos so pre-shift work was not compensated and routinely failed to approve post-shift overtime.

The court held:

> “After careful consideration, the Court finds that plaintiffs have sufficiently shown putative classes of correctional facility instructors, lieutenants and case managers were subjected to defendant’s alleged wage practices of uncompensated work in excess of forty (40) hours per week.”

It conditionally certified all three collectives for notice. On January 15, 2010, it denied GEO's request for interlocutory appeal, limited the notice period to employees from November 9, 2006 forward, and clarified that the instructor and lieutenant classes covered salary-exempt employees.

The evidentiary ceiling is important: the order applied the lenient initial notice-stage standard and repeatedly described the practices as alleged. It did not decide FLSA liability. The docket later records a joint settlement motion, approval, fees, and dismissal with prejudice on June 14, 2011. RECAP lacks the settlement agreement, fee motion, and approval order, so there is no defensible public amount from this review.

## 3. *Mayes*: 49-facility allegation and 1,446 accepted opt-ins

The August 6, 2008 *Mayes* complaint named correctional officers at Graceville Correctional Facility in Florida and Marshall County Correctional Facility in Mississippi. It alleged that GEO operated 49 correctional facilities in 17 states and stated:

> “The Defendant has a ‘rounding’ policy in place on its time-keeping system that denies all corrections officers their compensation for pre-shift and post-shift work.”

That is a complaint allegation, not a finding. The stronger systemic fact is procedural: on August 18, 2009 the docket says the court accepted 1,446 people who filed consents—including 135 late or returned forms—as party plaintiffs. A notice of settlement followed on September 1. On October 30, 2009 the court approved the FLSA collective settlement and dismissed with prejudice.

RECAP indexes but does not provide the settlement agreement or approval order. The scale of participation and final settlement are documented; the payment and any negotiated practice change are not.

## 4. *Perez*: cash-in-lieu regular-rate theory and separate state settlement

The November 1, 2021 federal complaint in *Perez*, S.D. Cal. No. 3:21-cv-01846, proposed a nationwide FLSA collective and a California class. It alleged that GEO failed to include cash-in-lieu benefit payments in the regular rate used to calculate overtime and California meal/rest premiums. The California theory included employees who received overtime or premium payments in the same pay period as cash-in-lieu health-and-welfare payments.

The federal case ended in a joint dismissal without prejudice on December 13, 2022. It therefore supplies allegations, not a federal merits result. In January 2024, however, the Eastern District of California judicially noticed an October 6, 2023 order granting final approval of a class and collective settlement in the related San Diego Superior Court Perez action, No. 37-2022-00000670-CU-OE-CTL. The state agreement and amount were not recovered.

The *Mazzei* mapping order also lists the five cases later consolidated in *Lopez* and describes a “global mediation.” It is the clearest primary-source bridge between the otherwise fragmented federal and state dockets.

## 5. *Giles*: legacy CEC class settlement after GEO acquisition

*Giles v. Community Education Centers*, C.D. Cal. No. 2:16-cv-04863, was filed in July 2016 against CEC. GEO completed its acquisition of CEC in April 2017, while the litigation was pending. The court granted final approval on December 19, 2017 and awarded $285,000 in fees, $5,584.32 in costs, and enhancement payments of $10,000 and $7,500.

The docket description is primary court metadata, but RECAP does not provide the settlement agreement or final order. A gross fund reported by secondary sources was not promoted because the primary settlement record was unavailable. The case is relevant to GEO's acquired liabilities and post-acquisition resolution, but it is not evidence that GEO originated the alleged wage practices.

## 6. *Garcia*: systemic pleading, individual outcome

*Garcia v. GEO Group*, E.D. Pa. No. 5:21-cv-01867, alleged straight-time-for-overtime and exempt misclassification on behalf of proposed nationwide and Pennsylvania groups. No class or collective was certified. The parties instead executed a $17,000 individual settlement:

- $4,191.11 in wages;
- $4,191.11 in alleged liquidated damages; and
- $8,617.78 in fees and costs.

The agreement says it “is not an admission of liability or responsibility on the part of any Party,” and GEO continued to deny liability. On December 28, 2021 the court approved it as a fair and reasonable resolution of a bona fide FLSA dispute and dismissed the plaintiff's claims with prejudice. Reporting this as a class settlement would be wrong.

## Current and lower-ranked matters

### *Pasillas*

The May 2024 complaint alleges that a correctional officer at GEO's El Centro location and other non-exempt employees were required to undergo metal-detector security checks before clocking in, keep radios on during meal/rest periods, and use break time for walking and security checks. It also alleges health-and-wellness payments were omitted from some regular-rate calculations. The federal docket reflects an arbitration posture and status reports through August 29, 2025. There is no public class-certification or merits result. The case is a current recurrence signal only.

### *Mazzei*

*Mazzei* is valuable mainly because its January 2024 order maps six California wage actions and their settlement trajectories. Its own result was individual: the December 3, 2024 closure order says no class had been certified or proposed for settlement, dismissed Mazzei's individual claims with prejudice, and left the putative-class claims dismissed without prejudice. The settlement terms are not public.

### *Burch*

The docket records a January 2020 notice of settlement, followed by a February 24 stipulated dismissal without prejudice. The available dismissal does not identify a class resolution or payment. It is lower priority absent the agreement or a certification record.

## Pattern analysis, carefully bounded

The record supports a medium-confidence synthesis that GEO and acquired entities faced repeated employee-payroll challenges across three periods and several theories:

- 2008-2011: pre/post-shift time, rounding, exempt classifications, and Kronos configuration;
- 2016-2017: California wage-class exposure at acquired CEC;
- 2021-2024: regular-rate treatment of benefit payments, meal/rest periods, expense and wage-statement claims, and security-check time.

This is repetition of allegations and settlements, not proof of a continuous unlawful corporate policy. The strongest adjudicated statements are procedural—conditional certification, approval, dismissal, and class scope. No reviewed employee-payroll case produced a trial verdict or published merits holding that GEO violated wage law. The California $3.69 million agreement and the *Garcia* agreement both contain express non-admission language.

## Characterization rule

Preferred summary:

> GEO's public employee-wage record includes a $3.69 million no-admission California settlement resolving five related actions, a 1,446-opt-in correctional-officer collective that settled on undisclosed terms, and a three-job-group FLSA case conditionally certified on alleged common classification and timekeeping practices. These records establish litigation scale and procedural outcomes, not a merits finding of a continuous unlawful corporate policy.

Do not merge this employee-payroll cluster with detainee voluntary-work-program cases. *Washington v. GEO*, *Nwauzor*, *Menocal*, and *Novoa* raise different worker-status, minimum-wage, unjust-enrichment, and federal-contract issues and have their own investigation track.
