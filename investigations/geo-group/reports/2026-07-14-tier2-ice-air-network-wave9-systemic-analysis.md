## Bottom line

The layered chain creates a **real practical public-reconstruction gap**, but the current evidence does **not** make that gap diagnostically meaningful as evidence of anything beyond ordinary prime/subcontract architecture, reporting limits, and redacted operational records.

CSI is plainly visible as ICE's legal prime and federal recipient. GEO publicly disclosed GTI's support subcontract and a bounded annualized revenue estimate. GlobalX publicly disclosed a current CSI/U.S.-government contract through the end of 2026, and an official state source names GlobalX and Eastern as ICE flight providers. Those facts weigh against a strong theory that layering uniformly hides downstream participants.

The residual gap is nevertheless operationally important: no reviewed structured subaward row quantifies GTI or carrier payments; GlobalX-to-ICE is composite rather than a single clean federal edge; Eastern-to-CSI is unresolved; the replacement-carrier package is redacted; and zero current tails are confirmed. Those absences prevent a recipient-only or fleet-only analyst from reconstructing who was paid for what and which aircraft performed which mission.

ACH therefore ranks the real null—ordinary prime/subcontract structure plus routine lawful redaction and reporting limits—as **least inconsistent**, not confirmed. The current record does not establish that every redaction is lawful or that every reporting obligation was satisfied. The decisive FSRS applicability, redaction-basis, subcontract, vendor-ledger, and preflight records remain missing.

## Compact evidence assessment

The full row treatment is in `2026-07-14-tier2-ice-air-network-wave9-evidence-matrix.csv`.

### Evidence against a stronger visibility-failure theory

- **Legal prime and order money are visible.** USAspending names CSI on the BPA and current/bridge orders (#12895, #12899, #12910).
- **GTI's support layer is visible outside recipient data.** GEO publicly named GTI, CSI, ICE, the support scope, the emergency predecessor, the option-inclusive term, and an approximately $25 million annualized forecast (#12892–#12894).
- **The operator layer is partly visible through participants and officials.** GlobalX directly disclosed its CSI/U.S.-government contract (#12976), and Massachusetts officially named GlobalX and Eastern as ICE flight providers (#12978).
- **Recipient data behaves differently when GTI is the legal prime.** GTI is directly visible on its separate 2021 ICE emergency contract (#12909), supporting the distinction between legal recipient and subcontractor rather than a uniform entity-suppression theory.

These points do not provide a complete chain, but they disconfirm a theory that layering uniformly conceals the participating companies.

### Evidence that establishes a practical gap but not its cause

- Five exact order/call subaward queries returned zero public rows (#12901). Without the FSRS applicability, exemption, waiver, or correction record, the zero is compatible with both hypotheses.
- The Classic Air amended complaint says the October 2024 replacement package included commitments, airworthiness certificates, OpSpecs, and teaming agreements, while the public version redacts the identities (#12981). That is a party pleading quoting the administrative record, not a merits holding.
- The SOW requires CSI to give the COR aircraft registration and crew qualification information before departure. An authoritative roster therefore should exist internally, but its nonpublication is compatible with both ordinary operational handling and a more consequential visibility problem.
- Eastern is officially connected to ICE without a primary CSI edge (#12978). That broken public chain is compatible with an undisclosed ordinary subcontract, a different contracting path, or incomplete public records.
- CSI's Air Wisconsin/CRJ200 acquisition establishes ownership and capacity, not an ICE assignment (#12979–#12980).

## ACH: material visibility failure versus the real null

### H1 — beyond-ordinary visibility failure

The combined public-recipient, subaward, operator, asset, and support records systematically fail to expose downstream economic and operational attribution in a way ordinary contracting and ordinary redaction do not adequately explain. This is a visibility proposition only; it does not allege illegality or intent.

### H0 — ordinary structure and routine lawful redaction

ICE accurately reports CSI as the legal prime; CSI coordinates the full service through carriers and support vendors; participant/official disclosures expose some downstream roles; FSRS coverage may be incomplete or inapplicable; and operational/proprietary records are lawfully redacted or not routinely published.

**Verdict:** H0 is least inconsistent. The public record shows a practical visibility gap, but it does not currently show a gap that requires a special concealment or disclosure-failure mechanism. This is not a finding that every redaction is lawful or every FSRS duty was satisfied; those are key assumptions still to be tested.

## Exact records that would discriminate

1. **FSRS applicability and compliance file for the five exact orders/calls.** Mandatory-but-missing material reports would favor H1. Reconciled reports or a documented exemption/non-applicability determination would favor H0.
2. **Unredacted or segregable Classic Air Tabs 91, 92b, 94, and 101 plus the withholding basis.** Unsupported withholding of identity-neutral carrier/certificate/aircraft fields would favor H1. A supported, narrow, segregability-tested redaction and internally consistent roster would favor H0.
3. **CSI-GTI subcontract; current carrier teaming/subcontract agreements; consent file; amendments; invoices; vendor ledger.** Material unreconciled payees/roles would favor H1. An ordinary chain that reconciles to reports and accounting would favor H0.
4. **Call `70CDCR26FC0000001` flight-leg/preflight records.** The discriminating extract is operating-carrier legal name/certificate, N-number, aircraft model, mission date/type, and no personal crew identifiers. Absent or inconsistent required records would favor H1; a complete internally reconciled roster with legitimately withheld sensitive fields would favor H0.
5. **CSI's response to the May 28, 2025 House letter.** The requested subcontract agreements and monthly counts would test the reporting-derived GlobalX share and the carrier chain.

## Disconfirming evidence and key assumptions

The strongest disconfirming evidence for H1 is that the downstream companies themselves disclosed material edges. GEO did not hide that GTI was under CSI, and GlobalX did not hide that its current U.S.-government relationship ran through CSI. Massachusetts also publicly named current ICE flight providers. A theory that treats every missing federal edge as affirmative concealment cannot accommodate those disclosures without becoming self-sealing.

H0 depends on unverified assumptions:

1. The five exact orders/calls were correctly handled under applicable FSRS rules.
2. The redacted carrier/aircraft fields have a sustainable legal or operational basis and non-sensitive portions were segregated appropriately.
3. CSI's internal vendor, invoice, and preflight records are complete and reconcile to actual performance.
4. Participant disclosures are not materially selective in a way that hides economically important counterparties or role changes.

If any assumption fails, H0 weakens sharply.

## Premortem

Assume the H0 ranking is wrong. The likeliest failure is over-crediting voluntary company disclosures and treating unknown reporting/redaction rules as benign. A visible company name can coexist with materially hidden payments, substitutions, or asset assignments. The fastest corrective check is the FSRS applicability/compliance file plus an identity-neutral current flight-leg/preflight extract reconciled to CSI's vendor ledger and carrier invoices.
