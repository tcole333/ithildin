# California detention-services competition reconstruction

**Lead:** 58084  
**Solicitation:** 70CDCR20R00000002  
**Awards examined:** 70CDCR20D00000006 (Management & Training Corporation, Imperial Regional Detention Facility) and 70CDCR20D00000007 (CoreCivic, Otay Mesa Detention Center)  
**Cutoff:** 2026-07-14  
**Confidence:** medium on the competition assessment; high on quoted solicitation terms and federal award fields

## Bottom line

ICE conducted this acquisition as a negotiated, full-and-open procurement, not as a sole-source procurement. The recovered final solicitation anticipated “several” hybrid firm-fixed-price/labor-hour IDIQ contracts to multiple contractors and separately evaluated Requirements A–D. USAspending records the one-offer value on each of the two examined IDIQ awards. Separate proposals and two awardees make those fields consistent with one offer for each award's associated requirement, but do not prove that interpretation. Without the proposal log, the fields cannot safely be read as one total proposal across the entire four-requirement procurement.

The acquisition was nevertheless structurally demanding. A viable offeror needed one or more already-existing or renovatable detention facilities, each with at least 250 beds, in a tight geographic radius; readiness within 60 days; the ability to meet detailed detention, transportation, medical, court, office, parking, environmental, and design requirements; and relevant past performance. New construction was excluded. These terms naturally favored incumbent or incumbent-like detention operators with suitable facilities. Anonymous Q&A Part 3 repeatedly sought changes to match a “current facility,” and ICE permitted evaluation of limited waiver requests while retaining many substantive requirements.

That evidence supports a finding of a constrained practical bidder pool. It does **not** establish that ICE improperly tailored the solicitation, excluded a known competitor, or made either award without competition. The missing market-research, acquisition-planning, bidder, evaluation, price-analysis, and source-selection records are necessary to test those stronger propositions.

## Requirements and competitive structure

The final solicitation separated the California need into four geographic requirements and instructed offerors to submit a separate proposal for each one. An offeror could propose one or more facilities for a requirement, but each offered facility had to house at least 250 detainees. The two awards examined here align with the southern California requirements:

| Feature | Requirement A / Imperial Regional | Requirement B / Otay Mesa |
|---|---|---|
| Approximate solicitation population | 700: updated addendum estimates 640 male and 60 female | 2,100: updated addendum estimates 1,700 male and 400 female |
| Geography | Within 50 miles of 1150 E. Birch St., Calexico | Within 50 miles of 880 Front St., San Diego |
| Facility structure | One or several facilities; each at least 250 beds | One or several facilities; each at least 250 beds |
| Core service scope | Detention, armed transportation, on-call guards, medical, food; ability to transport at least 300 detainees in 24 hours | Detention and support services, plus detailed ICE/ERO, medical, court, office, secure-perimeter, design, and parking requirements |
| Readiness/construction | Existing facility or renovation; no new construction; ready within 60 days | Same |
| Evaluation | Best-value tradeoff; technical/management and past performance collectively more important than price; environmental factor sufficient/insufficient | Same |

The solicitation allowed requested waivers from design and site-specific requirements if individually identified and explained; ICE would evaluate their scope, reasonableness, and feasibility. That mechanism broadened the range of technically possible facilities, but did not eliminate the principal entry constraints: scale, location, existing/renovatable physical plant, rapid transition, operating capability, and extensive compliance obligations.

Q&A Part 2 said an existing provider that had already completed transition-in and ramp-up could submit only a transition-out/ramp-down plan. Part 3 contains numerous anonymous questions asking ICE to delete or revise specifications to match a “current facility” or “current facility operations.” ICE accepted some administrative accommodations, added the waiver-evaluation language, and rejected multiple proposed deletions. The questions are consistent with participation by at least one incumbent operator, but they do not identify the questioner and cannot establish that ICE tailored the final terms for CoreCivic, MTC, or any other named bidder.

The award records must be read by metric. An IDIQ vehicle ceiling/current potential value is not an obligation, an outlay, an invoice, or recognized corporate revenue.

| Competition field | Full and open; negotiated proposal/quote; one offer | Full and open; negotiated proposal/quote; one offer |
| Current performance end date | 2029-12-19 | 2029-12-19 |
| FY2025 task-order obligations | $47,203,837.48 | $114,347,542.62 |
| FY2025 task-order outlays | $46,799,471.42 | $114,412,050.47 |

CoreCivic’s current IDIQ value is about 3.218 times MTC’s; its FY2025 task-order obligations were about 2.422 times MTC’s. Neither ratio is an occupancy ratio. The two vehicle values reflect different facility scale, scope, modifications, pricing, and ordering assumptions, while task obligations are funding actions for a specific performance period.

The pricing structure contained two detention-bed tiers—guaranteed minimum and above-guaranteed-minimum—plus transportation mileage, guard hours, detainee work-program payments, and facility-upgrade/other-direct-cost items. The IDIQ minimum order was $1 million over the possible 15-year term; that is not a bed guarantee or occupancy guarantee. Funding occurred at the task-order level.

Public pricing is incomplete:

- Imperial P00005 states “Detention Services Guaranteed Minimum up to 640 beds” for the option period, but its bed-day rates are redacted. The modification approved an MTC request for equitable adjustment and increased the guaranteed-minimum and above-guaranteed-minimum rates.
- A CoreCivic task-order funding description divides detention service into “beds 1-750” and “beds 751 and above.” The signed Otay Mesa IDIQ modifications reviewed redact the bed count/rate and not-to-exceed values, so the task description is the public basis for the 750-bed tier.
- Otay P00020 leaves 60,000 guard hours per year visible but redacts the associated rate and most pricing. P00022 leaves a transportation not-to-exceed quantity of 180,000 miles per year visible while redacting the mileage rate and retroactive equitable-adjustment amount.

The solicitation’s approximate populations, a pricing-tier quantity, and a guaranteed-minimum quantity are not actual occupancy. No facility-level average daily population, daily manifests, invoice quantities, or billed bed-day data were recovered, so an occupancy comparison cannot responsibly be calculated from this record.

## Assessment

### Supported facts

1. ICE advertised a full-and-open, negotiated procurement and contemplated multiple IDIQ awards across four separately evaluated requirements.
2. MTC’s and CoreCivic’s individual award records each report one offer received; the public award fields do not disclose total proposals across all four requirements.
3. The final requirements demanded large, geographically constrained, existing or renovatable detention capacity operational within 60 days and excluded new construction.
4. Anonymous Q&A documents engagement by a party describing a current facility; ICE partly accommodated waiver requests and retained many substantive specifications.
5. Public modifications and task descriptions expose selected quantities but redact the rates needed for an exact price comparison.

### Inference, not proof

The combination of capacity, location, readiness, physical-plant, operating, and past-performance requirements likely limited the practical bidder pool to established detention operators or teams controlling suitable facilities. That is a medium-confidence procurement-structure inference.

### Not established

The recovered record does not establish an improper restrictive specification, a procurement written for a named company, collusion, failure to consider an interested bidder, or a sole-source award. It also does not show why only one offer is recorded for each examined requirement or whether ICE obtained adequate price competition after receiving those offers.
