# *Burciaga v. The GEO Group, Inc.* — public-record case analysis

**Case:** *Daniel Burciaga v. The Geo Group Inc.*, No. 3:12-cv-02059-L-WVG (S.D. Cal.)  
**Research cutoff:** July 14, 2026  
**Lead:** #59716  
**Thread:** 113 — Litigation, Oversight & Detention Conditions

## Bottom line

The public record supports a narrower and more consequential conclusion than the earlier CourtListener inventory could make: *Burciaga* produced a **nonfinal summary-judgment ruling addressing False Claims Act scienter and materiality**, not merely an untested allegation. A later federal court described the unavailable February 28, 2017 order as finding evidence that GEO had “destroy[ed] records to keep them from ACA auditors” sufficient to satisfy the FCA scienter standard. An official DOJ publication separately quotes *Burciaga* for the rule that a defendant cannot make FCA liability depend on the government detecting misconduct that was designed to be difficult to detect.

That does **not** establish final FCA liability. The publicly available docket continued through pretrial management and multiple settlement conferences, then the federal statistical record coded the case as **voluntarily dismissed on May 25, 2018**. The official code list distinguishes voluntary dismissal (code 12) from dismissal as settled (code 13). No public document recovered in this wave establishes a settlement, payment, admission, damages award, dismissal with or without prejudice, or prevailing party.

The complaint, ECF 41, ECF 70–75, and the May 25 terminal record remain unavailable through CourtListener/RECAP and the associated Internet Archive item. Accordingly, the exact alleged false claim or certification, government agency and payment stream, whether the action included qui tam claims or only an individual retaliation theory, and any federal intervention or declination remain unresolved. Nothing in the recovered record is sufficient to classify the case as an ICE or DHS matter.

## Merits evidence preserved in later federal sources

### Scienter — later federal court description of *Burciaga*

The exact later source is U.S. District Judge Ronnie Abrams’s March 28, 2023 opinion and order in *United States ex rel. Hart v. McKesson Corp.*, No. 15-cv-0903 (RA), 2023 WL 2663528, Dkt. 190 (S.D.N.Y.). That order was reproduced at appendix pages 34a–62a of the [certiorari petition appendix in *Hart v. McKesson Corp.*, No. 23-1293](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/23/23-1293/314413/20240607160243917_appendix%20to%20certiorari%20petition%20-%20US%20ex%20rel.%20Hart%20v.%20McKesson.pdf). At appendix pages 56a–57a, Judge Abrams characterized *Burciaga* as:

> “finding that the evidence that ‘Defendant destroy[ed] records to keep them from ACA auditors’ was sufficient to meet the FCA scienter standard”

**Label:** later federal-court description of an earlier summary-judgment holding.  
**Permitted use:** *Burciaga* had evidence sufficient to meet the FCA scienter standard at the summary-judgment stage.  
**Prohibited use:** GEO destroyed records as an ultimately adjudicated fact; GEO committed fraud; “ACA” is a particular organization without the original order; or any final-liability characterization.

### Materiality and concealment — DOJ quotation of *Burciaga*

The exact DOJ source is the Executive Office for United States Attorneys’ *Journal of Federal Law and Practice*, volume 70, number 3, “Combatting Health-Care Fraud” (August 2022), page 55. The [official DOJ PDF](https://www.justice.gov/usao/page/file/1532986/dl?inline=) cites *Burciaga*, 2017 WL 10605270 at *6, for this proposition:

> “A defendant should not be permitted to ‘take advantage of [its] own wrong’ by making its liability hinge on the Government’s ability to detect something that is designed to be difficult to detect.”

The passage appears in DOJ’s discussion of continued government payment and actual knowledge in FCA materiality analysis. It supports that *Burciaga* addressed concealment and government knowledge; it does not identify the government customer or prove the underlying allegations.

## Procedural record

- **August 23, 2012:** case filed. CourtListener and FJC metadata classify it under 31 U.S.C. § 3729 and nature-of-suit code 375, False Claims Act liability. Metadata is classification, not proof of the complaint’s allegations.
- **July–August 2015:** ECF 29 and 31 are indexed as summary-judgment motions, followed by opposition and reply papers. The movants and requested claim-by-claim relief cannot be determined from the short classifications alone.
- **February 28, 2017:** ECF 41, indexed as an order on motion for summary judgment; full text unavailable. Later federal sources preserve the scienter and materiality propositions above.
- **March–July 2017:** the docket shows renewed scheduling, a reconsideration motion and order, pretrial papers, and trial-management orders. This sequence confirms that ECF 41 was not a final termination of the entire case.
- **June 27, 2017:** ECF 55 scheduled an August 30 mandatory settlement conference. The order said conference discussions would be “informal, off the record, privileged, and confidential.” That explains why the order cannot establish an outcome.
- **August 30, 2017 and February 28, 2018:** unnumbered docket entries are classified as mandatory settlement conferences. Neither text nor outcome is public.
- **April 9–17, 2018:** ECF 70–75 comprise an unavailable order-to-show-cause and response sequence, an order terminating deadlines and hearings, another response, and an order.
- **May 25, 2018:** CourtListener/FJC metadata reports termination. FJC disposition code 12 means voluntary dismissal and is distinct from code 13, dismissal as settled.
