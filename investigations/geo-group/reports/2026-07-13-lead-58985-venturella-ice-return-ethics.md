## Appointment and succession-law limits

6 U.S.C. § 113 lists the ICE Director among officers appointed by the President with Senate advice and consent. GAO's ICE-specific B-334562 decision describes DHS Delegation No. 00106, Annex N, as placing the ICE Deputy Director first in succession and making that position the first assistant for Federal Vacancies Reform Act purposes. GAO also recorded DHS's representation that no ICE Director function or duty met the FVRA definition of a function required by statute or regulation to be performed only by that officer.

That framework explains why “Acting Director” and “Senior Official Performing the Duties” are legally meaningful, but it does not itself establish Venturella's authority. A 2026 FEMA-specific GAO decision discusses DHS's use of SOPTD designations for delegable functions after acting-service limits; it is only analogous and is not evidence of Venturella's instrument. The exact ICE appointment, succession, delegation, position-description, and effective-date records were not found publicly. Lead #59228 requests those documents.

## Ethics framework and disclosure limits

Potentially relevant rules include 18 U.S.C. § 208 (personal and substantial participation in a particular matter with a direct and predictable effect on the employee's own or imputed financial interests), 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502 (appearance concerns and covered relationships, including a former employer generally for one year and potentially longer where another relationship continues), and 5 C.F.R. § 2635.503 (certain extraordinary payments from a former employer). Application depends on current holdings, payment rights, appointment date, duties, and agency ethics determinations.

The Biden ethics pledge under Executive Order 13989 was revoked on January 20, 2025 by Executive Order 14148. No current replacement pledge applicable to Venturella was located. Revocation of that pledge does not remove the statutory and regulatory standards above.
