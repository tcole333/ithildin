## What the pleadings allege—and what the court has not decided

The third amended complaint alleges that GEO operated Lawrenceville Correctional Center under contract with the Virginia Department of Corrections and that medical and correctional personnel failed to respond adequately before Damian Hernandez died on November 6, 2020. It advances competing factual and causation allegations concerning Hernandez's symptoms, the emergency response, sexual assault, self-harm, and the timing of the 911 call.

Those are allegations. The May 2025 opinion expressly said: “At this stage, the Court must accept as true the facts set forth in the Third Amended Complaint.” The court has not entered a public final liability or causation finding in the reviewed record. The June 2026 summary-judgment papers likewise contain party assertions and proposed expert opinions, not findings of fact.

## Monetary amounts

- The third amended complaint demanded $10 million jointly and severally “or such greater amount as may be determined at the trial,” $350,000 in punitive damages, costs, prejudgment interest, and fees. A demand is not an award, liability finding, or settlement valuation.
- The May 2025 opinion held that the medical-malpractice claims against Jordan were subject to Virginia's $2.45 million statutory cap. That is a legal ceiling applicable to those claims, not a judgment.
- Plaintiff's economist proposed lost-earnings figures ranging from $74,707 to $218,528 depending on assumptions. The May 2026 court excluded the economist and barred those opinions from trial. They are not recoveries or court-adopted damages calculations.
- No public settlement amount or term was identified.

## June 2026 summary-judgment motions

The public docket shows three defense motions. Only the GEO/officer supporting memorandum, ECF 242, was available in RECAP.

GEO and the officer defendants argue that all remaining claims fail because plaintiff lacks admissible expert proof on standard of care and causation, because self-harm purportedly constitutes contributory negligence and an illegal act under Virginia law, and because they contend the factual record shows a prompt emergency response. Their requested relief is dismissal with prejudice. These are defense arguments; no public ruling adopts them.

The Kamara and Jordan supporting memoranda, ECF 238 and ECF 240, were not publicly available in the reviewed RECAP record. Their grounds therefore cannot be responsibly reconstructed from captions or from another party's characterization.
