## Bottom line

Salus is a useful **existence proof**, not a base-rate estimate. Its public record shows that a non-GEO firm could react to a new administration's removal policy with an unsolicited proposal, undergo months of agency evaluation and refinement, move through an urgency-based limited competition, receive the strongest technical rating and lowest evaluated scenario price among four timely proposals, and then receive a large DHS vehicle. That sequence makes a policy-responsive procurement channel plausible without invoking GEO political access.

The case does **not** establish a general Trump-era DHS procurement architecture. The principal award, its later modifications, and Salus's later ICE aircraft-maintenance purchase order concern one vendor and closely related policy space; they are not three independent contractor contexts. The source record also preserves material procurement-integrity questions: DHS acknowledged appearances of biased ground rules, unequal access to information, and impropriety before approving an OCI waiver. The waiver documents agency treatment of risk; it proves neither an actual conflict nor the absence of one.

For the Salus award itself, the policy-responsive procurement-channel explanation is less inconsistent with the verified public record than an explanation requiring a demonstrated pre-award political-access or favor bridge. That is a bounded ACH reading, not an exoneration or probability estimate. The result is driven primarily by the seven-invitee/four-offer competition, comparative technical and evaluated-price record, antecedent mission experience, and finding #12975's bounded failure to locate a pre-award bridge. None rules out influence through sealed, protected, private, or unidentified channels.

## Competing explanations and boundaries

The analysis compares two non-exclusive explanations for the observed Salus outcome:

- **P — policy-responsive procurement channel:** executive policy and mission urgency created demand; Salus supplied an unsolicited operational concept and antecedent experience; DHS used limited competition, comparative evaluation, OCI review/waiver, and urgency authority to select and fund the contractor. This explanation permits process flaws, aggressive vendor initiative, and unresolved OCI risk. It does not assert that the procurement was error-free.
- **A — political-access or favor channel:** an unobserved political relationship or favor materially affected Salus's ground rules, waiver, evaluation, timing, or selection. The testable bridge would be a pre-award political contact, instruction, request, compensation path, specification intervention, evaluation intervention, or decision participation.

The rivals are assessed only for Salus. They are not database hypotheses because this single control does not pass the three-independent-context novelty gate. No finding is scored against GEO-specific hypothesis groups #335/#336 or #337/#338; Salus does not establish applied GEO performance consequences or coordination among GEO's personnel, consulting, and lobbying functions.

## What the control establishes

### 1. A policy-to-proposal-to-award path existed independently of GEO

Finding #12967 establishes the urgency competition's core shape: seven potential offerors, four timely proposals, a single-award vehicle, and a $915 million maximum, while preserving the unresolved solicitation-number conflict. Finding #12969 adds comparative evaluation: Salus alone received high confidence for technical approach and had the lowest $1,409,688 evaluated scenario price. Finding #12973 supplies an antecedent-experience rationale from the Afghan CARE mission, subject to an unresolved LLC/Corp. identity bridge.

Together these records are consistent with P. They are not strongly inconsistent with A because access could, in theory, shape solicitation scope, the invitee pool, ground rules, or waiver while leaving a facial competition and comparative evaluation intact. The evaluated scenario price is never treated as the $915 million ceiling, an obligation, an outlay, a payment, or revenue.

### 2. The OCI record is two-sided and non-diagnostic between broad rivals

Finding #12970 records that DHS acknowledged appearances of potential biased ground rules, unequal information, and impropriety, then relied on mitigation and urgency to waive potential OCI restrictions. This is consistent with P insofar as the agency identified and processed procurement-integrity risk. It is also consistent with A insofar as the record identifies a channel through which early vendor participation could have mattered. Because both explanations predict the observation, the waiver is not diagnostic between them.

The waiver cannot be used as proof that no conflict existed. Conversely, the appearance language cannot be upgraded into a finding that an actual conflict, political direction, or improper award existed.

### 3. The public political record is boundedly unfavorable to a demonstrated pre-award bridge

Finding #12975 is the most discriminating input. The reviewed public procurement, court, entity, subaward, federal-filing, and exact-name campaign-finance records did not identify a Salus–GEO relationship or verified pre-award political-appointee/favor bridge. Congressional exhibits later raised Lewandowski-related allegations; Salus denied value exchange and represented that the first Walters–Lewandowski contact occurred in December 2025. The allegations and the contractor's response are party positions, not adjudicated facts.

This evidence is inconsistent with A **only within the reviewed public/redacted coverage** and is consistent with P. It does not establish universal absence because sealed source-selection material, private communications, unidentified intermediaries, beneficial interests, and nonpublic payment records remain outside the review.

### 4. The court posture does not decide the political-access question

Finding #12971 must remain separated into its distinct dispositions:

1. dismissal for lack of Article III standing;
2. alternative dismissal for lack of statutory standing;
3. further alternative failure to prove interested-party status and prejudice; and
4. no GAO merits decision after outcome-predictive ADR and withdrawal.

The court's separate administrative-record observation of no bad faith or unfair dealings was record-bounded and arose while denying judicial notice. It is not an exoneration of all procurement criticism. The GAO ADR likewise is not a merits decision. Neither the threshold holdings nor later allegations discriminate materially between P and A.

### 5. Scale and downstream opacity do not identify causation

Finding #12968 correctly separates $697,901,214.99 in obligations from $386,730,317.83 in reported outlays for two identified child orders. Finding #12974 records zero structured CSRO subaward rows while partner identities remain redacted. These establish scale and a public reporting boundary, not selection causation, contractor profit, political influence, or the absence of CTAs, vendors, affiliates, or subcontractors.

Finding #12972 shows a later $4,317,331.04 ICE aircraft-maintenance purchase order using urgency/only-one-source procedures. It is consistent with repeated DHS reliance on Salus after the CSRO award, but it is the same vendor and cannot independently establish a government-wide or department-wide architecture.
