## Bottom line

The State case is much narrower and procedurally thinner than its caption suggests. The federal record does not contain the underlying Newark Municipal Court complaint. It contains a three-page exhibit whose only substantive page is a municipal case-search result for complaint S 2025 5303. That page listed an active “COMPLIANCE” charge, cited `NJAC 5:23-2.31B`, and simultaneously marked the charge status “Invalid.” GEO removed on June 23, 2025 and denied the apparent charge, while reserving the right to amend after service of the actual complaint. Through the July 14, 2026 public check, the federal docket contains no merits motion, injunction, opinion, trial disposition, or appeal. Its last substantive public event was a December 11, 2025 status conference.

GEO's removal notice supplies extensive federal defenses—federal-officer removal, Yearsley derivative sovereign immunity, intergovernmental immunity, field preemption, and obstacle preemption—but no court has accepted them in this case. The notice also incorporates facts and arguments from the separate City of Newark case. Those two matters overlap at Delaney Hall and the March 31, 2025 attempted inspection, but they are not the same case and should not be merged.

The related City case provides the fuller factual record. Newark sought inspection access and an order stopping occupancy and construction. The United States appeared as an interested nonparty and stated both that ICE expected GEO to comply with applicable federal, state, and local building codes and that ICE would schedule needed inspections. City inspectors entered on April 25, 2025 and reported a mixture of permit/maintenance issues and functioning safety systems. Newark later withdrew its emergency motion after obtaining access; GEO's Rule 19/dismissal motion was administratively terminated without prejudice; the case moved into mediation and remains unresolved.

The July 2025 Third Circuit CoreCivic decision does not settle the local-code dispute. It held that New Jersey's AB 5207 ban on new private immigration-detention contracts violated intergovernmental immunity as applied to CoreCivic, but expressly limited the holding to a contract ban in a market where the federal government was the only counterparty. It left neutral conditions on federal contractors open. The separate GEO v. Murphy case then ended in a consent permanent injunction against applying the contract ban to GEO—not a judgment exempting Delaney Hall from ordinary building, fire, health, or inspection requirements.

## Root case: what the record actually establishes

### Underlying municipal matter

The underlying matter is Newark Municipal Court complaint S 2025 5303. The case-search page attached to GEO's removal listed:

- court: Newark Municipal Court (0714);
- issue date: May 14, 2025;
- offense date: March 31, 2025;

- case status: “Active”; and

“Invalid” is a field in the captured search result; the record does not explain whether it reflects data validation, charging status, later disposition, or a clerical condition. It must not be treated as a judicial merits ruling. Conversely, “Active” on a May/June 2025 search page does not establish current municipal posture in July 2026.

GEO asserted that it had not been served with the complaint and that the municipal clerk declined to provide counsel a copy. Its June 27 answer denied the apparent N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.31(b) charge and reserved amendment after service. The federal docket reviewed still does not supply the complaint. Therefore, any statement that the State charged a specific refusal-of-entry subsection, permit violation, stop-work violation, or occupancy violation would exceed the record.

### What N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.31(b) covers

The official New Jersey text, current through the June 15, 2026 Register, describes a penalty framework rather than a single substantive offense. Subsection (b)(1) reaches violations of the Uniform Construction Code, construction contrary to permit conditions, failure to comply with enforcement orders, and false or misleading submissions. Subsection (b)(2) separately addresses knowing refusal of lawful inspector entry or unreasonable interference with inspection. Subsection (b)(4) generally requires a notice of violation and time to terminate before penalty assessment, with specified immediate-penalty exceptions.

Related provisions matter to the parties' theories. Section 5:23-2.1 states that the subchapter protects public safety, health, egress, sanitation, ventilation, and fire safety. Section 5:23-2.14 generally requires permits before regulated construction, alteration, equipment installation, or use changes, subject to exceptions. Section 5:23-2.31(d) requires a written stop-construction order to state its reasons and the conditions under which work may resume. The root record does not reveal which of these provisions the missing complaint invokes.

### GEO's removal and defenses

GEO removed under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331, 1441, 1442, and 1446. It argued:

1. it acted under ICE as a federal contractor and could not admit inspectors to secure portions without ICE approval;
2. Yearsley derivative sovereign immunity protected conduct authorized by a valid federal delegation;
3. state/local enforcement directly regulated or discriminated against the federal government and its contractor;
4. Congress occupied the field of immigration detention; and
5. local enforcement obstructed federal immigration and procurement choices.

GEO also asserted that the solicitation used Performance-Based National Detention Standards (PBNDS), while the National Detention Standards (NDS) controlled at Delaney Hall. It acknowledged that those standards contain general compliance obligations for applicable federal, state, and local safety, sanitation, and fire rules, but argued they did not confer unrestricted municipal inspection authority and could be satisfied through qualified third parties.

These propositions are GEO's litigating positions. The root court has not ruled on federal-officer jurisdiction, immunity, preemption, contract interpretation, access control, liability, or relief.

## Related City case: claims, federal participation, inspections, and disposition

### Newark's complaint and requested relief

Newark filed a verified state complaint on March 31, 2025 and GEO removed it on April 1. The complaint alleged that Delaney Hall was being prepared for new occupancy without required permits or a continued-occupancy certificate and that City officials were denied entry on March 31. Its four counts were two “Failure to Permit Entry for Inspection” counts and two “Violation of City Code” counts, with fictitious-entity duplicates. Newark sought access; cessation of occupancy and construction pending inspection/compliance; fire, health, and safety compliance; damages; fees; and costs.

Those are allegations and requested remedies, not findings. The City declarations described exterior observations and inspection concerns, but several asserted risks—particularly foodborne-disease scenarios—were prospective because officials had not entered the facility.

### Motions and competing legal theories

GEO moved under Rules 12(b)(7), 12(e), and 19 to join ICE and dismiss, or require a more definite statement. It argued that ICE controlled access, was necessary, could not be joined because of sovereign immunity, and that the complaint did not identify the actual “City Code” provisions. It separately opposed preliminary relief on Yearsley, intergovernmental-immunity, preemption, irreparable-harm, and public-interest grounds. GEO requested a 60 million dollar bond if an injunction issued, supported by Amber Martin's declaration that GEO expected more than 60 million dollars in first-full-year annualized revenue. No bond was ordered and no injunction issued.

Newark argued that the ICE contract and detention standards required state/local compliance and that ordinary building, fire, health, and elevator laws applied to GEO as owner. Its preliminary motion requested an immediate stop to occupancy/construction and inspection access. That motion was ultimately withdrawn rather than granted or denied.

### United States Statement of Interest

DOJ first noticed potential participation on April 17 and filed a Statement of Interest on April 23 under 28 U.S.C. § 517. The government said:

> “ICE is prepared to schedule any needed inspection of Delaney Hall.”

It also said GEO and ICE expected compliance with contract terms, including “applicable Federal, state, and local building codes.” The United States urged denial of preliminary relief and dismissal based on mootness or imminent mootness because inspection access would be scheduled. If the court reached joinder, the government said it would oppose and assert sovereign immunity.

This was the United States' litigating position, not a holding. It nevertheless undercuts any categorical proposition that federal status alone eliminated applicable local building-code obligations.

### Contract-document gap

The court ordered relevant February 2025 contract portions at ECF 16, then the complete contract at ECF 24. GEO said the contract was approximately 2,300 pages, contained security and proprietary information, and required ICE approval before release. ECF 19, described as GEO's response containing relevant contract portions, is unavailable in RECAP. GEO filed permits and certificates as ECF 28 with 14 exhibits, but only exhibits 1–6 and 13–14 are publicly retrievable; exhibits 7–12 are unavailable. The docket does not expose a public complete-contract filing after the extension order.

Available sworn and pleading evidence supports only carefully attributed statements: GEO called the award a 15-year fixed-price contract for a 1,000-bed facility and expected more than 60 million dollars in first-full-year annualized revenue; GEO said ICE had exclusive use and secure-access oversight. The actual PIID, ceiling, option structure, price schedule, complete standards hierarchy, permit clauses, access language, remedies, and modifications must come from the contract or separate procurement records, not this docket alone.

### April 25 inspection and competing reports

ICE approved City access and nine Newark officials inspected Delaney Hall on April 25. Newark's April 30 report and May 5 supplemental report identified, among other things:

- a front gate and associated electrical work the City said required permits;
- door closers and fire-rating penetrations;
- GFCI, laundry disconnect, conduit, and wiring issues;
- gas-valve, vacuum-breaker, waste-pipe, and other plumbing items;
- a barbershop sink the City said required a permit;
- need for an annual elevator inspection and current compliance certificate; and
- a disputed need for a new or continued certificate of occupancy.

The same reports also recorded current fire-extinguisher servicing, working exit signs, normal elevator fire service and alarms, no elevator safety issues, and general compliance under the code applicable to the 2007 addition, subject to recommendations and listed corrections. Newark's supplemental report distinguishes maintenance items from permit items.

GEO responded that most valid items were corrected or underway, disputed the occupancy premise, relied on a 2007 certificate describing work/use for 1,196 persons and a May 2024 state fire-registration certificate for 1,000–1,999 beds, and submitted vendor and sanitation records. These are competing administrative and party records. No court adjudicated whether the existing certificate remained sufficient, whether every cited item was a violation, or whether remediation was complete.

### Current City-case posture

On August 13, 2025 Newark told the court that access had been granted, the parties were trying to resolve remaining issues, and the emergency motion was withdrawn. ECF 57 later formalized withdrawal of ECF 15. On October 30, ECF 56 administratively terminated GEO's ECF 9 motion without prejudice and stayed GEO's response deadline during settlement discussions.

The case was referred to mediation before former Judge Noel L. Hillman on April 2, 2026. A June 30 conference was adjourned sine die on June 26. The fully paginated public docket contains 161 entry objects and remains unterminated. There is no merits opinion, final judgment, or appeal.

## Related contract-ban cases: relevant but not controlling the code dispute

### CoreCivic, Inc. v. Murphy

The Third Circuit's July 22, 2025 precedential opinion affirmed summary judgment against New Jersey's AB 5207, N.J.S.A. 30:4-8.15–8.16, as applied to CoreCivic. The majority held that the statute functionally directly regulated the federal government by banning all private counterparties for civil immigration detention in a federal-only market. Judge Ambro dissented. The mandate issued August 13, 2025.

The limiting language is essential. The majority said its holding addressed “only a state ban on contracting in a market where the federal government is the only available counterparty for services implementing a core federal power.” It added that neutral conditions on contracts and rules that merely burden contractors may pose different questions and “leave[s] such cases open.” Accordingly, CoreCivic is strong authority against New Jersey's immigration-detention contract ban; it is not a merits ruling on Newark's generally applicable construction, fire, health, or inspection laws.

### The GEO Group, Inc. v. Murphy

GEO separately challenged the same contract ban in D.N.J. No. 3:24-cv-04990. After the CoreCivic appeal, the district court entered an August 22, 2025 consent order permanently enjoining enforcement of N.J.S.A. 30:4-8.16(b)(2) against GEO's negotiation or contracting with the United States to operate immigration-detention facilities in New Jersey. The court closed the case August 28.

That consent order protects contracting activity. It does not say Delaney Hall is immune from safety codes, eliminate Newark inspection authority, or adjudicate the missing municipal complaint.

## Learnings

- [Source quality] CourtListener/RECAP supplied the core pleadings, motion papers, federal Statement of Interest, permits, inspection records, appellate opinion, and most operative docket text, but not the municipal complaint or complete ICE contract.
- [Methodology] A docketed case-search page is not a complaint. Treating it as one would invent the charging theory and collapse metadata into allegations.
- [Methodology] Contract-ban precedent and generally applicable safety-code enforcement must be analyzed separately. CoreCivic's limiting language makes that distinction explicit.
- [Methodology] A withdrawn emergency motion and an administratively terminated dismissal motion are not wins on the merits for either side.
- [Methodology] Mixed inspection reports should preserve both adverse observations and favorable system checks; neither the City report nor GEO's remediation response is a judicial finding.
- [Process gap] CourtListener's judge/person index did not return Judge Semper, preventing a person-constrained disclosure screen.
- [Friction] CourtListener investment search initially exited without the requested file or diagnostic, then succeeded on retry; papercut #920 preserves the reproducible context.
