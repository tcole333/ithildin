## Bottom line

A Mineral County, Nevada public-record release materially changes the known return chronology. An email dated March 17, 2025 identifies **David J. Venturella** in the signature block as **“Senior Advisor to the Director”** at U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement. The record places Venturella at ICE by that date: 45 days after the January 31, 2025 scheduled end of his filed GEO consulting agreement and two days after the March 15, 2025 scheduled vesting date of a prior GEO performance-share award. The timing does not establish that shares actually vested or were retained, that the consultancy was not extended, that Venturella participated in a GEO matter, or that an ethics screen did or did not exist.

This evidence requires an explicit correction to the prior synthesis. Finding #12535 remains valid only as the **May 17–19, 2026 public leadership-page transition** into ICE's top displayed role. Finding #12545's 472-day/429-day leadership-page intervals cannot be used as the government-return boundary and should be superseded on that point. The March 17, 2025 record reduces the publicly documented maximum separation between the scheduled consulting end and an ICE role to 45 days.

The CourtListener/RECAP review produced two useful boundaries, not proof of contracting influence. In *Arias v. ICE*, the court said Venturella was automatically substituted for Todd Lyons in an official-capacity role under Rule 25(d), then dismissed the claims against ICE and Venturella because the complaint did not establish a threatened injury fairly traceable to them. In *Washington v. The GEO Group, Inc.*, a Department of Justice filing named Venturella among the collective “U.S. Government Defendants” and said that collective group participated in mediation. The filing did not attribute attendance, authorization, direction, contracting, facility decisions, or GEO communications to Venturella individually.

No public appointment or delegated-authority instrument, GEO-specific approval or acquisition record naming Venturella, ethics screen, recusal, waiver, authorization, divestiture record, or agency response to congressional ethics questions was located. Those are agency-held-record gaps, not negative factual conclusions. Human actions #64–#66 convert them into targeted records requests.

## 1. Office title and nominal authority

The March 17, 2025 signature is the earliest public record located in this wave placing Venturella back at ICE. It supplies a nominal office title—Senior Advisor to the Director—but not an appointment instrument, organizational chart placement, position description, or delegation.

The *Arias* opinion supplies a different kind of nominal-authority evidence. It states that Venturella had replaced Lyons as the senior official performing ICE Director duties and that Rule 25(d) automatically substituted him in the litigation. The same opinion demonstrates why an official-capacity appearance should not be converted into a participation inference: the court independently tested traceability and dismissed the claims against ICE and Venturella without prejudice.

Prior finding #12541 remains an organizational boundary: ICE's public leadership material places acquisition and procurement activities under Management and Administration/Office of Acquisition. That does not exclude special delegation, review, influence, or supervisory action. No Venturella-specific delegation or acquisition designation was located.

## 2. Specific GEO-matter participation

No reviewed primary record attributes a GEO-specific contract, acquisition, facility, or operational decision to Venturella personally.

- The March 17 email arose in a Mineral County 287(g)-program exchange. It establishes ICE employment/title evidence, not a GEO matter.
- The July 6 *Washington v. GEO* filing uses the defined collective term “U.S. Government Defendants.” Its mediation statements apply to that group. The filing does not identify which person attended or made any decision.
- A May 26 congressional letter addressed to the DHS Secretary and “Senior Official Venturella” asks for action and records concerning Delaney Hall. It is an authentic inbound oversight request, not proof that Venturella took, declined, supervised, or answered any action.
- CourtListener name searches also returned many ordinary official-capacity immigration cases and historical matters from Venturella's earlier GEO period. Party status and semantic search hits were not treated as personal participation.

The correct public-record conclusion is unresolved matter-specific participation, not non-participation. Human action #66 requests records that would actually test the proposition: records naming both Venturella and a GEO entity, including meeting lists, correspondence, source-selection/acquisition designations, approval chains, decision memoranda, operational directives, and screening or reassignment records.

## 3. Ethics screening, recusal, waiver, or authorization

A May 27 letter from Senator Elizabeth Warren asks Venturella to provide ethics waivers, recusals, guidance, GEO matters, GEO communications, and conflict-avoidance steps by June 10, 2026. The letter also repeats reported allegations, including that a waiver existed. The letter is primary evidence only that a senator made those requests and allegations. Its factual assertions rely on reporting and are not the underlying waiver, screen, or participation records.

Official-domain searches through July 14 did not locate a public ICE/DHS response. The prior OGE public-collection search returned zero Venturella records but, as finding #12539 explains, that collection does not prove the absence of agency-held disclosure or ethics files. The 2026 GEO proxy contains one Venturella name occurrence, solely in a historical list of 2021 non-CEO named executive officers; it does not disclose current holdings, actual 2025 vesting, payments, or consulting status.

The public record therefore does not support either “cleared” or “conflicted” as a conclusion. Human action #65 requests the actual initial screen, public financial disclosure/Form 201 response, ethics agreement, disqualification or recusal, screening arrangement, 18 U.S.C. § 208 waiver, 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502(d) authorization, § 2635.503 determination, divestiture evidence, and any response to the May 27 letter.
