## Class scope and surviving theories

The operative class periods stated in the 2022 summary-judgment order are:

- **TVPA class:** all persons detained at the Aurora facility from **December 23, 2004 through October 22, 2014**. The court narrowed the original October 22 start date because older claims were time-barred.
- **Unjust-enrichment class:** all people who performed work under GEO's Voluntary Work Program at the facility from **October 22, 2011 through October 22, 2014**.

The two theories concern different conduct. The TVPA claim challenges alleged compelled cleaning of common areas and cleaning after other detainees under a threat of segregation. It does not challenge ordinary personal housekeeping. The unjust-enrichment claim concerns GEO's payment of $1 per day to participants in the VWP.

ECF 380 did not enter final liability on either claim. It held that common evidence could sustain the TVPA class at trial, that disputes over whether GEO benefited from VWP labor precluded summary judgment, and that GEO's standardized $1/day agreement did not bar the unjust-enrichment claim. It also ordered the equitable unjust-enrichment claim to be heard by the jury on an advisory basis.

## The post-Supreme Court motion: what the parties actually say

### GEO's ECF 450 position

GEO moves under Rules 56 and 12(c) for judgment **only on the TVPA claim**. Its stated theory is that GEO acted as an ICE agent under sufficient government direction and supervision to invoke qualified immunity under *Filarsky*. It argues that:

- the defense can be raised before trial despite not having been pleaded earlier;
- *Richardson v. McKnight* is narrow, arose under § 1983, and involved limited supervision;
- ICE's contracts, detention standards, on-site representatives, policy review, audits, and disciplinary scale establish close supervision;
- mandatory housekeeping did not violate the TVPA; and
- no sufficiently specific forced-labor right was clearly established during the class period.

Those are GEO's arguments. ECF 450 does not itself establish close supervision, the scope of permitted housekeeping, or qualified immunity.

### Plaintiffs' ECF 465 position

Plaintiffs argue that GEO waived or forfeited the late defense; that *Richardson* and Tenth Circuit authority foreclose immunity for the for-profit prison contractor; that material disputes remain over ICE's supervision and review; and that ECF 380 already found GEO independently developed and implemented policies exceeding its contractual obligations. They argue that the TVPA protects against compelled work beyond personal housekeeping and that the threatened segregation supplies a jury question.

Plaintiffs also state explicitly that even a GEO victory on qualified immunity would leave the unjust-enrichment claim for trial. That statement accurately describes the relief requested in ECF 450, but it remains a litigant's position rather than a new court holding.

### GEO's ECF 474 reply

GEO maintains that the defense is timely and non-waivable, that *Richardson* does not control under its view of ICE's supervision, and that plaintiffs cannot show either a TVPA violation or a clearly established right. The reply again asks the court to enter judgment only on the TVPA claim.

## ICE-contract defenses that still matter

The ICE contract is central evidence but not a blanket defense. The current defense landscape is:

1. **Yearsley remains a merits defense in the abstract.** The Supreme Court's ruling permits review after final judgment, but it did not disturb the district court's underlying conclusion that GEO went beyond what ICE directed. ECF 380 therefore remains the operative readable district-court ruling on the defense.
2. **Qualified immunity is newly contested.** GEO relies on ICE direction, supervision, standards, audits, and on-site personnel. Plaintiffs rely on *Richardson*, corporate/market incentives, limited contractual authority of the on-site representative, and the already-adjudicated distinction between ICE personal-housekeeping requirements and GEO's broader sanitation policy. The court's disposition is not readable at the cutoff.
3. **Substantive TVPA defenses remain for trial unless ECF 450 succeeds.** The scope of the work, the existence and effect of threats, causation, GEO's knowledge, and whether the alleged coercion violated the statute were held to present jury questions in ECF 380.
4. **Unjust-enrichment defenses remain independent of ECF 450.** GEO previously lost the arguments that ICE fixed a $1/day ceiling, that the VWP agreement barred relief, and that the government-contractor defense displaced Colorado law. The amount of any benefit, inequity, remedy, and damages has not been finally decided. GEO's ECF 451 separately invokes *Nwauzor*-related preemption and intergovernmental-immunity theories, but no readable Menocal ruling on those renewed theories was found.
5. **ICE's role is partly undisputed and partly disputed.** The parties agree that ICE is within DHS, contracted with GEO for the Aurora facility, incorporated NDS/PBNDS standards, required a VWP, and included a disciplinary scale. They dispute whether ICE authored, approved, directed, or closely supervised GEO's broader common-area cleaning policy and whether audits or on-site review supplied the control GEO claims.
