# CoreCivic CY2025 ICE revenue bridge: direct awards, IGSAs, and municipal cash

**Lead:** 58086  
**Profile:** `geo-group`  
**Research cutoff:** 2026-07-14  
**Question:** How much of CoreCivic's calendar-year 2025 ICE revenue can be tied to named direct-award and IGSA facilities, and what can public records establish about the local-government payment channel?

## Bottom line

CoreCivic reported **$770.7 million of calendar-year 2025 ICE revenue**. Its 2025 Form 10-K separately quantified revenue at five ICE facilities totaling **$206.9 million**, or **26.85%** of the company total. This is a defensible **facility-level disclosure floor**, not a measure of unreported revenue and not an estimate of an “undercount.” The remaining **$563.8 million (73.15%)** is simply not itemized by facility in the filing.

The five-site floor divides into:

| Contract channel | Named facilities | CY2025 revenue | Share of CoreCivic ICE revenue |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Direct ICE | California City ($39.2m); Midwest Regional ($14.8m) | **$54.0m** | **7.01%** |
| IGSA/local prime | Dilley ($118.1m); West Tennessee ($13.0m); Farmville ($21.8m) | **$152.9m** | **19.84%** |
| Named-site disclosure floor | Five sites | **$206.9m** | **26.85%** |
| Not itemized by facility in the 10-K | — | **$563.8m** | **73.15%** |

The separate official USAspending action ledger recorded **$266.391 million in CY2025 direct ICE obligations** to CoreCivic's parent UEI. Dividing those obligations by CoreCivic's ICE revenue gives **34.57%**, but this is a **different-metric context ratio**, not “coverage”: obligations are federal legal commitments recorded on award actions; SEC revenue is accounting recognition; and IGSA payments initially name a local government rather than CoreCivic.

The strongest payment-channel result is West Tennessee. The Town of Mason published the executed CoreCivic operating agreement, the ICE IGSA, the pricing schedule, and its December 2025 bank reconciliation. Those primary records show:

- ICE contracted with the Town of Mason under **70CDCR25DIG000021** for August 15, 2025–August 14, 2030, with the Town as prime and CoreCivic as the acknowledged subcontractor/operator.
- The Town agreed to remit all IGSA funds to CoreCivic within 30 days, less a **$1 per detainee-day** administrative fee.
- The first ordering year's schedule set a **$2,557,134.97 monthly facility operating charge**, tiered bed-day rates, and a separate **$259,844 monthly transportation amount**; the base award document nevertheless states **$0 obligated**, because task orders authorize and fund performance.
- Mason's bank reconciliation records three ICE Treasury receipts labeled October, November, and December totaling **$7,194,453.04** and two CoreCivic payments totaling **$4,364,608.89** by December 31. The $2,828,526.91 December receipt remained largely in the account at year-end, demonstrating ordinary cash timing rather than a revenue discrepancy.

That municipal ledger is direct evidence of the pass-through mechanism. It should not be added to the $13.0 million West Tennessee SEC revenue: it is a cash ledger with invoice and remittance lag, while the SEC figure is recognized revenue for the calendar year.

## Measurement rules

| Measure | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Federal obligation | Legal commitment recorded on an award action | Cash paid; contractor revenue; local pass-through remittance |
| Federal outlay | Federal cash disbursement when reported | Revenue recognition timing; prime-to-subcontractor payment |
| Local receipt/payment | Cash received or remitted by a local prime | Full earned revenue when invoices or remittances lag |
| Contract rate or ceiling | Pricing architecture or maximum authority | Amount obligated, paid, or earned |
| SEC revenue | Accounting recognition under the issuer's policy | Which federal action or local payment funded each dollar |

CoreCivic says fixed monthly revenue is recorded in the month earned and fixed per-diem revenue is recorded from the applicable rate and population housed or guaranteed. That policy makes a direct cash-to-revenue reconciliation inappropriate without invoice-level accruals and accounts-receivable detail.

## The aligned SEC facility floor

The Form 10-K states: **“During the year ended December 31, 2025, revenue from ICE was $770.7 million compared to $564.8 million during the year ended December 31, 2024.”** It also discloses these same-period facility figures:

| Facility | Channel | CY2025 revenue | Supporting disclosure |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Dilley Immigration Processing Center | City of Dilley IGSA | $118.1m | Operations resumed March 5; intake began April; activation completed September; graduated fixed monthly payment during the first six months. |
| West Tennessee Detention Facility | Town of Mason IGSA | $13.0m | ICE intake began September; fixed monthly plus incremental population payment. |
| Farmville Detention Center | Prince Edward County IGSA | $21.8m | CoreCivic acquired the operator July 1; contract expires March 2029. |
| California City Immigration Processing Center | Direct ICE | $39.2m | Letter agreement and then two-year direct contract; intake began August. |
| Midwest Regional Reception Center | Direct ICE | $14.8m | Startup/nominal fixed revenue was recognized although an injunction prevented intake. |

These are the only site amounts used in the floor. The filing discusses other ICE facilities and activations, including Diamondback, but does not give each a CY2025 revenue amount. Assigning the residual to any such facility would be speculation.

## West Tennessee: the pass-through is visible in primary records

### Executed structure

Mason's executed agreement is signed by Mayor Eddie Noeman and CoreCivic on August 12, 2025. The attached IGSA identifies the Town as the service provider, provides 600 adult beds, and names the performance period as August 15, 2025 through August 14, 2030. The IGSA expressly acknowledges and approves CoreCivic as subcontractor, states that ICE has privity only with the Town, and says ICE will not accept invoices from or make payments to a subcontractor.

The Town-CoreCivic agreement supplies the missing second leg:

> “The Town shall pay CoreCivic all funds received pursuant to the IGSA without any undue delay, and not more than 30 days after the Town's receipt of the funds from the government, less an administrative fee...”

It then defines the administrative fee as **$1.00 × detainees × days in the month**.

### Pricing is not funding

The first-year schedule establishes:

- Monthly facility operating charge: **$2,557,134.97**.
- Bed days 1–450: **$5.50 per day**.
- Bed days 451–600: **$62.88 per day**.
- Bed days 601+: **$159.00 per day**.
- Fixed transportation: **$259,844 per month** for 27,352 miles, then $9.50 per excess mile.

At 600 beds for 365 days, the scheduled facility operating and bed-day components would be about **$35.032 million** before the separate transportation component. This is only a rate-derived scenario, not an obligation, payment, or revenue figure. The SF-1449 says “TOTAL AWARD AMOUNT ... $0.00,” and the IGSA explains that task orders are placed when requirements and funding are identified.

### CY2025 cash ledger

Mason's December 2025 bank reconciliation identifies bank account `017 Contract - ICE` and records:

| Posting date | Ledger description | Cash direction | Amount |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 2025-12-01 | ACH Deposit ICE TREAS 310—October | ICE → Mason | $2,821,131.21 |
| 2025-12-01 | ACH Deposit ICE TREAS 310—November | ICE → Mason | $1,544,794.92 |
| 2025-12-30 | ACH Deposit ICE TREAS 310—Town of Mason | ICE → Mason | $2,828,526.91 |
| 2025-12-01 | CoreCivic Inc Payment—November | Mason → CoreCivic | $2,819,813.97 |
| 2025-12-19 | CoreCivic Inc Payment—December | Mason → CoreCivic | $1,544,794.92 |

Two equal and opposite $1,544,794.92 correction entries on December 1 cancel and are excluded from the totals. The official December bank statement independently confirms the $1,544,794.92 check, the $2,828,526.91 ICE ACH receipt, and the $2,830,269.94 ending balance.

**Bounded result:** CY2025 posted receipts totaled **$7,194,453.04**; posted CoreCivic remittances totaled **$4,364,608.89** by year-end. The difference is dominated by the December 30 receipt that remained in the account at December 31. Labels such as “October,” “November,” and “December” are the Town's posting descriptions and should not be assumed to be precise service-period allocations without the underlying invoices.

## Dilley: rate architecture is visible, but gross cash is not

ICE modification P00032 to 70CDCR18DIG000012 names the City of Dilley as contractor, reopens the site, and says a 60-day activation period begins on execution during which CoreCivic hires and trains staff and ICE is invoiced the applicable fixed monthly payment. Each displayed modification CLIN has **$0.00 obligated** and the pricing is redacted. This is a modification-level amount, not proof that task orders or invoices were unfunded.

The City's audited CoreCivic special-revenue fund for the year ended September 30, 2025 reports **$0 current-year revenue**, a $949,806 beginning balance, and a $949,806 transfer to the general fund, leaving zero ending balance. The note describes the transfer as “CoreCivic Fund [to] General Fund $949,806 Supplemental Operations.” The audit therefore does not disclose the gross federal receipts or remittances supporting CoreCivic's $118.1 million CY2025 Dilley revenue. A later budget projects CoreCivic revenue, but a budget estimate is not a payment.

**Bounded result:** the federal modification establishes the pricing/invoice structure and the city audit establishes no current-year revenue in the specifically labeled CoreCivic fund. Neither document supplies a complete CY2025 cash pass-through ledger.

## Farmville: the federal prime is Prince Edward County, not the Town

ICE's current Farmville modification P00003 to 70CDCR24DIG000006 identifies **PRINCE EDWARD COUNTY OF**, UEI **C6KGF5S6JJK6**, as contractor. The modification increased a redacted facility operating cost and added equipment, but the displayed modification obligation is **$0.00**. P00004 is administrative and permits funding at an associated task order; P00005 concerns a mental-health staffing waiver and adds no visible funding.

This resolves a recipient ambiguity: although ICE's web title references Farmville, the current federal contracting party is Prince Edward County. USAspending and HigherGov returned no prime award rows for that UEI in the tested CY2025 window. The Town of Farmville FY2025 audit ended June 30, 2025—one day before CoreCivic acquired the operator—and contains no detention/IGSA fund. The Town's FY2026 budget also contains no labeled detention line. Those Town records are therefore not a ledger for the County-prime pass-through.

**Bounded result:** CoreCivic recognized **$21.8 million** after its July 1 acquisition, but no public County check register, task-order ledger, or CY2025 remittance schedule was located. The missing local ledger is a source-availability limit, not evidence that payment did not occur.

## TransCor: embedded affiliate flow remains unresolved

CoreCivic's 10-K says the Safety segment includes TransCor America, LLC, which provides transportation services to government agencies, and attributes part of the 2025 expense increase to greater ICE transportation needs. It does not disaggregate TransCor's ICE revenue.

No CY2025 direct DHS/ICE award action was returned for TransCor's UEI **JJERE5VNFPL9**. HigherGov lists 25 lifetime TransCor subawards totaling $22.934 million; the latest action date in that dataset is 2024, when four records totaling $2.829 million sit under CoreCivic ICE primes. Those aggregator records are supplemental historical context only: they are not CY2025 payments and must not be added to CoreCivic's SEC revenue.

## What the bridge proves—and does not prove

### Supported

1. A same-period SEC facility floor of **$206.9 million / 26.85%** across five named ICE facilities.
2. A same-period direct-obligation context ratio of **$266.391 million / $770.7 million = 34.57%**, explicitly not coverage.
3. An executed West Tennessee chain of **ICE → Town of Mason → CoreCivic**, including rate terms and 2025 municipal cash postings.
4. A recipient correction at Farmville: the current federal prime is Prince Edward County.
5. A public-record limit at Dilley and Farmville: modifications and annual financial statements do not expose a complete gross pass-through ledger.

### Not supported

- Calling the SEC/USAspending difference an “undercount,” hidden revenue, or unreported federal spending.
- Adding obligations, local receipts, local remittances, and SEC revenue together.
- Treating a $0 base/modification obligation as proof that the agreement generated no funded task orders or payments.
- Assigning the $563.8 million unitemized residual to named facilities.
- Adding historical TransCor subawards to CY2025 ICE revenue.

## Correction to finding 12414

Finding 12414 compared calendar-year SEC revenue with federal-fiscal-year obligations and described a cross-company “measurement/attribution gap.” The evidence developed here supports a narrower and more exact statement: the figures measure different periods and accounting events, while at least one IGSA's local cash pass-through is independently visible. The prior finding should be superseded for CoreCivic by the aligned facility floor and the typed West Tennessee cash bridge, not interpreted as a quantified spending gap.
