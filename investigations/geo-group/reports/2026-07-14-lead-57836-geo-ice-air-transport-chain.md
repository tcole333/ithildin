## Bottom line

GEO's own SEC filing identifies **GEO Transport, Inc. (GTI)** as CSI Aviation's subcontractor for secure ICE air-operation support. GEO said GTI began under a nine-month emergency subcontract in July 2023, then received a new five-year contract inclusive of option periods in March 2024. GEO forecast about $25 million in annualized revenue from the later subcontract; that is a forward-looking estimate, not evidence of realized revenue or option exercise.

The prime vehicle is **ICE BPA 70CDCR24A00000001**, awarded to **CSI Aviation, Inc.** (UEI D5BNEHB3UL89) under GSA MAS contract GS33F0025V. An agency-certified administrative-record index calls it the contract awarded to CSI on March 8, 2024. The DHS statement of work and USAspending record align on a five-year maximum period ending March 7, 2029. This supports the mapping between GEO's described five-year subcontract and CSI's ICE Air BPA, but no publicly obtained GTI subcontract document names the BPA or discloses its payment terms.

The BPA has **$3.6 billion in potential ceiling authority**, not revenue. It was fully terminated for convenience on April 18, 2025, then reinstated in whole by modification P00005 on June 25, 2025. The current operating call identified here, 70CDCR26FC0000001, shows a **current cumulative USAspending snapshot** of $837.61 million obligated, $552.01 million outlaid, and $1.5 billion in current/potential award value. None of those amounts is GEO revenue.

## Contract and performance chain

1. **GSA MAS GS33F0025V** — CSI's federal supply schedule vehicle.
2. **ICE BPA 70CDCR24A00000001** — CSI is the single prime; $3.6 billion potential ceiling authority; March 8, 2024–March 7, 2029 maximum period.
3. **BPA calls / direct MAS bridge orders** — ICE obligates money at the order level.
4. **GEO Transport, Inc. subcontract** — GEO describes GTI as providing secure air-operation support/security; the public record reviewed does not show GTI supplying aircraft or flight crews.
5. **Air carriers and other vendors** — supply aircraft, crews, and other operational components. The current complete carrier roster is not public in the reviewed record.

This is not an IGSA chain. It is a federal procurement chain: **GSA MAS → ICE BPA or direct MAS order → CSI prime → subcontractors/vendors**. Secure ground transportation embedded in GEO detention-facility contracts is analytically separate.

## Scope and role boundaries

The DHS statement of work required a full service: aircraft, pilots and crews, guards, nurses, and other necessary personnel/materials/services. It projected at least 12 DSLA aircraft, 31–66 weekly flights, and 11,300–24,000 annual flight hours. It also made the prime responsible for the performance and omissions of teaming partners and subcontractors.

That source supports these role boundaries:

- **CSI:** prime accountable to ICE for the full service and subcontractor performance.
- **GTI:** GEO's Transportation Division and secure air-operation support/security subcontractor, based on GEO's filed statements.
- **Air carriers:** aircraft and flight-crew operators. Historical court materials identify iAero as part of CSI's original team and say it ceased normal operations in April 2024; replacement carrier names are redacted in the reviewed record.
- **GlobalX:** a May 2025 House oversight letter, explicitly attributing its statement to media reports, said GlobalX operated 74% of ICE removal flights over the prior year. This is a reporting-derived congressional assertion, not a primary subcontract or flight record.

No reviewed source establishes that GEO or GTI was the air carrier, aircraft owner, or flight-crew employer for the CSI prime.

## GEO revenue boundary

GEO's March 2024 filing is precise but limited: GTI was a subcontractor to CSI, the new contract included option periods, and GEO expected approximately $25 million in annualized revenue. It does **not** establish:

- the subcontract's face value, minimum guarantee, payment schedule, or realized revenue;
- that every option was exercised;
- the portion of CSI's prime obligations paid to GTI;
- that broader 2025–2026 transportation revenue statements are solely attributable to this air subcontract.

GEO later said support under its ICE air-transportation subcontract “continued to steadily increase,” but broader company disclosures bundle air support with ICE ground transport and U.S. Marshals work. They cannot be used to allocate GTI air-subcontract revenue.

## Broader DHS / Trump-administration court connection

CourtListener also surfaced *CSI Aviation, Inc. v. United States*, No. 25-1338C, a separate DHS procurement dispute over the Comprehensive Support to Removal Operations (CSRO) contract. The reported opinion says Salus submitted an unsolicited proposal on January 23, 2025, three days after President Trump's immigration executive orders; DHS then used limited competition for a contract with up to $915 million in authority. The court found the CSRO scope materially broader than ICE Air's flight operations/logistics and dismissed CSI's protest for lack of standing/prejudice. It found no administrative-record evidence of bad faith or unfair dealing.

That case is relevant to the wider DHS contracting environment, but the reviewed record does not connect GEO or GTI to the Salus CSRO award. It must remain a separate research track.
