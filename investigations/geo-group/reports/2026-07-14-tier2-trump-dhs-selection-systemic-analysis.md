## Question and competing explanations

The synthesis asks whether political access and relationships materially shaped GEO's selection or expansion under the Trump administration and DHS, versus a non-exclusive combination of public policy and budget growth, incumbent vehicles, operational readiness, fiscal and task-order mechanics, accounting/channel differences, and ordinary regulated-sector practices.

The current public record strongly establishes the access side of the premise. GEO and associated people or entities made substantial Trump-aligned political payments; federal lobbying increased sharply in 2025; company disclosures place former acting ICE director Matthew Albence in a title-level approval chain for external advocacy and political contributions; GEO has a greater ICE-specific leadership concentration than the reviewed CoreCivic public-company roster; Homan disclosed pre-government consulting associated with GEO Care; Venturella performed proposal and contract-renewal work for GEO before later appearing as ICE's senior official performing the duties of director; Ballard and Checkmate had administration-connected personnel; and the Trump disclosure household reported GEO securities.

What remains absent is the decision bridge. No accepted record identifies a donor, household trader, former official, lobbyist, consultant, or trade association as requesting or influencing a named GEO facility, PIID, solicitation, source-selection decision, modification, option, capacity action, price term, or award outcome. That absence is coverage-bounded, not proof that private or agency-held records do not exist.

## Two outcome axes

The ACH does not collapse aggregate growth and vendor selection into one outcome.

| Outcome axis | H1-relevant evidence | H0-relevant evidence | Weighted reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aggregate DHS/GEO expansion | Political activity, former-official concentration, and conflict-relevant relationships overlap the growth period, but mostly as adjacency | Independent policy and appropriation records, same-elapsed action mechanics, CoreCivic peer acceleration, incumbent capacity, and prime/subcontract channel structure directly explain observed growth mechanisms | H0 is substantially less inconsistent on the aggregate-expansion axis |
| Vendor/facility selection and terms | Albence's internal gate, Venturella's proposal background, political money, and access networks create testable pathways, but no accepted source links them to a named decision | Pre-existing vehicles, constrained readiness requirements, full-and-open structures, bounded Ballard/Checkmate negatives, and ready-capacity evidence supply partial ordinary explanations | Unresolved; H0 is weakly less inconsistent, but source-selection, contact, pricing, and ethics records are too incomplete for a decisive verdict |

These readings are based on source independence, causal proximity, metric fit, and coverage—not row counts. Government policy, federal action data, peer-company records, solicitation files, and person-attributed access records receive different weights. Derived arithmetic and repeated filings never gain weight merely by appearing in multiple rows.

## Evidentiary weight

The independent records that do the most work on aggregate expansion lean toward the policy/procurement-mechanics account. Vendor-specific selection remains materially less resolved. These are ACH least-inconsistent readings, not probabilities or proof that political influence did not occur.

First, public policy and funding preceded much of the measured expansion. The January 20, 2025 White House order directed detention-capacity action, and Public Law 119-21 supplied $45 billion for ICE detention capacity on July 4. Those records explain aggregate demand but not vendor choice. They are independent of GEO's lobbying filings, although the lobbying and public-law records form one temporal-overlap synthesis rather than two proofs of influence.

Second, exact USAspending action forensics identify measured mechanisms. Funding-only and initial task actions accounted for 98.3474% of FY2025 GEO net obligations and 85.6478% of FY2026-through-July-9 net obligations. Deobligations were less than 1% of gross positive obligations in both periods, and only one ICE action in scope was an explicit option exercise. A January 21–28, 2026 cluster spread across seven service or facility categories accounted for 40.5324% of FY2026-to-date net obligations. These records show how obligations were posted; they do not prove why demand arose, who influenced it, occupancy, or recognized revenue.

Third, CoreCivic supplies an independent peer context. Its direct ICE obligations rose by $126.324 million from FY2024 to FY2025. California City and Midwest Regional explained 59.33% of that increase, while legacy facilities explained the remainder. California City used a noncompeted urgency letter contract and existing idled 2,560-bed capacity; intake began roughly four months after activation started. The missing justification, alternatives, and pricing file prevents a fairness judgment, but the peer expansion makes a GEO-only political-access explanation insufficient for the sector-wide surge.

Fourth, the reconstructed 2019 California procurement shows a repeatable incumbent/readiness mechanism. ICE used a negotiated full-and-open, multiple-award structure, but required large, geographically bounded, existing or renovatable facilities capable of readiness within 60 days, and emphasized past performance. Those requirements likely constrained the practical bidder pool to established operators. The record does not establish improper tailoring, but it shows how incumbent assets and rapid readiness can narrow competition without a demonstrated political intervention.

Fifth, the strongest account-specific lobbying tests did not identify an award bridge. Ballard's complete 18-filing series and bounded DHS/OMB checks produced no person-attributed GEO procurement intervention. Checkmate's six-filing series used broad detention-contract wording, did not list DHS or ICE in the government-entity fields, and named no facility or procurement. The detention base actions in its engagement window used pre-existing parent vehicles; important policy, funding, and base-task events predated the engagement. These are meaningful bounded disconfirmations of an observed lobbying-to-award pathway, although unavailable work descriptions and agency records could change them.

Sixth, the air-transport result adds a distinct channel and metric boundary rather than an award-to-access bridge. The chain is GSA FSS to ICE BPA or direct order to CSI Aviation as prime, then to GEO Transport as a subcontractor. GEO forecast about $25 million in annualized subcontract revenue, while $3.6 billion of BPA ceiling authority, $1.5 billion of current/potential call value, and $837.61 million of call obligations are prime-level federal metrics that cannot be attributed to GEO. Public subaward queries returned no rows, so GTI's realized payments remain unknown.

The accepted P00005 evidence makes the BPA's correction chronology more precise without resolving selection causation. ICE opened a distinct reinstatement review by May 7, 2025; approval trackers still showed QAD-25-0230 and P00005 in review on June 5. The parties separately discussed Classic Air's bid-preparation-cost claim, reported on June 12 that ICE was implementing a proposal expected to produce voluntary dismissal, and dismissed the protest with prejudice on June 24. USAspending records P00005 the next day as a zero-obligation administrative action reinstating the terminated BPA in whole and restoring its ordering-period end to March 7, 2029. No accepted source identifies the proposal as P00005, discloses an exchange or payment, names the final approver or rationale, or establishes unchanged CSI or GTI terms. The chronology is therefore low-to-medium H0-consistent on documented vehicle-continuity mechanics, but neutral on vendor selection and terms.

A separate Salus removal-operations case has no GEO link and found no administrative-record bad faith. It remains neutral and not applicable to GEO-specific selection; it is never H0 evidence for the GTI chain.

Seventh, the state/local ledger adds recipient and payer specificity without adding a procurement bridge. Florida records place a $20,000 GEO corporate payment to Friends of Ben Albritton 22 days after an official immigration-policy proposal under Senate President Albritton's legislative leadership. That is a named relationship and timing fact, not evidence of a request, intervention, or federal procurement role. The 2025 GEO PAC total reconciles exactly to gross-positive Schedule B payments, while Florida and Georgia company-versus-primary differences remain bounded coverage or category residuals rather than unexplained influence measures. Separate Florida and Georgia filing systems also contain parent GEO corporate payer strings. Those are low-to-medium-weight counterexamples to an across-the-board rule that political payments avoid the direct-awardee parent; filed payer name does not identify the funding bank account or internal payer-selection decision.

## Evidence that keeps the political-access hypothesis live

The political explanation cannot be dismissed merely because a public policy surge exists. GEO's 3-of-21 ICE-specific leadership concentration differs from CoreCivic's 0-of-18 narrow ICE/DHS count. Albence's disclosed approval role is an actual internal governance mechanism, not just a résumé overlap. Venturella's former responsibility for proposals and renewals makes his later ICE leadership role especially relevant to ethics and screening. Corporate, PAC, and individual political money is repeated and financially material, and the state ledger now identifies specific recipients. The Trump household-reportable position creates a direct financial-interest exposure. Oversight evidence also presents questions: Folkston penalties were attempted but not enforced, and later Northwest expansion followed unresolved and repeat issues.

None of those facts currently connects to a particular selection. Peer evidence also shows that former senior detention and corrections officials are common across the sector, formal contribution review can be ordinary compliance, household disclosures do not identify the beneficial owner or trader, and oversight consequences are mixed. Closed recommendations, vendor-paid overtime, later no-finding inspections, and non-GEO penalty comparators prevent the consequence record from becoming a one-direction insulation claim.

## Dependency rules used

The matrix treats each underlying event or reporting system as one context unless a genuinely independent source answers a different question.

- Repeated GEO annual reports are a longitudinal company series, not independent corroboration.
- Multiple FEC filings can establish separate legal streams and recipients, but derived totals and reconciliation residuals are not new contexts.
- Florida and Georgia are separate official filing contexts; FEC payer rows and state recipient rows describing the same payment are one transaction, not corroborating votes.
- Company rosters and Senate LDA filings are complementary, not independent proof of contacts.
- The H.R.1 filing and Public Law 119-21 are two records in one timing synthesis; the law remains an independent policy mechanism but does not corroborate lobbying influence.
- USAspending action rows and calculations share one federal transaction denominator.
- SEC revenue, federal obligations, outlays, invoices, and local-prime payments remain different metrics and are never added or treated as interchangeable.
- Inspection grades, component deficiencies, corrective actions, financial remedies, and later award actions are separate evidence classes.
- Bounded no-record searches reduce support only within their stated custodian, date, selector, and public-availability limits.

## Final bounded conclusion

Public records establish a dense political-access network and several conflict-relevant relationships, but the accepted evidence does not show that those relationships materially shaped a specific GEO selection or expansion. On aggregate DHS/GEO expansion, H0 is substantially less inconsistent because the highest-proximity records show policy funding, incumbent and ready capacity, pre-existing vehicles, ordinary obligation actions, prime/subcontract channel structure, protest/correction mechanics, and cross-peer expansion. On vendor or facility selection and terms, the record is unresolved; H0 is only weakly less inconsistent because ready-capacity and vehicle evidence has some explanatory value while the decisive source-selection, pricing, contact, ethics, P00005, and settlement records remain unavailable.

This is not an exoneration or a probability estimate. It is a coverage-bounded ACH result: observed access is mostly adjacency, while observed procurement mechanisms are closer to the measured outcomes. A relationship-specific request, specification change, evaluation intervention, approval, pricing decision, or recusal failure tied to a named GEO outcome would materially change the vendor-selection assessment.
