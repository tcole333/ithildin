## Bottom line

One hunch clears the novelty and falsifiability gates, but it is **not currently the preferred explanation**.

The audited record already establishes that 26 scoped corporate-payment rows rotate from **GEO Corrections Holdings in 2016**, to **GEO Acquisition II in 2024 and early 2025**, to **GEO Reentry Services in late 2025**. The parent remains the central direct ICE/DHS awardee, while the three payer identities have no exact direct-award match in the bounded records. Repeating that fact would add nothing beyond finding `#12766`.

The genuinely new question is whether the rotation reflects a recurring, centralized **entity-selection step** that considers the proposed payer's federal-award/legal status. That mechanism predicts common approval records, entity-status checklists, and future or historical payer behavior. Its null competitor predicts ordinary bank-account ownership, cash location, taxable-REIT structure, financing, and consolidated reporting—with no award-status criterion.

The ACH comparison gives both hypotheses zero inconsistent findings. H0 has the only diagnostic-consistent evidence currently scored: GEO's consolidated company reporting and its documented REIT/legal-entity explanation. The working hunch therefore passes only as a proposed test, not as a finding and not as an allegation of evasion, illegality, influence, or quid pro quo.

## Cross-cycle evidence

### 2016: GCH and the contractor-status dispute

GCH acknowledged the two Rebuilding America Now checks in its MUR response. The same response argued that GCH was not the federal contractor, identified Reentry and Cornell as different contracting entities in the disputed examples, and tied the subsidiary structure to GEO's 2013 REIT conversion.

MUR 7180 supplies competing views rather than a dispositive answer. Commissioners Broussard and Weintraub emphasized shared management, intertwined finances, and uniform policies. Commissioners Dickerson, Cooksey, and Trainor emphasized the different named contract parties and rejected enforcement under OGC's separate-and-distinct theory. The Commission later closed after a probable-cause deadlock. These records establish legal awareness and disagreement; they do not establish the reason later payments used different subsidiaries.

### 2024 and early 2025: Acquisition II

Recipient filings attribute 13 2024 rows totaling $2.125 million and three early-2025 rows totaling $250,000 to Acquisition II variants. GEO's public reports disclose only consolidated corporate Federal/National committee totals, not the payer-level schedule. SEC filings list Acquisition II as a subsidiary/guarantor, but the bounded registration and award records provide no exact direct-award match.

### Late 2025: Reentry

Seven late-2025 rows totaling $1.415 million name Reentry. Reentry has a current SAM registration, but the bounded direct-contract and DHS action records remain zero. The current SAM activation field cannot establish historical status on the October 9 payment date. The within-year move from Acquisition II to Reentry is the strongest reason to test a repeatable selection process, but it does not reveal one.

## ACH competing hypotheses

### H1 — award-status-aware entity selection (`#357`)

The testable possibility is that a common parent-level approval process selects a legal payer after reviewing its federal-award or contractor status. This could be an ordinary compliance step; it is not inherently improper.

Predictions:

- common parent approvers or counsel across GCH, Acquisition II, and Reentry payment packets;
- payment-specific references to `52 U.S.C. § 30119`, MUR 7180, SAM, UEIs, contracts, or candidate-entity status;
- historical or future payer transitions that continue to select entities without exact direct-award matches when a candidate's status changes.

Disconfirmation:

- payer identity follows longstanding accounts with recurring nonpolitical activity and no award-status review;
- a complete denominator finds direct-awardee corporate payers at comparable rates;
- rotation tracks reorganizations or cash sweeps while award status remains unchanged or irrelevant.

### H0 — ordinary cash/REIT/legal separation (`#358`)

The null holds that distinct subsidiary accounts, taxable-REIT structure, financing and guarantor arrangements, cash location, and routine consolidated reporting explain the payer names without an award-status selection rule.

Predictions:

- each payer account also handles ordinary vendor, tax, payroll, or administrative activity;
- rotation aligns with segment cash, ownership, financing, cash-sweep, or reorganization dates;
- approvals contain ordinary authority and budget checks but no contractor-status comparison.

Disconfirmation:

- repeated approval packets explicitly compare candidates by award status;
- legal reviewers direct payer changes after award or MUR developments;
- payment-specific intercompany transfers fund otherwise inactive payer accounts and ordinary account explanations fail.
