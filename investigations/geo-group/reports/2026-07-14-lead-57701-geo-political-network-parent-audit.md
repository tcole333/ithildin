# GEO political-network parent audit

Lead: #57701
Analysis run: #100
Profile / thread: `geo-group` / 112
Evidence reviewed through: 2026-07-14

## Bottom line

GEO has a large, multi-layered political and government-relations network with direct links to both Trump administrations. The strongest primary records establish political spending, registered lobbying, compensated consulting, household-reportable stock ownership, former-official employment, and formal internal approval authority. They do **not** establish that any named donor, lobbyist, consultant, former official, trade association, or household security transaction caused or altered a specific contract award.

The clearest new synthesis is a 2025 change in mix. GEO's unaudited company reports say direct lobbying rose from **$3.971 million in 2024 to $5.371 million in 2025**. Federal lobbying rose about **128%**, from $1.065 million to $2.428 million, while state/local lobbying was nearly flat at about $2.9 million. Political contributions moved in the other direction geographically: federal/national contributions fell from $2.539 million to $1.722 million, while state/local contributions rose from $969,700 to $2.253 million. Florida corporate contributions reached **$1.923 million**, about 85% of the company-reported 2025 state/local corporate-plus-PAC category.

The organizational link is explicit but still bounded. GEO's political report says external advocacy requires approval by the Senior Vice President, Client Relations and political-contribution requests pass through that office before counsel review and, above $500, CEO/CFO approval. GEO's proxy identifies former acting ICE director **Matthew Albence** as Senior Vice President, Client Relations. That is a documented internal gatekeeping role. It is not evidence that Albence approved a particular payment, contacted an official, or participated in an agency procurement.

## Deduplication and evidence architecture

This parent audit did not redo completed transaction or relationship work. It integrates:

- the corrected GEO PAC, corporate-subsidiary, inaugural and individual political-money ledger;
- the 2024–2025 corporate federal-committee reconciliation and payer/awardee crosswalk;
- the complete Ballard and Checkmate filing/contact audits;
- the Homan, Venturella and Trump-household financial-disclosure work;
- the corrected run-89 network topology; and
- the GEO/CoreCivic/MTC/LaSalle former-official baseline.

The prior run-81 LaCivita topology is superseded. Christopher LaCivita Jr., the Checkmate lobbyist, is not his father Chris LaCivita, the Trump campaign adviser. Guidepost edges remain separate engagements unless a primary scope proves otherwise.

The evidence is organized into four layers that must not be collapsed:

1. **Aggregate company spending and approval governance** — documented by GEO's company reports and policies.
2. **Named former-official or administration roles** — documented by SEC, OGE, ICE/White House and LDA covered-position records.
3. **Activity-level lobbying issue/entity disclosures** — these identify issues, firms, lobbyists and government entities but generally not who contacted whom.
4. **Award-specific contact or intervention** — no reviewed public record reaches this layer.

Successive GEO reports are one longitudinal company-disclosure series, not independent corroboration. Multiple LDA reports from one account are repeated observations of the same engagement. The statute and the Q2 LD-2 create one timing comparison, not two independent influence contexts.

These are GEO's IRC §162(e) amounts. They are not reconciled to LDA income/expense fields because first-tier firms, sublobbyists and the company self-filer can produce double counting, and the two disclosure systems use different constructs.

GEO says its Client Relations Department manages lobbying, that an in-house lobbyist hired in 2023 reports to that department, and that external advocacy requires prior approval from the Senior Vice President, Client Relations. The political-contribution process likewise requires that titleholder's approval before General Counsel review, with CEO/CFO approval above $500 in 2025. The 2024 threshold was $5,000. The reports do not publish contribution-request forms, approval packets, approver identities, or payment-specific legal analysis.

## Activity-level policy overlap

GEO's self-filed Q2 2025 LD-2 reported **$340,000** in expenses and named the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, H.R. 1, together with ICE, immigration enforcement and alternatives to detention. The activity listed the House, Senate, OMB and ICE. H.R. 1 became Public Law 119-21 on July 4 and appropriated $45 billion for ICE single-adult detention and family-residential capacity.

This is a real topical and temporal overlap. It does not disclose GEO's position on the bill, who spoke with whom, what was requested, or whether the activity affected enactment, allocations, solicitations or awards. The LDA and statute are dependent inputs to one synthesis.

Checkmate is analytically separate. It began August 13, 2025—after both the January detention order and July appropriation. Its filings said only “contracts related to detention centers,” never named ICE or DHS in entity fields, and did not identify a facility, PIID, solicitation, official, milestone or requested outcome. All in-window detention base actions used pre-engagement parent vehicles; North Lake and South Texas annual base tasks preceded the engagement by 12 days and one day. Those facts continue to favor general capacity/policy advocacy over identifiable award-specific work.

Ballard is also separate. Its 2017–2021 GEO series reported $1.8 million and included Pamela Bondi for one quarter in 2019 and Justin Sayfie across a longer period. The activity fields named DHS and the White House, but did not identify which lobbyist contacted which entity. DHS reported no responsive political-appointee/Ballard records in its bounded search through February 14, 2019; OMB visitor records identify firm access but not client or purpose.

It reports chamber payments of $57,890 in 2024 and $77,542 in 2025, estimating $17,680 and $17,650 respectively as nondeductible lobbying. ## ACH assessment with independence labels

### #337 distributed ICE expertise channel vs #338 ordinary regulated-sector practice

The evidence now has four different weights:

- **Aggregate company spending/governance:** consistent with both; non-diagnostic. Scale and an approval chain do not prove coordination.
- **Named former-official roles:** Albence's title-based gatekeeping and the broader former-official consultant roster are consistent with #337's expertise prediction, but also with #338's ordinary staffing prediction.
- **Activity-level issue/entity fields:** ICE/H.R. 1, DHS, OMB and White House disclosures show relevant subject matter and institutional access, but not person-level contacts. This favors #338 over any mechanism requiring coordinated intervention.
- **Award-specific contacts/intervention:** absent in the bounded record. This is the most diagnostic layer and weighs against #337's procurement-channel version while remaining consistent with #338.

The 2024 and 2025 company reports count as one longitudinal context. Ballard, Checkmate, GEO self-lobbying, Homan, Venturella, and the peer baseline are separate contexts only where they rest on distinct engagements and records; quarterly reports within one engagement are not independent.

### #353 award-specific Checkmate work vs #354 general capacity advocacy

No new Checkmate-specific evidence changes the completed result. GEO's Q2 H.R. 1 filing is a different actor and predates Checkmate, so finding #12869 is neutral to both #353 and #354. The separate Checkmate chronology, broad issue text, pre-existing procurement vehicles, pre-engagement policy/funding and lack of a named procurement continue to favor #354.

### #351 household trading vs #352 ordinary portfolio management

The parent audit adds no new owner, trade instruction, nonpublic-information path or award-specific timing signal. Household-reportable holdings and public procurement timing remain temporal overlap only; no new ACH cell is warranted.

### #357 award-status-aware payer selection vs #358 ordinary cash/legal separation

The political report proves a centralized title-based approval and counsel-review process, which is consistent with both hypotheses. It does not say that approvers compare payer entities by federal-award status. The approval-chain finding is therefore non-diagnostic; #358 retains the only diagnostic-consistent evidence until payment packets, check registers, account ownership or intercompany transfers become public.
