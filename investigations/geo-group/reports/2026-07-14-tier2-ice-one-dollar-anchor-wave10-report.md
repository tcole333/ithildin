# Tier 2 review — did ICE's $1 detainee-work reimbursement act as an economic anchor?

Date: 2026-07-14  
Profile: `geo-group`  
Analysis runs: generate-hunches #117; systemic-analysis #118  
Boundary: persisted primary-source findings, local contract artifacts, and prior verified analysis only; no external query and no HigherGov.

## Result: real null

No new hypothesis pair, lead, finding, tag, or connection was created.

The repeated nominal **$1 per detainee-day** field at Karnes, Broward, and Tacoma is real, but the reviewed evidence does not show that the field caused GEO to pay $1, imposed a legal ceiling, or shifted all wage-law exposure to GEO through a hidden mechanism. The strongest current explanation is a common ICE administrative reimbursement allowance copied across related detention vehicles, combined with contractor-level pay choices. That explanation is **least inconsistent**, not confirmed.

The candidate “economic anchor” mechanism is conceptually distinct from a legal ceiling: a contractor could be legally free to pay more while a salient government reimbursement amount still influences its choice. But the evidence needed to distinguish influence from ordinary cost minimization is absent. The three schedule occurrences are not independent causal contexts because they share the same agency, program, and contract-template ancestry; two also involve the same contractor. The direct judicial evidence of discretion comes from only two independent facilities/courts—Aurora (*Menocal*) and Adelanto (*Novoa*). Tacoma (*Nwauzor/Washington*) supplies a final wage-liability and profit context, but the persisted findings do not adjudicate whether the reimbursement field behaviorally anchored GEO's original pay decision.

## Required category separation

| Category | What the record establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| ICE reimbursement | A contract/IGSA schedule amount or reimbursable line, often stated as $1 per detainee-day | The detainee's legally permitted maximum compensation |
| Contractor pay | The amount GEO actually chose or agreed to pay detainees under facility programs | That ICE ordered the amount merely because the schedule used the same number |
| Contractor cost | The contractor's wage/stipend expense, potentially reimbursable at the scheduled amount | That all marginal wage cost was automatically passed through to ICE |
| Legal ceiling | A binding maximum the contractor could not exceed | No reviewed contract/court finding establishes a $1 ceiling; *Menocal* and *Novoa* say the opposite |
| Contractor discretion | Ability to pay more, subject to contract administration and applicable law | Why GEO selected $1 in each facility or whether a behavioral anchor influenced the choice |
| Liability/remedy | State/federal law may impose wage or unjust-enrichment consequences independently of reimbursement | That a federal reimbursement term immunizes the contractor or predetermines damages |

## Contract pattern

Three historical facility records use the same nominal field:

1. **Karnes, 2010 IGSA EROIGSA-11-0004.** Article I.C says the bed-day agreement is fixed-rate, then separately lists “Detainee Work Program Reimbursement $1.00 per day.” The separation matters: the work line is not the bed-day rate, a staffing cost, a damages term, or a wage ceiling. Finding 12986 verifies the associated schedule and distinct bed-liability architecture.
2. **Broward, 2009 contract HSCEDM-09-D-00006.** CLIN 0005 lists 21,000 units of “Detainee Volunteer Work Program Wages” at $1.00. The available schedule shows a line item; it does not state that GEO was forbidden to add its own funds.
3. **Tacoma, 2009 HSCEDM-10-D-00001 and 2015 HSCEDM-15-D-00015.** The CLIN says reimbursement “will be at the actual cost of $1.00 per day per detainee,” subject to a lot/amount ceiling absent contracting-officer approval. This is the clearest cost-allocation language, but it still addresses what ICE reimburses and the amount available under the CLIN—not a legal cap on detainee pay.

Aurora provides a useful internal control: its 2011 contract uses materially identical “actual cost of $1.00” language, yet *Menocal* held that ICE neither required a $1 maximum nor made GEO pay $1 “and no more” (finding 12557). The order also held GEO's standardized $1 agreement unenforceable on the reviewed Colorado theory (finding 12559).

## Judicial controls

### Aurora — direct no-cap control

*Menocal* is the clearest causal discriminator. The court stated that ICE neither directed nor required GEO to compensate VWP participants only $1 and that GEO could not show the government made it pay $1 “and no more” (#12557). This is inconsistent with any **legal-ceiling** theory. It does not fully falsify a softer behavioral-anchor theory, but it means the theory must explain a contractor choice made within retained discretion.

The prior litigation-forensics report also records that another GEO facility paid as much as $4 per day while ICE reimbursement was at the $1 level. That within-firm deviation is a negative control against a mechanically binding anchor. It is court-order background, not a separate damages holding.

### Adelanto — second direct no-cap control

*Novoa* found no genuine dispute that GEO controlled detainee wages. ICE's Rule 30(b)(6) witness said ICE did not restrict contractors from paying more and the contract contained “literally no limit” on what GEO could pay (#12780). The court nevertheless entered nonfinal California minimum-wage and UCL liability (#12779). This independently separates federal reimbursement from contractor pay and state-law exposure.

### Tacoma — liability/profit context, not an anchor adjudication

The current Ninth Circuit merits record reports $17.287 million in back pay, $5.950 million in unjust-enrichment relief, and an injunction (#12553). It also reports a minimum $700 million ten-year contract and facility gross profit of $18.6–$23.5 million annually for 2010–2018 (#12554). Those facts show that the $1 program coexisted with substantial private contract economics and legal exposure. They do **not** identify whether the $1 reimbursement term caused GEO's pay choice.

## Dependency controls and base rates

Counting Karnes, Broward, and Tacoma as three independent demonstrations would be a false-independence error:

- all share ICE's detention/VWP policy environment;
- the schedule wording is template-like and repeated across option years;
- Broward, Tacoma, Aurora, and Adelanto involve GEO, so firm-wide practice is a common parent;
- repeated nominal values in related government vehicles have a high administrative base rate even without behavioral anchoring.

The decisive missing negative controls are:

- non-GEO contractors operating under the same $1 reimbursement language;
- GEO facilities with the same reimbursement but systematically different detainee pay;
- a reimbursement-rate change followed by a pay change, holding facility/law constant;
- internal pricing, budget, or compensation memoranda saying the ICE amount was used as the pay reference;
- invoice data separating ICE reimbursement from GEO-funded incremental pay.

The one located within-GEO deviation (up to $4 at another facility) weighs against a mechanically determinative interpretation but cannot estimate prevalence.

## ACH verdict

The companion ACH artifact evaluates two **unregistered candidates**:

- **H1 candidate:** the $1 reimbursement allowance operated as a behavioral/economic anchor, causing GEO to set detainee pay near the reimbursed amount even though it could legally pay more.
- **H0 candidate:** the $1 line was a common ICE administrative allowance; GEO retained pay discretion and its compensation choices were independently driven by contractor economics, policy, and local law.

H0 has the least evidence against. Most contract repetition is neutral because it is predicted by both candidates. The discriminating evidence—two no-cap/control holdings and a reported above-$1 facility—fits H0 better. H1 remains possible only in a softer, unobserved behavioral sense. It is not registered because the record lacks three independent causal contexts and the predicted internal decision evidence.

## Novelty gate

The descriptive conclusion is not new. The prior litigation-forensics report already states that a $1 reimbursement rate is not necessarily a lawful compensation ceiling, and findings 12557 and 12780 encode the two strongest direct holdings.

The candidate is not a duplicate of hypotheses 335/336 in mechanism: those concern whether ICE contract design/monitoring insulates GEO from proportional financial consequences, whereas this candidate concerns behavioral anchoring of contractor pay. But importing contract continuity, Hartel allegations, or the newly authenticated defense-cost denials would collapse the distinction and relabel the existing `geo-performance-consequence-gap` competition. The Hartel/REA findings therefore remain independent and are not scored as H1 evidence.

Because the exact mechanism is under-supported and the broader version is already occupied by 335/336, the correct output is a real null rather than a new pair.

## Reconsideration rule

Re-run only when at least one of the following creates a genuinely discriminating third context:

1. a non-GEO operator's reimbursement-versus-pay ledger under the same ICE clause;
2. a time-series change in ICE reimbursement paired with contractor pay before/after;
3. internal GEO/ICE budgeting or pricing records explicitly treating $1 as the compensation reference;
4. facility invoices showing ICE reimbursed $1 while GEO routinely paid a different amount; or
5. a third adjudicated contract-interpretation record directly resolving contractor discretion under the $1 line.

More copies of the same ICE schedule language, more complaint allegations, or more award-total rows do not satisfy this rule.
