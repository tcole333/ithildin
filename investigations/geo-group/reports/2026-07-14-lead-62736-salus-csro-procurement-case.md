## Bottom line

DHS awarded Salus Worldwide Solutions Corp. a single-award Comprehensive Support to Removal Operations (CSRO) IDIQ on May 20, 2025, two days before the principal task order. The IDIQ had a **$915 million maximum/base-and-all-options value**, a **$250 minimum obligation**, one base year, and two one-year options. The two identified child orders currently total **$697,901,214.99 in obligations** and **$386,730,317.83 in outlays**. These are separate metrics; neither the ceiling nor obligations should be described as payments, profit, or Salus revenue.

The public administrative-record narrative shows a highly compressed, urgency-based limited competition after DHS spent nearly three months evaluating and refining Salus's unsolicited proposal. DHS circulated the RFP to seven potential offerors, received four timely proposals, rated only Salus high confidence for technical approach, and reported Salus's **$1,409,688 total evaluated price** as lowest. That evaluated price was a source-selection scenario, not the award ceiling or award value.

CSI Aviation protested at GAO and later at the Court of Federal Claims. GAO conducted outcome-predictive ADR but issued no merits decision; CSI withdrew. In *CSI Aviation, Inc. v. United States*, No. 25-1338C, the court dismissed for lack of Article III standing; alternatively dismissed for lack of statutory standing; and, in a further alternative, granted the government and Salus cross-MJARs because CSI failed to prove interested-party status and prejudice. Those are distinct grounds. The court's statement that the administrative record yielded no evidence of bad faith or unfair dealings arose while denying judicial notice and was expressly record-bounded; it is not a general exoneration of every procurement criticism.

No operational, ownership, subcontract, investment, donation, or procurement relationship between Salus and GEO Group was identified in the reviewed public court record, SAM entity extract, USAspending CSRO subaward data, or bounded federal-filing searches. That is a **bounded negative result, not proof of no relationship**. Congressional exhibits discuss GEO and Salus as separate subjects/contractors in broader Lewandowski allegations; they do not establish that GEO and Salus partnered with one another.

## Procurement chronology and scope

President Trump issued Executive Orders 14159 and 14165 on January 20, 2025. Salus sent DHS its initial unsolicited proposal on January 23, resubmitted it February 10, and received favorable initial and comprehensive evaluations between February 22 and March 7. DHS and Salus discussed scope, price, and funding through April. On April 4, DHS separated involuntary removals and voluntary returns; Salus's concept projected 276,000 movements and three international staging areas over 12 months. Those were proposal projections, not performance results.

An inadvertent April 18 email disclosure triggered contracting and counsel review. The resulting record identified similarities between Salus and DHS draft concepts across background, objectives, mission integration, regional support, capacity building, travel/logistics, identity verification, medical services, data, aircraft, and lodging. DHS changed course from a contemplated sole source to limited competition.

The compressed competitive phase was:

- May 13: industry meeting with six firms; draft SOW/pricing schedule sent to those firms and a seventh;
- May 14: presolicitation conference, one-on-ones, and questions;
- May 15: RFP sent to seven potential offerors;
- May 17: Amendments 0001 and 0002;
- May 19: proposals due; four timely proposals received; CSI filed its GAO protest; and
- May 20: DHS overrode the CICA stay and awarded the IDIQ to Salus, two days earlier than the previously anticipated May 22 award date.

The six scope categories were flight coordination and management, international staging areas, facilities management, security management, health-care provision, and logistics/administrative support. The market-research narrative said Salus alone had integrated experience across the scope; existing ICE Air vendors were assessed as flight-operations providers without the full detention, foreign-government coordination, and voluntary-return functions. The reported opinion says DHS consulted the State and DHS contracting officials for the Afghan CARE task order and relied on the prime's exceptional CPARS assessment attributable to the missions Salus performed.

Four firms submitted timely proposals. Salus received high confidence for technical approach, while the other three received low or some confidence. All four received some confidence for prior corporate experience. DHS selected Salus as technically superior and lowest evaluated price/best value. Offeror identities and substantial source-selection detail remain redacted or protected.

## OCI waiver: what the record shows and does not show

On May 18 the contracting officer executed, and the Head of Contracting Activity approved, a waiver addressing potential organizational conflicts arising from Salus's unsolicited proposal and subsequent communications. The contracting officer characterized the communications as well-meaning and not an intentional effort to subvert procurement rules. The HCA nevertheless acknowledged appearances of possible biased ground rules, unequal access to information, and impropriety. DHS cited a government-created common pricing scenario, common information, one-on-ones, and urgency as mitigation and waiver grounds.

Those are documented **agency procurement judgments**, not findings by an independent adjudicator that no actual conflict existed. Conversely, the public record also does not establish an actual conflict. The best supported statement is that DHS identified appearances/potential OCIs, attempted mitigation, and approved a waiver based on mission urgency.

## GAO and Court of Federal Claims

CSI's May 19 GAO protest alleged that DHS failed to publish a synopsis/solicitation, allowed too little response time, failed to use a small-business set-aside, and lacked approved flight-program standards. DHS implemented the CICA stay, then overrode it on May 20 as urgent and compelling. CSI did not challenge the override in the later COFC action.

GAO conducted outcome-predictive ADR on July 21. The opinion attributes to CSI the statement that GAO expected to deny the protest and requested withdrawal. CSI notified GAO of withdrawal on August 4; GAO confirmed withdrawal August 6. There is **no GAO merits decision or holding** to characterize as a denial.

CSI filed the COFC action August 11, 2025. The public file includes the redacted complaint, principal briefs, declarations, orders, the February 2026 replacement-presolicitation exhibit, judgment, and the 66-page reported opinion. Four substantive filings are identified as sealed and unavailable in the RECAP set: ECF 32, 39, 56, and 62. The administrative record itself was filed under the protective order and is not in the public RECAP set.

The court's May 2026 disposition must be stated in this order:

1. dismissal under RCFC 12(h)(3) for lack of Article III standing;
2. alternatively, grant of Salus's RCFC 12(b)(6) motion for lack of statutory standing;
3. alternatively, grant of the government and Salus cross-MJARs because CSI failed to prove interested-party status and prejudice;
4. denial of CSI's MJAR, motion to amend, and request for judicial notice.

While denying judicial notice, the court said its administrative-record review yielded no evidence of bad faith or unfair dealings and described DHS as having properly considered the unsolicited proposal, shifted to limited competition, and awarded Salus. The opinion expressly says those merits-related conclusions responded only to the judicial-notice request and did not resolve the MJARs. The same opinion flagged a possible FAR problem: the contracting officer may have misread the contemporaneous documentation required to support options beyond one year. CSI did not identify the possible error, and the court did not reach it because of the standing/prejudice failures.

The exact-party CourtListener case universe contains this one case. A bounded July 14, 2026 CourtListener Federal Circuit search returned no appeal. This is a current database result, not a guarantee that no later or differently indexed appeal exists.

## Allegations and party representations

Public congressional inquiry letters attached to CSI's judicial-notice request repeated media-based allegations that a Salus representative sought to route compensation to a Lewandowski-associated consultant. They also sought records concerning Salus, William Walters, affiliated companies, DHS officials, and Lewandowski. These are **oversight questions and allegations**, not agency findings, sworn factual findings, or court holdings. The court denied judicial notice, explaining that the materials supplied questions and suspicions rather than adjudicative facts relevant to the case.

Salus's public response materials state that its internal review found no email to or from Lewandowski, no exchange of value, no pre-award assistance or favor, and that Walters's first contact with Lewandowski occurred in December 2025. Salus also stated that Daedalus Aviation Corporation—not Salus—was engaged November 21, 2025 to locate, inspect, and procure aircraft, and described later Walters–Lewandowski interactions as limited to aircraft-related operational matters. These are **Salus's representations**, not independent corroboration or adjudicated exoneration.

The court's administrative-record chronology independently establishes only that Salus reacted quickly to the new administration's removal policy and worked with career procurement/program personnel on an unsolicited proposal. It does not establish a donation, investment, political-appointee instruction, or quid pro quo caused the award. Exact FEC searches for the Salus employer/name and named principals returned zero results, but that is bounded by names, employer spellings, committees, and FEC coverage.

## Bounded GEO and political-link conclusion

The reviewed sources support three narrow conclusions:

1. **Policy chronology:** the January 2025 executive orders preceded Salus's proposal by three days and the May urgency procurement by four months. Temporal sequence supports policy responsiveness, not causation by a political relationship.
2. **Later contact allegation/response:** congressional letters raised Lewandowski-related allegations; Salus denied value exchange or pre-award contact and placed Walters's first contact in December 2025. Neither side's submission independently resolves the allegation.
3. **No GEO bridge located:** the public COFC/RECAP filings, Salus corporate disclosure, SAM entity row, USAspending award/subaward searches, and bounded federal filing searches disclosed no Salus–GEO ownership, investment, subcontract, donation, or operational relationship. GEO appears in attached oversight/reporting material as a separate contractor and comparator.

These statements are limited to public and redacted records retrieved through July 14, 2026. They do not reach sealed/protected source-selection material, unreported lower-tier vendors, private bank/payment records, undisclosed beneficial interests, or future disclosures.
