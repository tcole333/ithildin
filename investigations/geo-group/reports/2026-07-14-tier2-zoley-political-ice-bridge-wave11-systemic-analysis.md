## Bottom line

The verified George C. Zoley package does **not** establish a novel executive bridge between GEO political/governance activity and a DHS/ICE procurement, contract-performance, or enforcement decision.

Four record families can be placed beside one another: founder/governance authority, personal political giving, direct ICE/Adelanto contract-remedy contacts, and House testimony. They do not form a demonstrated causal chain. The records establish relationship or access capacity, but they do not establish the two decisive links: a named decision mechanism and a resulting procurement or performance outcome attributable to that mechanism.

The only direct ICE-contact evidence belongs to one already-analyzed 2018 voluntary-work-program litigation-cost dispute. Zoley's sworn declaration describes one meeting with ICE to request an equitable adjustment and one meeting with Adelanto officials regarding possible IGSA termination (#13049). His May 30 letter separately sought DOJ defense and reimbursement (#13004). The same episode's accepted record shows awareness and access at senior ICE levels (#13015), but the contracting officers denied the requests. That is evidence of executive advocacy and contract administration; it is not evidence that personal political giving or founder authority altered a government decision.

The ordinary explanation is therefore less strained: a founder-chairman-CEO retained central governance authority, made substantial personal political contributions, participated in a major company-wide contract dispute, and later appeared at a congressional oversight hearing. This conclusion is bounded. It does not show that influence was absent; it shows that the accepted record does not identify the joining mechanism or a favorable named outcome.

Verified finding #12939 remains controlling: H0 is substantially less inconsistent for aggregate DHS/GEO expansion, while vendor/facility selection and terms remain unresolved with H0 only weakly less inconsistent. Wave 11 does not alter either axis.

## Dependency collapse

The apparent volume is much larger than the number of independent contexts. The analysis counts the following once:

1. **SEC governance and insider family.** The proxy identity bridge, beneficial ownership, compensation, retirement balance, 2025 sale programs, and 2026 gifts (#13039–#13043) are dependent governance/insider disclosures. They establish authority, economic exposure, and estate planning—not DHS access or an official decision.
2. **Florida registry/officer family.** Sixteen active and six inactive officer-code rows (#13044–#13045) are one registry family. Officer codes are not ownership findings and the entities sit inside the founder's corporate orbit; they do not create 22 independent network contexts.
3. **Foundation role.** The GEO Group Foundation return lists Zoley as uncompensated president (#13046). It is a distinct IRS record family, but it identifies no political payment, government contact, or contract decision.
4. **Personal asset family.** The Boca Raton parcel (#13047), compensation, retirement balance, stock sales, and gifts are neutral absent a money-flow or decision mechanism.
5. **Personal political-money family.** The Zoley ledger is one FEC identity and arithmetic context, not one context per receipt or committee. The broader roster ledger (#12984) and earlier bounded Trump-receipt synthesis containing two identity-resolved Zoley exceptions (#12523), as refined by the identity-control work (#12985), establish giving but no reimbursement, bundling, request, access event, or influence.
6. **2018 dispute episode.** The Novoa declaration, the May 2018 Zoley letter, the Homan/OPLA awareness record, the equitable-adjustment request, defense-cost material, meetings, and denials are one shared contract-remedy episode (#13004, #13015, #13048–#13049 and the verified defense-cost package). Multiple documents trace a single course of conduct; they are not independent political-access mechanisms.
7. **House oversight.** The July 2020 House Homeland Security testimony (#13050) is a separate public-oversight context. It is not procurement contact and does not identify a performance-consequence decision.

## Candidate executive-bridge mechanism

The strongest possible candidate would be:

> Zoley's founder authority and personal political giving gave him a political-access route through which he intervened in a named ICE decision affecting GEO.

This candidate has predicates but no joining record. Founder authority supplies organizational capacity. Contributions may supply a reason to test access. The 2018 meetings establish direct advocacy. None of the accepted records says that a contribution, campaign relationship, White House contact, lobbyist, former official, consultant, or political intermediary initiated, routed, supported, or altered the request. No record connects the House hearing to procurement or remedy administration. The observed 2018 outcome—denial—also does not support a claim of successfully converted influence.

The candidate cannot be rescued by counting record volume. Twenty-two Florida rows remain one registry family; 186 Zoley receipts remain one FEC ledger; multiple 2018 letters, emails, meetings, and decisions remain one dispute episode; and testimony remains oversight.

## Strong innocent explanation and symmetric tests

The ordinary founder-governance/contract-advocacy explanation predicts:

- the founder-chairman-CEO appears in central governance, beneficial-ownership, compensation, and estate-planning records;
- he participates in unusually material or company-wide legal and contract disputes while disclaiming day-to-day facility operations;
- company representatives request relief through ordinary contracting and agency channels, and contracting officers may deny that relief;
- personal political giving remains in an individual FEC ledger without corporate reimbursement or a named official-matter communication; and
- congressional testimony records public oversight rather than an acquisition contact or favorable remedy.

Evidence that would weaken this explanation and strengthen the executive-bridge candidate must be equally specific:

- a calendar, email, visitor record, call log, or message showing a campaign, White House, DHS, or ICE political actor arranging access for the named GEO matter;
- a source-selection, modification, equitable-adjustment, option, price, enforcement, or performance file recording an intervention, directive, recusal issue, or nonstandard routing attributable to that relationship;
- contribution reimbursement, bundling, solicitation, or approval records tied to the same official matter; or
- a contemporaneous decision memorandum showing that relationship-linked advocacy changed the disposition or terms of a named outcome.

Absent those records, property, compensation, retirement, estate planning, testimony, contributions, and a meeting are non-diagnostic for influence.

## Decisive missing records

The public-record gaps are defined, not treated as positive evidence. The records most capable of changing this conclusion would be:

1. person-attributed communications or calendar entries joining Zoley's political relationships to the 2018 remedy request or another named GEO procurement/performance matter;
2. the complete agency decision and routing file for a named award, modification, option, price, equitable adjustment, enforcement action, or performance consequence;
3. contribution reimbursement, bundling, solicitation, or corporate approval records tied to the same official matter; and
4. ethics, recusal, contact, or visitor records showing that a political intermediary participated in the government decision chain.

These are discriminators, not authorized Wave 11 searches. No Layer 1 lead is created because existing competitions and completed analyses already preserve the relevant research routes.

## Conclusion

Wave 11 increases descriptive precision about Zoley's authority, wealth, giving, one direct ICE/Adelanto dispute contact, and public testimony. It does not convert those facts into an executive political-to-decision bridge. The strongest innocent explanation—ordinary founder governance, personal giving, contract advocacy, and public oversight—accounts for the record with fewer unsupported links. The executive-bridge candidate fails the named-decision-mechanism and procurement/performance-outcome gates, and the broader #12939 conclusion remains unchanged.
