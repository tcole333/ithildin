## Hunch 1: two B.I. skip-tracing procurement channels may overlap operationally

**Competition group:** `geo-bi-dual-skip-tracing-channels`  
**Working hypothesis:** #350 (#346 was superseded after a persistence correction)  
**Null:** #347  
**Layer-1 lead:** #58687

### Pattern

ICE added **$86,361,425** of skip-tracing work to B.I.'s first ISAP V task through P00002–P00004, equal to 79.71% of that task's current obligations (#12467). Two modification descriptions call the work post-award Attachment 4 pricing-schedule item 42, while the public pre-award workbook stops at item 39 (#12468).

During the same period, ICE separately created a full-and-open, multiple-award skip-tracing vehicle. B.I. received one of 14 prime IDIQs after a parent competition that generally reports 51 offers, then received a $1,624,500 first task (#12471). The resulting fact pattern is not simply “B.I. has a skip-tracing contract.” It is one contractor performing skip tracing inside a case-management/electronic-monitoring task while also participating in a distinct multi-vendor skip-tracing pool.

### Competing explanations

**#350 — operational overlap.** The two channels may share cases, data fields, workflow, or technical infrastructure, with B.I. functioning as an integration point between ISAP case management/location data and commercial-data research.

**#347 H0 — ordinary separation.** ISAP item 42 may concern participants already inside B.I.'s supervision workflow, while `26-SOL-DCR-01` addresses a different 1.5-million-case backlog or surge population through separate, competed per-case tasks. B.I. could appear in both because it is an established provider that won separate competitions.

### Key assumptions check

1. “Skip tracing” is substantively comparable across the two instruments, rather than the same label for different service units. If false, #350 loses its premise.
2. Modification and task dates reasonably reflect when the work was ordered and operationally available. If false, temporal overlap may be illusory.
3. The signed records expose case, unit, and data definitions sufficiently to test overlap. If false because of lawful redaction or missing task files, the pair remains unresolved rather than favoring #350.

### Ordinary-course and disconfirming evidence

- ISAP I–V were recorded as full-and-open, with parent offer counts of 3, 3, 4, 2, and 2; GAO records identify G4S and CoreCivic as actual protesters at two generations (#12466).
- The separate vehicle had 14 prime awards and generally 51 offers, described no incumbent, and provided for competed task orders (#12471).
- B.I.'s first task under the separate vehicle was $1.6245 million, far below the $86.361 million added inside ISAP V. The values are not directly comparable without units, quantities, cases, and performance periods.
- Missing item 42, suppliers, task scopes, and allocation records are gaps. They are not evidence of privileged work, duplicate billing, common cases, or a hidden vendor chain (#12468, #12472).

### Predictions, falsification, and stop rule

If #350 is correct, the signed records should show one or more of the following: common case identifiers or schemas; overlapping case populations and periods; shared data pipelines; item-42 deliverables matching the separate vehicle; cross-references between the procurements; B.I. integration responsibilities; or disproportionate follow-on tasking not explained by fair opportunity.

Evidence that would disconfirm #350 and favor #347 includes distinct eligibility rules, case cohorts, units, operational owners, data extracts, performance periods, and invoices; a separate acquisition rationale for item 42; and ordinary documented allocation across the 14 awardees.

Lead #58687 is bounded to the ISAP V P00002–P00004 packages, post-award item 42, IGCE/acquisition approvals, B.I. task `70CDCR26FR0000021`, and a 14-award task-allocation ledger. Stop if those records establish non-overlap and ordinary competed allocation. Escalate only if primary records show the same cases/functions moving through both channels without a documented distinction.

## Hunch 2: public privacy governance may lag new operational capabilities

**Competition group:** `geo-isap-privacy-governance-timing`  
**Working hypothesis:** #348  
**Null:** #349  
**Layer-1 lead:** #58689

### Pattern

The 2023 ICE ATD PIA says the identified SmartLINK monitoring app was not continuously monitoring a participant's location and transmitted coordinates at login/check-in. The 2025 ISAP V requirement directs the supervisory biometric application to support continuous tracking, store coordinates at intervals no longer than three minutes, upload at least every four hours, and answer government on-demand requests. The reviewed solicitation package did not include a superseding PIA for that functionality (#12470).

The separate skip-tracing procurement adds commercial-data research and physical observation to verify residence or employment (#12471 and the underlying contract report). ISAP V separately assigns ICE ownership and unrestricted rights in program data, requires human review/auditability, and limits contractor reuse or AI training (#12473).

### Competing explanations

**#348 — governance timing gap.** Operational requirements may have expanded faster than the publicly identified privacy assessment, leaving continuous-location and commercial-data/observation workflows incompletely assessed at deployment.

**#349 H0 — ordinary internal review.** The 2023 PIA covered a prior implementation. New or differently scoped systems may have received a PTA, PIA update, SORN compatibility determination, privacy-office approval, and security authorization outside the public solicitation attachment set.

### Key assumptions check

1. The 2023 PIA is the relevant public baseline and was not already superseded by a record outside the reviewed package. If false, the apparent public timing gap may disappear.
2. The three-minute capability reached or was intended for production use in a system requiring an updated privacy determination. If it remained an unused requirement, #348 weakens substantially.
3. Approval and production dates can be established from releasable records. If they cannot, absence from public files remains non-diagnostic.

### Ordinary-course and disconfirming evidence

- Contract requirements include government data ownership, one-business-day access, PII protections, record return, human-in-the-loop review, provenance, auditability, and restrictions on contractor AI reuse (#12473).
- The skip-tracing amendment removed direct contractor access to named DHS systems and instead contemplated government-furnished extracts, according to the reviewed solicitation record.
- A solicitation package is not a complete privacy-compliance file. No inference of missing internal review follows from the absence of an attachment.
- The record does not establish that the continuous-location capability was deployed in production, which participants used it, or when any privacy approval became effective.

### Predictions, falsification, and stop rule

If #348 is correct, records should show a required PTA/PIA update or risk acceptance completed after production use; a pre-deployment assessment that omits three-minute location or commercial-data/observation flows; or later remediation of notice, authority, retention, sharing, access, or human-review controls.

Evidence that would falsify #348 and favor #349 is a dated pre-deployment privacy record expressly covering the operative continuous-location, on-demand request, commercial-data, observation, retention, sharing, and review functions.

Lead #58689 is bounded to the controlling PTA, PIA/update, SORN determination, privacy-office approval, data-flow diagram, retention schedule, authority-to-operate privacy controls, risk acceptance, and actual production dates. Stop when those records are obtained and matched. Do not equate non-public status with nonexistence.
