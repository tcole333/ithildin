## Bottom line

ICE began an internal approval process to reinstate the CSI Aviation ICE Air BPA no later than May 7, 2025. An ICE approval tracker identified the package as **QAD-25-0230**, described it as “Reinstatement of ICE Air contract 70CDCR24A00000001 / P00005,” and still listed it **In Review** on June 5. A May 14 ICE email said the reinstatement had been added back to an approval list and that the sender had heard it was moving through “S1” rather than “S2” review. The public production does not define those labels or show the final approval.

In the parallel *Classic Air Charter* protest, the parties asked on May 29 for time to discuss a consensual resolution of Classic Air’s bid-preparation-cost claim. On June 12 they told the court that ICE was implementing a proposal expected to produce voluntary dismissal. Classic Air dismissed with prejudice on June 24, and the court awarded no costs. USAspending records P00005 the next day, June 25, as a zero-obligation administrative action that “restate[d] the terminated contract in whole” and restored the ordering-period end date from April 18, 2025 to March 7, 2029.

That chronology is well supported. **Causation is not.** No public filing says the settlement proposal was P00005, that dismissal was exchanged for reinstatement, or that any settlement term required ICE to restore CSI’s BPA. The signed P00005, QAD-25-0230 approval package, decision memorandum, settlement instrument, and May 29 transcript were not public in the reviewed sources.

## What the public record establishes

### 1. Termination required senior DHS action

On April 10, government counsel told the Court of Federal Claims that ICE procurement staff intended to recommend terminating CSI’s award and cancelling the solicitation, and that authority for the decision resided with the Deputy Secretary of Homeland Security. An April 18 filing said the recommendation remained within the Deputy Secretary’s office. ICE terminated the award on April 18 and cancelled the solicitation on April 22.

These are government representations about the termination path. They do not establish who later approved reinstatement.

### 2. ICE started a distinct reinstatement review by May 7

ICE’s FOIA-produced approval tracker supplies the clearest internal process evidence:

- May 14 email: “The ICE Air reinstatement was added back to the list as awaiting S2 approval (though I hear it’s actually S1 now for this one).”
- Tracker row: QAD-25-0230, DCR, “Reinstatement of ICE Air contract,” submitted May 7, status “In Review.”
- May 29 “S1 Approval Tracker”: the row identifies “70CDCR24A00000001 / P00005,” still “In Review.”
- June 5 “S1 Approval Tracker - 05JUNE2025 Update”: the same P00005 row remains “In Review.”

The records prove a documented approval process and its status on those dates. They do not disclose the reviewers’ recommendations, final approver, rationale, approval date, or signed modification.

### 3. The court case moved from mootness to settlement discussions

After cancellation, Classic Air told the government it wanted information about ICE’s next procurement steps before dismissing. ICE responded on May 7 that it was evaluating options and had not made a final decision about next steps or timing. The court later recorded settlement discussions concerning Classic Air’s bid-preparation-cost claim. On June 12, the parties said ICE was implementing a proposal expected to result in voluntary dismissal. Classic Air dismissed with prejudice on June 24; the judgment says no costs were awarded.

The public docket does not disclose whether there was a payment, release, waiver, non-monetary promise, or agreement affecting CSI, Classic Air, or future protest rights. “No costs are awarded” is the judgment’s treatment of court costs; it is not proof that no settlement consideration or bid-preparation payment existed outside the judgment.

### 4. P00005 restored the BPA’s ordering period without an action-level obligation

USAspending’s raw IDV transaction history reports:

| Field | P00004 termination | P00005 reinstatement |
|---|---|---|
| Action date | 2025-04-18 | 2025-06-25 |
| Action type | Terminate for convenience | Other administrative action |
| Federal action obligation | $0 | $0 |
| Base and all options value, action row | $0 | $0 |
| Ordering-period end | 2025-04-18 | 2029-03-07 |
| Public description | Full termination for convenience | “Reinstate the terminated contract in whole” |

The download repeats the award’s current $3.6 billion potential value and original competition metadata on multiple transaction rows. Those are inherited/current award fields, not proof that P00005 independently reauthorized a $3.6 billion ceiling, conducted a new competition, or left every historical term unchanged.

P00006 and P00007, recorded in October and November 2025, are separately described as supplemental agreements updating terms and conditions. That helps distinguish later documented terms updates from P00005’s public reinstatement description, but it does not prove the nonpublic P00005 had no attachments or collateral terms.

## Terms that remain unresolved

The reviewed public record does **not** establish whether P00005 changed or reaffirmed:

- the BPA ceiling or minimum/maximum ordering commitments;
- rates, escalation clauses, or price schedules;
- service scope, flight volumes, routes, or performance standards;
- CSI’s carrier, security, medical, or other subcontractor team;
- GEO Transport’s subcontract, compensation, or option status;
- releases, waivers, protest rights, bid-preparation costs, or other settlement consideration;
- the basis for reinstatement, urgency determination, competition rationale, or final approving official.

The correct bounded conclusion is: **P00005 restored the terminated BPA in whole as an administrative action and restored its original ordering-period end date, but the signed terms and decision rationale were not public in the sources reviewed.**

## CourtListener coverage and sealed-record boundary

Yes, CourtListener was used for this issue. The complete public RECAP docket for *Classic Air Charter, Inc. v. United States*, No. 25-286C, supplied the termination, cancellation, mootness, settlement-discussion, dismissal, and judgment chronology.

Material gaps remain:

- ECF 46–49 (the government’s motion to dismiss, two exhibits, the two responses, and reply) were unavailable or sealed in the public RECAP record reviewed.
- The May 29 oral-argument proceeding was sealed; the transcript was not public.
- ECF 57, entered May 26, 2026 and described by CourtListener as an “Order,” was not available for download.

Those absences are important because the unavailable filings or transcript could contain more precise settlement or procurement context. They cannot be treated as evidence of any particular term.

## GAO and procurement search boundary
