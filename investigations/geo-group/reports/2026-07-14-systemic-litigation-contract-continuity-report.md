## Result

Correcting the *Novoa* posture changes one narrow result from prior run #84: the descriptive pattern “an adjudicated adverse detainee-labor ruling coexists with later ICE facility award actions” now recurs in three independent facility contexts. It did not before, because *Novoa* had been treated as allegation-plus-stay only.

That recurrence does **not** justify a new hypothesis, lead, or causal finding. Tacoma, Aurora, and Adelanto are analytically different legal events, and the public action ledger does not expose ICE’s litigation-risk, present-responsibility, CPARS, option, acquisition, legal-review, or suspension/debarment decisions. The strongest supported statement is descriptive:

- Tacoma ICE award actions continued after an affirmed final lower-court monetary and injunctive judgment.
- Aurora ICE award actions continued after nonfinal contractor-defense and contract-enforceability rulings; no final TVPA or unjust-enrichment liability judgment or damages award was located.
- Adelanto ICE award actions continued after nonfinal partial California minimum-wage and UCL liability; the case was later stayed and no final judgment or remedy was located.

The consequence record is mixed rather than uniform. Folkston directly documents attempted staffing penalties that ICE did not enforce while full funding continued. Northwest has an unresolved/open minimum recommendation and later repeat deficiencies after a completed UCAP. Conversely, South Texas and Golden State document ordinary closure responses, and Adelanto later had an inspection with no findings. Later extensions, capacity additions, and funding actions remain neutral without the underlying decision records.

No favoritism, political influence, or litigation-to-contract causation inference is supported.

## Legal propositions retained without posture collapse

### Tacoma: final affirmed lower-court judgment

The Ninth Circuit held Washington minimum-wage law applicable to work essential to facility operations (#12402) and reported the $17.287 million back-pay award, $5.950 million unjust-enrichment award, and injunction (#12553). It also reported GEO’s estimate that ending the work program would require about 85 outside full-time employees (#12550) and a ten-year ICE contract with a $700 million minimum (#12554). Those capacity and economic facts make incumbent operational need an unusually important null explanation; they do not excuse or negate the judgment.

### Aurora: nonfinal defense and contract rulings

The district court held that ICE neither directed the challenged labor nor capped VWP pay at $1 per day, defeating GEO’s Yearsley and government-contractor defenses (#12557), and held the standardized $1/day agreement unenforceable as unconscionable (#12559). The Supreme Court later decided only that Yearsley is a merits defense rather than an immediately appealable immunity (#12551). The record reviewed does not contain a final underlying TVPA or unjust-enrichment liability judgment or damages award.

### Adelanto: nonfinal partial liability followed by a stay

The corrected *Novoa* order entered nonfinal partial summary judgment on California minimum-wage and UCL liability (#12779), held that the ICE contract did not cap pay at $1/day and that GEO controlled wages (#12780), and later stayed the case because related appeals could affect immunity and preemption defenses (#12781). The stay order recognized that federal TVPA claims fell outside those defenses. Through the audited cutoff, no final judgment, damages, restitution, injunction, verdict, settlement, or stay-lifting order was located.

These are three adjudicated contexts, but they are not three equivalent outcomes.

## Base-rate, selection, and incumbent controls

1. The CourtListener universe contains 1,489 identity-confirmed dockets across decades and operating lines. That is a discovery denominator, not an adverse-outcome rate.
2. Deep reads selected material labor, conditions, securities, governance, procurement, and civil-rights cases. They cannot estimate how often GEO wins, loses, settles, or affects procurement in the full universe.
3. District, appellate, rehearing, and Supreme Court proceedings are collapsed into one dispute family. *Nwauzor* plus Washington remains one Tacoma context; the many *Menocal* proceedings remain one Aurora context.
4. Final judgment, nonfinal partial liability, contractor-defense holdings, stays, settlements, pleading rulings, and allegations are not pooled.
5. CourtListener/RECAP coverage is contribution-dependent, and the contract ledger does not cover every IGSA, invoice, deduction, responsibility record, or source-selection file.
6. GEO or its public partners operate purpose-built incumbent facilities. Capacity, geography, ownership, switching cost, statutory detention demand, and continuity of operations can dominate short-run vendor substitution.
7. Parent IDVs and annual/incremental task orders predate the selected legal milestones. Action dates are not responsibility or award-evaluation dates.
8. Finding #12619 is the controlled timing check: none of 12 exact-date facility events was discriminatory after same-facility/same-federal-quarter permutation and Holm correction for 90-day action counts, net obligations, or proximity.

## Reconsideration trigger

Reopen the mechanism only if primary records across at least three independent facilities show a repeated, non-routine treatment of adjudicated liability after controlling for legal posture, incumbent capacity, vehicle and option cycle, performance record, occupancy need, and non-GEO comparators. The decisive records are:

- responsibility determinations and acquisition plans;
- ICE legal-review and source-selection records;
- CPARS narratives and option-exercise justifications;
- cure/show-cause, deduction, invoice, and corrective-action files; and
- suspension/debarment referrals or explicit decisions not to refer.

Without those records, the permissible conclusion is continuity after three different adjudicated legal events—not that ICE disregarded them.
