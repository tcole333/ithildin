# DHS contracting-scandal coverage map

**Coverage window:** 2025-01-20 through 2026-07-28  
**Research cutoff/access time:** 2026-07-28 22:30 EDT  
**Purpose:** Phase-0 map of what journalism and public oversight bodies have already established, with the boundary of each cluster made explicit.

## Reading notes

- **CONFIRMED** means the publisher page, a publisher-authorized copy, or the underlying oversight release was inspected in this research session. **UNCONFIRMED** means only a headline, metadata, paywalled preview, or secondhand citation was available.
- “Ceiling,” “potential value,” “estimated order value,” and “obligation” are not interchangeable. The reviewed reporting sometimes blurs them. This map preserves the term used by the source and flags the sharper federal-award accounting gaps.
- A cluster’s **coverage boundary** is what the reviewed reporting did not establish. It is not proof that no outlet has ever mentioned the subject; `gaps.md` records the systematic negative searches.

## C1_NOEM_ADS_FINANCE — Noem-linked advertising, political vendors, and private income

### What has been reported

- **CONFIRMED — AP, 2025-03-10, Adriana Gomez Licon,** [“How the White House hired Republican political firms to launch an anti-migrant ad campaign”](https://apnews.com/article/e7939619195791493e828dfd20604d65). AP reported a roughly **$200 million** “leave now” advertising campaign awarded without ordinary full competition under an urgency rationale. It identified **People Who Think LLC**, **Safe America Media**, Jay Connaughton, and Mike McElwain, and connected vendor principals to Republican campaigns, Corey Lewandowski, and Louisiana Gov. Jeff Landry. **Evidence:** procurement justifications, business records, campaign biographies, and vendor/government statements.
- **CONFIRMED — ProPublica, 2025-06-30, Justin Elliott, Joshua Kaplan, and Alex Mierjeski,** [“Kristi Noem Secretly Took a Cut of Political Donations”](https://www.propublica.org/article/kristi-noem-political-donations-income-dark-money-dhs-ethics). It traced **$80,000** paid through **Ashwood Strategies LLC** from the pro-Noem **American Resolve Policy Fund** and examined the omission from Noem’s public disclosure. **Evidence:** IRS filings, ethics disclosures, corporate records, interviews, and expert analysis.
- **CONFIRMED — ProPublica, 2025-11-14, Justin Elliott and Joshua Kaplan,** [“Firm Tied to Kristi Noem Secretly Got Money From $220 Million DHS Ad Contracts”](https://www.propublica.org/article/kristi-noem-dhs-ad-campaign-strategy-group). It reported approximately **$143 million** to Safe America Media and **$77 million** to People Who Think and exposed the previously hidden subcontractor **Strategy Group**, run by Ben Yoho, the husband of a DHS spokesperson. It also documented Strategy Group’s prior work for Noem and Lewandowski. **Evidence:** procurement and corporate records, interviews, internal communications, and vendor responses.
- **CONFIRMED — ProPublica, 2025-11-24, Justin Elliott and Joshua Kaplan,** [“Lawmakers Call for Probe of How Firm Tied to Kristi Noem Got Piece of $220 Million DHS Ad Contracts”](https://www.propublica.org/article/kristi-noem-dhs-strategy-group-senate-probe). Five senators and two representatives sought records and an inspector-general review after the November report. **Evidence:** congressional letters and the earlier contract reporting.
- **CONFIRMED — CREW, 2026-01-16, Matt Corley,** [“Kristi Noem’s company was paid almost $140,000 by a pro-Noem group in 2024”](https://www.citizensforethics.org/reports-investigations/crew-investigations/kristi-noems-company-was-paid-almost-140000-by-a-pro-noem-group-in-2024/). CREW found **$137,842** paid to Ashwood Strategies in 2024 and **$265,500** paid to **Lewandowski Strategic Advisors**. **Evidence:** the fund’s IRS Form 990 and public corporate/disclosure records.
- **UNCONFIRMED — Bloomberg, 2026-03-06, Irene García Pérez and Eric Fan,** [“Noem DHS Ad Blitz Benefits Firms Linked to Trump Campaigns”](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-03-06/noem-dhs-ad-blitz-benefits-firms-linked-to-trump-campaigns). The accessible preview describes a roughly **$240 million** blitz and identifies **Strategic Media** and **Smart Media** as firms with Trump campaign/PAC ties. The full evidence chain and subcontract amounts were behind the paywall.

### Coverage boundary

The ads-to-political-network story is heavily claimed territory. The reviewed pieces do **not** publish complete prime-to-subcontractor invoices, ad-placement commissions, media-buy margins, performance metrics, or a reconciled total across the “leave now,” recruiting, and “thank you” campaigns. They also do not connect Ashwood’s private-income stream to a specific award decision by documentary evidence.

## C2_LEWANDOWSKI_CONTROLS — SGE status, approval authority, conflicts, and alleged vendor solicitation

### What has been reported

- **CONFIRMED — Axios, 2025-08-14, Brittany Gibson and Marc Caputo,** [“White House, DHS clash over Corey Lewandowski’s role with Kristi Noem”](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/14/white-house-dhs-lewandowski-noem). It described Lewandowski’s **special government employee (SGE)** arrangement, the 130-day limit, his private consulting work, and internal concern that days worked were being undercounted. **Evidence:** current/former officials and agency responses.
- **UNCONFIRMED — Politico, 2025-08-27, Myah Ward and Zack Colman,** [“Corey Lewandowski’s veto power over DHS contracts frustrates administration officials”](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/08/27/tk-00527043). The accessible syndication and congressional citations say Lewandowski reviewed or could veto DHS contracts and grants above **$100,000** before they reached Noem. **Evidence reported:** three administration/FEMA sources, including one who said they had seen his signature. The publisher page was robots-blocked.
- **CONFIRMED — House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform Democrats, 2025-09-05,** [“Ranking Member Robert Garcia Demands Public Release of Corey Lewandowski’s Financial Disclosures After Violating Special Government Employee Disclosure Laws”](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/ranking-member-robert-garcia-demands-public-release-corey-lewandowskis). Garcia demanded Lewandowski’s **OGE Form 278e**, work-day records, recusals, and ethics agreements. The page links the signed demand letter. **Evidence:** committee letter and cited public reporting.
- **CONFIRMED — Axios, 2026-01-23, Marc Caputo, Brittany Gibson, and Alex Isenstadt,** [“Lewandowski back for another year as DHS’s part-time power broker”](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/23/corey-lewandowski-kristi-noem-homeland-security). It reported that Lewandowski discussed vendors and major contract decisions while his confidential financial disclosure remained nonpublic. **Evidence:** officials familiar with the workflow and DHS responses.
- **CONFIRMED — ProPublica, 2026-03-04, Justin Elliott and Joshua Kaplan,** [“Kristi Noem Misled Congress About Corey Lewandowski’s Role in DHS Contracts”](https://www.propublica.org/article/kristi-noem-dhs-misled-senate-judiciary-corey-lewandowski-contracts). Routing sheets and interviews showed Lewandowski approving or reviewing contracts, including a multimillion-dollar equipment purchase, contradicting Noem’s testimony that he had no contract role. **Evidence:** internal routing documents and four current/former officials.
- **UNCONFIRMED — NBC News, 2026-03-19, Julia Ainsley, Matt Dixon, Jonathan Allen, and Laura Strickler,** [“Some DHS contractors told White House officials they were asked to pay Corey Lewandowski”](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/dhs-contractors-told-white-house-officials-asked-pay-corey-lewandowski-rcna263744). The accessible headline, preview, and authorized mirrors report that **GEO Group founder George Zoley** alleged a success-fee request and a **Salus Worldwide** representative alleged pressure to hire a Lewandowski-linked consultant. The subjects denied wrongdoing. The direct article body was inaccessible.
- **CONFIRMED via CNN affiliate — CNN, 2026-03-26,** [“DHS internal watchdog launches investigation into handling of contracts under Noem, Lewandowski”](https://ktvz.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/03/26/dhs-internal-watchdog-launches-investigation-into-handling-of-contracts-under-noem-lewandowski/). It reported a broad DHS OIG review of contract handling and internal complaints about Inspector General **Joseph Cuffari’s** response. **Evidence:** internal records and interviews.
- **CONFIRMED — NOTUS, 2026-04-02, Reese Gorman and Anna Kramer,** [“Democrats’ Probe Into Noem, Lewandowski, Hones in on Pro-Trump Donor’s Contracts”](https://www.notus.org/congress/democrats-probe-noem-lewandowski-contracts-walters). Lawmakers asked about five companies tied to **William Walters**, hundreds of millions of dollars in awards, and a **$10,000** donation to American Resolve. **Evidence:** congressional demand letters, contract records, and campaign-finance records.
- **UNCONFIRMED/SECONDARY — AOL/New York Post, 2026-07-11, Victor Nava,** [“DHS probe finds Corey Lewandowski may have been involved in improper contracts”](https://www.aol.com/news/dhs-probe-finds-corey-lewandowski-022704018.html). It summarized Wall Street Journal reporting that investigators found possible improper involvement and were considering a DOJ referral, including review of roughly **$1 billion** in warehouse-related awards. The primary WSJ investigation was paywalled and was not independently inspected.

### Coverage boundary

The existence of an informal approval layer and potential conflicts is well reported, but the reviewed coverage does not publish Lewandowski’s Form 278e, a complete client list, an award-by-award recusal log, or a reproducible table of his signatures and approval dates. No reviewed article established that **David Bossie**, **Turnberry Solutions**, or any named Turnberry client paid Lewandowski in connection with a DHS award.

## C3_JETS_APPROVAL — Luxury aircraft, the $100,000 gate, and procurement bottlenecks

### What has been reported

- **UNCONFIRMED — New York Times, 2025-08-21,** [“Kristi Noem’s Tight Grip on Spending Is Slowing Homeland Security”](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/08/21/us/kristi-noem-spending-contracts-homeland-security-department.html). The preview and widespread citations report that Noem required personal approval above **$100,000**, stalling hundreds of routine transactions. The full article was paywalled.
- **CONFIRMED — Washington Post, 2025-10-18, Marianne LeVine and Gerrit De Vynck,** [“Noem pushed to buy two luxury jets for DHS”](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/10/18/noem-jets-dhs-purchase/). It reported a roughly **$200 million** plan for two Gulfstream G700s after a much smaller request to Congress. **Evidence:** budget documents, officials, and procurement records.
- **UNCONFIRMED — New York Times, 2025-10-18,** [“Kristi Noem Plans $172 Million Purchase of Gulfstream Jets”](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/10/18/us/politics/kristi-noem-dhs-gulfstream.html). The accessible metadata reported **$172 million** for two Coast Guard aircraft. The full sourcing was paywalled.
- **CONFIRMED — Axios, 2025-11-21, Brittany Gibson,** [“The $1 workaround to Kristi Noem’s accountability pledge”](https://www.axios.com/2025/11/21/kristi-noem-contracts-homeland-security/). A POGO-assisted review found eleven awards between **$99,999 and $99,999.99**, including a **$99,999.99 Palantir** action. **Evidence:** federal procurement records and POGO analysis.
- **CONFIRMED — Washington Post, 2026-04-07, Brianna Sacks, Maria Sacchetti, and Marianne LeVine,** [“What spending probes at DHS reveal about Kristi Noem’s time in office”](https://www.washingtonpost.com/immigration/2026/04/05/noem-trump-dhs-fema-contracts/). It synthesized roughly **$1 billion** for Salus-related work, approximately **$200 million** for jets, and the ad campaign while noting inspector-general inquiries. **Evidence:** federal records, oversight correspondence, and interviews.
- **CONFIRMED — Axios, 2026-06-03, Brittany Gibson,** [“The last holdouts at Noem’s DHS”](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/03/noem-mullin-homeland-security-contracts). It reported more than **$9 billion** in less-than-fully-competed awards during Noem’s tenure and a **$200 million** Salus extension that brought the reported total to about **$915 million**. **Evidence:** procurement data and agency responses.

### Coverage boundary

The “$100,000 gate” is reported, but no reviewed outlet measured processing-time effects across components or tested whether awards were split to avoid review. The eleven near-threshold actions are a lead set, not proof of evasion. Aircraft reporting does not provide a full life-cycle cost comparison, mission-utilization forecast, or vendor/subcontractor chain.

## C4_DETENTION_SURGE — Camps, beds, letter contracts, and contractor capacity

### What has been reported

- **CONFIRMED — Texas Tribune/ProPublica, 2025-04-11, Jeff Ernsthausen, Mica Rosenberg, and Avi Asher-Schapiro,** [“Meet the tent company making a fortune off Trump’s deportation plans”](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/04/11/trump-deportations-deployed-resources-tent-company/). It mapped **Deployed Resources**, its former-government personnel, emergency tent work, and the planned **Fort Bliss** camp. **Evidence:** contract records, corporate histories, interviews, and prior performance.
- **CONFIRMED — ProPublica, 2025-04-24, Jeff Ernsthausen, Mica Rosenberg, and Avi Asher-Schapiro,** [“ICE Awarded a $3.8 Billion Contract to Hold Immigrants on a Military Base. Days Later, It Was Canceled.”](https://www.propublica.org/article/ice-cancels-contract-immigrant-detention-camp-fort-bliss). ICE terminated the initial **$3.8 billion** Deployed Resources vehicle after three days. **Evidence:** procurement records and government/vendor responses.
- **CONFIRMED — AP, 2025-06-16, Heather Hollingsworth and John Hanna,** [“ICE is using no-bid contracts, boosting big firms, to get more detention beds”](https://apnews.com/article/e92b67a388f041b84593d7a29fd93c54). AP identified nine five-year arrangements covering about **10,312 beds**, including **GEO Group** and **CoreCivic**, plus a roughly **$73 million** support action. It explained “letter contracts” that begin performance before all terms are settled. **Evidence:** federal records, company filings, local records, and interviews.
- **CONFIRMED — ProPublica, 2025-07-25, Avi Asher-Schapiro and Jeff Ernsthausen,** [“His Former Company Got Caught Employing Undocumented Workers. Now He’s Profiting Off an Immigrant Detention Camp.”](https://www.propublica.org/article/nathan-albers-fort-bliss-immigration). It traced the subcontracting role of **Disaster Management Group** and owner **Nathan Albers**, including prior allegations that another Albers company hired undocumented workers. **Evidence:** corporate, court, labor, and procurement records.
- **CONFIRMED — AP, 2025-08-28, Michael Biesecker and Joshua Goodman,** [“Mystery surrounds $1.2 billion Army contract to build huge detention tent camp in Texas desert”](https://apnews.com/article/3595746cd420c6f83c4ffd0b331ae056). AP profiled **Acquisition Logistics LLC**, whose prior largest federal award was reported at about **$16 million**, and the losing bidder **Gemini Tech Services’ GAO protest**. **Evidence:** procurement files, protest documents, corporate records, and interviews.
- **CONFIRMED — Public Citizen, 2026-04-13, Douglas Pasternak,** [“Billion-Dollar Collapse”](https://www.citizen.org/article/billion-dollar-collapse/). The investigation reconstructed Acquisition Logistics’ selection, implementation failures, and replacement by **Amentum**. **Evidence:** contract records, protest filings, government documents, and interviews.
- **CONFIRMED — GAO, 2026-06-09,** [“Immigration Detention: ICE Should Improve Acquisition Planning and Oversight for Fort Bliss” (GAO-26-108886)](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108886). GAO found inadequate acquisition planning and pre-award inspection, estimated up to **$11.5 million** in avoidable initial costs, and issued four recommendations. **Evidence:** audit access to contract files, site work, invoices, and agency interviews.
- **CONFIRMED — Washington Post, 2026-06-09, Douglas MacMillan,** [“ICE detention camp wasted millions and endangered migrants, GAO finds”](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/06/09/ice-detention-camp-wasted-millions-endangered-migrants-gao-finds/). It contextualized GAO’s findings, the roughly **$1.3 billion** program, Acquisition Logistics’ shortcomings, and Amentum’s takeover. **Evidence:** GAO audit and contract records.
- **CONFIRMED — Washington Post, 2026-06-16, Douglas MacMillan,** [“ICE removed detainee protections after private outreach from top contractor”](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/06/16/ice-removed-detainee-protections-after-private-outreach-top-contractor/). It reported that **GEO Group** had private input into revised standards and highlighted former GEO executive **Kenneth Venturella’s** government role. **Evidence:** internal emails, revised standards, and interviews.
- **CONFIRMED — AP, 2026-07-27, Ryan J. Foley,** [“ICE extends no-bid contract for major detention center in Texas, rejecting calls for closure”](https://apnews.com/article/560827ad4d605f4069d673be12f2544d). AP reported a **$776 million** extension through September 2027 atop roughly **$452 million** already awarded, pushing Amentum’s reported work above **$1.2 billion**. **Evidence:** procurement records, GAO findings, and agency/company responses.

### Coverage boundary

Fort Bliss and the largest prison operators are saturated compared with the rest of the surge. The reviewed stories do not provide comparable **per-bed/day**, mobilization, vacancy, or change-order metrics across **GEO, CoreCivic, MTC, LaSalle, Deployed Resources, Target Hospitality, Akima, Loyal Source, and SLSCO**. No article reconciles parent IDIQ ceilings to site-level orders across the entire portfolio. Delaney Hall’s reported **15-year/$1 billion** GEO ceiling is widely covered, but the coverage is mainly site conditions and politics rather than award workflow.

## C5_STATE_FACILITIES — Alligator Alcatraz and federally reimbursed state detention

- **CONFIRMED — Tampa Bay Times, 2025-07-12, Romy Ellenbogen,** [“Alligator Alcatraz firm donated to Florida GOP before getting a contract”](https://www.tampabay.com/news/florida-politics/2025/07/12/alligator-alcatraz-florida-immigration-detention-desantis-uthmeier-irg/). It linked **IRG Global Emergency Management** to a **$10,000** Florida GOP donation made before its award. **Evidence:** state contract and campaign-finance records.
- **CONFIRMED — Miami Herald, 2025-07-21 (updated 2025-07-24), Churchill Ndonwie,** [“Florida commits more than $200 million to Alligator Alcatraz contractors”](https://www.miamiherald.com/news/local/immigration/article311075435.html). It itemized vendors and commitments, including **Longview Solutions ($36.8M), Gothams ($21M), SLSCO and Garner Environmental Services (about $19.8M each), CDR Maguire ($18M), Critical Response Strategies (about $78M), Doodie Calls ($22M), and IRG (about $2.9M)**. **Evidence:** Florida purchasing records and vendor documents.
- **CONFIRMED — Miami Herald, 2025-10-05, Churchill Ndonwie,** [“FEMA approves Florida request for up to $608 million for Alligator Alcatraz”](https://www.miamiherald.com/news/local/immigration/article312370946.html). It distinguished FEMA’s maximum approval from final reimbursement, which remained subject to expense review. **Evidence:** FEMA/state correspondence and spending records.
- **CONFIRMED — AP, 2026-01-08, Mike Schneider,** [“Officials withheld evidence on Florida’s ‘Alligator Alcatraz’ funding, environmental groups say”](https://apnews.com/article/b20629fad416797eab9499af899a14d8). AP reported that officials withheld records concerning possible reimbursement of up to **$608 million**. **Evidence:** public-record emails, court records, and government statements.
- **CONFIRMED — Miami Herald, 2026-06-05, Claire Heddles and Churchill Ndonwie,** [“Florida’s immigration detention contracts reach $1 billion”](https://www.miamiherald.com/news/politics-government/state-politics/article315981332.html). It counted **55 contracts**, roughly **$1 billion** committed, approximately **$335 million** paid and **$220 million** outstanding; **Doodie Calls** was reported at roughly **$219 million** across sites. **Evidence:** state purchasing data, invoices, and agency/vendor responses.
- **CONFIRMED — Axios New Orleans, 2026-01-08, Chelsea Brasted,** [“What’s inside Angola’s ICE detention contract”](https://www.axios.com/local/new-orleans/2026/01/08/angola-prison-ice-facility-cost-immigration). It reported a roughly **$949,000 monthly** state charge subject to federal reimbursement and a **LaSalle Corrections** role. **Evidence:** state budget/contract records and official statements.

### Coverage boundary

Florida is unusually well documented by local press, but no reviewed national story compares the legal and financial mechanics of Florida’s emergency procurement with Louisiana, Indiana, Nebraska, or other state/local IGSAs. FEMA’s **Shelter and Services Program or other account-level reimbursement path**, disallowed-cost risk, interest/carrying costs, and vendor payment timing remain fragmented. Political-donor analysis outside Florida is sparse.

## C6_DEPORTATION_LOGISTICS — ICE Air, brokers, carriers, and Salus

### What has been reported

- **CONFIRMED — POGO, 2025-03-20, Nick Schwellenbach,** [“Meet the ICE Contractor Running Deportation Flights”](https://www.pogo.org/investigates/meet-the-ice-contractor-running-deportation-flights). POGO reported a no-bid, roughly **$128 million** short-term action for **CSI Aviation**, more than **$1.6 billion** to CSI since 2005, political connections, and carrier subcontracting. **Evidence:** federal award records, political records, court files, and company material.
- **CONFIRMED — ProPublica, 2025-04-01, McKenzie Funk, Perla Trevizo, and Mica Rosenberg,** [“Inside ICE Air: Flight Attendants on Deportation Planes Say Disaster Is ‘Only a Matter of Time’”](https://www.propublica.org/article/inside-ice-air-deportation-flights). It exposed safety and humanitarian problems aboard **GlobalX** flights and identified contract guards and ICE Air procedures. **Evidence:** seven current/former flight attendants, ICE manuals, flight records, and company/agency responses.
- **CONFIRMED — Washington Post, 2025-04-11, Andrea Sachs,** [“Avelo Airlines begins ICE deportation flights”](https://www.washingtonpost.com/travel/2025/04/11/avelo-airlines-ice-deportation-flights/). It reported **Avelo** as a CSI subcontractor using three Boeing 737-800 aircraft and placed CSI’s potential current work above **$151 million**. **Evidence:** contract records, company statements, and interviews.
- **CONFIRMED — Washington Post, 2025-12-17, Isaac Arnsdorf,** [“DHS gave a massive deportation contract to a Trump donor’s company”](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/12/17/dhs-contract-trump-donor/). It reported a **Salus Worldwide** “Project Homecoming” deal ultimately valued near **$915 million**, a three-day handpicked competition, about 40 pre-award emails involving **William Walters**, and a CSI challenge. It also identified a roughly **$140 million** aircraft arrangement involving **Daedalus Aviation**. **Evidence:** procurement and protest records, emails, political records, and interviews.
- **UNCONFIRMED — Bloomberg Law, 2026-05-12, Daniel Seiden,** [“DHS Award Stays With Company Accused of Lewandowski Kickbacks”](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/federal-contracting/dhs-award-stays-with-company-accused-of-lewandowski-kickbacks). The accessible preview says CSI lacked standing to dislodge the Salus award. The full decision analysis was paywalled.

### Coverage boundary

CSI, Avelo/GlobalX, and Salus are well reported. The reviewed press did not identify **Acquisition Logistics LLC** as a deportation-flight or charter broker; its prominent GAO protest story concerns Fort Bliss detention. No portfolio story maps each broker to aircraft owner, operating carrier, guard/medical subcontractor, route, per-flight price, deadhead cost, and protest outcome. “ICE Air” is often treated as a single operator even though it is a layered brokerage system.

The Intercept page itself was inaccessible.
- **CONFIRMED — WIRED, 2025-11-25, Dell Cameron,** [“ICE Offers Up to $280 Million to Immigrant-Tracking ‘Bounty Hunter’ Firms”](https://www.wired.com/story/ice-bounty-hunter-spy-program/). - **CONFIRMED via syndication — The Intercept, 2025-12-19, Sam Biddle,** [“ICE Hires Immigrant Bounty Hunters From Private Prison Company GEO Group”](https://theintercept.com/2025/12/19/ice-bounty-hunters-location-surveillance-geo-group/). Incorporated**, GEO Group’s electronic-monitoring subsidiary, had received about **$1.6 million** with potential work around **$121 million**. **Evidence:** procurement records and company history; direct page inaccessible.
