## 1. Ballard Green protest grounds — **highest value**

**Agency:** GAO (protest file) and/or DHS/ICE FOIA (agency report)
**Records:** The complete protest file for **B-424186.1, B-424186.2, B-424186.3, B-424186.4 and B-424186.5**,
protester **Ballard Green, LLC**, concerning ICE solicitation **26-SOL-DCR-01** — including the protest
submissions, the agency report, and the dismissal notices dated 2026-01-13 (×2), 2026-01-29 (×2) and 2026-03-13.

**Why:** Five protests were filed against this solicitation and all five were dismissed with **no published
decision**, so the grounds are not public. The dismissals bracket both the 2026-01-22 delivery-order approvals

we do not know whether it was a disappointed bidder, a subcontractor, or something else. This is the single
biggest unexplained gap in the procurement record.

**Note:** GAO protest files are often obtainable directly from GAO's Procurement Law Division; try that before
the DHS route, it is usually faster.

## 3. UAC evaluation record — how 18 offers became 18 awards

**Agency:** DHS / ICE FOIA
**Records:** For solicitation **70CDCR26R00000015** ("Safety Verification Initiative"): the source-selection
decision document, the technical and price evaluation record, the competitive-range determination if any,
the list of offerors, and any sources-sought/RFI or industry-day records that preceded the solicitation.

**Why:** Every award record reports **18 offers received** and there are **18 awardees** — every offeror won,
under full and open competition with no set-aside. That is the most unusual structural fact in the file. The
evaluation record shows whether price was meaningfully evaluated at all, which matters because first task-order
per-case rates span **$1,055.54 to $11,965.00** — an 11.3× spread for an identical stated deliverable. The
pre-solicitation records also answer how a pool of exactly 18 formed, which is the buy-side test of whether
bidders were assembled or self-selected.

**Fallback if the full record is withheld:** request the **Factor 4 pricing sheets** specifically — they are
less likely to be withheld in full than the source-selection decision.

## 5. Delaney Hall emergency procurement

**Agency:** DHS / ICE FOIA
**Records:** The **FAR 6.302-2 (unusual and compelling urgency) justification** for purchase order
**70CDCR26P00000013** — Response AI Solutions LLC, $250,275.48 obligated against a $573,375.48 ceiling,
signed **2026-05-30**, "EMERGENCY FENCING AND LIGHTING AT DELANEY HALL DETENTION FACILITY, NEWARK NEW JERSEY,"
one offer, not competed. Also any subcontractor or supplier documentation.

**Why:** Two open questions. First, ICE's own account of the triggering event — the order was signed on a
Saturday during a nine-day hunger strike, after crowd-control barriers were destroyed and a night curfew was
imposed. Second, **GEO owns Delaney Hall and its 10-year contract already covers security and maintenance**, so
why did ICE separately and urgently rent perimeter fencing and lighting? Response AI is a logistics broker, not
a fencing contractor, and reported **zero subawards** — so who physically installed it is unknown.

## 6. Privacy compliance for the UAC program

**Agency:** DHS Privacy Office
**Records:** Any **Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA)** and **Privacy Act System of Records Notice (SORN)** covering
the Safety Verification Initiative or contractor-collected UAC location data; plus the records-retention schedule
governing data held by contractors under 70CDCR26R00000015.

**Why:** The program collects, per child, the **A#**, verified home address, sponsor name/relationship/contact/
address, school enrollment, and employment status — held by contractors, several of which are one-to-four-person
firms. Section I of the solicitation invokes a real technical regime (**HSAR 3052.204-72 Alt I**: ATO required,
independent NIST SP 800-53 assessment, one-hour PII incident reporting), but the **PWS contains zero technical
security requirements and the QASP measures none of them** — and the regime attaches only to systems "operated
on behalf of the agency." **A null result here is itself the finding**: a federal program collecting household
data on ~100,000 children with no PIA and no SORN is a documented compliance gap.
