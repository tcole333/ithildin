## Verdict

**VERDICT - CANNOT DETERMINE.** **CONFIRMED:** No affirmative public evidence was found that a licensing, discipline, exclusion, or contractor-standing problem caused ICE not to extend Gravitas's order. The records that are available lean strongly toward **NO**: Ohio PISGS currently lists the company as an **Approved** Class A provider; Kentucky currently lists both Gravitas and Adam Visnic as **Active** with **Disciplinary Actions: No**; Indiana currently lists Gravitas as **Active**; and the March 1, 2026 SAM public extract recorded Gravitas as active through January 27, 2027, before the March 10-11 extension decisions. **BLOCKED:** A categorical NO is not supportable because the public Ohio PISGS result omits issue date, expiration date, qualifying agent, and status history. Gravitas's current Ohio license number also differs from the older number still advertised by the firm, and no public record found in this pass explains or dates that transition. Ohio Secretary of State history was inaccessible behind a Cloudflare challenge. Therefore, a short-lived Ohio licensing or corporate-status problem at the precise decision date cannot be conclusively excluded, but the hypothesis has no affirmative support in the records checked.

- **CONFIRMED:** The same public page says that only currently licensed companies are displayed. This establishes current approval as of 2026-07-28, not historical status on 2026-03-10 or 2026-03-11. [S1]

- **CONFIRMED:** An exact public PISGS search for the firm's advertised older number, **20152100145022**, returned no record. Because the public page displays only current companies, this shows that the old number is not the current public license number; it does **not** show revocation, suspension, or discipline. [S2]

- **CONFIRMED:** Gravitas's current Adam Visnic biography still advertises Ohio Class A license **20152100145022**, while the regulator lists **20252100581834**. The website identifies Visnic as president and founder. The page is self-published and is not licensing proof. [S5]

- **UNCONFIRMED:** The reason and effective date for the number change are not public in the PISGS search result. The official renewal guide's example retains the same license number through renewal, and two longstanding currently approved comparison companies retained license numbers beginning in 2002 and 2011. This makes an automatic system-wide 2026 renumbering unlikely, but it does not establish an adverse event. Ohio law also provides ordinary non-disciplinary reasons for issuing a new corporate license, including a change in qualifying agent, a transfer to another corporation, or a change in license classification. [S3] [S4] [S15]

- **CONFIRMED:** Ohio Revised Code 4749.03, effective April 9, 2025, says licenses issued before the amendment expire on the first March 1 after initial issue and every two years thereafter; licenses issued on or after the amendment expire two years after initial issue. It also says a corporation's license may be transferred or reissued under specified circumstances. [S4]

- **REFUTED:** The PISGS public page's blanket note that all company licenses expire March 1 is no longer a complete statement of current law. The April 9, 2025 amendment created a two-year-from-issue rule for licenses issued on or after that date. Therefore, the first four digits of the current license number cannot by themselves establish that Gravitas faced a March 1, 2026 renewal deadline. [S1] [S4]

- **BLOCKED:** The public search does not provide the exact issue/expiration dates for **20252100581834**, the termination or transfer date for **20152100145022**, renewal/application status, returned-correction history, insurance expiration, or any period of lapse. Those fields are described in the PISGS guide as visible inside the licensed company's authenticated account, which was not accessed. [S3]

### Adam Visnic and the Ohio qualifying-agent issue

- **CONFIRMED:** Ohio law says a corporate Class A/B/C license is considered also to license the individuals who qualified the corporation while they remain associated with it. It also requires a corporate qualifying officer or agent to surrender a similar personal license. The absence of a separate personal-provider result for Visnic therefore would not, by itself, be adverse. [S4]

- **BLOCKED:** The public PISGS company result does not name Gravitas's qualifying officer or agent. A public registrant-search URL returned an application error, and Ohio eLicense's stated list of 24 participating boards does not include PISGS or the Department of Public Safety. Its search also presents a CAPTCHA, which was not attempted. Visnic's exact Ohio qualifying-agent status and historical dates therefore remain unverified. [S14]
