# Philippines/Reodica Wave 2 Report

## Bottom line

The Philippines track materially strengthens hypothesis #310's chronology but does not establish a Marcos, Khashoggi, or laundering bridge.

The strongest reached primary source is the government's sentencing position in *United States v. Reodica*, C.D. Cal. 2:94-cr-00121-SJO, Document 170. It states that, while potential Grand Wilshire bankruptcies were still being explored, Reodica wired **$500,000 from a Grand Chevrolet bank account to an account in the Philippines**, wrote his then-wife a **$250,000 check**, and left for the Philippines. Assistant U.S. Attorney Ruth C. Pinkel's declaration identifies the underlying transfer requests as forms signed on **July 25 and July 26, 1988**. Document 132 says Imperial Savings discovered the fraud during **June and July 1988**; the five bankruptcies followed on **August 4, 1988**. This places the alleged transfer after discovery and immediately before collapse.

The underlying seven-page transfer exhibit, Document **170-5 / Exhibit E / PACER document 031025718914**, is not available on RECAP or Internet Archive. Consequently, the originating bank, Philippine receiving bank, account holder, account number, correspondent bank, lawyer, memo/reference, and ultimate use of funds are unknown. Those are the necessary bridge selectors.

The defense memorandum says that during Reodica's 1988 Philippine stay he met **Fidel V. Ramos** and **Cardinal Jaime Sin** hoping to establish a car dealership. No appointment record, visitor log, correspondence, proposal, license application, company filing, or bank record independently confirming those meetings was located. Official Philippine records establish that Ramos was Secretary of National Defense in 1988; that fact does not connect him to the transfer.

Exact comparisons against documented Marcos recovery vehicles and intermediaries returned **zero exact matches** in the reached Reodica court corpus. No reached Philippine primary record supplied an Adnan Khashoggi selector. Political access and shared geography remain non-financial facts.

## Disconfirming and benign evidence

1. **Stated innocent business purpose:** the defense says the Ramos/Sin meetings concerned establishing a car dealership. Without the receiving account or proposal, that explanation remains viable.
2. **Independent marital chronology:** the certified agreement states separation on June 21, before the July 25-26 requests. A pre-existing marital breakdown is a plausible alternative to a hurried sham divorce, although it does not explain the transfer.
3. **Failed Philippine plan:** the defense says Reodica concluded he had no foreseeable future in the Philippines, returned to the United States for a Seattle real-estate course, and moved to Australia only in October 1989.
4. **Prominence explains access:** Ramos was Defense Secretary and Sin was one of the country's most prominent religious leaders. A diaspora business figure meeting them is not itself evidence of asset movement.
5. **Fernandez role is non-financial:** official records support a diplomatic/consular identity; they do not place him in a bank, escrow, company, or PCGG transaction.
6. **No exact Marcos/Khashoggi overlap:** the reached Marcos cases provide numerous high-specificity selectors, none shared by Reodica records.
7. **Departure timing is disputed:** July versus August prevents false precision about whether the transfer, bankruptcy, and meetings preceded or followed each other by days.
8. **Manilabank trap:** it is one of five U.S. victim banks, not evidence of a receiving Philippine bank.

These points do not prove a benign transfer. They define what a non-concealment explanation would look like and what records would falsify it.
