# OWENS-A — 1988–1990 primary-record reconstruction

**Profile:** `epstein-oslo`  
**Lead / thread:** #72691 / 175  
**Research date:** 2026-07-18  
**Question:** Can the 1989 Wayne Owens Middle East trip attributed to Jeffrey Epstein in 2025 participant interviews be elevated from recollection to contemporaneous documentation?  
**Result:** Owens's travel, legislative purpose, later Epstein campaign contributions, and archival retrieval paths are documented. Epstein's presence on any 1989 Owens trip is not yet contemporaneously documented.

## Summary

- **1988-02-14 through 1990-12-13 — T1/T2:** Seven individually dated Owens Middle East trips were located, plus two additional 1989–1990 Middle East trip slots documented only by a later aggregate count. Three 1988 and two 1990 trips have T1 House Foreign Affairs travel-table entries. Two 1989 trips—Tunis in February and a four-country development-bank trip from about March 23 through April 5—are documented by contemporaneous T2 reporting but not by an Owens row in the reviewed official quarterly tables.
- **1989-03-23 through approximately 1989-04-05 — T2:** Contemporaneous reporting firmly documents the development-bank trip to Egypt, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, and Israel. It names meetings with Hosni Mubarak, Chaim Herzog, King Hussein, King Fahd, and Shimon Peres. It does **not** name Epstein, Dan Gordon, Michael Yeager, Benjamin Netanyahu, Crown Prince Abdullah, or any traveling companion.
- **1989, month unresolved — T2:** The December 16, 2025 *New York Times* investigation remains the only located public provenance for Gordon's and Yeager's Epstein-trip recollections. The article says “1989” but does not identify which Owens trip. Later republications are the same source, not corroboration.
- **1989-03-21 — T1:** Owens introduced H.J.Res. 213, the Middle East Development Bank resolution. It had zero cosponsors, was referred to two Foreign Affairs subcommittees, and has no recorded hearing or markup. Owens's March 9 floor explanation credited S. Daniel Abraham, David Jan Mitchell, Joyce Starr, and three House members among the idea's sources; it did not name Epstein.
- **1990-06-22 — T1:** The FEC records two distinct, non-amended $1,000 receipts from “EPSTEIN, JEFFREY,” New York 10022, reported by the Wayne Owens for Congress Committee. This is the first T1 financial link located between Epstein and Owens, but it postdates the March 1989 trip by about fifteen months and does not establish trip funding or attendance.
- **Catalogs current to 2026-07-18 — T1:** Wayne Owens's papers are MS 0108 at the University of Utah's J. Willard Marriott Library. The separately cataloged photograph collection, P0108, has concrete travel targets: International Travel, boxes 12–16; “Tunis, 1989,” box 12/folder 11; and “Middle East Trip (from Photo Album), 1990,” box 2/folder 11.
- **2006 memoir; catalog reviewed 2026-07-18 — T2 author description / coverage limit:** Google Books describes more than sixty Abraham–Owens trips from 1988–2002, but the available preview exposes no searchable page text. No page-cited 1989 delegation list or Epstein reference could be responsibly extracted.

#### What the official tables establish—and do not

**1988 — T1.** Owens himself described “my December trip and my two earlier official visits last year” on March 9, 1989. The three 1988 House Foreign Affairs entries independently fit that description. [*Congressional Record*, 1989-03-09, pp. 3843–44](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1989-pt3/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1989-pt3-9-1.pdf).

**1989–1990 — synthesis, medium confidence; assessed 2026-07-18.** The reviewed quarterly House Foreign Affairs travel tables contain no Owens row for 1989 and two for 1990. That cannot be treated as a complete trip census. The later Roll Call-derived report says there were six taxpayer-funded Middle East trips during those two years, and Owens told the House on September 28, 1990, that he had visited the region eight times in the preceding 2½ years. The most defensible conclusion is that the quarterly tables are a strong official backbone but not exhaustive for Owens's travel. The record presently contains two undated 1989–90 Middle East trip slots.

**Target-trip date — T2; assessed 2026-07-18.** The March 23 article called the planned trip week-long; the next day's article called it eleven days; the April 7 retrospective said Owens had returned Wednesday after ten days. The defensible date range is departure March 23 and return approximately April 5. The *New York Times* gave only “1989,” so the March trip cannot yet be equated with the Epstein trip.

**Meeting mismatch — T2; assessed 2026-07-18.** The March/April articles name Peres and King Fahd but not Netanyahu or Crown Prince Abdullah. This does not prove that the NYT recollection concerns a different trip—Owens could have met additional people omitted by local coverage—but it prevents a confident match.

### 2. The Middle East Development Bank resolution

**1989-03-21 — T1.** Owens introduced [H.J.Res. 213, 101st Congress](https://www.congress.gov/bill/101st-congress/house-joint-resolution/213), “To establish a Middle East Development Bank.” Its stated purpose was to foster regional development and economic cooperation among private businesses and governments. [Introduced text](https://www.congress.gov/101/bills/HJRES213/BILLS-hjres213ih.htm); [*Congressional Record*, 1989-03-21, p. 5012](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1989-pt4/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1989-pt4-5.pdf).

**1990-07-17 retrospective — T1 primary correspondence, date corrected by T1 official record.** A Utah Jewish community member's letter in [American Jewish Archives MS-630, box 1/folder 3](https://collections.americanjewisharchives.org/ms/ms0630/ms0630.001.003.pdf) says Owens introduced the bank bill in May 1989. The official bill and *Congressional Record* establish the actual introduction date as March 21; the letter is useful context but not controlling on legislative chronology.

**1989-04-03 — T1.** Congress.gov records zero cosponsors and six actions. The House Foreign Affairs Committee referred it to the Subcommittee on Europe and the Middle East and the Subcommittee on International Economic Policy and Trade; executive comment was requested from State and Treasury. No hearing, witness list, markup, reported version, or later legislative action is recorded.

**1989-03-09 — T1.** Before introduction, Owens told the House that he had helped organize the Center for Middle East Peace and Economic Cooperation with S. Daniel Abraham the previous month. He credited project ideas to Abraham; New York investment banker David Jan Mitchell; CSIS senior associate Joyce Starr; Representatives Lee Hamilton, Henry Waxman, and Mel Levine; and others. Epstein is not named in the statement. [Floor statement, pp. 3843–44](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1989-pt3/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1989-pt3-9-1.pdf).

**1989-03-08 — T1.** One day earlier, Owens placed Dan Gordon's tripartite-confederacy proposal in the *Congressional Record*. Owens called Gordon a friend, a Jewish American who had lived nine years in Israel as a kibbutznik, an Israeli Defense Forces veteran, a screenwriter, and a Southern California community leader. This contemporaneously explains why Gordon was a plausible informal policy interlocutor; it does not prove he traveled. [*Congressional Record*, Extensions, p. 3808](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1989-pt3/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1989-pt3-8-3.pdf).

### 3. The 1989 private-travel disclosure branch

**Law in force for the March 1989 trip — T1.** The Ethics in Government Act then required a House filer to identify the source and briefly describe gifts of transportation, lodging, food, or entertainment aggregating $250 or more from a nonrelative source, subject to personal-hospitality and small-item exclusions. It separately required the source and a brief description of reimbursements aggregating $250 or more. “Reimbursement” excluded government-provided, foreign-gift-law-reported, and campaign-reported travel. [Public Law 95-521, title I, §§102(a)(2), 109(8)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-92/pdf/STATUTE-92-Pg1824.pdf). The Ethics Reform Act was enacted on November 30, 1989, after the target trip.

**Retrieval consequence — T1/synthesis; assessed 2026-07-18.** If a non-government source paid reportable March 1989 travel expenses, the source should be sought in Owens's annual financial disclosure covering calendar 1989, not inferred from silence in the quarterly committee table. That report is outside the Clerk's online portal, which begins with 2008. The period compilation is *Financial Disclosure Reports of Members of the U.S. House of Representatives of the 101st Congress*, House Document 101-218, volumes 1–2 (1990). The authoritative retrieval path is the House Legislative Resource Center, B-81 Cannon House Office Building, or a library holding of the bound House document. [LRC public-records page](https://clerk.house.gov/About/ViewLRCDetails); [online disclosure portal](https://disclosures-clerk.house.gov/FinancialDisclosure/ViewReport). No contact or filing request was made.

**Boundary.** A quarterly-table null is therefore informative but not dispositive. It neither proves private funding nor identifies a payer. A 1991 account quotes Owens saying only one of his sixteen Middle East trips over four years was paid by an outside group, without identifying that trip.

The Commission warns that its standardized individual-contribution file includes reporting-period amounts of $500 or more for 1975–1988 and $200 or more for 1989–2014. **Inference boundary.** The receipts establish money from a contributor reported as Jeffrey Epstein to Owens's committee. They do not establish that the money financed travel, rewarded access, or came from the same Jeffrey Epstein solely by name; the matching city, ZIP, occupation, era, and later reporting make that identity highly likely, while the FEC record itself reports only the committee's submitted fields.

**1991–1993 Senate-cycle null — T1; search performed 2026-07-18.** Committee `C00199992` later reported as the Owens for Senate Committee. The reviewed 1992 and 1994 bulk files returned no Jeffrey Epstein receipt to that committee dated in 1991–1993, subject to the same name and itemization limits stated above.

### 6. Gordon and Yeager: provenance and roles

**2025-12-16 — T2.** The [*New York Times* investigation](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/12/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-money-scams-investigation.html) ([archived copy](https://archive.ph/XNZdB)), based on participant interviews, reports that Epstein joined an Owens Middle East trip in 1989 as an apparent finance expert. Dan Gordon said he warned Owens about Epstein's attire in meetings with Netanyahu, Peres, and Crown Prince Abdullah. Michael Yeager, identified as an Owens aide, recalled Epstein boasting about pursuing hidden assets and his Wexner ties. **Pre-publication provenance search — bounded T2/T3 null; searched 2026-07-18.** Exact-name and phrase searches across the open web, the public JTA archive, and the *Deseret News* archive located no Gordon or Yeager public telling of the Epstein-trip story dated before December 16, 2025. Post-publication articles, translations, podcasts, and summaries trace to the NYT article. This is a public-index null, not proof that neither man previously made a private or unindexed statement.

**Role resolution.** Gordon's Israel ties and policy role are contemporaneously documented at T1 by Owens's March 8, 1989 floor submission. Gordon's presence on an Owens trip is still supported only by his 2025 reported recollection. Yeager's trip-era title could not be independently resolved in the searchable 1989 Congressional Directory or contemporaneous press; the NYT description “one of Owens's aides” remains the located source. No manifest, staff travel voucher, diary, photograph caption, or meeting memorandum names either man on a 1989 trip.

## Angles & Hypotheses (not findings)

> [!CAUTION]
> **UNVERIFIED EXPLORATION ONLY. Nothing in this section is a finding, and none of it is staged in the findings JSON.**

1. **Which 1989 trip?** The NYT recollection may describe the March bank trip because Peres and Saudi travel overlap, but the contemporaneous report names King Fahd rather than Abdullah and does not name Netanyahu. The February Tunis trip or one of the two undated 1989–90 slots remains possible.
2. **The unnamed Saudi financier.** Owens said that in Saudi Arabia “one of the world's richest men” flew “us” to a Red Sea weekend palace near Jeddah and expressed interest in the bank project. The individual and the “us” are unidentified. This passage is a high-value archival selector, not evidence for Abdullah, Epstein, or any candidate financier.
3. **Contribution sequencing.** The two June 1990 Epstein receipts may reflect a relationship continuing after a 1989 introduction. They could also be ordinary political contributions unrelated to travel. Only correspondence, invitations, check images, finance committee records, or donor-event records can distinguish those possibilities.
4. **Gordon's policy role.** Gordon's proposal entered the Record immediately before Owens publicly detailed the bank and Center, giving a non-cinematic reason for his presence in the policy circle. That makes his later claim plausible but supplies no contemporaneous travel proof.
5. **Abraham as the logistical hub.** 1991 *Deseret News* reporting says Owens traveled extensively with Abraham and had accepted a loan from him. That raises a payer/logistics hypothesis for at least some trips, but the article does not identify the March 1989 payer and cannot substitute for the financial disclosure or travel vouchers.
