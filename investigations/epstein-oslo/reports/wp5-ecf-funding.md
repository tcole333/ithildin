# WP5 — ECF early funding, 1991–1993

Research completed 2026-07-17 for lead #72586, thread 172. “ECF account” below means a receipt shown as paid to the Economic Cooperation Foundation legal entity; support for its principals, projects, or travel is kept separate.

## Summary

- **Roster — T2 synthesis; events 1991–1993; assessed 2026-07-17:** the named early support map is European Community/Commission, Friedrich Ebert Stiftung, and Norwegian MFA/Fafo; a scholarly account also indicates additional unnamed European development agencies.
- **Year one — T2; event September 1991:** FES began its ECF collaboration when Beilin and Hirschfeld presented the newly founded foundation in Bonn; later FES documentation explicitly says it financially supported Hirschfeld and Pundak's Oslo activity.
- **Year two — T2; event September 1992:** an EC Commission research contract supported an ECF-associated Hirschfeld study. No amount or recipient ledger was found for either the Commission or FES line.
- **Earliest MFA money — T2; event 1992-09-09:** Jan Egeland committed Norwegian MFA support on 9 September 1992; Fafo payroll and logistical spending are documented by the first Oslo round on 20–22 January 1993.
- **Direct Norway-to-ECF record — T1; event 2013:** the earliest named Norwegian government donation visible in GuideStar is NIS 118,900 in 2013; the public donation series begins only in 2012.
- **American-private-donor answer — T1/T2 synthesis; events 1991–1993; assessed 2026-07-17:** **no American private donor to ECF in 1991–1993 was verified**, so pre-committed rule 4 did not fire.
- **Completeness limit — T1/T2; events 1991–1993; assessed 2026-07-17:** this is a support roster, not an account-complete donor schedule. GuideStar exposes no 1991–1993 accounts, and the identified sources omit amounts and payee ledgers.

### 2. The registrar fixes ECF's legal identity but not its early accounts or founding officers

**Claim — T1 registrar record; event 1991; retrieved 2026-07-17.** [GuideStar Israel](https://www.guidestar.org.il/organization/580187078) identifies `580187078` as `הקרן לשיתוף פעולה כלכלי (ע"ר) THE ECONOMIC COOPERATION FOUNDATION (R.A.)` and gives the founding year as **1991**. Its statutory purposes are:

> פיתוח רעיונות ויזום תוכניות לפרוייקטים כלכליים משותפים המיועדים לקידום המשקים של מדינת ישראל ושטחים מוחזקים המנוהלים על ידי מדינת ישראל. בחינת אפשרויות של שיתוף פעולה כלכלי אזורי ותכנון כלכלי של המזרח התיכון

Translation: “Developing ideas and initiating programs for joint economic projects intended to advance the economies of the State of Israel and territories administered by Israel; examining possibilities for regional economic cooperation and economic planning for the Middle East.”

**Claim — T3 commercial-registry mirror; reported event 1991-01-31; retrieved 2026-07-17.** [CAP](https://info.cap.co.il/israeli-companies/580187078/) reports an exact registration date of **31 January 1991** for amuta `580187078`. GuideStar's public primary record exposes only the year, and the incorporation certificate requires a request/payment flow. The day and month therefore remain unconfirmed and are not promoted to T1.

**Claim — T1 current registrar metadata; events 2012–2026; retrieved 2026-07-17.** The public record exposes annual reports for **2017–2024**, financial series data for **2018–2024**, proper-management certificates for **2017–2026**, and named-donation data for **2012–2025**. It exposes **zero** financial or donor filings for 1991–1993. The public founders call returned an empty list; officer details were CAPTCHA-gated. No CAPTCHA was bypassed and no certificate or filing was ordered. The visible address—ז'בוטינסקי 35, רמת גן 5251108—is current metadata and is not evidence of ECF's 1991 address.

**Claim — T2/T1 source comparison; event 1991; assessed 2026-07-17.** [ECF's current founders page](https://ecf.org.il/founders/) names Yair Hirschfeld, Boaz Karni, and Yossi Beilin. A [participant retrospective by Gidi Grinstein](https://web.archive.org/web/20240705025401id_/https://gidigrinstein.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Insights-Grinstein-Afilalo-FULL-Text-for-private-distribution.pdf) describes a five-person founding leadership group—Beilin, Hirschfeld, Karni, Nimrod Novik, and Ron Pundak—and says Karni handled finance and operations. Neither source substitutes for a historical registrar officer list, and neither proves that any founder personally funded ECF.

### 3. European Commission support is the clearest named year-two project-funding line

**Claim — T2 academic literature; event September 1992; retrieved 2026-07-17.** [Sven Behrendt's study](https://www.nomos-elibrary.de/10.5771/0946-7165-2000-1-79.pdf) of the secret negotiations says Hirschfeld produced *Israel, the Palestinians and the Middle East: From Dependency to Interdependence* `im Rahmen eines Forschungsauftrags der EG-Kommission`—“under a research contract from the EC Commission.” [Hirschfeld's later account](https://fathomjournal.org/?p=8088&post_type=post) dates the report to September 1992. The accessible indexed passage associates the Commission with ECF and cites Behrendt's interviews with former Commission official Eberhard Rhein and Hirschfeld.

**Claim — T2 corroboration; event 1991; publication 2001.** [Wanis-St. John](https://albayanres.com/library/book/download/1693576987.pdf#page=170) writes that “Beilin and Hirschfeld, with the financing of European development agencies, set up an NGO called the Economic Cooperation Foundation (ECF) in 1991.” The plural phrasing corroborates a European institutional funding base, but it cannot establish how many agencies existed beyond the Commission and FES, how much each paid, or whether payments went into ECF's account.

### 4. FES was an early German institutional supporter

**Claim — T2 institutional history; events September 1991 and 1993; publication 2018.** [FES records](https://library.fes.de/pdf-files/iez/14766.pdf) that Beilin and Hirschfeld presented the newly founded ECF at its Bonn headquarters in September 1991 and calls that encounter the start of a fruitful collaboration. It later says Beilin sent Hirschfeld and Pundak to Oslo `unter anderem auch mit finanzieller Unterstützung der FES`—“among other things, with financial support from FES.” The same history cites FES's 1993 annual report for support of their participation in international seminars in Brussels and Washington.

**Limit — T2 evidence boundary; events 1991–1993; assessed 2026-07-17.** The source does not provide an amount, award date, grant number, or payee account. It supports naming FES in the early support map; it does not justify calling FES a cash donor directly into ECF's legal account.

### 5. Norwegian MFA money began as channel finance through Fafo, not a proved ECF grant

**Claim — T2 academic history based on participant/government interviews; event 1992-09-09.** [Hilde Henriksen Waage](https://cdn.cloud.prio.org/files/dc704558-ffa6-40f9-bf49-9f90ba1588b7/Waage%20Hilde%20Henriksen%202004%20Peacemaking%20is%20a%20Risky%20Business%20PRIO%20Report%201-2004.pdf?inline=true#page=47) reports that during a secret Tel Aviv meeting on 9 September 1992, Jan Egeland said “the Norwegian foreign ministry was behind Fafo and would back and finance a back channel project.” This is the earliest precise Norwegian MFA commitment located.

**Claim — T2; events January 1993 onward.** Waage reports that Hirschfeld and Pundak were put on Fafo's payroll and that Norway supplied the resources. At the first Sarpsborg round on 20–22 January 1993, Terje Rød-Larsen told the participants, “We can provide money, houses, services.” Beilin's account says Norway met flights, accommodation, materials, secrecy, and security costs. The evidentiary classification is **Norwegian public support to the channel and ECF personnel via Fafo**, not a Norwegian grant shown in ECF's books.

**Claim — T1 registrar disclosure; event 2013; retrieved 2026-07-17.** [GuideStar's named-donation series](https://www.guidestar.org.il/organization/580187078/donations) first shows Norway in 2013 as `ממשלת נורבגיה` (“Government of Norway”), **NIS 118,900**, for `שיפור החיים בשטחים` (“improving life in the territories”). The 2012 list contains no Norway entry. Because online donation records start in 2012, 2013 is only the earliest *visible named direct donation*, not proof that it was Norway's first actual transfer to ECF.

### 6. ECF remained materially lean even after European funding expanded

**Claim — T2 participant retrospective; events 1992 and after 1996; publication 2023.** [Grinstein](https://web.archive.org/web/20240705025401id_/https://gidigrinstein.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Insights-Grinstein-Afilalo-FULL-Text-for-private-distribution.pdf) recalls a post-office box in ECF's first couple of years, a shabby apartment and furniture by 1992, modest salaries, and possible paycheck delays. He says European funding became readily available after Netanyahu's 1996 election, with the European Commission and countries pledging millions for peace work, but that ECF still operated frugally. This supports the “shoestring” context; it neither identifies a 1991–1993 donor nor proves the size of ECF's early budget.

### 7. No verified American private donor appears in the target window

**Claim — T1 document-search null; annual-report events 1991, 1992, and 1993; searched 2026-07-17.** Each of Ford Foundation's official annual reports for [1991](https://www.fordfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/1991-annual-report.pdf), [1992](https://www.fordfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/1992-annual-report.pdf), and [1993](https://www.fordfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/1993-annual-report.pdf) was searched for `Economic Cooperation Foundation`, `Yair Hirschfeld`, `Ron Pundak`, `Ron Pundik`, `Hirschfeld`, and `Pundak`; each report returned **0/6 term hits**. The 1991 and 1992 scanned reports were OCRed locally before searching; the 1993 report contained searchable Israel grant text, providing a basic positive control for extraction.

**Claim — T1/T2 bounded synthesis; events 1991–1993; assessed 2026-07-17.** Across the public registrar, ECF history and supporter pages, early Wayback index, participant and academic sources, exact Hebrew/English press-index searches, Ford reports, and five local corpus tools, no American private ECF funder was identified. This null cannot exclude an anonymous donor, an intermediary/regrant, a spelling/OCR miss, or a payment absent from public filings.

## Update-Rule Triggers

**No trigger.** Pre-committed rule 4 states: **“Channel funding (WP4/WP5). Unexplained US private money in FAFO or ECF accounts 1991-93 → major update H1.”**

**Assessment — T1/T2 synthesis; target events 1991–1993; assessed 2026-07-17.** WP5 found no American private money in an ECF account. The named early support is European Commission, FES, and Norwegian MFA/Fafo; none is an American private donor. WP4's identified American foundation support to Fafo was program-attributed and is not evidence of an ECF receipt. The absence of early ECF accounts prevents an account-complete null, but weakening the rule after seeing that limitation would violate the pre-commitment.
