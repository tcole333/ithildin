## Summary

- **[T1 | 1989-01-23 to 1989-04-07]** The precise legal founding date is **January 23, 1989**. District of Columbia file **890233** records that effective date for the domestic nonprofit now called the **S. Daniel Abraham Center for Middle East Peace**. On March 9, Wayne Owens told the House that he and S. Daniel Abraham had helped organize the Center “last month.” The Center therefore existed before the separately documented March 23–approximately April 5, 1989 mission on which Owens promoted his bank proposal. The month of the Epstein-accompanied 1989 trip in finding #13911 remains unresolved, so this does **not** establish that Epstein traveled under Center auspices or that the Center predated that specific trip.
- **[T1 | 1994-06 to 2026-07-18]** EIN **52-1621126** is a 501(c)(3) private foundation with an IRS ruling date of June 1994. Twenty-two public Form 990-PF returns were located for 2001–2018 and 2021–2024. No Form 990 or 990-EZ was located. The 2019–2020 and pre-2001 annual-return layers remain gaps.
- **[T1 | 2001-01-01 to 2002-12-31]** The filings directly verify Owens’s Center pay: **$379,999 in 2001** and **$378,754 in 2002**, each for a reported 40-hour week as president. The returns do not characterize this compensation as lobbying pay.
- **[T1/T3 | 1989 to 2002-12-18]** The Center operated as a recurring private diplomatic-travel and briefing platform. Its archived site claimed more than 40 fact-finding missions by 2000 and published a collective list of 1998 congressional participants. A trip-by-trip manifest for the relevant early 1989 mission was not located. Owens died in Tel Aviv on **December 18, 2002**, immediately after seven days of Center travel, a contemporaneous participant account that anchors the institution’s method.
- **[T1/T2 | 1989-03-09 to 2026-07-18]** Owens’s 1989 Middle East Development Bank proposal and the mid-1990s MENA Bank share a documented economic-cooperation concept, but the reviewed founding record does not establish legal or organizational succession. Later sources credit a 1994 Egypt–Israel–Jordan–PLO proposal and the multilateral/summit process, not Owens or the Center. The 1996 treaty remains **“NOT YET IN FORCE”** with nine signatories and three parties; a transition team worked in Cairo, but no evidence of lending operations was found.
- **[T1 | coverage run 2026-07-18]** No Jeffrey Epstein, Leslie Wexner, Michael Yeager, or identified Epstein-vehicle name appeared in the 22 returns. No such person appeared as an identified participant in the reviewed early mission pages. One bounded content hit exists: a 2014 Terje Rød-Larsen office bulletin sent to Epstein’s `jeevacation@gmail.com` included an S. Daniel Abraham op-ed and a one-line Center biography. That is circulation of Abraham-authored content into Epstein’s inbox—not evidence of Center office, funding, mission participation, or direct Abraham–Epstein contact.
- **[T1/T2/T3 | observed 2026-07-18]** The current-status record is split. DC reports the corporation **Dissolved**; the current IRS Business Master File still lists the exempt organization; the Center filed a 2024 return; and `centerpeace.org` redirects to an active **The Center Project** site whose schema identifies the organization as the S. Daniel Abraham Center and whose page was modified in 2026. Abraham died on June 29, 2025; Robert Wexler is identified as president on the current site.

### 3. Mission program and operating method

**[T3 | captured 2000-02-29; claims cover 1989–2000] Institutional account.** The archived [“About the Center” page](https://web.archive.org/web/20000229094305id_/http://www.centerpeace.org:80/about_the_center_new.htm) says the organization was established in 1989 by Abraham and Owens; recruited Stephen Cohen in 1990; and, by 2000, had sponsored “more than 40 fact finding missions” to Israel and every Arab country except Libya. It describes participants as members of Congress, government officials, and businesspeople and identifies repeated meetings with Israeli, Palestinian, Egyptian, Jordanian, Syrian, Saudi, Gulf, and North African leaders. These are the Center’s own claims and are used to describe its program, not independently to prove every meeting.

**[T3 | captured 1999-05-28; claims cover 1988–1999] Owens chronology.** The archived [Owens biography](https://web.archive.org/web/19990528131426id_/http://www.centerpeace.org:80/bio_owens.htm) states that he traveled to the region “virtually every three months” during his last five House years, usually with Abraham; that they organized the Center in 1989; that Owens became vice chairman in January 1993; and that he became president in February 1995.

**[T3 | Fall 1998, archived 2000-05-10] Early participant publication.** A Center [Fall 1998 newsletter](https://web.archive.org/web/20000510184517id_/http://www.centerpeace.org:80/newsletter/newsletter_F98.htm) says it had taken one senator and four House members during the prior year and names **Robert Torricelli, Gary Ackerman, Tom Campbell, Jim Moran, and David Price**. It lists Syria, Egypt, Israel, Jordan, Qatar, Lebanon, Gaza, and the West Bank as destinations. This answers the participant-list question narrowly: the Center did publish a **collective list** of named early participants, but the page does not map each person to a particular itinerary or supply a complete delegation manifest.

**[T3 | 2010-02, captured 2010-04-21] Later trip documentation.** The archived [2010 trips page](https://web.archive.org/web/20100421010137id_/http://www.centerpeace.org:80/trips.htm) names February meetings in Israel, the Palestinian territories, Jordan, Egypt, and Bahrain and identifies Abraham, Toni Verstandig, Robert Wexler, and Zvika Krieger in trip captions. This confirms that the participant/meeting publication practice continued, but it is too late to resolve the 1989 party.

**[T1/T2 | event 2002-12-18; published 2003-01-28] Death anchor.** Rep. David Price’s [contemporaneous participant account](https://rollcall.com/2003/01/28/owens-commitment-to-mideast-peace-should-inspire-others/) states that Owens died of a heart attack while walking on a Tel Aviv beach on December 18, after Price, Rep. Jim Davis, and Owens completed seven days of travel. Price reports Center meetings with the heads of state in Syria, Lebanon, and Israel and targeted sessions with Omar Suleiman, Abu Mazen, Salam Fayyad, and Sari Nusseibeh. This is direct evidence of the Center’s operational method: small congressional missions, leadership access, issue briefings, and off-record discussions. Contemporary [Deseret News reporting](https://www.deseret.com/2002/12/20/19694677/colleagues-remember-wayne-owens/) independently places his death in Tel Aviv during a Center mission.

**[T3 | 2006 publication metadata] Abraham book coverage.** Google Books identifies *Peace Is Possible: Conversations with Arab and Israeli Leaders from 1988 to the Present* and its publisher description says Abraham and Owens made more than 60 trips between 1988 and 2002. The [Google Books volume](https://books.google.com/books/about/Peace_Is_Possible.html?id=Y0SLqMh9PqMC) returned `searchable:false` through the search-within-volume endpoint during this pass. No page-cited snippet could therefore be responsibly supplied, and no inside-book Epstein/Wexner null is claimed. Publisher metadata supports the scale claim only at T3.
