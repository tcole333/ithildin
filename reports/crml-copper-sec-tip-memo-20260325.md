# Draft SEC Tip Memorandum

## Subject

Critical Metals Corp. (`NASDAQ: CRML`) — possible materially misleading disclosure, omission of a material share recipient, inadequate support for a material non-cash asset transaction, and suspicious timing around resale registration and subsequent dilution.

## Summary

This memorandum concerns a transaction in which Critical Metals Corp. stated that it acquired 40 kilograms of copper powder in exchange for 2,000,000 ordinary shares nominally valued at $20,000,000. The company publicly described the seller, Swiss Commodity Re Limited, as a "long-only multi-generational European based single family office." Public Hong Kong registry records instead show a Hong Kong private company incorporated on October 30, 2025, with USD 100 paid-up capital, a registered office at a corporate-services address in Central Hong Kong, Kenneth Raymond Deayton as sole director, Hong Kong Corporate Services Group Limited as company secretary, and International Fiduciaries Limited as the sole shareholder at incorporation.

The subsequent resale registration disclosed that 1,800,000 of the 2,000,000 shares went to Swiss Commodity Re Limited and 200,000 went to Malcolm Scott Macintyre, an Australian infrastructure financier. That separate 200,000-share allocation does not appear to have been disclosed in the initial transaction announcement and was still not cleanly reflected in the later half-year financial presentation, which described the shares as issued to "the Seller."

The half-year filing later carried the copper asset at $15.8 million as non-current inventory, not the $20 million initially announced, and disclosed a material weakness related to valuation and accounting for the copper powder acquired in the period. I have not found any filed Asset Sale Agreement, custody document, storage location, assay certificate, warehouse receipt, shipping record, customs document, insurance document, or independent valuation supporting this asset.

I am not asserting that the copper definitively did not exist. I am asserting that the public record presents a sufficiently serious disclosure and accounting concern to warrant SEC review.

## Key Facts

### 1. The company announced a $20 million share-for-copper transaction

In the November 25, 2025 filing package, CRML announced that it had acquired 40 kilograms of "ultra-high-purity" copper powder in exchange for 2,000,000 ordinary shares valued at $20,000,000.

The same filing package contained broad promotional language about the copper and the seller, including statements about a traceable G7/EU-origin supply chain and immediate allied-military relevance.

### 2. The seller description conflicts with the registry record

The press release described Swiss Commodity Re Limited as a "long-only multi-generational European based single family office."

The Hong Kong registry documents show instead that:

- Swiss Commodity Re Limited was incorporated in Hong Kong on October 30, 2025.
- It was a private company limited by shares.
- It had USD 100 issued and paid-up capital.
- Its registered office was `6/F Wyndham Place, 40-44 Wyndham Street, Central, Hong Kong`.
- Its sole director was Kenneth Raymond Deayton.
- Its company secretary was Hong Kong Corporate Services Group Limited.
- Its sole shareholder at incorporation was International Fiduciaries Limited.
- Its incorporation form listed business nature as `Nil`.

On the face of those records, the company publicly described to investors does not look like a multi-generational European family office.

### 3. A separate 200,000-share recipient was disclosed later

The February 23, 2026 F-3 and March 4, 2026 424B3 disclosed that:

- Swiss Commodity Re Limited held 1,800,000 shares from the Asset Sale Agreement.
- Malcolm Scott Macintyre held 200,000 shares from the same Asset Sale Agreement.
- Kenneth Raymond Deayton had voting and investment control over Swiss Commodity Re's shares.

By contrast:

- The November 25, 2025 announcement described the transaction as 2,000,000 shares to the seller.
- The later half-year financial presentation likewise described the shares as issued to "the Seller."

This appears to leave a material recipient outside the main disclosure narrative for months.

### 4. The carrying value shifted from $20 million to $15.8 million

The transaction was announced at $20,000,000, but the copper was later carried at $15.8 million in the December 31, 2025 half-year filing.

That filing classified the asset as non-current inventory and disclosed a material weakness related to valuation and accounting for the copper powder acquired in the period.

### 5. I have not found basic support documents for a $15.8 million inventory asset

Across the filings reviewed, I have not found:

- the Asset Sale Agreement as a filed exhibit
- a storage location
- a named custodian
- an assay certificate or certificate of analysis
- a warehouse receipt
- a shipping or customs record
- an insurance document
- an independent valuation or fairness opinion

The absence of these items may have an innocent explanation, but for a transaction of this stated size and balance-sheet impact, their absence from the public file is notable.

### 6. The resale window opened immediately before a large GEM share issuance

The copper-deal F-3 became effective on March 3, 2026 and the 424B3 followed on March 4, 2026, making the copper-deal shares freely resalable through the registration statement.

CRML then disclosed a March 5, 2026 deed of settlement with GEM that resulted in 4,153,686 shares being issued to GEM. The timing does not by itself prove wrongdoing, but it creates a compressed sequence in which:

- the copper recipients became able to resell,
- the market was then hit with additional equity issuance almost immediately afterward.

## Why This Merits Review

The following issues appear substantial enough to justify SEC attention:

1. Possible materially misleading description of the counterparty.
2. Possible omission or obscuration of a separate 200,000-share recipient.
3. Failure to file, or at minimum publicly present, the controlling Asset Sale Agreement for a material non-cash transaction.
4. Material weakness tied specifically to valuation and accounting for the copper asset.
5. Lack of publicly available support for the existence, custody, provenance, and valuation of a $15.8 million inventory asset.
6. Suspicious timing between resale enablement and a near-immediate subsequent GEM equity issuance.

## What Is Not Yet Established

This memorandum does **not** establish any of the following as fact:

- that the copper did not exist
- that Swiss Commodity Re or Malcolm Scott Macintyre sold their shares
- that Kenneth Raymond Deayton was directly controlled by CRML insiders
- that the transaction was definitively a related-party transaction

The concern is that the public disclosure record, taken as a whole, is materially inconsistent, incomplete, and weakly supported for a transaction of this size.

## Suggested Investigative Questions

1. What due diligence did CRML perform on Swiss Commodity Re Limited before describing it as a European family office?
2. Why was Malcolm Scott Macintyre not disclosed as a separate recipient in the initial announcement and later half-year presentation?
3. Why was the Asset Sale Agreement not filed as an exhibit?
4. What documents supported the existence, custody, assay, provenance, and valuation of the copper powder?
5. What board, audit, or management materials addressed the copper valuation and the subsequent material weakness?
6. Were any of the copper-deal shares sold after the registration statement became effective?
