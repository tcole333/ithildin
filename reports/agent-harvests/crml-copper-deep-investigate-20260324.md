The 40kg copper powder transaction between Critical Metals Corp (CRML, NASDAQ) and Swiss Commodity Re Limited, closed December 16, 2025, cannot be verified against any physical reality from any public source. No custody location, chain of custody, assay certificate, warehouse receipt, shipping record, import/customs document, or insurance policy appears in any SEC filing, corporate registry, trade database, or open web source. ## Key Findings

### 1. Physical Non-Existence — Unverifiable
The $15.8M copper powder asset has no disclosed physical address in any public document. The accounting policy acknowledges "its present location" without naming it. EDGAR full-text search of all CRML filings for custody/warehouse/assay/storage/insurance/shipping returned zero results. No trade database record of physical copper movement was found. (Finding #7786)

### 2. The Fraud Template — Exact Match
Financely Group published the copper powder SKR fraud template in July 2025 — four months before the CRML deal. CRML's press release uses the exact price range ($1,500-2,500/gram) documented by Financely as the fraud price range. Real copper powder trades under $15/gram on Bloomberg/LME/Fastmarkets. CRML's own $15.8M valuation ($395/gram) is still 26-65x above the real market maximum for any quantity. (Finding #7789, #7738)

### 3. Equity-Market Innovation of Classic SKR Fraud
The classic copper SKR fraud solicits private lenders who can demand documentation. CRML's variant issues public NASDAQ shares — the "lender" is the public market, which cannot demand a warehouse receipt. Result: Swiss Commodity Re (Deayton) receives $15.8-20M in liquid NASDAQ shares; Macintyre receives $1.58-2M shares as apparent arranger; Jett Capital's established CRML relationship facilitated the deal. (Finding #7789)

### 4. CBIZ Never Opined on the Copper
The half-year financials are entirely unaudited. CBIZ's last opinion covers June 30, 2025 — before the deal. The material weakness disclosure is management-identified, not auditor-identified. CBIZ's 2024 PCAOB inspection found a Long-Lived Asset deficiency (not looking beyond management's analysis) directly relevant to this asset. CBIZ was ratified as CRML's auditor after the copper problem already existed. (Finding #7788, #7775)

### 5. Two-Tier Disclosure Architecture
All marketing claims — "European family office," "G7/EU-origin," "NATO supply capabilities," "$1,500-2,500/gram," "traceable supply chain" — appear only in the press release exhibit (Exhibit 99.1), not in the regulated 6-K form body. The form body states only that CRML "entered into an Asset Sale Agreement with Swiss Commodity RE Limited." This bifurcation limits regulatory exposure while preserving stock-price impact. (Finding #7787, #7779)

### 6. NINGI Timing — Counter-Narrative Trigger
NINGI Research published their CRML short thesis on November 18, 2025 (stock dropped ~10.87%). The copper deal was announced November 21, 2025 — three days later (stock rose ~11.51%). The copper announcement appears to have been a prepared counter-narrative catalyst. (Findings #7737, #7764)

### 7. Deayton's Audit Obstruction Pattern
Deayton's prior entity Sprocket HK withheld financial information from auditors for 1-2 years (2015-2016), directly causing Ubiquity Inc to miss SEC periodic reports, resulting in SEC administrative proceeding 3-17884. Creating HK shells that obstruct auditor verification is structurally consistent with Swiss Commodity Re's role. (Findings #7573, #7766)

### 8. Azulinvest SA — 40kg Minimum Order Match
Azulinvest SA (Geneva, CH-103448.857) sells UHP copper powder with a minimum order of exactly 40kg in "40kg boxes" — precise match to the CRML deal. This identifies a plausible (but unconfirmed) real supply source. If Swiss Commodity Re purchased one 40kg lot from Azulinvest before re-selling to CRML via share issuance, the supply chain would be complete on paper. (Finding #7757)
