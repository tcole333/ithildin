# CRML Copper Transaction Evidence Memo

## Purpose

This memo packages the strongest current evidence concerning Critical Metals Corp's copper powder transaction with Swiss Commodity Re Limited for use in an SEC tip, regulator-facing summary, or tightly framed journalist outreach.

This is not a publication draft and it is not a final fraud conclusion. It is an exhibit-backed statement of what is established, what is strongly suspicious, and what remains unproven.

## Evidence Status Note

Many related findings in `investigation.db` still carry `unverified` status. In this case, that mostly means the second-pass verification workflow has not yet been completed. It does **not** by itself mean the underlying facts are unsupported. The core points below are tied to direct-source exhibits or to clearly labeled analytical synthesis built from those exhibits.

## Bottom Line

The current record strongly supports a disclosure, accounting, and counterparty-opacity case.

It shows that:

- CRML exchanged 2,000,000 shares for 40kg of copper powder in a transaction nominally valued at $20 million.
- The seller described to the market as a "European" multi-generational family office was, in registry records, a Hong Kong private company formed 22 days earlier with USD 100 paid-up capital.
- A second recipient, Malcolm Scott Macintyre, received 200,000 shares under the same agreement but was omitted from the primary disclosure flow for months.
- No public filing discloses where the copper was stored, who had custody, what assay supported it, whether it was insured, or how it was independently valued.
- The controlling Asset Sale Agreement was never filed as an SEC exhibit.
- The resale window for the copper-deal recipients opened on March 3-4, 2026, and CRML then executed a discretionary GEM share settlement on March 5, 2026.

What the current record does **not** prove is that the copper did not exist, that Swiss Commodity Re or Macintyre sold their shares, or that Tony Sage or another CRML insider directly controlled the counterparty.

## Core Factual Record

### 1. The transaction happened, but the valuation shifted immediately

- On November 21, 2025, CRML announced that it had acquired 40kg of "ultra-high-purity" copper powder in exchange for 2,000,000 CRML ordinary shares valued at $20,000,000. Source: Nov. 25, 2025 6-K / press release exhibit, `SEC:0001213900-25-114760`.
- By the December 31, 2025 half-year financials, filed March 13, 2026, the copper was carried at $15.8 million as non-current inventory rather than the announced $20 million figure. Source: half-year 6-K / interim financials, `SEC:0001213900-26-027768`; see also [crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/reports/agent-harvests/crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md).
- Management disclosed a material weakness related to valuation and accounting for the copper powder acquired in the period. Source: same March 13, 2026 filing set, `SEC:0001213900-26-027768`.

### 2. The seller was a newly formed Hong Kong company, not an established European family office

- Swiss Commodity Re Limited was incorporated in Hong Kong on October 30, 2025 as a private company limited by shares. Source: [Companies Registry - Company Particulars.pdf](/Users/travcole/Downloads/Companies%20Registry%20-%20Company%20Particulars.pdf).
- Its registered office was `6/F Wyndham Place, 40-44 Wyndham Street, Central, Hong Kong`. Source: same document.
- Its sole director was Kenneth Raymond Deayton. Source: same document.
- Its company secretary was Hong Kong Corporate Services Group Limited. Source: same document.
- Its sole shareholder at incorporation was International Fiduciaries Limited, holding 100 ordinary shares. Source: same document and [22302564522-NNC1-20260325065606.pdf](/Users/travcole/Downloads/22302564522-NNC1-20260325065606.pdf).
- Its paid-up capital was USD 100. Source: same documents.
- The NNC1 lists the business nature as `Nil` and shows Hong Kong Corporate Services Group as presenter. Source: [22302564522-NNC1-20260325065606.pdf](/Users/travcole/Downloads/22302564522-NNC1-20260325065606.pdf).

### 3. The public description of Swiss Commodity Re conflicts with the registry record

- CRML's press release described Swiss Commodity Re as a "long-only multi-generational European based single family office." Source: `SEC:0001213900-25-114760`.
- The Hong Kong registry documents instead show a private Hong Kong company formed on October 30, 2025 with USD 100 capital, Deayton as first director, and a corporate-services infrastructure at Wyndham Place. Sources: [Companies Registry - Company Particulars.pdf](/Users/travcole/Downloads/Companies%20Registry%20-%20Company%20Particulars.pdf), [22302564522-NNC1-20260325065606.pdf](/Users/travcole/Downloads/22302564522-NNC1-20260325065606.pdf).
- On the current record, the market-facing description and the corporate records cannot both be accurate in the ordinary sense.

### 4. A second share recipient existed and was omitted from the main disclosure flow

- The resale registration documents disclosed that Swiss Commodity Re held 1,800,000 shares and Malcolm Scott Macintyre held 200,000 shares from the same Asset Sale Agreement. Source: F-3 / 424B3 for Reg. No. 333-293656, including `SEC:0001213900-26-023653` and the March 4, 2026 424B3.
- The Nov. 25, 2025 6-K / press release and the Dec. 31, 2025 interim financial presentation both described the transaction as 2,000,000 shares to "the Seller," without naming Macintyre as a separate recipient. Sources: `SEC:0001213900-25-114760`, `SEC:0001213900-26-027768`.
- Macintyre was first surfaced in the F-3 filed on February 23, 2026, roughly 94 days after the ASA date. See [crml-timeline-analysis-20260324.md](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/reports/agent-harvests/crml-timeline-analysis-20260324.md).

### 5. No public custody, assay, shipping, or insurance support has been found

- No filing identified a storage location, warehouse, custodian, assay certificate, insurance policy, shipping record, or customs documentation for the copper. See [crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/reports/agent-harvests/crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md).
- The half-year filing classifies the copper as non-current inventory, not expected to be consumed or processed within 12 months. Source: `SEC:0001213900-26-027768`.
- The Asset Sale Agreement itself has never been filed as an exhibit in any SEC filing reviewed. See [crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/reports/agent-harvests/crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md).

### 6. The March 2026 resale and dilution sequence is suspicious enough to merit review

- The copper F-3 became effective on March 3, 2026 at 4:30 PM ET; the 424B3 followed on March 4, 2026. Source: [crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/reports/agent-harvests/crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md).
- On March 5, 2026, CRML entered the GEM settlement that resulted in 4,153,686 shares being issued to GEM. See findings summarized in [crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/reports/agent-harvests/crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md).
- The reconstructed GEM terms indicate the March 5 settlement was discretionary, not a fixed contractual maturity that had to occur on that date. See [crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/reports/agent-harvests/crml-copper-deep-investigate-20260324.md) and [crml-timeline-analysis-20260324.md](/Users/travcole/projects/osint-research/reports/agent-harvests/crml-timeline-analysis-20260324.md).

## Why This Is Actionable

### A. Potentially false or misleading description of the counterparty

The press-release description of Swiss Commodity Re as a European multi-generational family office is hard to reconcile with the Hong Kong registry record. This is one of the strongest presentable points because it is simple, documented, and legible to a regulator or reporter.

### B. Omission of a material share recipient

Macintyre's 200,000-share allocation was not disclosed in the initial announcement and was still not reflected cleanly in the later half-year presentation, even though the F-3 had already named him. That is a concrete disclosure problem, not just a theory.

### C. Missing support for a large non-cash asset transaction

For a transaction carried at $15.8 million on the balance sheet, the public file set still lacks the most basic supporting documents: ASA, custody location, assay, warehouse receipt, and independent valuation.

### D. Management-admitted control deficiency

CRML itself disclosed a material weakness tied to valuation and accounting for the copper powder. That does not prove fraud, but it materially weakens the credibility of management's handling of the asset and related disclosure judgments.

### E. Suspicious timing around the resale window

The copper-deal resale rights opened on March 3-4, 2026 and were followed almost immediately by the GEM equity settlement on March 5, 2026. That timing does not prove coordination, but it is an intelligible red flag.

## What Is Strongly Suspicious But Still Inferential

- That Swiss Commodity Re was purpose-built as a liquidity vehicle rather than a genuine operating counterparty.
- That Macintyre was functioning as a finder or deal arranger rather than as a commodity owner.
- That the two-tier 6-K / Exhibit 99.1 disclosure structure was used to maximize promotional effect while minimizing verifiable statements in the body of the filing.
- That the March 3-5, 2026 timing was coordinated to provide dilution cover for resale activity.

These points are analytically strong, but they are still inferences from structure and timing rather than admissions or direct documentary proof.

## What We Still Cannot Say

- We cannot prove from public sources that the copper did not exist.
- We cannot prove from public sources where the copper was before or after closing.
- We cannot prove that Swiss Commodity Re or Macintyre sold their shares.
- We cannot prove a direct undisclosed relationship between Deayton and Tony Sage or another CRML insider.
- We cannot identify the true source of the copper lot with confidence.

## Recommended Framing

### For an SEC tip

Frame this as a suspected disclosure and accounting matter with suspicious counterparty opacity:

1. Misleading counterparty description in the Nov. 25, 2025 filing package.
2. Omission or obscuration of Macintyre's separate 200,000-share receipt.
3. Failure to file the controlling Asset Sale Agreement for a material non-cash transaction.
4. Material weakness tied to copper valuation/accounting.
5. No public support for custody, valuation, or physical verification of a $15.8 million inventory asset.
6. Suspicious timing of resale enablement and near-immediate GEM dilution.

### For a journalist

Frame it more narrowly:

"Nasdaq critical-minerals issuer issued 2 million shares for copper powder to a Hong Kong shell formed 22 days earlier, described it as a European family office, omitted a second recipient from primary disclosure, and never filed the contract or any custody support."

That is stronger and safer than claiming outright that the asset was fictitious.

## Decision

The case is compelling enough to do something with now.

The strongest immediate use is an SEC tip built around misdescription, omitted recipient, missing contract/custody support, and material-weakness disclosure. It is also strong enough for targeted journalist outreach if kept at that same level of precision.

It is **not** yet strong enough to present as a fully proved fake-asset-and-dump scheme.
