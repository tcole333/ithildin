# FOIA Submission Channel Verification — DHS Components + CPARS Legal Gate
Verified 2026-07-28 from live official pages (fetch dates = today unless noted). Read-only research; nothing submitted.

**Headline change (affects items 1-4):** Effective **January 22, 2026, DHS no longer accepts hard-copy (mail) or emailed FOIA/Privacy Act requests department-wide.** Verbatim from https://www.dhs.gov/foia-contact-information (Last Updated 07/15/2026): "Effective January 22, 2026, DHS is no longer accepting hard copy (mail) or emailed Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)/Privacy Act requests for DHS records. To ensure timely processing, you must submit your records request through https://www.foia.gov or one of the DHS FOIA portals." Implementing rule: Federal Register, Dec 23, 2025, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/23/2025-23783/privacy-act-of-1974 (updates Appendix A to 6 CFR Part 5 Subpart A). Narrow exception: "In limited circumstances (e.g., incarceration), DHS FOIA public liaisons may facilitate an alternative method for submitting requests if you are unable to submit electronically."

**Portal roster (same page):** DHS Headquarters, CBP, FEMA, ICE, TSA, USCG, USSS -> **SecureRelease(TM)** (https://www.securerelease.us/); USCIS -> its own portal (https://myaccount.uscis.gov / first.uscis.gov); **OIG -> its own PAL portal via Login.gov** (https://foia.oig.dhs.gov/). Caveat: securerelease.us is a **.us domain, not .gov** — its legitimacy is established only by the dhs.gov/ice.gov/cbp.gov links pointing to it.

## 1. ICE FOIA

- **Portal:** SecureRelease, https://www.securerelease.us — in the portal select Agency "Department of Homeland Security" -> Component "U.S. Immigration & Customs Enforcement (ICE)". Verbatim from https://www.ice.gov/foia/submit-request (parent page https://www.ice.gov/foia updated 07/06/2026): "FOIA requests for ICE records must be submitted electronically through the SecureRelease Portal. **ICE does not accept FOIA requests by email or hard copy through the mail.**"
- **Email submission: dead.** The only published emails are contact/liaison, not intake: ICEFOIAPublicLiaison@ice.dhs.gov, (202) 732-2159 (ice.gov/foia); DHS contact page lists icefoiapublicliaison@ice.dhs.gov, 866-633-1182.
- **Sole mail exception — detained/incarcerated requesters only:** Freedom of Information Act Office, U.S. Immigration & Customs Enforcement, 500 12th Street SW, MS 5009, Washington, DC 20536-5009. "Mailed FOIA requests that do not meet the detained/incarcerated exception will not be processed **and will be destroyed**."
- **Status checks:** through the SecureRelease account (portal generates tracking number format `XXXX-ICFO-XXXXX`; "Track the status of your request" is a listed portal function). Per https://www.ice.gov/foia/after-request-submission, releases come "through the SecureRelease portal, or transmitted by email or mail, as appropriate"; rolling/interim releases for large requests.
- **Identity/forms:** first- or third-party requests require ICE Certification of Identity form (https://www.ice.gov/doclib/foia/iceIdentityCert.docx) or equivalent with a **handwritten (non-digital) signature**, notarized **or** signed under a penalty-of-perjury statement (18 U.S.C. 1001 / 5 U.S.C. 552a(i)(3) language quoted on the page); missing certification -> administrative closure. Attorneys: DHS Form G-28. Detailed minor-records rules (under-14 vs 14+ capacity tiers).
- **Fees/waivers:** requester categories (commercial, news media, educational/noncommercial scientific, all other) selected at submission; fee waiver and expedited processing each have dedicated portal checkboxes plus required written justification (public-understanding standard; expedition grounds: imminent threat to life/safety, urgency to inform the public).
- **Appeals:** within 90 days, via SecureRelease (preferred) or in writing to Office of the Principal Legal Advisor, Government Information Law Division, 500 12th Street SW, Stop 5900, Washington, DC 20536-5900 (marked "FOIA Appeal").
- **Caveats:** ICE warns its FOIA office phone number is being spoofed by scammers; ICE FOIA never calls to collect personal info. The DHS contact page's ICE "create request" deep link shares an identical component GUID with the USSS entry (`.../component/47f5988a-05f8-4fe0-ba9d-cbb45c741ba7`) — likely a site linking bug; use the portal root and pick the component manually.

**SUBMIT VIA:** https://www.securerelease.us (DHS -> ICE component); email/mail intake abolished (mail only if detained/incarcerated); status via same portal.

## 2. DHS Headquarters FOIA (Privacy Office / PRIV)

- **Portal:** SecureRelease account — https://www.securerelease.us/account-page/sign-in/ (or route via https://www.foia.gov). Verbatim (dhs.gov/foia-contact-information): "Submit a request to DHS Headquarters (HQ) Privacy Office (PRIV) by creating a SecureRelease account." The Privacy Office is the HQ processor; the page notes "DHS Headquarters includes CISA, CRCL, FPS, I&A, IDENT, and S&T" for intake purposes (HQ offices such as OGC, OPO, PLCY, and the Under Secretary for Management are covered by the HQ/PRIV intake per Appendix A to 6 CFR Part 5 Subpart A, as updated by the Dec 23, 2025 rule above).
- **Old email channel foia@hq.dhs.gov is no longer a submission path** (Jan 22, 2026 cutoff). Questions/liaison: DHSFOIAPublicLiaison@hq.dhs.gov, 202-343-1743 or 866-431-0486. Appeal-status inquiries: 202-343-1743 / FOIA@hq.dhs.gov (contact only, per https://www.dhs.gov/dhs-foia-handbook).
- **Leadership (per contact page):** Roman Jankowski, Chief Privacy Officer/Chief FOIA Officer; Catrina Pavlik-Keenan, Deputy Chief FOIA Officer; Jimmy Wolfrey, Sr. Director FOIA Operations; Gregory Bridges, Public Liaison (Acting).
- **Forms:** DHS suggests DOJ-361 Certification of Identity for non-immigration first-party requests; G-639 for immigration records (-> USCIS). Fee waiver/expedited standards at https://www.dhs.gov/dhs-foia-handbook (Fee Structure and Waivers / Processing subpages).
- **Stale-page warning:** https://www.dhs.gov/submit-freedom-information-act-foia-request (Last Updated 03/15/2022) still says "You may mail, email, or fax your FOIA" — **outdated and contradicted** by the contact page; https://www.dhs.gov/steps-file-foia (Last Updated 01/14/2026) now says "You must submit your request online in most cases."

**SUBMIT VIA:** https://www.securerelease.us/account-page/sign-in/ (DHS Headquarters/PRIV component) or foia.gov; foia@hq.dhs.gov and mail are defunct intake channels as of 2026-01-22.

## 3. DHS Office of Inspector General FOIA

- **Portal:** OIG's own Public Access Link (PAL), https://foia.oig.dhs.gov — **not** SecureRelease. Home page: "This portal is for DHS OIG records only." Account creation requires **Login.gov** (SSO through dhsauthportalext.dhs.gov). dhs.gov contact page: "OIG: Submit your request by creating a login here: Login.gov" / "Submit a request to the DHS Office of the Inspector General (OIG) online," phone 202-981-6100.
- **FOIA.OIG@OIG.DHS.GOV status:** still published on https://www.oig.dhs.gov/foia/request, but **as the FOIA Public Liaison contact** ("Any questions about how to submit a proper FOIA request can be directed to the OIG FOIA Public Liaison. ... OIG Office of Counsel, Phone: 202-981-6100, Email: FOIA.OIG@OIG.DHS.GOV"). Under the Jan 22, 2026 DHS-wide policy it is no longer a valid **submission** channel — treat it as questions-only.
- **Status checks:** sign in to PAL -> Request Status grid; in-portal messaging with the FOIA office; responsive records delivered through PAL downloads.
- **Processing:** three-track system (Track 1 = expedited-standard requests, Track 2 = simple, Track 3 = sensitive/voluminous/multi-consultation), per oig.dhs.gov/foia/request.

**SUBMIT VIA:** https://foia.oig.dhs.gov (PAL, Login.gov required); FOIA.OIG@oig.dhs.gov survives only as liaison/questions contact, not intake.

## 4. CBP FOIA

- **FOIAonline exit:** FOIAonline was decommissioned government-wide Sept 30, 2023; CBP replaced it with **SecureRelease**. Current notice, verbatim from https://www.cbp.gov/site-policy-notices/foia (Last Modified Feb 11, 2026) and repeated on the FAQ (Last Modified Jul 16, 2026): "Effective January 22, 2026, CBP is no longer accepting hard copy (mail/fax/email) Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request for CBP records. To ensure timely processing, you must submit your records request through CBP SecureRelease portal or www.FOIA.gov."
- **Portal:** https://www.securerelease.us (CBP component). FAQ: "You can submit a FOIA Request through SecureRelease." Phone: (202) 325-0150; liaison CBPFOIAPublicLiaison@cbp.dhs.gov (per dhs.gov contact page).
- **Site inconsistency to flag:** https://www.cbp.gov/site-policy-notices/foia/records (also Last Modified Feb 11, 2026) still says "Requests can also be sent in the physical mail to: 1300 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Mail Stop 1181, Washington, DC 20229" — directly contradicting the Jan 22, 2026 notice on the same site; and the FAQ fees answer still references "FOIAonline" (stale copy, including the "agreement to pay fees up to $25" default). Treat the portal as the only dependable channel.
- **Status checks:** via SecureRelease after acknowledgment/tracking number; appeal status also via SecureRelease.
- **Identity/forms:** first-party — DOB, A-number, aliases, plus signed Certification of Identity form **or** perjury statement/notary; third-party/attorney — Form G-28 or other signed (notarized) consent. Travel records only go back to 1982; pre-2000 Border Patrol apprehensions and A-File records are at USCIS (submit there via https://myaccount.uscis.gov), not CBP.
- **Fees:** standard four FOIA fee categories described in FAQ (commercial; news media/educational/noncommercial scientific — first 100 pages free; all other — 100 pages + 2 hours search free).
- **Appeals:** through SecureRelease; if unable, mail within 90 days to U.S. Customs and Border Protection, FOIA Appeals and Policy Branch, 90 K Street NE, Mail Stop 1177, Washington, DC 20229.

**SUBMIT VIA:** https://www.securerelease.us (CBP component) or foia.gov; FOIAonline is dead (Sept 30, 2023) and mail/fax/email intake ended 2026-01-22 (ignore the stale mail address on the /records page).

## 5. DHS Privacy Office — PIA / SORN / PTA publication

- **PIA public inventory:** https://www.dhs.gov/privacy-impact-assessments (Last Updated 06/24/2026) — organized by component (DHS-Wide, CBP, ICE, USCIS, etc.; component subpages like https://www.dhs.gov/privacy-documents-us-customs-and-border-protection, /privacy-documents-department-wide-programs). Page states the Section 208 E-Government Act basis for PIAs.
- **SORN public inventory:** https://www.dhs.gov/system-records-notices-sorns (Last Updated 07/20/2026) — complete DHS/ALL-, component- and government-wide SORN list with Federal Register citations and Privacy Act exemption final rules per system (e.g., DHS/ALL-001 FOIA/PA Record System, 79 FR 6609).
- **PTAs are NOT published as an inventory — FOIA them.** Verbatim from https://www.dhs.gov/publication/privacy-threshold-analysis (Last Updated 04/11/2025): "The Privacy Threshold Analysis (PTA) is an **internal DHS questionnaire**..." used to determine whether a PIA/SORN is required. The privacy compliance cycle (PTA -> PIA -> SORN -> periodic review) is described at https://www.dhs.gov/privacy-compliance, with no PTA repository. Precedent that FOIA works: CBP posts individual FOIA-released PTAs in its FOIA records library, e.g. https://www.cbp.gov/document/foia-record/privacy-threshold-analysis-pta.

**SUBMIT VIA:** n/a — PIAs at dhs.gov/privacy-impact-assessments and SORNs at dhs.gov/system-records-notices-sorns are already public; PTAs must be FOIAed (HQ/PRIV or the owning component via SecureRelease).

## 6. Legal gate: FAR 42.1503 and CPARS releasability

- **Correction to the premise:** the non-release rule is in **FAR 42.1503(d)**, not (h). Verbatim from https://www.acquisition.gov/far/42.1503: "These evaluations may be used to support future award decisions, and should therefore be marked 'Source Selection Information'. ... **The completed evaluation shall not be released to other than Government personnel and the contractor whose performance is being evaluated during the period the information may be used to provide source selection information.** Disclosure of such information could cause harm both to the commercial interest of the Government and to the competitive position of the contractor being evaluated as well as impede the efficiency of Government operations."
- **What (h) actually says:** 42.1503(h) covers FAPIIS "other contractor performance information" (defective pricing, terminations for default/cause, trafficking violations, unjustified small-business payment history). **(h)(2)** verbatim: such FAPIIS postings do "not constitute a 'past performance review,' which would be **exempted from public availability in accordance with section 3010 of the Supplemental Appropriations Act, 2010 (Pub. L. 111-212)**. Therefore, all such information posted in FAPIIS will be publicly available, unless covered by a disclosure exemption under the Freedom of Information Act (see 9.105-2(b)(2))." (h)(4) sends FOIA-exemption handling to FAR 9.105-2(b)(2)(iv). So: integrity items = public; **past performance reviews = statutorily excluded from public availability**.
- Related: 42.1503(f) requires access controls "in accordance with 42.1503(d)" (CPARS at https://www.cpars.gov); 42.1503(g) sets the use window — evaluations "within three years (six for construction and architect-engineer contracts) of the completion of performance."
- **How agencies treat CPARS in FOIA:** the official CPARS Guidance (https://www.cpars.gov/cparsweb/assets/documents/CPARS-Guidance.pdf, sec. 6.3) states: "Contractor performance information is privileged source selection information. ... the completed evaluation shall not be released to other than Government personnel and the contractor whose performance is being evaluated. **This information is not releasable under the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA)**." Sec. 6.2.2: disclosure to any other contractor "or other entities outside the Government, is strictly prohibited"; evaluations are marked "FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY/SOURCE SELECTION INFORMATION - see FAR 2.101, 3.104, and 42.1503." In practice agencies deny third-party CPARS requests citing the source-selection bar (FAR 2.101/3.104; Procurement Integrity Act, 41 U.S.C. 2102, the successor to 41 U.S.C. 423) — typically invoked as FOIA Exemption 3 and/or Exemption 5, with Exemption 4 for embedded contractor proprietary data [inference from the above primary texts; individual denial letters not pulled in this pass].
- **Practical gate:** a FOIA request to ICE/CBP/DHS for a contractor's CPARS evaluations should be expected to fail during the 3-year (6-year construction/A-E) source-selection use window; FAPIIS integrity records and the contract file's non-CPARS past-performance documentation are the obtainable alternatives.

**SUBMIT VIA:** n/a — legal check. FAR 42.1503(d) + (h)(2) (acquisition.gov) bar release of CPARS evaluations outside Government/the evaluated contractor; plan FOIAs around it, not through it.

## Processing caveats (all components)

- DHS remains the highest-volume FOIA department; per MuckRock's July 1, 2026 analysis of foia.gov data, DHS entered FY2025 with 221,068 pending requests and a total workload over 1.2M, and had not yet published its FY2025 annual FOIA report (secondary source: https://www.muckrock.com/news/archives/2026/jul/01/navigating-foia-in-2026-new-data-shows-agencies-adapting-to-record-demand/; verify against https://www.foia.gov/data.html when DHS posts FY2025 data).
- The Jan 22, 2026 electronic-only mandate is uniformly stamped on every component entry of dhs.gov/foia-contact-information; expect legacy email/mail addresses found in older guides (e.g., foia@hq.dhs.gov, ICE-FOIA@ice.dhs.gov, CBP mail stop) to be dead or destroy-on-receipt for intake.
- ICE explicitly destroys non-qualifying mailed requests; CBP says mailed requests "divert resources" (stale page) — both push everything to SecureRelease.
- OIG is the odd one out (PAL + Login.gov, not SecureRelease); USCIS is the other (its own myaccount.uscis.gov portal) — relevant when a request spans components (A-Files -> USCIS, not CBP/ICE).
