## Executive Summary

This memo documents a pattern in which a Cyprus entity called Oscrivia Limited — the corporate secretary of Alexei Mordashov's master holding company — took security interests on US patents at companies backed by a Moscow private equity firm tied to Mordashov, then transferred those patents to Russian entities when the companies defaulted. The Russian receiving entities are all chaired by the same individual: Vladimir Yakushev, the PE firm's Managing Partner and former COO of Mordashov's Severstal Resources.

Three confirmed cases are documented below, involving at least 24 US patents in photonics, spectroscopy, and optical computing — fields with dual-use applications. All transfers occurred between 2015 and 2020, years before Mordashov was sanctioned in February 2022. This was not sanctions evasion. It was technology acquisition through opaque but then-legal corporate structures. However, the same infrastructure (Oscrivia/Unifirm) was later used for what ICIJ has documented as sanctions evasion related to Mordashov's TUI stake.

ICIJ's Cyprus Confidential investigation (2023) documented Mordashov's use of the Unifirm/Oscrivia/PwC Cyprus apparatus for financial sanctions evasion. This memo extends that reporting to show the same entity simultaneously operated a technology extraction pipeline — a dimension not covered in existing journalism.

Every factual claim in this memo is verifiable through publicly available records. Verification sources are provided inline and consolidated at the end.

## II. The Mechanism

The pattern observed across three companies follows a consistent sequence:

1. S-Group Capital Management invests in a US photonics or advanced materials company, often founded by Russian-born scientists.
2. S-Group places board members — typically Vladimir Yakushev and/or Aidar Gabidullin — on the company's board.
3. Oscrivia Limited takes a security interest on the company's US patents via filings with the USPTO.
4. The company defaults on its obligations.
5. Patents are transferred — either directly to a Russian OOO entity or through intermediary shell/nominee entities.
6. All Russian receiving entities are chaired by Vladimir Yakushev.

[ANALYTICAL INFERENCE — The mechanism is inferred from the consistency of the pattern across three independent companies. Each individual step is documented in public records. The inference is that this represents a systematic pipeline rather than three coincidental outcomes.]

**Statistical anomaly:** Analysis of 11.8 million USPTO patent assignment records (1970–2024) reveals the pipeline is a statistical outlier across multiple dimensions:

- **Cyprus security interests:** Only 6 Cyprus entities have ever held security interests on US patents. Two of them are Oscrivia Limited. No other Cyprus entity has used this mechanism more than once.
- **Offshore foreclosures:** Only 8 patent foreclosures in the database went to non-US entities. Only one — the Readsboro/Centice case — involved a Cyprus entity. The use of strict foreclosure by a Cyprus shell company to seize US patents has zero precedent.
- **Pattern match:** Zero additional cases match the full pattern (offshore security interest → offshore strict foreclosure → Russian OOO entity transfer). This is not a common financial practice — it is a specific, singular operation.

[CONFIRMED — USPTO bulk patent assignment database, 11.8 million records analyzed]

## III. Confirmed Cases

### Case 1: Centice Corporation (Morrisville, NC)

**Technology:** Raman spectroscopy and pharmaceutical verification instruments, including the MFL-3000 drug identification system used by law enforcement. [CONFIRMED — USPTO patent records, company filings]

**Origin:** Duke University spinoff, originally incorporated as Optopo Inc. in 2003. [CONFIRMED — North Carolina Secretary of State, USPTO assignment records]

**Federal funding:** $2.3 million in NIH SBIR awards across 4 grants (2005-2008), with a 100% Phase I-to-Phase II conversion rate. Additionally, a $12,000 Navy procurement contract. [CONFIRMED — SBIR.gov, USASpending.gov]

**S-Group investment:** $9.86 million Series E preferred stock (March 2013), plus an earlier bridge round (2009). [CONFIRMED — SEC EDGAR, CIK 1415617, Form D filings]

**Board composition:** Vladimir Yakushev (S-Group), Aidar Gabidullin (S-Group). Gabidullin's SEC filing address is listed as "c/o Oscrivia Ltd, Attn: Directors" in Limassol, Cyprus — directly linking S-Group's board representative to Mordashov's corporate secretary. [CONFIRMED — SEC EDGAR Form D]

Also on the board: Serguei Kouzmine, co-founder of QWave Capital, which invested $7 million in Centice in June 2013 (three months after S-Group's Series E). Spanish prosecutors identified Kouzmine as the leader of the Tambovskaya-Malyshevskaya organized crime group; he was tried in absentia in Operation Troika (2008) and has an outstanding Spanish arrest warrant. Kouzmine co-owned Bank Rossiya with Putin associate Gennady Petrov. Frank Creer, Managing Director of QWave Capital and former DFJ Capital officer, also served on the board. [CONFIRMED — SEC EDGAR, TBCA Archives (Operation Troika indictment), Crunchbase]

**Patent extraction sequence:**

- June 2008: Vogen Funding, L.P. — the third fund of CapX Partners (Chicago-based SBIC, SBA License 05/05-0292, principals Jeffry Pfeffer and James Hallene) — took security interest on Centice patents. Vogen was an independent US equipment finance lender with no S-Group connection. [CONFIRMED — USPTO Reel 21194/475, Delaware Corp #3923599, SBA SBIC database]
- February 2012: Fulcrum Financial Partners, L.P. (independent Raleigh, NC family office, principal Paul Priu) took new security interest covering all 9 Centice patents. [CONFIRMED — USPTO Reel 28026/275]
- February 2012: Vogen released its security interest on Centice patents. [CONFIRMED — USPTO Reel 28052/668]
- February 2013: Joint release of security interests by "Fulcrum Financial Partners, L.P. AND Oscrivia Limited." This confirms Oscrivia also held a security interest on Centice patents alongside the independent US co-lender. [CONFIRMED — USPTO Reel 30410/175]
- August 2016: 7 patents transferred via "strict foreclosure resulting from promissory note default" to Readsboro International Corporation. Correspondent attorney: F. Michael Sajovec (Williams Mullen, Raleigh NC). [CONFIRMED — USPTO Reel 41190/466]
- December 2020: The same 7 patents transferred from Readsboro to ASB International Services Ltd (Cyprus, HE385193). Correspondent: Williams Mullen (same firm). [CONFIRMED — USPTO Reel 55638/474]

**Note:** The promissory note that Readsboro foreclosed on is undocumented in USPTO records. All previously known security interests (Vogen, Fulcrum, Oscrivia) were released by February 2013, three years before the August 2016 foreclosure. This implies an additional lending transaction occurred between 2013 and 2016 that is not reflected in the patent assignment records. [ANALYTICAL INFERENCE]

**Readsboro International Corporation** is registered in **Larnaca, Cyprus** — not a US entity as initially assumed. The address is confirmed from the USPTO patent assignment bulk data (assignee city/country on reel 41190/466). No corporate registration was found in any US state or on OpenCorporates. Zero web presence. "Readsboro" is a town in Vermont (population ~800) — the use of a quaint American place name for a Cyprus shell entity is a deliberate misdirection. The pipeline now involves three different Cyprus cities: Limassol (Oscrivia), Larnaca (Readsboro), and Nicosia (ASB) — using different service providers at each location to create opacity through jurisdictional separation within a single country. [CONFIRMED — USPTO bulk assignment data, OpenCorporates, web search]

**ASB International Services Ltd** (HE385193) was incorporated June 14, 2018 in Cyprus, at Prevezis 12, 1065 Nicosia. Its sole director and secretary is DGH Services Ltd (HE369802), a nominee company run by Demetrios Chalios. DGH Services manages approximately 50 entities. A related entity, DGH Nominees Ltd (HE389315), was established October 2018. The beneficial owner of ASB International is unknown from public records. Note: ASB's service provider (DGH/Nicosia) is different from Oscrivia's (Unifirm/Limassol), adding a layer of separation in the pipeline. [CONFIRMED — Cyprus Registry]

**Outcome:** 7 patents extracted. Current holder: ASB International Services Ltd (Cyprus). Centice Corporation is dead after raising approximately $40 million over 15 funding rounds. [CONFIRMED — USPTO, Crunchbase]

**How to verify:** Search USPTO Patent Assignment database (assignment.uspto.gov) for "Centice Corporation" as assignor. Search SEC EDGAR for CIK 1415617. Search SBIR.gov for "Centice Corporation."

## IV. Cases Where the Pattern Did Not Apply

Three S-Group portfolio companies with significant patent portfolios were not subject to extraction. These cases are important for understanding the boundaries of the pattern.

- **Innolume GmbH** (Germany, 19 patents): The company remains active. No patent extraction occurred. [CONFIRMED] German jurisdiction may provide stronger IP protections than US state-level corporate law. [ANALYTICAL INFERENCE]
- **Advanced Diamond Technologies** (Romeoville, IL, 14 patents): Acquired by John Crane Inc., a legitimate UK-based buyer, in 2019 — before any extraction could occur. [CONFIRMED — press releases, USPTO]
- **FiberZone Networks** (Israel, 5 patents): Liquidated under Israeli court supervision. Judicial oversight over the liquidation process prevented opaque transfer of assets. [CONFIRMED — Israeli court records]

[ANALYTICAL INFERENCE — The pattern specifically failed where: (a) a legitimate buyer intervened before default, (b) judicial oversight governed the liquidation process, or (c) the company operated in a jurisdiction with stronger IP protections. This suggests the extraction mechanism depended on the relative weakness of US patent security enforcement and the absence of independent oversight.]

## VIII. Limitations and Caveats

1. **Beneficial ownership of ASB International Services Ltd is unknown.** The nominee director structure (DGH Services Ltd / Demetrios Halios) obscures the ultimate owner of the 7 Centice patents. We cannot confirm these patents are currently controlled by Mordashov or any Mordashov-linked entity — only that they reached ASB International through the documented chain.

2. **Readsboro International Corporation could not be located in any corporate registry.** This does not prove it is fictitious — it may be registered in a jurisdiction we did not search, or under a slightly different name. However, its complete absence from OpenCorporates and all 50 US state registries is unusual for a patent-holding entity.

3. **The "systematic pipeline" characterization is an analytical inference.** Each individual transfer is documented. The conclusion that these transfers represent a coordinated extraction mechanism — rather than three independent commercial outcomes — is based on the consistent involvement of Oscrivia, Yakushev, and the same corporate structure. Journalists should independently evaluate whether the pattern supports this characterization.

4. **Russian OOO corporate registry data has limited accessibility.** Confirmation that Yakushev chairs OOO Spectralus and OOO Nanooptic Devices is based on available Russian registry data, which may be incomplete or outdated.

5. **Mordashov's direct knowledge of or involvement in the patent transfers is not established.** The connection runs through corporate infrastructure (Oscrivia/Unifirm) that is documented as his, but we have no evidence of direct instructions or personal involvement in the patent extraction decisions.

6. **The characterization of Kouzmine as an organized crime leader comes from Spanish prosecutors.** He was tried in absentia and may dispute these characterizations. His QWave Capital co-investment in Centice is a confirmed fact; his criminal background is sourced from judicial proceedings.

7. **Federal funding amounts for Centice ($2.3M NIH SBIR) are confirmed, but no evidence was found that specific terms of the SBIR awards prohibited or restricted IP transfer.** The policy implications of federally funded IP being extracted through this mechanism would require legal analysis beyond the scope of this memo.

## IX. Questions for Further Investigation

1. **Who is the beneficial owner of ASB International Services Ltd (HE385193)?** The 7 Centice patents currently reside here. Obtaining the ultimate beneficial ownership — potentially through Cyprus corporate filings, journalistic inquiry, or further leaked document searches — would determine whether this IP is currently under Mordashov-linked or Russian state-adjacent control.

2. **What is Readsboro International Corporation?** Where is it registered? Who controls it? Its role as an intermediary entity in the Centice patent chain — appearing in 2016 and disappearing by 2020 — is unexplained.

3. **Are there additional S-Group portfolio companies where this pattern applies?** S-Group's full portfolio is not publicly documented. Additional cases may exist in companies that did not file with the SEC or that held trade secrets rather than patents.

4. **What are the dual-use implications of the extracted technologies?** Raman spectroscopy (Centice), compact solid-state lasers (Spectralus), and holographic optical computing (Nano-Optic Devices) all have potential defense and intelligence applications. Were any of these technologies subject to export control review?

5. **Did CFIUS review any of these transactions?** The Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States reviews foreign acquisitions of US companies with national security implications. It is unclear whether the security interest/foreclosure mechanism would trigger CFIUS jurisdiction, particularly when the initial investment (S-Group) predated the 2018 FIRRMA reforms.

6. **What is the current status of Fulcrum Financial Partners (Raleigh, NC)?** Fulcrum co-held the Oscrivia security interest on Centice patents. Its role — independent co-lender or coordinated participant — is unclear.

7. **Did NIH SBIR award terms include IP disposition clauses?** If Centice's federally funded patents are now held by a Cyprus nominee entity, this may represent a gap in federal IP protection for SBIR-funded companies.

8. **What is the relationship between QWave Capital and S-Group?** QWave invested in Centice three months after S-Group. Kouzmine (QWave) and Yakushev (S-Group) both sat on the Centice board. The degree of coordination between these two entities warrants further examination.
