# SoftBank caper: Shearman investigation and preserved-evidence repository map

Lead: `#51835`  
Research date: 2026-07-09  
Scope: lawful public repositories for the SoftBank board investigations into the Arora/Sama campaign, with specific tests for Shearman workpapers, tasking, payers, devices, K2/Kroll, Powerscourt, Boies Schiller, CH Communication, Mintz & Gold, Epstein, Weingarten, Brad Karp, and the January 2019 `DB` shorthand.

## Bottom line

No publicly accessible Shearman workpaper, privilege log, interview list, custodian index, device image, vendor invoice, payer ledger, or final report surfaced. The public record does, however, establish a more precise engagement chronology, identifies the likely institutional custodians, and exposes several repositories that preserve source material or adjacent Benedetti records.

The strongest repository hierarchy is:

1. **SoftBank special-committee / corporate-secretary files** — highest relevance, not public.
2. **A&O Shearman legacy Shearman matter file** — highest relevance, presumptively privileged and not public.
3. **Kroll engagement file** — high relevance to identifying the campaign's sources; not public.
4. **Geneva prosecutor files** — potentially contain Kroll-derived evidence and reciprocal complaints; dispositions and files are non-public.
5. **SEC Rule 14a-8 file** — public and already retrieved; preserves 51 pages of campaign-side documents but no Shearman workpaper.
6. **Benedetti/Schwarzfield v. EDF/EDEV civil record** — an adjacent, high-volume Benedetti repository with 92 exhibits; no SoftBank link is visible in the published decisions.

The Epstein/Weingarten hypothesis remains live as a hypothesis, but this lead found no Shearman-mediated bridge. Epstein's known 2014 Shearman contact was described contemporaneously as an international trusts-and-estates lawyer, and exact cross-corpus pair searches produced no SoftBank/Misra/Benedetti/Karp/Weingarten linkage.

## Distinct investigations and engagements

The public record does **not** support describing this as one “2020 SoftBank probe.” At least four strands must be kept separate:

| Window | Client / forum | Counsel or vendor | Publicly stated scope and result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feb.–20 Jun. 2016 | Special Committee of independent SoftBank Group board members | Shearman & Sterling LLP and Anderson Mori & Tomotsune | Reviewed allegations about Nikesh Arora raised in letters from counsel claiming to represent unidentified SoftBank/Sprint shareholders. SoftBank announced the claims were “without merit.” |
| By 19 May 2017 | SoftBank | Morrison & Foerster LLP | A “second, more recent investigation” into the allegations reached the same no-wrongdoing conclusion, according to SoftBank as reported by WSJ/Dow Jones. This appears to be a merits review distinct from source attribution. |
| By 19 May 2017 | SoftBank | Kroll Inc. | Hired to identify Giannakopoulos's backers and who else was behind the campaign. This is the earliest publicly named source-attribution vendor. |
| Disclosed 27–28 Mar. 2018; ended 2019 | Special Committee of the SoftBank Group board | Reported as led by Shearman & Sterling | Investigated the campaign and possible links between Alessandro Benedetti and a SoftBank insider. SoftBank's contemporaneous statement said the committee had reached no conclusions. Later reporting says SoftBank ended the investigation in 2019 without resolving the matter. |

## Custodian map

### Confirmed institutional custodians

- **SoftBank Group special committee(s):** board resolutions, committee charters, minutes, counsel instructions, reports, interview approvals, and final disposition.
- **SoftBank Group corporate secretary / legal function:** retention letters, matter numbers, invoices, preservation notices, vendor approvals, board distributions, and regulator responses.
- **Shearman & Sterling, now A&O Shearman:** conflicts intake, retention letters, billing narratives, matter logs, interview memoranda, collection and review logs, and any final oral or written presentation. No individual Shearman lawyer was named in the public sources reviewed.
- **Kroll:** source-attribution work, Giannakopoulos/CH Communication research, and any material transmitted to SoftBank or Swiss counsel.
- **Morrison & Foerster:** the later merits review described in May 2017 reporting.
- **Geneva prosecutor:** the reciprocal 2016 complaints by Giannakopoulos and SoftBank/Sama, if still retained under Swiss criminal-file rules.

### Probabilistic board custodians

The committee members are not named in the public releases. Official SoftBank shareholder materials permit a ranked candidate list rather than a dismissal of the issue:

- **2016 review:** Tadashi Yanai, Mark Schwartz, and Shigenobu Nagamori were the directors identified as external and independent in the FY2015/36th AGM materials. They are the strongest candidates for the committee pool, not confirmed committee members.
- **March 2018 source investigation:** Tadashi Yanai, Mark Schwartz, and Yasir O. Al-Rumayyan were identified as external/independent directors in the FY2017 materials. Shigenobu Nagamori had resigned on 30 September 2017. Masami Iijima joined as an external director on 20 June 2018 and is a possible later-phase custodian, but not a March 2018 founding member.
- **Ronald D. Fisher:** a board member quoted in May 2017 about the campaign and the compensation review, but not identified as an independent external director in those materials. He is a likely fact witness or board-record custodian, not a confirmed special-committee member.

## Ranked repositories

### 1. SoftBank and special-committee files — highest relevance, inaccessible publicly

Likely objects include committee charters, board resolutions, counsel retention letters, interview lists, custodial notices, collection reports, vendor bills, Kroll deliverables, presentations, and closure memoranda. Neither the 2016 nor 2018 SoftBank statement attached a report. Searches of SoftBank annual reports and AGM materials for 2016–2020 produced no reference to Shearman, Benedetti, Giannakopoulos, Kroll, the smear campaign, or a special-committee report.

The likely privilege holder may be the special committee rather than SoftBank management. That distinction matters because the 2018 inquiry was expressly testing a possible SoftBank insider.

### 2. A&O Shearman legacy file — highest relevance, privileged

The matter file is the best path to:

- client identity and privilege holder;
- exact matter number and dates;
- engagement partner and team;
- interviewees and custodians;
- device or mailbox collections;
- Kroll or other vendor coordination;
- billing narratives;
- final written or oral disposition; and
- references to Paul Weiss, Brad Karp, Deutsche Bank, David Boies, `DB`, K2, Powerscourt, CH Communication, Mintz & Gold, Weingarten, or Epstein.

No public docket or filing reviewed compelled production of such a file. The absence is consistent with ordinary attorney-client and work-product protection, not proof that the records do not exist.

### 3. Kroll file — high relevance to source attribution

The 19 May 2017 report states that SoftBank hired Kroll “to unmask any backers” of Giannakopoulos and identify who else might be behind the campaign. Kroll and K2 Intelligence must not be conflated:

- **K2 Intelligence** was reportedly hired by Benedetti in 2015 to investigate Arora.
- **Kroll** was reportedly hired by SoftBank by May 2017 to investigate the campaign's source and backers.

No public Kroll workpaper, invoice, declaration, or later-court exhibit surfaced.

### 4. Geneva prosecutor files — high relevance, non-public

Two reciprocal complaints were reported in 2016: Giannakopoulos against Sama/Arora concerning alleged hacking, and SoftBank/Sama against Giannakopoulos for defamation. The files could contain Kroll findings, attack logs, communications, source documents, and party declarations. No public docket number, disposition, privilege log, or exhibit index surfaced; Swiss prosecutor confidentiality remains the access barrier.

### 5. SEC Rule 14a-8 file — public, campaign-side source documents

The 51-page file is the best public preserved artifact from the campaign itself:

<https://www.sec.gov/divisions/corpfin/cf-noaction/14a-8/2016/chcommunication050416-14a8.pdf>

It contains Sprint correspondence, Giannakopoulos emails, CH Communication's proposal, address and Gmail account, UBP ownership evidence, and the SEC no-action response. It does not contain Shearman workpapers. Findings `#11809`–`#11811` already capture the file, so this lead did not duplicate them.

The separate complaints reportedly sent to the SEC and India's Enforcement Directorate did not surface in public regulatory files. Complaint and tip confidentiality makes those low-probability public-access paths, though index-level FOIA or regulator correspondence searches could still be tried.

### 6. Benedetti/Schwarzfield v. EDF/EDEV — adjacent high-volume repository

The following litigation preserves a substantial Benedetti record from the years immediately preceding the SoftBank campaign:

- Tribunal de commerce de Paris, RG `15/17205`, complaint served 11 February 2015; interlocutory ruling 22 September 2016; merits judgment 9 October 2019.
- Cour d'appel de Paris, RG `19/21157` and `20/00733`, joined 15 December 2020; decision 1 June 2021, Portalis `35L7-V-B7D-CBAAL`.
- Cour de cassation, pourvoi `21-23.407`, rejected 8 March 2023, ECLI `FR:CCASS:2023:CO10165`.

The appellate decision says 92 exhibits were before the court and quotes or summarizes:

- Benedetti's EDF assistance-and-advice contract;
- contract payment terms and bank-payment condition;
- emails from July–November 2013;
- meetings and witness requests;
- the Edison, Subbotina, and Matera projects;
- a COGEED expert report; and
- the parties' competing expense and compensation claims.

Public decisions:

- Cour d'appel text: <https://app.livv.eu/decisions/LawLex202400008022JBJ>
- Cour de cassation: <https://www.courdecassation.fr/decision/64085c5366b1bafb02f12048>
- Pappers mirror with the attached cassation arguments: <https://justice.pappers.fr/decision/cb5bed72492213e0882d0530d951be44fef93946>

The 30 September 2024 Paris criminal judgment, case `16246000066`, adds that Benedetti told investigators on 6 September 2022 that he had supplied numerous reports to EDF and that they were annexed to the civil proceeding. It also identifies the contract as exhibit `D10`, the Court of Accounts report as `D3`, and the 2022 interview as a mutual-legal-assistance examination:

<https://www.leclubdesjuristes.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Jugement-EDF.PDF.pdf>

No published decision mentions SoftBank, Misra, Arora, Sama, K2, Powerscourt, Epstein, or Weingarten. The repository is important because it may preserve Benedetti's working methods and communications, not because a SoftBank bridge is established.

### 7. Zalmanoff v. Hardy — low-value caption hit

`Zalmanoff v. Hardy`, Del. Ch. C.A. No. `12912-VCS`, named Alessandro Benedetti and Bertrand des Pallières in the caption. Discovery closed, but the public Chancery opinion and Delaware Supreme Court briefs use Broadridge/Computershare affidavits, SEC filings, and a Measley affidavit; Benedetti and des Pallières do not appear substantively. The full Chancery docket is available through paid File & ServeXpress access, but the current evidence does not justify paid retrieval for this lead.

Public opinion: <https://www.morrisjames.com/assets/htmldocuments/Zalmanoff%20v%20Hardy%20et.pdf>

## Epstein, Weingarten, Brad Karp, and `DB`

Epstein's calendars show a 22 September 2014 appointment with C. Jones Perry Jr. of Shearman. Separate correspondence in `EFTA02518792` has Alan Halperin describe Perry to Epstein as a trusts-and-estates lawyer who did substantial international work. `EFTA02098482` confirms scheduling but gives no meeting subject.

This context lowers the probability that the known Perry meeting concerned investigations, but it does not prove what the meeting was about. It should remain a ranked, unresolved contact—not be treated as irrelevant.

Exact pair searches across DOJ volume 11, LMSBAND, the unified corpus, House Oversight 20K, and Kabasshouse found no document combining:

- Shearman + SoftBank;
- Shearman + Misra;
- Shearman + Benedetti;
- Shearman + Brad Karp;
- Shearman + Weingarten; or
- Kroll + SoftBank.

The one Kabasshouse `Weingarten Shearman` result was a false co-occurrence in `EFTA00044963`: Weingarten appeared in a news clipping about Bernard Ebbers, while Shearman appeared thousands of lines away in an unrelated party/counsel list.

No Shearman or public-docket artifact resolved the 25 January 2019 `DB` shorthand. Deutsche Bank and David Boies remain live candidate readings; the lack of a workpaper hit is not a basis to mark either interpretation irrelevant.
