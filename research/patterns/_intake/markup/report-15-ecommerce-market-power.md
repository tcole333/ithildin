# The Markup Evidence Ontology — Cluster 15: E-commerce, Advertising & Market Power

Compiled July 29, 2026. Seven substantive evidence units from the census's 14-page C7 cluster, covering the two bounded series [Banned Bounty](https://themarkup.org/series/banned-bounty) and [Amazon's Advantage](https://themarkup.org/series/amazons-advantage).

## Scope and attribution

The 14-page census consists of four original investigations, one reporting follow-up on Amazon's moderation workforce, one public-facing detector, one methodology page for each series, a quiz, an award notice, and four legislative/regulatory impact updates. This report treats the method pages, quiz, detector, award notice, and impact updates as evidence attached to the underlying investigations rather than as seven additional investigations. Two impact stories are retained as distinct entries because the brief specifically calls for antitrust follow-through and because their evidence acquisition—official-document tracing—is different from the marketplace experiments.

All pages below are pre-merger, first-party `themarkup.org` work with no stated co-publishing newsroom. Under the census's domain-structural attribution rule, they are The Markup originals; no separately canonical partner copy is added. The Amazon's Advantage series won the [2022 Gerald Loeb Award for personal finance and consumer reporting](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2022/10/06/the-markup-wins-loeb-award-for-amazons-advantage). No Banned Bounty-specific award or co-publisher was identified on the [series page](https://themarkup.org/series/banned-bounty) or The Markup's [awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).

“Runnable today” below means that an outside investigator can obtain fresh decisive inputs through the same core public path as of July 29, 2026. It does **not** mean that a vanished 2020 listing can be recreated. “Degraded today” means the historical analysis can still be reproduced, but fresh acquisition needs significant collector repair, authenticated/gated data, or countermeasure handling.

### Amazon's Enforcement Failures Leave Open a Back Door to Banned Goods—Some Sold and Shipped by Amazon Itself (2020) — Amazon's own rules, search, recommendations, warehouses, and seller tools converged on prohibited goods

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/06/18/amazons-enforcement-failures-leave-open-a-back-door); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no co-publisher or story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Reporters converted Amazon's nearly 2,000 prohibited items across 38 rule categories into a 279-item searchable test list. They documented **97 listings representing 57 prohibited item types**: 36 weapons-related listings, 31 drug/paraphernalia listings, 10 lock-picking/theft listings, 12 hazardous-item listings, and eight surveillance listings. Each candidate was independently checked by both reporters; two weapons experts checked the gun items. ([method and category totals](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com))
  - At least **39 listings shipped from Amazon warehouses**, at least eight carried “Amazon's Choice,” and five said “ships from and sold by Amazon.com.” The sample included pill presses and dies, AR-15 parts, lock-picking tools, hidden recorders, bongs disguised as vases, and an AR-15 vise block disguised as a “paperweight desk organizer.” ([findings](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/06/18/amazons-enforcement-failures-leave-open-a-back-door); [methodology totals](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com))
  - In a controlled seller test, reporters successfully posted an AR-15 armorer's wrench and 10-round magazine from a new account after earlier precise descriptions were rejected. A purchased UPC, omitted keywords, and miscategorization bypassed the filters; the reporters removed both listings within minutes. Amazon's seller tool separately suggested classifying a bong as a vase. ([controlled test](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com))
  - The article connected the *type* of pill press then prohibited by Amazon to a prior Tennessee counterfeit-opioid operation: Eric Falkowski said he had bought two presses and dies on Amazon; officials and records tied his fentanyl-laced pills to two deaths and more than 20 nonfatal overdoses. Amazon said its policy had allowed pill presses when Falkowski bought them in 2016, so this is a harm-chain illustration, not proof that those historical sales violated the later rule. ([harm chain and policy caveat](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/06/18/amazons-enforcement-failures-leave-open-a-back-door))
- **Finding type(s)**: **policy-enforcement gap** (new tag: instances explicitly barred by an institution's own rulebook remain available); **platform-aided circumvention** (new tag: the platform's discovery or submission system helps users evade another platform control); **harm-chain linkage** (new tag: a present control failure is tied, with a carefully bounded causal claim, to documented downstream harm from the same product or mechanism); **first-party complicity signal** (new tag: the platform is seller, warehouse, recommender, or badge-granter—not merely host).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Platform rulebook** — Amazon Seller Central's restricted-product pages, archived as timestamped policy screenshots; selected only rules that were clear, specific, and visually distinguishable. Amazon still directs sellers to a restricted-products policy, but the current help shell requires JavaScript/login for category text. ([selection criteria](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com); [current official policy shell](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/200164330); [current Amazon policy overview](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/selling-policies))
  - **Constructed marketplace corpus** — hundreds of manual Amazon and Google site-restricted searches in February and April 2020 using formal names, slang, misspellings, brands, and synonyms; listing pages, reviews, photos, search suggestions, recommendation modules, and fulfillment labels were captured by ASIN and timestamp. ([search protocol](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com))
  - **Controlled adversarial submission** — a new personal seller account, legally sellable items, manufacturer photos, a purchased UPC, altered language, and false categories; the items were removed immediately after confirming publication. ([test protocol](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com))
  - **External classification references** — federal drug-paraphernalia law, DEA product descriptions, WADA/USADA substance status, weapons experts, manufacturers, YouTube demonstrations, and specialist retailers. The investigators excluded candidates needing lab tests, nonpublic government information, or detailed physical inspection. ([classification rules](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com))
  - **Harm and organizational records** — federal court records, law-enforcement documents, autopsy reports, interviews with Falkowski and affected family members, current/former Amazon safety staff, a seller, and Amazon's written response. ([main story](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/06/18/amazons-enforcement-failures-leave-open-a-back-door))
  - **Released evidence** — [`investigation-amazon-banned-items`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-banned-items) contains the findings and additional-items CSVs; [`investigation-amazon-banned-items-screenshots`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-banned-items-screenshots) preserves listing and policy screenshots named by `ASIN-timestamp`.
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today with bounded countermeasure caveat.** The decisive inputs were public rules, public marketplace pages, public legal/regulatory references, and a rebuildable controlled submission. A manual interactive-browser audit remains possible: Amazon continues to publish restricted-product guidance and the marketplace remains searchable. A fresh high-volume direct-HTTP path is less reliable—a July 29, 2026 spot-check of a public [Amazon search URL](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=coffee+grinder) returned Amazon's automated-access interstitial to the batch client—and current detailed policy pages are more JavaScript/login-dependent than the 2020 snapshots. That countermeasure does not defeat the story's primarily manual instrument, but it raises the cost of scaling it.
- **Detection signature**: **policy-to-catalog conformance diff + adversarial-submission bypass** — Amazon's 279 searchable prohibitions were compared with live listings on `item identity / policy clause / ASIN`, revealing 97 prohibited listings; `seller / shipper / badge` fields then measured Amazon's operational role. In the controlled test, the same prohibited object submitted under an accurate description was compared with the object submitted under altered `keyword / category / UPC`, revealing that metadata—not product identity—determined acceptance.
- **Corroboration structure**: Two-reporter classification and expert review established the policy mismatch; timestamped screenshots froze transient pages; reviews, recommendations, and specialist references rebutted euphemistic descriptions; the controlled seller test demonstrated a mechanism; fulfillment labels and former employees established Amazon's role; court/autopsy/law-enforcement records grounded the harm example. Amazon removed most identified listings but disputed or declined to explain key classifications and its own direct sales. ([response and removals](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/06/18/amazons-enforcement-failures-leave-open-a-back-door))
- **Methodology notes**: The [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com) states the sampling, inclusion/exclusion, double-check, and submission procedures. The two GitHub repositories release the final spreadsheets and screenshots, **not** executable discovery code, the full search-term variants, or a replayable seller submission. The historic findings are auditable from artifacts alone; the decisive *detector* is **not re-runnable from the artifacts alone** without rebuilding the search and submission instruments against the live site.
- **Official impact**: After contact, Amazon removed all but 16 reported listings and removed six reported autocomplete phrases; at least three listings later resurfaced, and 13 disputed listings remained. Amazon also said it would add the peptide compounds to its banned list. ([documented response](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/06/18/how-we-investigated-banned-items-on-amazon-com); [resurfacing and disputed items](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/06/18/amazons-enforcement-failures-leave-open-a-back-door))
- **Generalization**: Apply to app stores, ad exchanges, payment processors, procurement portals, crowdfunding sites, rental platforms, or registries. Generic detector: normalize the platform's rulebook into testable predicates; collect a bounded sample of live instances; join each instance to the exact rule and hard identifier; separately code the institution's roles (host, recommender, certifier, payer, warehouse, seller); then use a reversible, legal controlled submission only when observation cannot reveal the gate.

### Unlike Other Tech Giants, Amazon Won't Say How Many Workers Review Posts (2020) — Three-minute review quotas sat behind a transparency denominator Amazon would not disclose

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items); related [six-item moderator quiz](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items-quiz)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no co-publisher or story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - A former marketplace-abuse investigator said a timer allowed about **three minutes per case**; he and former employee Rachel Johnson Greer described expectations of roughly **20 review tasks an hour** and performance management for missing quota. ([employee accounts](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items))
  - Amazon would not disclose how many employees specifically moderated listings, how many posts/accounts were flagged or removed, which rules were violated, or how cases were detected. It instead cited **$500 million and more than 8,000 employees** working broadly on fraud and abuse—a denominator that could include executives, engineers, lawyers, and other non-moderators. ([Amazon response and missing denominators](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items))
  - The comparison set disclosed far more: Facebook reported about 30,000 moderators, Twitter 1,500, and YouTube/Google more than 10,000 reviewers, while those platforms also published imperfect transparency reports. Amazon cited six billion blocked “suspected bad listings” but did not disclose the intake, removal, account, violation, or appeal denominators recommended by the Santa Clara Principles. ([peer comparison](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items))
- **Finding type(s)**: **moderation-capacity opacity** (new tag: a platform publishes a broad safety input or output number while withholding the narrower staff, workload, error, or appeal denominator needed to evaluate it); **human-review throughput pressure** (new tag: performance quotas compress contextual judgment into a measured task interval).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Insider testimony** — two former Amazon employees, one anonymous, describing task timers, quotas, and performance consequences. ([story](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items))
  - **Company response** — Amazon's written statements about broad fraud-and-abuse spending/headcount and its refusal to provide moderator-specific figures; a statement that accuracy and productivity were among multiple evaluation dimensions. ([response](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items))
  - **Peer disclosures and governance standard** — Facebook, Twitter, and YouTube staffing announcements, platform transparency reports, and the Santa Clara Principles' recommended minimum disclosures. ([comparison and links](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items))
  - **Outcome anchor** — the 97-listing Banned Bounty corpus and the six-item quiz show what a moderator had to classify; the adjacent evidence repositories preserve listings, but not workforce records. ([quiz](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items-quiz); [`investigation-amazon-banned-items`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-banned-items))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(c) leak/insider-dependent.** The exact three-minute/20-per-hour throughput finding is inert without former employees; Amazon did not confirm the quota and no released public record reproduces it. The nearest public substitute is Amazon's broad fraud-and-abuse headcount/spending, trust-and-safety job postings, seller appeal records, and peer transparency reports. Those can establish **opacity and denominator mismatch**, but they lose the decisive internal timer, workload, and performance-consequence evidence.
- **Detection signature**: **capacity-accountability denominator gap** — peer `moderator headcount / removals / appeals` disclosures were compared with Amazon's `all fraud-and-abuse personnel / blocked suspected listings` figures on `function and reporting scope`, revealing incomparable denominators; insider `minutes per task × tasks per hour` supplied the missing throughput estimate and linked capacity design to likely review quality.
- **Corroboration structure**: Two former workers independently described roughly 20 tasks an hour; Amazon acknowledged measuring productivity but did not confirm the quota. The already verified prohibited-listing corpus made the quality concern concrete. Peer public disclosures established that finer-grained reporting was feasible, not that the platforms were necessarily effective. The central throughput fact remains source-dependent.
- **Methodology notes**: No story-specific Show Your Work page or code/data repository was found. **[Inferred]** reporting method: targeted former-employee interviews, written questions to Amazon, and a scoped peer-transparency comparison; basis is the source inventory in the article. The adjacent Banned Bounty repos do not contain interview notes or workforce data. The detector is **not re-runnable from released artifacts alone**.
- **Official impact**: No story-specific official staffing disclosure or policy change was identified. In the published response, Amazon continued to give only its broader 8,000-person fraud-and-abuse figure. ([story response](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/07/09/amazon-content-moderation-banned-items))
- **Generalization**: For content moderation, claims review, fraud investigation, medical utilization review, police dispatch, or benefits adjudication, require matched denominators: trained reviewers, incoming cases, auto-decisions, escalations, reversals, handling time, and appeal outcomes. Broad “safety employees” or “events blocked” counts should be flagged when the institution withholds function-specific workload and error data.

### Labeled “Research” Chemicals, Doping Drugs Sold Openly on Amazon.com (2020) — Reviews and Q&A contradicted “not for human use” labels on 66 peptide listings

- **URL**: [Investigation](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs); [released evidence repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-peptides)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no co-publisher or story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Reporters documented **66 injectable-peptide listings** in August and September 2020 after Amazon had said in May it would restrict them. The substances were absent from FDA's approved-drug list, and several were WADA-prohibited doping drugs. ([scope and regulatory status](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs))
  - Reviews or product questions on at least **18 listings** clearly described human consumption, injection, therapeutic use, dose preparation, benefits, or an adverse allergic reaction. Yet all but nine listings claimed “research,” “lab chemical,” or “not for human consumption”; the remainder sat in “Industrial and Scientific.” ([review evidence and disclaimers](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs))
  - Named sellers included Paradigm Peptides, Pharma Grade Peptides, and Quality Research Chemicals. One Paradigm representative said the company sold under a “research umbrella” but acknowledged that the real market was human use and that she took two peptides herself. ([seller tracing and admission](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs))
  - Amazon initially called the products permitted research chemicals, then said it would no longer allow them “out of an abundance of caution.” All 66 identified listings came down after The Markup presented its findings. ([Amazon response](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs))
- **Finding type(s)**: **disclaimer-reality mismatch** (new tag: declared purpose or legal category conflicts with observed customer behavior, instructions, or seller admissions); policy-enforcement gap; **review-as-use-evidence** (new tag: customer reviews/Q&A reveal how an ambiguously marketed product or service is actually used).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Constructed listing census** — 66 listing observations keyed by capture date, substance, URL, ASIN, seller, and full-page screenshot. ([repository data description](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-peptides))
  - **Behavioral text** — listing copy, reviews, customer questions, preparation instructions, efficacy claims, and an adverse-event report preserved in screenshots/raw links. ([article examples](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs))
  - **Regulatory classification** — FDA approval/compounding rules and WADA/USADA status. USADA still describes BPC-157 as unapproved for human clinical use and prohibited under WADA's S0 category. ([2020 sourcing](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs); [current USADA status](https://www.usada.org/spirit-of-sport/bpc-157-peptide-prohibited/))
  - **Seller/entity tracing and interviews** — seller storefronts, Facebook marketing, business/address checks, calls and emails with sellers, a purchaser, clinicians, a former prosecutor, USADA/WADA experts, and Amazon. ([story](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs))
  - **Released evidence** — [`investigation-amazon-peptides`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-peptides) contains the 66-row findings spreadsheet and timestamped full-page screenshots.
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The decisive evidence is public listing/review/Q&A content joined to public regulatory and platform rules. A fresh manual browser audit can reuse the substance/status table even though the 2020 ASINs are historical and all identified listings were removed. As with the broader banned-item audit, unattended collection meets platform countermeasures and exact Amazon category policies are more login/JavaScript-dependent; the core manual path remains available.
- **Detection signature**: **disclaimer-to-behavior contradiction join** — listing `declared purpose / category / disclaimer` was compared with review and Q&A `injection / dose / therapeutic-use language` on `ASIN`, then joined to FDA/WADA/USADA status on `substance name`, revealing that the “research chemical” frame did not describe actual use.
- **Corroboration structure**: Full-page screenshots preserve both sides of each contradiction; regulator lists establish drug/doping status; multiple customer statements show a pattern rather than one anecdote; one seller representative directly acknowledged the human-use market; clinicians and anti-doping officials addressed safety; Amazon's removal of all identified listings is an action, not an admission that every legal characterization was correct.
- **Methodology notes**: No separate `themarkup.org/show-your-work/` page was found. The repository README is a brief methods/evidence note, and the article describes the review/Q&A, regulator, seller, and interview checks. **[Inferred]** discovery likely iterated peptide/substance names and related recommendations from the earlier Banned Bounty work; neither a complete query frame nor collection code is released. The historical 66-listing claim is auditable, but the decisive detector is **not re-runnable from released artifacts alone**.
- **Official impact**: Amazon removed all 66 identified listings and told The Markup it had decided to stop allowing the products. ([response and removals](https://themarkup.org/banned-bounty/2020/09/17/amazon-sales-peptides-doping-drugs))
- **Generalization**: Use whenever a market relies on “for research only,” “not financial advice,” “educational,” “collectible,” “not for human consumption,” “independent contractor,” or similar boundary labels. Generic detector: join declared category to reviews, support tickets, usage instructions, transaction companions, and regulator status; flag repeated first-person use verbs or bundled accessories inconsistent with the declared purpose.

### Amazon Puts Its Own “Brands” First Above Better-Rated Products (2021) — Brand affiliation predicted the first organic result far better than ratings or reviews

- **URL**: [Investigation](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/amazon-puts-its-own-brands-first-above-better-rated-products); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; Amazon's Advantage won the [2022 Gerald Loeb Award](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2022/10/06/the-markup-wins-loeb-award-for-amazons-advantage).
- **What they found**:
  - Reporters assembled **11,342 unique high-frequency queries** from Amazon/Walmart autocomplete and nine Amazon Seller Central categories, ran all queries from Washington, D.C., on Jan. 21, 2021, and analyzed the **3,492** whose first page contained at least one Amazon brand/exclusive. They separately built a **137,428-ASIN affiliation table** from Amazon's “our brands” filter, an undocumented query interface, device best-seller pages, and trademark research. ([query and affiliation construction](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - Amazon brands/exclusives occupied only **5.8% of sampled product slots** but received **19.5% of first positions**. When Amazon put one of its products ahead of competitors, **65.3%** had lower star ratings than a lower-ranked competitor, **51.7%** had fewer reviews, and **28.0%** had both. ([incidence and quality comparison](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - A seven-feature random forest predicted Amazon's first organic product with **73.2% accuracy**. Removing Amazon affiliation reduced accuracy by 9.7 points—far more than removing any other feature—and affiliation alone predicted the winner **70.7%** of the time. Ratings and review volume performed little better than chance. ([ablation analysis](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - Affiliation was also obscured: **37.4%** of sampled Amazon brands/exclusives had neither a disclosure nor a broadly recognizable Amazon name; source code marked **87%** of top-ranked “featured from our brands” placements as `sponsored` even though the visible page did not. A YouGov panel of 1,000 adults found only 17% expected Amazon affiliation to determine the first nonsponsored result. ([disclosure and survey findings](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results); [story examples](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/amazon-puts-its-own-brands-first-above-better-rated-products))
- **Finding type(s)**: **self-preferencing uplift** (new tag: institutional affiliation predicts rank or selection after observable quality/popularity features are considered); **affiliation opacity** (new tag: ownership or exclusivity material to a ranking is not consistently disclosed); **pay-to-remain-visible pressure** (new tag: unpaid access to a gatekeeper's audience degrades enough that participants treat paid placement as compulsory).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Large-scale search snapshots** — Firefox/Selenium desktop emulator, fixed Washington, D.C. egress, 11,342 query pages, saved HTML and screenshots; sponsored carousels were excluded from the core search grid. ([collector design](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - **Affiliation ground truth** — Amazon “our brands” filters and query responses, Amazon device best-sellers, USPTO trademark records, ASINs, and manual spot checks. The method explicitly notes that Amazon's filter was incomplete. ([affiliation method and limitation](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - **Product characteristics and modeling** — rank, sponsorship, Amazon affiliation, seller, shipper, ratings, review counts, and top-click indicators; random forests, five-fold hyperparameter search, 10-fold evaluation, and feature ablation. ([model method](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - **Disclosure/expectation evidence** — visible labels, hidden source-code fields, and a nationally representative YouGov internet panel of 1,000 adults. ([survey and disclosure method](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - **Qualitative and documentary corroboration** — seller interviews, former Amazon brand manager JT Meng's account of historical “search seeding,” Amazon's congressional statements, Seller Central guidance, and Amazon's written denial. ([main story](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/amazon-puts-its-own-brands-first-above-better-rated-products))
  - **Released artifacts** — [`investigation-amazon-brands`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-brands) contains notebooks, processed tables, 187,534 search positions, 157,405 product pages, training sets, the 137,428-ASIN affiliation table, and download scripts for raw S3 captures; [`tool-amazon-brand-detector`](https://github.com/the-markup/tool-amazon-brand-detector) releases the browser extension that labels affiliated results.
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today for fresh acquisition.** The decisive ranking evidence came from a constructed instrument over public pages, and the full historic input/output chain is public. Fresh collection is degraded: the investigation collector and extension were last pushed in [October](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-brands/commit/b5ceaff65b1185bb619a6e950b445a23524fae65) and [December 2021](https://github.com/the-markup/tool-amazon-brand-detector/commit/09c5439fb07f7c255891cddc2dfb6e2f6d6b93a6); Amazon's “our brands” surface was undocumented/incomplete even then; current Top Search Terms data is limited to Brand Registry sellers in [Seller Central](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/721dd873-f35d-43d5-9c9e-2355e73849cf); direct batch fetching now receives an automated-access interstitial; and the Firefox extension remains downloadable but at version 0.0.9, [last updated Dec. 14, 2021](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/amazon-brands/). A 2026 rerun needs new page parsers, brand ground truth, session/countermeasure handling, and validation.
- **Detection signature**: **affiliation-lift ranking audit + feature-ablation test** — product positions were joined to the 137,428-ASIN affiliation table on `ASIN`; Amazon-affiliated share of all slots was compared with affiliated share of `rank=1`, revealing 5.8% inventory presence versus 19.5% first-position incidence. Pairwise rank outcomes were then modeled with and without `is_amazon`; the 9.7-point accuracy loss when affiliation was removed revealed its exceptional predictive importance among observed features.
- **Corroboration structure**: Distributional lift, pairwise modeling, and quality/popularity comparisons independently point in the same direction; a generic-search sensitivity dataset reproduced the pattern; visible/source-code labels document disclosure; survey data establish consumer expectations; sellers and an ex-manager supply mechanism/context. This remains a black-box audit: unavailable sales, returns, click-through, price comparability, personalization, and internal ranking features prevent a causal claim that affiliation alone *caused* every rank. ([limitations](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
- **Methodology notes**: The unusually complete [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results) states acquisition dates, query sources, page schema, exclusions, equations, model validation, sensitivity tests, and limitations. The [reproduction repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-brands) and still-live raw S3 links make the **historic decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone**. The [Brand Detector post](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/11/29/introducing-amazon-brand-detector) and repo operationalize affiliation labeling for shoppers, but their 2021 rule lists and selectors should not be treated as current ground truth.
- **Official impact**: Four days after publication, House Judiciary members cited the investigation and demanded sworn clarifications and documents from Amazon; the committee later referred possible criminal conduct to DOJ. The series won the 2022 Loeb Award. ([first congressional response](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/18/citing-markup-investigation-lawmakers-demand-answers-from-amazon); [referral follow-up](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2022/03/09/house-antitrust-committee-accuses-amazon-of-lying-to-congress-asks-doj-to-investigate); [award](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2022/10/06/the-markup-wins-loeb-award-for-amazons-advantage))
- **Generalization**: Join hidden ownership/affiliation to ranked outcomes in search, procurement, grants, licensing, referrals, app stores, media recommendations, or inspections. Minimum design: time-stamped ranked results, a defensible affiliation graph, observable quality/popularity controls, and a stable query frame. Compare representation to top-rank incidence; then use matched pairs or ablation to test whether affiliation adds predictive power.

### When Amazon Takes the Buy Box, It Doesn't Give It Up (2021) — Repeated snapshots showed Amazon winning and retaining the default-offer gate

- **URL**: [Investigation](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/when-amazon-takes-the-buy-box-it-doesnt-give-it-up); shared [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; Amazon's Advantage won the [2022 Gerald Loeb Award](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2022/10/06/the-markup-wins-loeb-award-for-amazons-advantage).
- **What they found**:
  - In the exact-comparison subset of **1,209 popular products with multiple sellers**, Amazon's default seller changed on only **23.5%** over roughly 12 weeks, versus about seven in 10 products over six weeks in a 2016 Northeastern study. ([panel comparison](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - Amazon gave itself the Buy Box/default offer for **40.0%** of multi-seller products; no individual third-party seller won more than **0.06%**. When Amazon initially held the Buy Box, it still held it 12 weeks later for **98%** of those products. ([story findings](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/when-amazon-takes-the-buy-box-it-doesnt-give-it-up); [exact method totals](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - Roughly nine in 10 selected offers used Amazon shipping; fewer than 8% switched between Amazon and third-party shipping at recheck. Prime delivery was an acknowledged factor, and merchants generally pay Amazon's Fulfillment by Amazon service to obtain it. ([shipping result and company position](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/when-amazon-takes-the-buy-box-it-doesnt-give-it-up))
- **Finding type(s)**: **default-gate lock-in** (new tag: the entity controlling a high-conversion default disproportionately awards and persistently retains it); **adjacent-service tying signal** (new tag: access to a dominant gate correlates with purchasing the gatekeeper's complementary service, without by itself proving unlawful tying).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Time-separated product-page panel** — February 2021 product captures and a 12-week spot-check, keyed by ASIN and coded for default seller and shipper; missing/sold-out/removed/no-default pages were explicitly measured. ([panel method](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results))
  - **Comparison baseline** — a 2016 Northeastern University study using a similar multi-seller frame. The Markup restricted its comparison to products with multiple sellers to avoid a denominator mismatch. ([story comparison](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/when-amazon-takes-the-buy-box-it-doesnt-give-it-up))
  - **Platform and policy material** — Buy Box/featured-offer labels, seller/shipper metadata, Amazon congressional statements about the featured-merchant algorithm, Prime delivery criteria, and Amazon's refusal to answer story questions. ([story](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/when-amazon-takes-the-buy-box-it-doesnt-give-it-up))
  - **Regulatory/contextual documents** — the European Commission's Buy Box/FBA investigation and the D.C. attorney general's marketplace suit; expert review by Christo Wilson and interviews with competition/e-commerce scholars. ([story sources](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/when-amazon-takes-the-buy-box-it-doesnt-give-it-up))
  - **Released artifacts** — the shared [`investigation-amazon-brands`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-brands) repository includes product-page datasets, raw captures, spot-check pages, notebooks, and tables used for this story.
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today for fresh acquisition.** The historic detector uses public product pages and released code/data. Fresh high-volume acquisition inherits the ranking audit's stale selectors, session/personalization risk, automated-access countermeasures, and lack of a stable open Amazon offer API. A smaller manual panel remains possible, but a comparable 1,200-product unattended panel requires collector reconstruction and repeated validation.
- **Detection signature**: **time-separated default-stickiness panel** — product-page snapshot `t1` was joined to snapshot `t2` on `ASIN`, and `default seller / default shipper` transitions were compared by initial seller type; that revealed both Amazon's 40% share of multi-seller defaults and its 98% retention when initially selected.
- **Corroboration structure**: Same-item/time comparison removes product identity as an explanation; the multi-seller restriction ensures there was a choice to make; seller and shipper transitions separate Amazon Retail from FBA; the 2016 study supplies a historical benchmark; regulatory inquiries show the mechanism was independently salient. The panel cannot observe every price, inventory, service-quality, profitability, or algorithm input and therefore measures outcome concentration/persistence, not unlawful intent.
- **Methodology notes**: There is no separate Buy Box Show Your Work page; the shared [Amazon brands methodology](https://themarkup.org/amazons-advantage/2021/10/14/how-we-analyzed-amazons-treatment-of-its-brands-in-search-results) contains the collection-gap sensitivity test, spot-check design, transition rates, and limitations. The [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-brands) explicitly states that it reproduces both Amazon's Advantage stories. The **historic detector is re-runnable from released artifacts alone**; a fresh detector is degraded for the reasons above.
- **Official impact**: In December 2022, the European Commission made Amazon commitments legally binding across the EEA, including nondiscriminatory Buy Box access and display of a second competing offer; these remedies arose from the Commission's own investigation, so they are **independent follow-through, not demonstrated causal impact of The Markup story**. ([European Commission commitment summary](https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2022-12/2022_Amazon_Marketplace_en.pdf); [Commission announcement](https://germany.representation.ec.europa.eu/nachrichten-und-veranstaltungen/pressemitteilungen/kartellrecht-kommission-akzeptiert-verpflichtungsangebote-von-amazon-2022-12-20_de))
- **Generalization**: Measure any consequential default—recommended vendor, insurer network tier, app default, procurement framework winner, featured donation, preferred pharmacy, route, or search answer—as a transition panel. Join repeated snapshots on the same object; stratify retention by gatekeeper affiliation and purchase of adjacent services; compare against eligible alternatives and historical/peer turnover.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### P1. Rule-to-instance conformance join

- **Mechanics**: Convert a rulebook into atomic, testable predicates; enumerate live instances; join each instance to the exact rule on a hard identifier plus product/action characteristics; code the institution's role separately. Escalate a sampled mismatch with a legal, reversible controlled submission when necessary.
- **Minimum data**: Versioned rule text, bounded instance corpus, hard IDs, capture timestamps, and enough content to classify each predicate.
- **Recognition cues**: Long prohibition/eligibility lists; euphemistic or miscoded instances; the same institution hosting and certifying/fulfilling; complaints that enforcement is “automated”; removed items that reappear.
- **Any-domain applications**: Debarred vendors still receiving contracts; sanctioned entities in payments; revoked professionals on rosters; prohibited political ads; unlicensed rentals; ineligible grant recipients.

#### P2. Latent-affiliation uplift audit

- **Mechanics**: Build an ownership/control/exclusivity graph; join it to ranked or selected outcomes; compare affiliated share of the eligible pool with share of top positions/wins; control for observable quality/popularity and use matched pairs or ablation.
- **Minimum data**: Ranked/selected outcomes, eligible-pool denominator, entity resolution, affiliation ground truth, and at least a few plausible non-affiliation predictors.
- **Recognition cues**: House brands with opaque names; recurring winners connected through subsidiaries; better-rated rivals below affiliates; hidden source-code labels; inconsistent public ownership disclosure.
- **Any-domain applications**: Procurement awards to related firms, grants to board-linked nonprofits, insurer steering to owned clinics, referrals to affiliated brokers, regulator choices involving former employers.

#### P3. Sticky-default gate panel

- **Mechanics**: Reobserve the same eligible choice set at fixed intervals; build a transition matrix for default winner/provider; stratify retention by gatekeeper affiliation and use of adjacent paid services; compare with peers or an earlier baseline.
- **Minimum data**: Stable object ID, at least two timestamped snapshots, eligible alternatives, default winner, and affiliation/service-use fields.
- **Recognition cues**: A default drives most conversions; the operator also competes; low turnover despite many eligible alternatives; winning correlates with the operator's logistics/payment/advertising service.
- **Any-domain applications**: Preferred pharmacies, default apps, network tiers, framework-contract call-offs, featured charities, routing/recommendation systems.

#### P4. Disclaimer-reality contradiction

- **Mechanics**: Join formal label/category/disclaimer to independent use evidence—reviews, Q&A, instructions, bundled items, support records, transactions, or seller admissions—then join the underlying object to regulator status.
- **Minimum data**: Instance ID, declared purpose, behavioral text or companion transactions, and authoritative classification.
- **Recognition cues**: “Research only,” “not for human use,” “educational,” “collectible,” or “independent” labels paired with first-person use, dosing, evasion jokes, or function-specific accessories.
- **Any-domain applications**: Securities promotions, off-label health products, labor misclassification, shell “consulting” payments, residential property operated as commercial lodging.

#### P5. Investigation-to-enforcement lineage monitor

- **Mechanics**: Create a citation graph from story URL/metric/quote into official records; separately match later official allegations at the mechanism level; label direct citation, independent convergence, contradiction, referral, charge, remedy, and adjudication as different states.
- **Minimum data**: Versioned reporting claims, official full text/dockets, dates, named actors, and disposition tracking.
- **Recognition cues**: Story URLs in footnotes; copied metrics; demand letters issued days after publication; regulator allegations using internal evidence for a previously measured outcome; referrals with no visible resolution.
- **Any-domain applications**: Legislative oversight, inspector-general work, enforcement complaints, sanctions designations, procurement suspension, professional discipline, and civil litigation.

### 5. What this platform could run now, and what is missing

**Runnable now with current holdings**

- **P5, investigation-to-enforcement lineage**, is the strongest immediate fit. CourtListener/RECAP, government releases, corporate registries, OpenAleph, and entity resolution can trace a public finding into complaints, opinions, hearings, sanctions, and corporate consequences while preserving allegation/adjudication state.
- **P1, rule-to-instance conformance**, is runnable on structured holdings: OpenSanctions/ICIJ entities against corporate registries; debarment or eligibility rules against USASpending/FPDS awards; professional or corporate status against CourtListener matters; 990 eligibility/related-party rules against nonprofit filings.
- **P2, latent-affiliation uplift**, is partly runnable where the outcome ledger already exists. USPTO plus Companies House/OpenCorporates/GLEIF/ICIJ/OpenSanctions can build affiliation ground truth; USASpending/FPDS, grants, patents, 990s, and litigation can supply selections/wins. The platform can test affiliated award concentration, shared officers, or repeated related-party beneficiaries now.
- **P3, sticky-default gate**, can be adapted to repeated public records—contract incumbency, recurring grant recipients, registered-agent persistence, or repeated counsel/expert selection—though those are slower-moving than an e-commerce Buy Box.
- **P4, disclaimer-reality contradiction**, can run where filings, dockets, transaction ledgers, or archived claims supply both declared purpose and observed conduct—for example nonprofit purpose versus grants/payments, corporate object versus contracts, or patent owner versus operating affiliate.

**Missing for a fresh Markup-style marketplace audit**

- A maintained browser-collection fleet with ethical rate controls, session/location management, CAPTCHA/interstitial detection, page-schema tests, and screenshot/HTML snapshot storage.
- A versioned marketplace rule harvester and diff engine; current detailed Amazon policy text is increasingly JavaScript/login-dependent.
- Current Amazon brand/exclusive ground truth. USPTO ownership is necessary but not sufficient for exclusives, licensed brands, devices, and unlabeled merchandising relationships.
- A fresh representative query frame. Amazon Top Search Terms remains Brand Registry-gated, while autocomplete alone can bias the universe.
- A scheduler for repeated product/offer snapshots and robust ASIN/variant/seller resolution.
- Human-review or insider acquisition for class-(c) workforce claims; no public registry can recover an internal three-minute quota.

The most portable lesson is not “scrape Amazon.” It is to separate five ledgers—**rule, instance, affiliation, gate outcome, and official follow-through**—and make each join explicit. That structure lets the same detector move from e-commerce into contracts, corporate networks, sanctions, nonprofits, patents, and litigation without importing Amazon-specific assumptions.
