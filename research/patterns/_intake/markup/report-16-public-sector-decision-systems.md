# The Markup Evidence Ontology — Cluster 16: Public-Sector & Institutional Decision Systems

Compiled July 29, 2026. Six substantive evidence units from the census's 22-page C3 cluster, covering the four bounded series [Digital Book Banning](https://themarkup.org/series/digital-book-banning), [Neighborhood Watch](https://themarkup.org/series/neighborhood-watch), [Prediction: Bias](https://themarkup.org/series/prediction-bias), and [Remote Justice](https://themarkup.org/series/remote-justice).

## Scope and attribution

This report selects six original empirical investigations from the 22-page cluster. The remaining frame includes four methodology pages, a school-test interactive, a student records guide, a student gallery, a consumer guide, explanatory and interview follow-ups, award notices, and an official-impact update. This report treats those methodology, service, award, and impact pages as evidence attached to the selected investigations rather than inflating them into separate stories. The selection covers every census-noted method family: distributed school tests, public-records audits, algorithm-output validation against observed events, and court/process comparison.

The census's attribution rule is domain-structural: a dated editorial page on `themarkup.org` counts once, while a separately self-canonical CalMatters, partner, translation, or syndication copy does not. All six empirical stories selected here were published before The Markup's April 2024 merger announcement. Partner credits are still preserved story by story: USA TODAY; the Los Angeles Times, Insider, and AfroLA; Gizmodo; and WIRED. No CalMatters copy has been added. The corpus rule is documented in the [local Markup census §1](report-10-census.md#1-method-and-sources); The Markup separately announced the [CalMatters merger](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/04/18/the-markup-is-joining-forces-with-calmatters).

“Runnable today” below means an outside investigator can obtain fresh decisive inputs through substantially the same public path as of July 29, 2026. “Degraded today” means the historical calculation may still replay, but a fresh equivalent collection is no longer comparable because a platform changed, an API or cloud bucket closed, or the source product shut down. “Re-runnable from released artifacts alone” is narrower: it asks whether the published package itself contains the decisive inputs and executable or sufficiently exact transformation instructions.

### Schools Were Just Supposed to Block Porn. Instead They Sabotaged Homework and Censored Suicide Prevention Sites (2024) — District filter logs showed safety and educational resources swept into a system justified as a pornography control

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/schools-were-just-supposed-to-block-porn-instead-they-sabotaged-homework-and-censored-suicide-prevention-sites); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/04/13/how-we-investigated-web-censorship-in-schools); [crowdsourced school test](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/does-your-school-block-these-sites); [public-records guide](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/students-how-you-can-investigate-internet-censorship-in-your-school-district)
- **Partner/awards**: Copublished with **USA TODAY**. The Digital Book Banning package won the 2024 Online News Association Excellence in Technology Reporting award for a small newsroom; judges specifically cited its use of technology, FOIA, and crowdsourcing. ([partner credit](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/schools-were-just-supposed-to-block-porn-instead-they-sabotaged-homework-and-censored-suicide-prevention-sites); [award and judges' rationale](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/08/17/the-markups-clear-and-beautiful-reporting-wins-online-journalism-award))
- **What they found**:
  - Records from **16 districts in 11 states** collectively logged more than **1.9 billion blocks in about one month**, including requests for page elements as well as whole pages. Pornography or nudity was not a top-10 blocking category in any district and appeared in the top 20 in only two, while education, news, search engines, social media, games, and audiovisual content were commonly blocked. ([main findings](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/schools-were-just-supposed-to-block-porn-instead-they-sabotaged-homework-and-censored-suicide-prevention-sites); [category analysis](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/04/13/how-we-investigated-web-censorship-in-schools))
  - Districts blocked sex-education or abortion categories in Beaufort, Granite, Houston, Jefferson Parish, Manatee County, Rockwood, Texarkana, and Washington County. Rockwood's ContentKeeper “human sexuality” category caught almost 50 LGBTQ-oriented sites—including It Gets Better, GLAAD, and The Trevor Project for some grades—even though none of the reviewed sites hosted pornography. ([district/category findings](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/04/13/how-we-investigated-web-censorship-in-schools))
  - Rockwood allowed anti-LGBTQ material from Focus on the Family and Alliance Defending Freedom while blocking supportive LGBTQ resources; Manatee records included blocked dictionaries, Google Scholar, academic journals, churches, news outlets, and its public-library catalog. ([viewpoint and academic examples](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/schools-were-just-supposed-to-block-porn-instead-they-sabotaged-homework-and-censored-suicide-prevention-sites))
  - Only **three of the 16** responding districts let students directly request that a site be unblocked; 10 had no student appeal route and three routed requests indirectly through teachers. ([appeal comparison](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/schools-were-just-supposed-to-block-porn-instead-they-sabotaged-homework-and-censored-suicide-prevention-sites))
- **Finding type(s)**: **policy-purpose drift** (new tag: an operational system justified by a narrow legal or declared purpose acts on a much broader universe); **viewpoint-asymmetric restriction** (new tag: a gate blocks supportive material about a topic while allowing opposing advocacy); **appeal-channel deficit** (new tag: affected users face an automated restriction without a direct, intelligible correction path).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Public-record filter logs** — requests to 26 districts across 14 states sought 30 days of blocked URLs, category labels, attempt counts, custom allow/block lists, challenge policies, and vendor contracts. Sixteen districts released some data; 10 withheld the requested URL-level evidence, mostly invoking cybersecurity. ([request design and response table](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/04/13/how-we-investigated-web-censorship-in-schools))
  - **Procurement and sampling records** — PEN America's book-ban index supplied the first 15 targets; GovSpend contracts identified eight more iboss districts; two student-linked districts and one trial district completed the purposive sample. This is a broad audit, not a random national sample. ([sampling method](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/04/13/how-we-investigated-web-censorship-in-schools))
  - **Constructed edge tests and distributed confirmation** — reporters wrote software and ran automated website-access tests on Rockwood's Wi-Fi; students and districts separately confirmed whether highlighted sites and categories were blocked for in-district users. The public quiz then invited K–12 students to click a fixed list of sites while connected to school Wi-Fi and submit school-level results. ([reporter's account of the Rockwood tests](https://themarkup.org/hello-world/2024/04/13/what-i-learned-while-reporting-on-school-censorship); [confirmation method](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/04/13/how-we-investigated-web-censorship-in-schools); [public instrument](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/does-your-school-block-these-sites))
  - **Policy and legal baselines** — the Children's Internet Protection Act, FCC guidance on social-media blocking, district challenge policies, filter-company category definitions, and the Missouri viewpoint-discrimination case supplied the rulebook against which actual blocks were assessed. ([legal and policy sources in the main story](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/schools-were-just-supposed-to-block-porn-instead-they-sabotaged-homework-and-censored-suicide-prevention-sites))
  - **Affected-user evidence** — student, teacher, parent, advocate, district-official, filter-company, and legal-expert interviews linked log rows to homework failure, workarounds, health-resource access, and appeal friction. A five-student companion gallery preserved examples in students' own words. ([main story](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/schools-were-just-supposed-to-block-porn-instead-they-sabotaged-homework-and-censored-suicide-prevention-sites); [student gallery](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/five-high-schoolers-explain-how-web-filters-inhibit-their-learning-and-safety))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today with access caveats.** The decisive evidence is government-held filter output, public policies/contracts, and a rebuildable on-network browser test. The Markup's [records guide](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/students-how-you-can-investigate-internet-censorship-in-your-school-district) remains live and the [quiz front end](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2024/04/13/does-your-school-block-these-sites) still serves its compiled test bundle; however, an investigator must be physically or legitimately connected to each school's network, and districts may again resist URL-level disclosure. The public quiz was a **prospective extension and corroboration instrument**, not the source of the published 1.9-billion count. No test submission was made during this current-status review.
- **Detection signature**: **normative-output diff + distributed edge confirmation** — district `blocked URL / vendor category / attempt count` logs were compared with `CIPA's narrow target / FCC guidance / known site function` on the key `URL and category`, revealing educational, health, and viewpoint-sensitive overblocking; the same URLs were then tested from the affected network and confirmed with students or district staff, revealing which log-level blocks were user-visible.
- **Corroboration structure**: Government logs established scale; written category definitions and laws established meaning; on-network tests and student screenshots checked the operational edge; interviews established consequence; cross-district variation showed local discretion rather than one universal vendor rule. The analysis explicitly counted page-element blocks in the total and did not claim the 16-district sample represented every U.S. district. ([counting and confirmation caveats](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/04/13/how-we-investigated-web-censorship-in-schools))
- **Methodology notes**: The [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/04/13/how-we-investigated-web-censorship-in-schools) states the target selection, request fields, refusals, district-level findings, and validation procedure. The award account says the team wrote testing software, but **no story-specific GitHub repository, raw district-log release, or source release for the automated tests was identified**. The decisive detector is therefore **not re-runnable from released artifacts alone**; an investigator can rebuild it from the method and public guide but must reacquire logs and run fresh authorized school-network tests.
- **Official impact**: The Center for Democracy & Technology said the reporting inspired additional questions in its next nationally representative survey; about 70 percent of surveyed teachers and students then said filters interfered with assignments. This is documented agenda-setting, not a government remedy. ([2025 follow-up and CDT attribution](https://themarkup.org/digital-book-banning/2025/01/16/online-censorship-in-schools-is-more-pervasive-than-expected-new-data-shows))
- **Generalization**: Use wherever a rule engine mediates access—libraries, benefits portals, prisons, workplaces, health networks, app stores, or sanctions screening. Generic detector: obtain the decision log and rule/category label; join each output to the legal or declared target and a verified object-purpose table; stratify by protected topic and user class; then run authorized edge tests from representative access contexts and audit the appeal path.

### Amazon's “Neighborhood Watch” Might Be Turning Police Officers Into “Reddit Moderators” (2023) — A citizen-classified alert stream put non-crimes and an enormous message burden into LAPD inboxes

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/amazons-neighborhood-watch-might-be-turning-police-officers-into-reddit-moderators); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments)
- **Partner/awards**: Copublished with the **Los Angeles Times**, **Insider**, and **AfroLA**; the project was supported by the Pulitzer Center's AI Accountability Network. Four Neighborhood Watch illustrations won a 2024 Society for News Design award of excellence in the investigative/public-service line-of-coverage category. ([partner and funder credit](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/amazons-neighborhood-watch-might-be-turning-police-officers-into-reddit-moderators); [SND award](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/05/14/the-markup-wins-six-awards-of-excellence-from-the-society-for-news-design))
- **What they found**:
  - LAPD released **202,552 Neighbors emails** spanning July 11, 2020 through September 30, 2022. As of the end date, **260 officers** had opted into crime alerts; Detective Jeffry Poole received **10,651 purported-crime emails** plus 89 other Neighbors alerts in roughly 18 months. ([email corpus and recipient finding](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/amazons-neighborhood-watch-might-be-turning-police-officers-into-reddit-moderators); [data provenance](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - Of 191,554 messages labeled as crime alerts, grouping by `date + title + description` produced **13,053 unique alerts**. A random sample of 1,000, each reviewed three times against California criminal law, found that **more than 30 percent did not describe criminal activity**. Theft was 38 percent of the sample; user-labeled suspicious behavior was 16 percent; confusing events 12 percent; lost people or pets 2 percent. ([sampling and codebook](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - The corpus contained nearly **400 notifications of user responses to police-authored posts**; at least **26 LAPD officers** had posted at least once and received responses, including requests to act against unhoused people. ([officer-post interaction findings](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/amazons-neighborhood-watch-might-be-turning-police-officers-into-reddit-moderators))
  - Ring's recruitment records contradicted LAPD's public distancing: Ring trained officers, asked for beat ZIP-code spreadsheets to construct alert zones, and used early adopters to recruit peers, while an LAPD spokesperson said the department did not “work with” Ring. ([recruitment emails and agency response](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/amazons-neighborhood-watch-might-be-turning-police-officers-into-reddit-moderators))
- **Finding type(s)**: **participatory-surveillance spillover** (new tag: a consumer/community reporting channel routes unverified suspicions into state enforcement); **platform-imposed public workload** (new tag: a private product generates a measurable operational burden for public employees); **partnership-denial conflict** (new tag: operational records show structured coordination that a participant publicly minimizes).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **FOIA email archive** — a CUNY student reporting team and The Markup requested every Neighbors message received by 25 large-city departments selected for Ring use and high gentrification; seven initially produced records, and LAPD supplied the most complete corpus in Outlook PST files totaling 32.89 GB. ([public-records audit and conversion method](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - **Manual content coding against law** — 1,000 deduplicated random alerts were reviewed three times and classified for criminal conduct using California criminal statutes, then coded for topic. ([classification method](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - **Partnership and product records** — Ring training, recruitment, alert-zone, onboarding, and notification emails; a public screenshot of the police portal; EFF's Atlas of Surveillance; and Ring's public agency map documented the institutional channel. ([sources and target selection](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - **Expert and agency response** — policing, surveillance, privacy, misinformation, and porch-theft experts interpreted the alert burden; LAPD and Ring were given findings and methodology before publication. ([main story responses](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/amazons-neighborhood-watch-might-be-turning-police-officers-into-reddit-moderators); [method acknowledgements and responses](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today for a fresh comparable collection.** The decisive evidence was email held by a public agency and obtainable by records request; it did not require access to Ring's internal systems. Historical agency-held messages can still be requested. The live product path has changed materially: Ring sunset its in-app Request for Assistance feature in January 2024, while public agencies retained public profiles/posts; the current successor “Community Requests” routes evidence requests through Axon Evidence. ([Ring's 2024 change](https://blog.ring.com/about-ring/ring-announces-new-neighbors-app-features-sunsets-request-for-assistance-post/); [current Community Requests workflow](https://ring.com/support/verified-help/articles/5t05p/Community-Request)). A 2026 records audit can still seek Ring/Neighbors/Axon communications, but should not assume the same no-reply alert firehose, fields, or institutional workflow.
- **Detection signature**: **statute-label conformance diff + recipient-load concentration** — 191,554 platform messages labeled “crime” were grouped on `calendar date + title + description` into 13,053 unique posts; a random 1,000 were compared with California criminal-law predicates on `described act`, revealing that more than 30 percent were non-crimes. The full email ledger was then grouped by `recipient`, revealing extreme workload concentration in one officer's 10,651 alerts.
- **Corroboration structure**: The agency's own mailbox established receipt rather than merely platform publication; triple review and a legal codebook tested the user/platform label; recipient metadata quantified workload; Ring's recruitment emails established mechanism; expert interviews bounded the institutional interpretation. LAPD's export may omit deleted mail or users without email alerts, so the published count is a minimum rather than a complete measure of platform use. ([limitations](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
- **Methodology notes**: The [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments) states the target selection, request language, PST conversion, deduplication key, sample size, triple coding, and limitations. **No story-specific GitHub repository was released.** The Markup withheld the email and academic post datasets because of identifiable information, offering case-by-case research access instead. The detector is **not re-runnable from released artifacts alone**; the method is explicit, but the decisive mailbox corpus must be reacquired.
- **Official impact**: No official change was found that Ring, LAPD, or another authority attributed to this story. Ring's later product changes are relevant to present-day reproducibility but should not be claimed as story-caused impact. ([Ring's stated reasons and current product change](https://blog.ring.com/about-ring/ring-announces-new-neighbors-app-features-sunsets-request-for-assistance-post/))
- **Generalization**: Audit any private alert channel embedded in government work—fraud tips, school-safety apps, hospital flags, vendor risk alerts, 311 feeds, or social-media monitoring. Generic detector: request the agency's received-message archive; normalize and deduplicate alerts; compare platform labels with an authoritative legal/operational codebook; measure recipient load, response, and action; then compare the operational record with public descriptions of the relationship.

### Accidental Spies: Amazon Ring Owners May Be Unknowingly Emailing Police (2023) — Wealthier Los Angeles neighborhoods generated far more Neighbors posts despite not having higher rates of the examined crimes

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/accidental-spies-amazon-ring-owners-may-be-unknowingly-emailing-police); shared [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments)
- **Partner/awards**: Copublished with the **Los Angeles Times** and **AfroLA**; supported by the Pulitzer Center's AI Accountability Network. The shared Neighborhood Watch visual package received the 2024 Society for News Design award of excellence described above. ([partner and funder credit](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/accidental-spies-amazon-ring-owners-may-be-unknowingly-emailing-police); [SND award](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/05/14/the-markup-wins-six-awards-of-excellence-from-the-society-for-news-design))
- **What they found**:
  - In 2019 Los Angeles data, Neighbors posting rose from **0.5 posts per 1,000 residents** in the lowest-income decile to **3.5 per 1,000** in the richest—about seven times the rate. Across 25 cities, four (Los Angeles, Phoenix, Fresno, and Houston) showed a statistically significant positive income/posting correlation after correction for multiple tests; 13 showed no significant relationship and five showed the reverse. ([income analysis](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - In Los Angeles, the lowest-income decile recorded simple battery/assault at **8.7 incidents per 1,000 people**, versus **2.4** in the richest; vehicle-burglary rates did not significantly correlate with income. The higher posting rate in rich neighborhoods therefore did not track higher rates of either examined crime. ([crime baseline comparison](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - More than **13,000 unique Angeleno posts** were automatically forwarded to LAPD personnel over a little more than two years. When The Markup contacted 24 people whose posts had been forwarded, three responded and none knew their information had entered police monitoring. ([forwarding and user-awareness findings](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/accidental-spies-amazon-ring-owners-may-be-unknowingly-emailing-police))
  - The national post corpus contained more than **870,000 posts from over 650,000 unique users**, covering November 2016 to February 2020; the reporting used its approximate coordinates to identify who participated, not to infer exact home addresses. ([dataset scope and location caveat](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
- **Finding type(s)**: **representation-skewed surveillance** (new tag: the people generating enforcement-relevant signals are not demographically or economically representative of the places being watched); **participation-to-police amplification** (new tag: unequal platform participation becomes unequal state attention through an automated forwarding channel); **invisible secondary audience** (new tag: users publish to one perceived community while an institutional audience receives the content without salient notice).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Closed-platform crawl supplied by a researcher** — Princeton researcher Dan Calacci shared more than 870,000 Neighbors posts scraped from the platform API in March 2020, with post ID, user ID, text, category, time, and approximate latitude/longitude. His coauthored paper documents moving a virtual “home” region across the continental United States to collect the feed. ([Markup methodology](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments); [research paper](https://www.dcalacci.net/papers/Calacci%20et%20al.%20-%202022%20-%20The%20Cop%20In%20Your%20Neighbor%27s%20Doorbell%20Amazon%20Ring%20a.pdf))
  - **Public demographic and crime ledgers** — 2019 ACS tract populations/income, Census shapefiles, Census Reporter city intersections, and Los Angeles open crime incidents from 2010–2019 supplied denominators and a ground-truth proxy. Posts were spatially joined to tracts using their approximate coordinates. ([data sources and spatial join](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - **Public-record forwarding corpus** — LAPD's 202,552 Neighbors emails established that app content actually reached law enforcement and supplied the 13,053 unique Los Angeles posts used for the forwarding finding. ([mail data](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
  - **Field observation and interviews** — visible-camera counts on one block, interviews with residents whose posts reached police, and legal/surveillance experts translated geographic rates into user awareness and institutional consequence. ([field and user reporting](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/accidental-spies-amazon-ring-owners-may-be-unknowingly-emailing-police))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(d) closed-platform-dependent.** The decisive rich/poor participation denominator requires the broad, geolocated API corpus, and the API collector cannot be rebuilt now: Calacci scraped in March 2020 before Ring shut off access. ([contemporaneous description of the shutdown](https://just-tech.ssrc.org/articles/is-the-next-misinformation-crisis-fear-based-and-local/); [original collection method](https://www.dcalacci.net/papers/Calacci%20et%20al.%20-%202022%20-%20The%20Cop%20In%20Your%20Neighbor%27s%20Doorbell%20Amazon%20Ring%20a.pdf)). The nearest public substitute is a combination of agency-held alert emails, Ring's current public feed/incident map, ACS data, and local open crime data. ([current public-feed description](https://ring.com/support/verified-help/articles/ydpm0/Navigating-the-Feed)). That substitute loses a complete national post denominator, consistent historical collection, stable post/user IDs, and the approximate coordinates needed for a reproducible 25-city spatial join.
- **Detection signature**: **participation-to-ground-truth geographic diff** — geocoded Neighbors posts were spatially joined to ACS tracts on `latitude/longitude → tract GEOID`, converted to `posts per 1,000 residents` by local income decile, and compared with Los Angeles `burglary-from-vehicle / simple-battery incidents per 1,000` on the same income bins; the mismatch revealed that affluent posting intensity, not higher observed crime in the tested categories, drove the louder signal sent toward police.
- **Corroboration structure**: The broad API corpus established participation; Census and open crime records supplied independent denominators; the LAPD mailbox proved institutional receipt; current and former user interviews tested notice; a Bonferroni-adjusted threshold reduced multiple-city false positives. Limitations were explicit: the API and LAPD data cover different periods, coordinates are approximate, tract-border duplicates required deterministic assignment, crime data captures only reported incidents, and five cities showed the opposite income trend. ([tests and limitations](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments))
- **Methodology notes**: The shared [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/neighborhood-watch/2023/10/11/how-we-investigated-rings-crime-alert-system-for-police-departments) states the data schema, cleaning, spatial join, 25-city sample, deciles, regressions/Pearson tests, correction threshold, crime comparison, and limitations. **No story-specific GitHub repository or public copy of the post-level corpus was released**; The Markup withheld it for privacy and offered restricted research access. The decisive detector is **not re-runnable from released artifacts alone**.
- **Official impact**: No authority or company action was found that was expressly attributed to this story. Ring's current agency/feed architecture remains relevant context, but its later product changes do not establish causal impact. ([current agency/feed documentation](https://ring.com/support/verified-help/articles/ydpm0/Navigating-the-Feed))
- **Generalization**: Apply to 311 apps, fraud hotlines, school tip tools, patient portals, neighborhood forums, regulatory complaints, or crowdsourced maps. Generic detector: spatially join who submits signals to population and resource denominators; join the same geography to independently observed harm; then trace which submissions reach enforcement or allocation systems. The key question is not merely “who is harmed?” but “whose observations become institutional input?”

### Crime Prediction Software Promised to Be Free of Biases. New Data Shows It Perpetuates Them (2021) — Millions of actual PredPol outputs concentrated recommended patrols in poorer, Black, and Latino neighborhoods

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2021/12/02/crime-prediction-software-promised-to-be-free-of-biases-new-data-shows-it-perpetuates-them); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods); [code/data repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-prediction-bias)
- **Partner/awards**: Coreported with **Gizmodo**. Prediction: Bias won the 2022 national Edward R. Murrow Award for investigative reporting and the National Association of Black Journalists' 2022 Salute to Excellence Award in digital-media business reporting. ([partner credit](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2021/12/02/crime-prediction-software-promised-to-be-free-of-biases-new-data-shows-it-perpetuates-them); [awards](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2022/10/25/the-markup-receives-honors-from-adweek-and-the-national-edward-r-murrow-awards))
- **What they found**:
  - The final corpus contained **more than 5.9 million predictions for 38 U.S. law-enforcement agencies** over nearly three years. Neighborhoods receiving fewer or zero predictions tended to be Whiter and wealthier; about 10 percent of analyzed block groups had zero predictions. ([scope and top-line pattern](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2021/12/02/crime-prediction-software-promised-to-be-free-of-biases-new-data-shows-it-perpetuates-them); [methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
  - In **32 of 38 jurisdictions (84 percent)**, the most-targeted block groups contained a higher proportion of Black or Latino residents than the jurisdiction overall. Averaged across jurisdictions, the most-targeted groups' Black and Latino proportions were 28 and 16 percent higher, while their White proportion was 17 percent lower. ([disparate-impact results](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
  - In **27 jurisdictions**, a disproportionate share of households earning $45,000 or less lived in the most-targeted block groups. In 22 jurisdictions, more than three quarters of identified public/subsidized housing facilities lay in groups targeted above the median; in 10, PredPol generated predictions around public housing nearly every day of local use. ([income and housing analysis](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
  - More than 100 public-records requests produced over **600,000 arrest, stop, and use-of-force records**, but only 11 departments supplied usable arrest data and almost none supplied reliable patrol-response (“dosage”) data. In those 11, high-prediction areas already had high arrest rates and higher Black/Latino populations; the study did **not** establish that PredPol caused particular arrests or force. ([policing-data scope and causal limit](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
- **Finding type(s)**: **proxy-amplified disparate exposure** (new tag: a system omits protected traits yet reproduces unequal treatment through historically structured inputs); **feedback-loop institutionalization** (new tag: existing enforcement data directs future enforcement back toward the same places); **transparency-without-response data** (new tag: outputs are visible, but missing action/dosage records block causal claims about implementation).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Open but inadvertently exposed operational output** — a public LAPD webpage linked to an unauthenticated PredPol AWS space containing PDFs, HTML, and geospatial reports. Gizmodo downloaded it in June 2020 and again in January 2021; after filtering non-U.S., short, trial, and out-of-contract periods, 5.9 million predictions in 38 jurisdictions remained. The portal was later secured. ([discovery and filtering](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
  - **Census and housing denominators** — 2018 ACS race and household-income estimates, 2010 Census blocks for stability checks, jurisdiction/patrol maps, Census Reporter, and 4,001 HUD-listed subsidized/public housing and shelter locations. ([demographic and housing methods](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
  - **Public-record enforcement outcomes** — local arrest, stop, use-of-force, crime, and dosage records, plus FBI UCR arrest rates, provided context and tested whether predicted locations merely mirrored existing police activity. ([records and analyses](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
  - **Contract and agency verification** — contracts, agencies, and media records established each department's real use dates; predictions outside verified contract windows were excluded. PredPol authenticated the reports as appearing to come from its software while disputing completeness. ([date validation and company response](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
  - **Affected-community and expert reporting** — residents, police officials, defense lawyers, researchers, and advocates linked output patterns to public housing, reporting behavior, disclosure problems, and bounded real-world consequences. ([main investigation](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2021/12/02/crime-prediction-software-promised-to-be-free-of-biases-new-data-shows-it-perpetuates-them))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today for fresh acquisition.** This is not class (b): discovery did not require an insider or leak—the source was an unpassworded page linked by a police department—and The Markup subsequently released the decisive inputs. The [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-prediction-bias) includes the R/Targets pipeline, parsed predictions, demographic and enforcement inputs, raw-report archive, outputs, and jurisdiction datasheets. The original bucket is now closed and Geolitica ceased operations at the end of 2023, so a fresh same-vendor output census cannot follow the 2020 path. ([bucket closure](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods); [shutdown and successor context](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2023/10/02/predictive-policing-software-terrible-at-predicting-crimes))
- **Detection signature**: **decision-footprint demographic gradient** — each prediction point was geocoded and joined to `Census block group / jurisdiction` on coordinates, then prediction counts were ranked into `top, median, bottom, and decile` exposure bins; those bins were compared with `race / ethnicity / household-income / public-housing` composition, revealing exposure that increased with Black, Latino, and lower-income population share and decreased with White and higher-income share.
- **Corroboration structure**: Vendor outputs established what the system recommended; contract/use-date checks removed “zombie” and trial records; ACS and HUD supplied independent denominators; continuous correlation and binned comparisons tested the same gradient; a stable-block sensitivity analysis showed the pattern persisted at smaller geography; public enforcement data showed alignment with preexisting policing but did not support causal attribution. PredPol received the data and method before publication and did not dispute report appearance. ([robustness and response](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods))
- **Methodology notes**: The unusually detailed [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/12/02/how-we-determined-crime-prediction-software-disproportionately-targeted-low-income-black-and-latino-neighborhoods) states every acquisition, geography, binning, correlation, income, housing, policing, limitation, and response step. [`investigation-prediction-bias`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-prediction-bias) says `tar_make()` rebuilds the pipeline from the released `in` archive and should reproduce committed outputs. - **Official impact**: In January 2024, seven Democratic members of Congress cited mounting evidence on discriminatory and ineffective predictive policing and asked DOJ to halt funding absent safeguards, audit more than a decade of grants, and assess accuracy and bias. The Markup's impact update expressly tied the request to both Prediction: Bias investigations. - **Generalization**: Apply to risk scores, inspection routing, fraud flags, child-welfare referrals, health allocation, school discipline, and credit or insurance triage. Generic detector: obtain actual outputs—not a recreated model—join the decision footprint to local population and resource denominators, compare exposure across the full distribution, validate the institution's use window, and keep “where the model pointed” separate from “what staff did.”

### Predictive Policing Software Terrible at Predicting Crimes (2023) — Geolitica's Plainfield forecasts matched later reported crime in the predicted type, place, and time less than one percent of the time

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2023/10/02/predictive-policing-software-terrible-at-predicting-crimes); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software); [code/data repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-geolitica-plainfield)
- **Partner/awards**: Copublished with **WIRED**. No award specific to this follow-up was identified; the earlier Prediction: Bias investigation won the Murrow and NABJ awards described above. ([partner credit](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2023/10/02/predictive-policing-software-terrible-at-predicting-crimes); [series award scope](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2022/10/25/the-markup-receives-honors-from-adweek-and-the-national-edward-r-murrow-awards))
- **What they found**:
  - After excluding prediction boxes with recorded police presence, **23,631 forecasts** from February 25 through December 18, 2018 produced fewer than 100 type/place/time matches with later reported crimes—a success rate below **0.5 percent**. Robbery/aggravated-assault forecasts succeeded **0.6 percent** of the time and burglary forecasts **0.1 percent**. ([headline accuracy results](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2023/10/02/predictive-policing-software-terrible-at-predicting-crimes); [calculation method](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software))
  - Reversing the denominator, Geolitica correctly anticipated only **34 of 336 (10 percent)** later reported crimes in the types it forecast. If any reported-crime type counted as a hit, only 18 percent of predictions had an event in the box. ([recall and broad-match analyses](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software))
  - Plainfield averaged roughly **80 predictions per day**, while no day in the study period had more than 22 reported crimes of any type. The city paid $20,500 for the first annual subscription and $15,500 for an extension; officials said the tool was rarely, if ever, used to direct patrols and had been dropped. ([volume, contract, and agency response](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2023/10/02/predictive-policing-software-terrible-at-predicting-crimes))
  - Plainfield reported visiting only **129 of 23,760 prediction locations**. None of five arrests occurring in prediction areas could plausibly be attributed to officers responding to a forecast, and none of the other 37 agencies from the earlier investigation identified an arrest directly caused by a Geolitica prediction. ([dosage and arrest trace](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2023/10/02/predictive-policing-software-terrible-at-predicting-crimes))
- **Finding type(s)**: **forecast-without-skill** (new tag: a marketed prediction system produces many outputs but rarely aligns with later observed events under its own target definition); **volume-inflated apparent coverage** (new tag: issuing many overlapping forecasts can make some events appear “covered” while precision remains negligible); **unused-procurement signal** (new tag: an agency pays for a system but operational records and officials show little implementation).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Vendor prediction ledger** — the Plainfield subset from the 2021 PredPol release contained 23,760 dated, typed prediction boxes linked to four police shifts. ([prediction data](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software))
  - **Observed-event ledger via public records** — Plainfield supplied crime reports; after removing ambiguous/out-of-city records and truncating the period before a suspicious December drop-off, 3,066 reports remained, including 336 in forecastable categories. ([crime-data cleaning](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software))
  - **Intervention/dosage ledger via public records** — dispatcher records identified 212 unique patrol-presence entries averaging about 20 minutes and matching 129 prediction locations, which were excluded from the principal precision analysis to honor the vendor's deterrence claim. ([dosage data and exclusion](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software))
  - **Contracts, agency statements, and arrest trace** — subscription records established cost; Plainfield officials described actual use; the 38-department follow-up asked for any arrest directly resulting from a prediction. ([main story](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2023/10/02/predictive-policing-software-terrible-at-predicting-crimes))
  - **Manual and geospatial classification** — reporters manually mapped 336 report narratives to Geolitica's four forecast classes, expanded nominal 500-foot boxes to a generous 600 feet, and used GeoPandas to test location containment. ([crime-type and spatial matching](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today for fresh same-vendor acquisition.** The exact historical analysis is public: prediction data, Plainfield crime and dosage inputs, manual exclusions, processing scripts, requirements, Makefile, and notebook are in [`investigation-geolitica-plainfield`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-geolitica-plainfield). A new Geolitica cohort cannot be collected because the firm ceased operations at the end of 2023 and its customers were transitioned toward SoundThinking products; an equivalent contemporary audit must request a successor vendor's outputs, incident reports, and response/dosage log from agencies. ([shutdown and transition](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2023/10/02/predictive-policing-software-terrible-at-predicting-crimes))
- **Detection signature**: **intervention-excluded spatiotemporal forecast match** — each forecast box was joined to reported incidents on the compound key `crime class + 600-foot spatial containment + assigned 11-hour-15-minute shift`; forecasts with recorded officer dosage were removed, revealing precision below 0.5 percent. Reversing the join from `forecast → event` to `event → prior forecast` revealed recall of 34/336 for forecastable crimes.
- **Corroboration structure**: Three primary ledgers supplied forecast, event, and intervention; generous larger boxes biased toward counting more hits; both dosage-included and dosage-excluded runs yielded similar results; manual report review established type comparability; the agency confirmed low use; cost and arrest records tested operational value. Limits remain: “crime” means reported crime, underreporting varies by group, and the method cannot observe unreported incidents. ([robustness and limitations](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software))
- **Methodology notes**: The [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/10/02/how-we-assessed-the-accuracy-of-predictive-policing-software) states the data period, exclusions, class mapping, spatial rule, two denominators, and limitations. [`investigation-geolitica-plainfield`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-geolitica-plainfield) releases inputs and processed data plus a `make reproduce` pipeline. The decisive detector is **re-runnable from released artifacts alone**.
- **Official impact**: The January 2024 congressional letter requesting a DOJ funding halt and audit cited the combined evidence that predictive-policing systems were discriminatory and did not reduce crime; The Markup's impact page expressly summarized this follow-up's sub-one-percent result. ([impact update](https://themarkup.org/prediction-bias/2024/01/29/senators-demand-justice-department-halt-funding-to-predictive-policing-programs))
- **Generalization**: This is the portable “score the scorer” pattern. For forecasts of fraud, inspections, defaults, disease, maintenance, recidivism, or demand, obtain timestamped predictions, a later outcome ledger, and any intervention log. Match on the vendor's declared type/time/place horizon, report both precision and recall, exclude or stratify intervened cases, and run a deliberately generous robustness version so a low score cannot be blamed on a narrow match rule.

### Payday Lenders Are Big Winners in Utah's Chatroom Justice Program (2022) — Mandatory online dispute resolution increased default judgments in one court, almost entirely in business-filed cases

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/remote-justice/2022/03/16/payday-lenders-are-big-winners-in-utahs-chatroom-justice-program); [released data and methodology](https://github.com/the-markup/Online-dispute-resolution)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no co-publisher or story-specific award was identified on the [Remote Justice series page](https://themarkup.org/series/remote-justice) or The Markup's [awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - In West Valley City Justice Court, default judgments rose from about **43 percent before ODR to 59 percent after** its September 2018 launch. Holding the prior rate constant would have produced **603 fewer defaults** during the roughly two-and-a-half-year ODR window analyzed. ([pre/post result](https://themarkup.org/remote-justice/2022/03/16/payday-lenders-are-big-winners-in-utahs-chatroom-justice-program))
  - The increase was concentrated in cases filed by institutions: their default rate rose from **46 to 62 percent**, equivalent to 583 additional defaults above the old rate, while the individual-plaintiff rate remained around one in five. In Orem, the only other court with a usable comparison period, defaults declined from 51 to 46 percent—important evidence that the direction was not universal. ([plaintiff-stratified and court comparison](https://themarkup.org/remote-justice/2022/03/16/payday-lenders-are-big-winners-in-utahs-chatroom-justice-program))
  - Five payday lenders—Money 4 You, Mr Money, Tosh Inc./Check City, Dollar Loan Center, and Action Rent to Own—filed **83 percent of all West Valley City cases** from September 2018 through January 2021. Action Rent to Own's default rate rose from 49 to 65 percent across hundreds of cases. ([filer concentration](https://themarkup.org/remote-justice/2022/03/16/payday-lenders-are-big-winners-in-utahs-chatroom-justice-program))
  - Utah was the only jurisdiction The Markup reviewed where failure to register for ODR could itself yield automatic default; the operative summons buried the requirement on page three, imposed a 14-day deadline, and offered two long, inconsistent URLs. Samantha Thompson missed it, then faced a $995.42 judgment on a $671.42 loan-and-interest balance and wage garnishment. ([process comparison and named case](https://themarkup.org/remote-justice/2022/03/16/payday-lenders-are-big-winners-in-utahs-chatroom-justice-program))
- **Finding type(s)**: **procedural-default trap** (new tag: an access reform creates a new procedural step whose noncompletion automatically causes loss); **repeat-player capture** (new tag: institutions familiar with a system gain disproportionate benefit over one-time participants); **digital-burden shift** (new tag: convenience for the institution is purchased by moving navigation, registration, and deadline risk onto the affected person).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Statewide case-disposition data via public records** — Utah Courts supplied all small-claims cases for the two years before ODR and ODR-court cases after launch. The published repository contains 16,808 pre-ODR and 114,208 post-launch party/case rows. ([GitHub method and data dictionary](https://github.com/the-markup/Online-dispute-resolution))
  - **Policy-cutover construction** — pending cases and incomparably coded Carbon County government cases were removed; post data was right-censored at January 31, 2021; plaintiff names were normalized; court + case number formed a unique ID; dispositions were reduced to default/other; plaintiffs were classified as institutional or individual. ([cleaning and cohort method](https://github.com/the-markup/Online-dispute-resolution))
  - **Court/process comparison** — West Valley and Orem supplied within-court pre/post cohorts; other states' voluntary or hearing-fallback systems supplied a rule comparison; summons versions and Utah's 2020 commissioned usability study documented interface friction. ([main story process comparison](https://themarkup.org/remote-justice/2022/03/16/payday-lenders-are-big-winners-in-utahs-chatroom-justice-program))
  - **Case files and human impact** — dozens of lender motions filed immediately after missed registration deadlines, Samantha Thompson's summons and judgment, borrower interviews, lender responses, court officials, debt-law experts, and academic usability researchers linked the aggregate discontinuity to mechanism and consequence. ([case-level corroboration](https://themarkup.org/remote-justice/2022/03/16/payday-lenders-are-big-winners-in-utahs-chatroom-justice-program))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The decisive pre/post case ledgers came from public records and are now fully released with a data dictionary and transformation instructions. Utah's program remains live: the current court page lists participating courts, requires defendants to register within 14 days or “lose automatically,” and now describes exemptions for disability, lack of internet, or language difficulty. ([current Utah Courts instructions](https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/consumer/small-claims.html)). A current replication can request the same disposition fields and extend the post period, while treating later summons, exemption, and court changes as new policy eras.
- **Detection signature**: **policy-cutover repeat-player diff** — small-claims cases were joined within court on `court + case ID`, split on `filing date before/after ODR`, and compared on `default disposition`; stratifying by `institutional versus individual plaintiff` revealed that West Valley's 16-point default increase sat almost entirely in repeat-player cases, while a second eligible court supplied a directionally opposite comparison.
- **Corroboration structure**: State microdata established the cutover; right-censoring reduced disposition-time bias; Orem showed the increase was not inevitable; plaintiff-name normalization exposed filer concentration; motions and a named summons demonstrated the 14-day mechanism; the commissioned usability study had independently predicted that the summons would prevent engagement. Limitations were preserved: court disposition coding varied, service failures could not be identified, and the analysis was descriptive rather than a causal controlled experiment. ([method and cautions](https://github.com/the-markup/Online-dispute-resolution))
- **Methodology notes**: There is **no separate Show Your Work page**. **[inferred from released methods package]** The public [`Online-dispute-resolution`](https://github.com/the-markup/Online-dispute-resolution) repository serves that function: it releases both raw CSVs, exact cohort/cleaning/classification/pivot steps, a data dictionary, and a BSD-3-Clause license. It does not release executable analysis code, but the decisive rates, filer counts, and plaintiff stratification are **re-runnable from the released artifacts alone** using the specified pivots.
- **Official impact**: Before publication, Utah replaced the criticized summons with a shorter URL, QR code, and phone number, but still placed ODR notice on page three; the story does not claim credit for that change. As of July 2026, ODR and the 14-day default consequence remain in current court guidance, alongside an exemption request. ([contemporaneous change](https://themarkup.org/remote-justice/2022/03/16/payday-lenders-are-big-winners-in-utahs-chatroom-justice-program); [current official process](https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/consumer/small-claims.html))
- **Generalization**: Audit any digitized procedure—benefits recertification, tax filing, debt collection, licensing, immigration, health enrollment, insurance appeal, or procurement protest—at the policy cutover. Build matched pre/post cohorts within the same office, right-censor unfinished cases, stratify by repeat-player status and representation, compare a second office with different timing, and inspect whether an interface failure has been converted into a substantive loss.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### P1. Normative-output conformance audit

- **Mechanics**: Convert the governing law, rulebook, contract promise, or reform objective into testable predicates; obtain the operational decision log; join each decision to the exact predicate and independently verify the underlying object or event.
- **Minimum data**: Versioned rule/promise text; timestamped outputs with stable IDs or URLs; a content/event classification source; institution and user-class fields.
- **Recognition cues**: A narrow public justification paired with broad category controls; vendor labels treated as truth; “safety” or “access” claims without error rates; no direct appeal route; internal records that use different language from public descriptions.
- **Any-domain applications**: Sanctions screening, benefits eligibility, prohibited procurement, content moderation, medical prior authorization, environmental enforcement, insurance exclusions.

#### P2. Forecast-to-truth audit with intervention exclusion

- **Mechanics**: Join timestamped predictions to later observed events on the system's declared type/place/time horizon; separately join any action taken because of the prediction; report precision and recall, then exclude or stratify intervened cases.
- **Minimum data**: Prediction ledger, outcome ledger, intervention/dosage ledger, match horizon, and stable geography or subject IDs.
- **Recognition cues**: Very high forecast volume; vendor claims that action prevents the predicted event; only success anecdotes; agencies that retain outputs but not response logs; a product sold for years without independent validation.
- **Any-domain applications**: Fraud, defaults, inspections, recidivism, equipment failure, disease outbreaks, hospital readmission, demand forecasting.

#### P3. Participation-to-enforcement amplification audit

- **Mechanics**: Measure who submits reports or complaints; normalize by local population or eligible users; compare submission rates with independent harm/event rates; trace which submissions enter enforcement, allocation, or prioritization systems.
- **Minimum data**: Post/tip/complaint ledger with geography or user attributes, population denominators, independent incident data, and a receiving-agency log.
- **Recognition cues**: “Community-driven” surveillance; unequal technology adoption; affluent or organized repeat submitters; automated forwarding to government; users unaware of secondary institutional audiences.
- **Any-domain applications**: 311, code enforcement, school tips, benefits-fraud hotlines, public-comment systems, patient complaints, crowdsourced hazard maps.

#### P4. Policy-cutover burden-shift audit

- **Mechanics**: Construct comparable cohorts before and after a process/interface change within the same office; right-censor unresolved cases; stratify by representation, repeat-player status, and user vulnerability; compare another office or staggered rollout.
- **Minimum data**: Case-level filing and disposition dates, office, party type, policy start date, pending status, and ideally service/notice/representation fields.
- **Recognition cues**: Digitization framed as access or efficiency; missed registration causing substantive loss; institutional filers with hundreds of cases; default or abandonment spikes immediately at launch; long delays in implementing usability fixes.
- **Any-domain applications**: Courts, licensing, benefits, tax, immigration, insurance appeals, student enrollment, procurement challenges.

#### P5. Distributed records census with edge validation

- **Mechanics**: Select a bounded multi-jurisdiction target frame; issue the same structured record request; preserve refusals as data; normalize heterogeneous responses; validate a sample at the real user edge with an authorized, fixed test instrument.
- **Minimum data**: Target-frame rationale, request template, response/refusal ledger, shared schema, authorized test locations, fixed test objects, and timestamps.
- **Recognition cues**: Local discretion hidden behind the same federal/state mandate; vendors call configuration proprietary; agencies invoke security against aggregate operational records; affected users can cheaply test visible outcomes.
- **Any-domain applications**: School filters, public Wi-Fi, jail communications, government chatbots, benefits portals, library databases, hospital pricing tools.

### 5. What this platform could run now, and what is missing

**Runnable or substantially runnable with current holdings**

- **P1, normative-output conformance**, is the strongest immediate fit where both sides already exist. OpenSanctions plus Companies House/OpenCorporates/GLEIF/ICIJ can be joined to USASpending/FPDS recipients to find awards involving sanctioned, dissolved, or concealed affiliates. CourtListener/RECAP can provide an adjudicated baseline against corporate status, professional claims, or later public awards. Property/deed data can test whether a declared entity or address function matches observed ownership and transactions.
- **P4, policy-cutover burden shift**, is partly runnable in litigation and procurement. CourtListener/RECAP can identify rule dates and case outcomes; USASpending/FPDS can identify policy-era awards and repeat vendors. A credible court implementation still needs more structured filing/disposition/service data than appellate opinions alone usually provide.
- **P3, participation-to-enforcement amplification**, can be adapted to public institutional participation rather than Ring: 990 officers/grants, procurement bidders/awardees, patent applicants/assignees, and litigation parties/counsel can reveal whose repeated participation becomes institutional selection. The current holdings can build denominators and affiliation graphs, though they cannot recreate a closed social feed.
- **P5, distributed records census**, can use the platform's registries, property, CourtListener, spending, patents, FAA, nonprofit, and blockchain tools to choose targets and prefill identifiers. The actual record-request, refusal, and local edge-test layer is not yet among the stated holdings.

**Not currently runnable without new decisive inputs**

- **P2, forecast-to-truth**, generally needs the model's actual historical outputs and an intervention/dosage log. None of the listed holdings supplies police patrol predictions, benefits-fraud scores, risk flags, or staff response telemetry. Public contracts can reveal the vendor and agency, but not score the system.
- A fresh **Ring participation audit** lacks a complete, geolocated post corpus and stable API. Current public feeds and agency emails are partial substitutes, not a population-level denominator.
- A fresh **school-filter census** needs public-records acquisition, standardized URL/category-log ingestion, and authorized students/teachers or field testers on school networks. The platform's current corporate and government databases do not observe the access edge.
- A durable **FOIA/public-records audit layer** is missing: request templates, jurisdictional deadlines/exemptions, response/version storage, PST/mbox and heterogeneous-export ingestion, refusal coding, appeal tracking, and provenance-preserving publication.
- A **distributed test-panel system** is missing: consent and authorization controls, per-location test assignments, safe URL manifests, client-side reachability checks, screenshot capture, duplicate/fraud controls, and rules preventing sensitive user data from becoming part of the evidence package.

The cluster's most transferable lesson is to separate five ledgers—**norm, operational output, observed event, intervention, and affected population**. The strongest findings arose not from any ledger alone but from a precise join between two or more: school blocks against lawful site purpose, “crime” alerts against criminal law, platform participation against crime rates, predictions against population and later incidents, and procedural reform against defaults and repeat-player identity.
