# Platform Ranking, Moderation & Information Ecosystems

Structured evidence-pattern extraction from ten The Markup investigations, compiled July 29, 2026.

## Scope and attribution

This cluster follows the census's [domain-structural attribution rule](report-10-census.md#1-method-and-sources): it counts the dated editorial work exposed on `themarkup.org`, including a named co-publication, but does not create a second card for a CalMatters copy, syndication copy, or translation. The sample deliberately combines the census-flagged pages with the instrument-heavy work around them: one inbox audit, four Google search/advertising audits, three Citizen Browser analyses, one Instagram field experiment, and one multilingual information-ecosystem investigation.

“Re-runnable from released artifacts alone” is narrower than “the instrument can be rebuilt.” The former asks whether a third party can reproduce the published decisive calculation without obtaining new evidence; the latter asks whether a new present-day measurement is possible. The input-dependency labels follow the four classes in the assignment. Finding-type names below are free-form and defined on first use.

### Swinging the Vote? (2020) — Gmail's default classifier sent almost nine in ten sampled political emails away from the primary inbox

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/wheres-my-email)
- **Partner/awards**: Copublished with [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2020/feb/26/gmail-algorithm-political-email-inbox); no story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - In a four-month experiment, The Markup received **5,134 emails from 171 political groups and politicians**. Only **11%** reached the primary inbox; **50%** went to Promotions and **40%** to spam. Excluding senders represented by fewer than two messages, **42%** never reached Primary at all ([method](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/show-your-work-wheres-my-email#key-takeaways)).
  - The candidate differences were large in the model account: Pete Buttigieg reached Primary **63%** of the time and Andrew Yang **47%**, while Elizabeth Warren, Joe Biden, Kamala Harris, and several others were at **0%**. Candidate mail overall reached Primary **6%**, compared with **9%** for the other political and advocacy mail sampled ([story and candidate table](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/wheres-my-email#which-presidential-candidates-emails-landed-in-gmails-primary-inbox)).
  - A separate longitudinal check of data supplied by Change.org, CREDO, Democracy for America, and SumOfUs found Gmail-relative open rates fell by at least **47%** between January and May 2018; Change.org's relative decline was **79%**. The team inspected the raw rates and found no statistically significant contemporaneous increase among non-Gmail subscribers ([method](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/show-your-work-wheres-my-email#introduction)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Default-channel gate** — an opaque default routes lawful material away from the interface users see first, before personalization or an affirmative user choice.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Constructed inbox telemetry** — a fresh Gmail account, created with a new phone number and Tor, subscribed to more than 200 senders; unique plus-address aliases identified which subscription produced each message. Google Takeout supplied the mailbox files and Gmail labels ([collection protocol](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/show-your-work-wheres-my-email#email-data)).
  - **Donated operational data** — historical open-rate series from four advocacy organizations, separated into Gmail and non-Gmail recipients; raw absolute rates were inspected but withheld under the source agreement ([analysis and disclosure](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/show-your-work-wheres-my-email#introduction)).
  - **Validation and interviews** — a 400-message manual attribution check found four alias mismatches; organizer interviews and Google's response supplied consequence and platform-policy context ([quality check](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/show-your-work-wheres-my-email#email-attribution)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable (constructed instrument); runnable today.** The published detector can be rerun from the released mailboxes, cleaned tables, and notebooks, while a new outside investigator can still create a controlled inbox and retrieve system labels. Google's current Gmail API still documents `CATEGORY_PROMOTIONS`, `CATEGORY_SOCIAL`, and `SPAM` labels ([Gmail labels guide](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/labels)). The path remains live, though a new account is only a model case and Gmail's personalized classifier may produce different results.
- **Detection signature**: **Zero-history inbox-placement audit** — one standardized, interaction-free inbox joined incoming message aliases to Gmail system labels on message ID, then compared Primary/Promotions/Spam shares by sender and candidate; the sender-level zero-Primary tail revealed the gate.
- **Corroboration structure**: The fresh-account outcome measures the default classifier directly; four organizations' Gmail-versus-non-Gmail open-rate break independently establishes a real-world change consistent with diversion, while interviews document lost petition and fundraising activity. It does **not** prove why Gmail made each classification or population-wide treatment: The Markup explicitly limited the experiment to one account ([limitations](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/show-your-work-wheres-my-email#limitations)).
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/show-your-work-wheres-my-email) page and [`investigation-wheres-my-email`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-wheres-my-email) repository release the collection products, 5,134-message cleaned data, processing code, and notebooks. **Historical detector re-runnable from artifacts alone: yes**; the only nonreleased component is the four groups' absolute open-rate data, so that corroborating analysis cannot be independently recomputed in full.
- **Official impact**: No platform remedy was attributed to this story. Google said the categories help users organize mail and rejected the suggestion that paid political placement in Primary was on its roadmap ([response](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/02/26/wheres-my-email)).
- **Generalization**: Apply to any default classifier—Focused Inbox, app-notification priority, marketplace “recommended” queues, fraud holds—by opening zero-history accounts, sending traceable matched inputs, preserving raw output labels, and measuring diversion before user behavior can confound the test.

### Google Ad Portal Equated “Black Girls” with Porn (2020) — A matched race-and-gender query exposed sexualized keyword recommendations and a broken adult filter

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/23/google-advertising-keywords-black-girls)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no partner or story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Google's Keyword Planner returned hundreds of ideas—many pornographic—for “Black girls,” “Latina girls,” and “Asian girls,” and similar results for boys. The same tool returned **no suggested terms** for “White girls” or “White boys” in the test ([story](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/23/google-advertising-keywords-black-girls)).
  - For “Black girls,” the tool returned **435** ideas. Google's adult-idea filter marked **203**; among the remaining **232**, the reporters found many still led predominantly to self-described porn sites when searched on Google ([analysis](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/23/google-advertising-keywords-black-girls#google-blocked-some-but-not-all-pornographic-keywords)).
  - After The Markup sought comment, Google blocked combinations of racial or ethnic terms with “girls” and “boys” and acknowledged that its safeguards had not worked as intended ([platform response](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/23/google-advertising-keywords-black-girls#update)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Matched-input disparity** — otherwise identical requests that differ only in an identity-bearing token receive systematically different recommendations or enforcement.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Controlled product tests** — Keyword Planner exports collected July 8–12, 2020, both with and without Google's “adult ideas” filter, for matched race/ethnicity and gender seeds ([repository inputs](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-google-keyword-planner/tree/master/data)).
  - **Rendered-search validation** — the team saved Google-result HTML for roughly **1,900** recommended keywords and classified the dominant sites returned, rather than accepting Google's adult flag as ground truth ([repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-google-keyword-planner)).
  - **Manual domain coding** — 132 of the 200 most frequent domains in the checked result sets were identified as pornographic from their own descriptions or content ([story methodology](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/23/google-advertising-keywords-black-girls#how-we-did-our-analysis)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable (constructed instrument); runnable today.** Historical exports, search-result captures, domain coding, and notebooks are public. Google still offers Keyword Planner, although an investigator needs a Google Ads account with campaign setup and billing information to unlock full planning features ([current Google Ads instructions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9247190?hl=en)). The exact 2020 disparity was patched, so today's run tests whether the fix persists or a new asymmetry has appeared.
- **Detection signature**: **Matched-seed recommendation asymmetry** — identity-substituted seed phrases joined to Keyword Planner's recommended terms on request settings, then diffed by output count, adult-filter flag, and the content class of corresponding Google results, revealed both disparate generation and filter leakage.
- **Corroboration structure**: Three layers narrow the claim: the portal produced asymmetric recommendations; Google's own filter failed to mark much of the sexual content; independent inspection of the corresponding search results verified that the residuals were not merely ambiguous words. Google's block and admission corroborate the product defect, not an intent to sexualize particular groups.
- **Methodology notes**: There is no separately titled Show Your Work page; the story's [“How we did our analysis” section](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/23/google-advertising-keywords-black-girls#how-we-did-our-analysis) links the [`investigation-google-keyword-planner`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-google-keyword-planner) repository. It includes raw portal exports, saved search pages, coded domains, and notebooks. **Historical detector re-runnable from artifacts alone: yes.**
- **Official impact**: Google changed the product after the inquiry, blocking race/ethnicity-plus-child-gender combinations and stating that the filter “did not work as intended” ([update](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/23/google-advertising-keywords-black-girls#update)).
- **Generalization**: Use matched semantic seeds to audit ad planners, autocomplete, risk scores, job recommendations, or government lookup portals. Change one protected-class token, retain every suggestion and suppression state, and validate apparent harmful output against an independent content source.

### Google’s Top Search Result? Surprise! It’s Google (2020) — Measuring rendered screen area showed Google products dominated its own first page

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/google-search-results-prioritize-google-products-over-competitors)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; the work was later cited in a House antitrust investigation ([impact follow-up](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/06/11/lawmakers-propose-federal-legislation-to-stop-google-preferencing-itself-in-search-results)).
- **What they found**:
  - Across **15,269** popular Google Trends queries collected from November 2019 through January 2020, Google-owned properties and Google's “direct answers” occupied **41%** of the first mobile result page and **63%** of the first screen, on average. About **one in five** pages had no external link visible before scrolling ([story](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/google-search-results-prioritize-google-products-over-competitors)).
  - Non-Google results occupied **44.8%** of the full page. Google's AMP format appeared on **80.7%** of sampled queries and occupied **13.3%** of total page area, showing that nominally external results could still pass through Google-controlled presentation ([method and category results](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/how-we-analyzed-google-search-results-web-assay-parsing-tool#results)).
  - A manual review of **741** pages found 74 parser or measurement errors (**9.99%**), including **1.89%** classification and **8.77%** measurement error; the error audit made the limitations of the spatial detector visible rather than treating parsed HTML as exact ([quality assurance](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/how-we-analyzed-google-search-results-web-assay-parsing-tool#quality-assurance)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Self-preferencing by surface share** — ownership advantage is expressed not only in rank order but in how much high-attention interface real estate the platform reserves for its own products.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Public query frame** — trending Google searches sampled every day from Google Trends, deduplicated into 15,269 usable topics ([sampling](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/how-we-analyzed-google-search-results-web-assay-parsing-tool#collecting-search-terms)).
  - **Constructed browser census** — Selenium and Firefox rendered each query at an iPhone X-sized **375×812** viewport; the instrument saved HTML and screenshots and recorded element bounding boxes ([Web Assay](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/how-we-analyzed-google-search-results-web-assay-parsing-tool#web-assay)).
  - **Ownership and product coding** — **68 custom parsers** classified modules as Google product, direct answer, AMP, advertisement, or non-Google result; pixel area and vertical position converted the page into a spatial dataset ([parsing method](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/how-we-analyzed-google-search-results-web-assay-parsing-tool#parsing-google-search-results)).
  - **Manual audit** — stratified human review quantified classification, corrupt-page, and bounding-box failures ([quality assurance](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/how-we-analyzed-google-search-results-web-assay-parsing-tool#quality-assurance)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable (constructed instrument); degraded today.** The released HTML, screenshots, intermediate records, and notebooks are sufficient to recompute the 2020 result. Live recollection is public in principle, and Google Trends remains available, but the decisive collector is layout-bound: its 68 selectors predate [AI Overviews' 2024 rollout](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/generative-ai-google-search-may-2024/) and [AI Mode's 2025 expansion](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/google-search-ai-mode-update/). A present-day audit therefore needs a new parser and category map; simply rerunning the repository would silently miss or mismeasure major modules.
- **Detection signature**: **Spatial search-surface assay** — a public query frame joined to rendered DOM elements on query ID, with each element compared by owner, category, vertical position, and pixel occupancy, revealed that first-screen dominance was greater than ordinary link-rank counts suggested.
- **Corroboration structure**: The query census establishes scale; screenshots preserve auditable visual evidence; the spatial parser makes a reproducible measurement; and manual error coding bounds the instrument's mistakes. The result measures one U.S. mobile, logged-out configuration during a fixed period, not all personalized or international search experiences ([limitations](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/how-we-analyzed-google-search-results-web-assay-parsing-tool#limitations)).
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2020/07/28/how-we-analyzed-google-search-results-web-assay-parsing-tool) page documents Web Assay. The [`investigation-google-search-audit`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-google-search-audit) repository releases code, a sample, analysis notebooks, and links to the complete historical HTML and intermediate datasets. **Historical detector re-runnable from artifacts alone: yes; live collector reusable without substantial repair: no.**
- **Official impact**: The House antitrust subcommittee cited the reporting; its final report discussed Google's search self-preferencing, and lawmakers later introduced the American Choice and Innovation Online Act ([The Markup's impact account](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/06/11/lawmakers-propose-federal-legislation-to-stop-google-preferencing-itself-in-search-results)).
- **Generalization**: Replace “rank” with visible-area and attention-weighted measures in procurement portals, registry search, app stores, travel aggregators, or news homepages. The generic detector needs a query frame, rendered pages, element geometry, and an ownership/control crosswalk.

### Google Has a Secret Blocklist that Hides YouTube Hate Videos from Advertisers—But It’s Full of Holes (2021) — Differential API responses exposed a porous ad-placement blocklist

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/google-youtube-hate-videos-ad-keywords-blocklist-failures)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no partner or story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Experts supplied **86** hate terms. At baseline, only **18** produced the portal's blocked state; **10** more blocked in their spaced form could be bypassed by removing spaces, leaving **58** fully available and making almost every nominal block perturbable ([story findings](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/google-youtube-hate-videos-ad-keywords-blocklist-failures)).
  - Terms including “white power,” “blood and soil,” and “racial holy war” returned millions of targetable videos. The first results often involved reporting, but deeper results included overt white-nationalist content—showing that the safety problem concerned the entire eligible result set, not simply offensive search text ([result inspection](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/google-youtube-hate-videos-ad-keywords-blocklist-failures#google-doesnt-block-these-hate-terms)).
  - After being contacted, Google blocked **44** more tested terms, then later removed most of the remaining terms The Markup identified. A controlled ad campaign was accepted as eligible against selected far-right videos but never delivered, accrued a charge, or generated a rejection notice ([platform response and ad test](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/google-youtube-hate-videos-ad-keywords-blocklist-failures#we-tried-to-place-an-ad)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Policy–enforcement mismatch** — a platform's stated safety rule is compared with the actual machine-readable enforcement boundary, including simple transformations that reveal brittle exact-match logic.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Expert vocabulary set** — 86 terms associated with white supremacy, neo-Nazism, and other hate movements, gathered from specialists and then tested rather than assumed harmful solely by the reporters ([term construction](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/how-we-discovered-googles-hate-blocklist-for-ad-placements-on-youtube#identifying-hate-terms)).
  - **Portal/API response corpus** — authenticated Google Ads placement-search responses, stored as full, partial, empty, or explicit-blocked reference states ([response classification](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/how-we-discovered-googles-hate-blocklist-for-ad-placements-on-youtube#testing-the-terms)).
  - **Mutation tests** — spaces removed, plural forms varied, and related phrases tried to identify exact-string vulnerabilities ([story examples](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/google-youtube-hate-videos-ad-keywords-blocklist-failures#the-blocklist-was-easy-to-bypass)).
  - **Controlled paid-ad attempt and content review** — the team created a campaign targeted to identified far-right videos and manually examined result content and delivery state ([ad-test method](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/how-we-discovered-googles-hate-blocklist-for-ad-placements-on-youtube#trying-to-place-an-ad)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable (constructed instrument); degraded today.** The historical terms, raw response examples, scripts, and outputs are public and reproduce the published classification. Google still documents YouTube placement targeting in Google Ads ([current placement instructions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2470108?hl=en)), but after the reporters' questions it changed the response so a blocked query looked like gibberish or a legitimate query with no matching videos. The story explicitly says this removed the discriminator needed for the same outside audit ([countermeasure](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/google-youtube-hate-videos-ad-keywords-blocklist-failures#google-changed-its-code)). A new audit can measure returned targets, but cannot reliably distinguish secret blocking from an empty corpus.
- **Detection signature**: **Block-signal differential fuzzing** — known profanity/blocked controls and nonsensical/empty controls compared to candidate hate terms on the Ads API response-state field, then re-compared after whitespace and morphology mutations, revealed the hidden list and its exact-match holes.
- **Corroboration structure**: Response-state clustering identifies suppression; mutation tests demonstrate mechanism; video inspection shows why missed terms matter; and the paid campaign probes downstream delivery. The non-delivering campaign prevents the stronger claim that ads actually appeared, a limitation the story preserves.
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/how-we-discovered-googles-hate-blocklist-for-ad-placements-on-youtube) page and shared [`investigation-youtube-ad-placements`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-youtube-ad-placements) repository release inputs, reference responses, output JSON, and analysis code. **Historical detector re-runnable from artifacts alone: yes; same live hidden-state detector: no, because Google collapsed the response states.**
- **Official impact**: Google expanded the blocklist during the investigation and changed the portal response. The first change closed many holes; the second also made independent blocklist auditing harder ([story update](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/08/google-youtube-hate-videos-ad-keywords-blocklist-failures#google-changed-its-code)).
- **Generalization**: Hidden eligibility systems often leak their state through error wording, response shape, counts, or latency. Establish known-blocked, known-empty, and known-valid controls; cluster responses; fuzz spacing, punctuation, inflection, and encoding; then verify that an “eligible” item can proceed to the next stage.

### Google Blocks Advertisers from Targeting Black Lives Matter YouTube Videos (2021) — Paired social-justice and hate terms revealed an asymmetric ad-placement blocklist

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/google-blocks-advertisers-from-targeting-black-lives-matter-youtube-videos)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no partner or story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - The final test set contained **62** social-justice terms drawn from 175 suggestions by Color of Change, MediaJustice, Mijente, and Muslim Advocates and vetted with Harvard's Shorenstein Center. At baseline, **17** were fully blocked and three more partly blocked—about **32%** of the set ([method](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/how-we-discovered-googles-social-justice-blocklist-for-youtube-ad-placements#identifying-social-justice-terms)).
  - “Black Lives Matter” produced no targetable suggestions while “White Lives Matter” returned roughly **33 million**; “Black power” returned none while “White power” returned about **169 million**. All tested Muslim-identity terms were blocked even as anti-Muslim phrases such as “white sharia” and “civilization jihad” were available ([paired results](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/google-blocks-advertisers-from-targeting-black-lives-matter-youtube-videos)).
  - After the inquiry, Google added **32** more terms, bringing the tested social-justice block rate to **83.9%**, and also blocked several hate-side comparisons. It then altered its responses so outside observers could no longer separate an enforced block from a no-result query ([update](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/google-blocks-advertisers-from-targeting-black-lives-matter-youtube-videos#google-expanded-the-blocklist)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Blocklist asymmetry** — paired vocabularies for protected-group advocacy, identity, and hostility are suppressed at different rates in a way that changes which public discourse advertisers can support.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Community-organization vocabulary** — civil-rights groups supplied candidate terms; an academic misinformation center helped narrow and vet the list ([construction](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/how-we-discovered-googles-social-justice-blocklist-for-youtube-ad-placements#identifying-social-justice-terms)).
  - **Controlled Ads API queries** — each term was assigned full, partial, empty, or blocked status from the authenticated placement-search response ([testing](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/how-we-discovered-googles-social-justice-blocklist-for-youtube-ad-placements#testing-the-terms)).
  - **Semantic pairs and string mutations** — Black/White, Muslim/anti-Muslim, and religious-fashion contrasts were tested alongside space-stripped variants; result counts supplied a magnitude as well as a yes/no state ([story tables](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/google-blocks-advertisers-from-targeting-black-lives-matter-youtube-videos#the-blocklist)).
  - **Platform policy and response** — Google's personalized-advertising rules and direct explanations provided the claimed rationale against which the uneven implementation was assessed ([Google response](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/google-blocks-advertisers-from-targeting-black-lives-matter-youtube-videos#what-google-says)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable (constructed instrument); degraded today.** The public repository can recompute the historical term classifications and paired differences. The Ads placement tool still exists, but Google's post-inquiry response change removed the decisive hidden-state signal, exactly as in the hate-term companion. A current investigator can observe positive target suggestions but cannot tell whether zero means a policy block, no matching videos, or another product rule ([methodological shutdown](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/google-blocks-advertisers-from-targeting-black-lives-matter-youtube-videos#google-changed-its-code)).
- **Detection signature**: **Paired-vocabulary blocklist diff** — advocacy and identity terms joined to semantically opposed or hostile comparison terms on topic pair, then compared by block state and available-video count before and after whitespace mutation, revealed selective suppression.
- **Corroboration structure**: Civil-rights organizations ground the vocabulary; controlled responses measure enforcement; semantic pairs isolate asymmetry; and Google's changes confirm that the observed states belonged to an intervention surface. The test does not establish a single intentional political motive: Google said the restrictions were intended to prevent discriminatory ad targeting.
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/how-we-discovered-googles-social-justice-blocklist-for-youtube-ad-placements) page uses the same [`investigation-youtube-ad-placements`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-youtube-ad-placements) release as the hate-term story. **Historical detector re-runnable from artifacts alone: yes; live hidden-state detector: degraded by the platform response change.**
- **Official impact**: Google added 32 tested social-justice terms and several hate terms to the blocklist, raising the tested social-justice block rate to 83.9%, while its response change curtailed subsequent third-party verification ([story update](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/04/09/google-blocks-advertisers-from-targeting-black-lives-matter-youtube-videos#google-expanded-the-blocklist)).
- **Generalization**: Build conceptually paired lexicons for sanctions screening, grant eligibility, payment risk, content moderation, or job-ad targeting. Compare both the overt decision and the number/content of eligible records; include orthographic perturbations to distinguish policy categories from fragile strings.

### In Georgia, Facebook’s Changes Brought Back a Partisan News Feed (2021) — A paid user panel captured partisan ad sites displacing conventional news after Facebook lifted its ban

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/in-georgia-facebooks-changes-brought-back-a-partisan-news-feed)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original. Citizen Browser as a project won the 2022 National Edward R. Murrow Award for Excellence in Innovation ([award notice](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2022/10/25/the-markup-receives-honors-from-adweek-and-the-national-edward-r-murrow-awards)).
- **What they found**:
  - The Markup recruited **58 paid Georgia Facebook users**; 54 submitted captures on at least two days in December 2020. Before Facebook lifted its political-ad ban on December 16, nearly all election-post links in the panel's feeds led to traditional news. Afterward, just over **one-third of the most common domains** were partisan campaign-ad sites ([story and before/after chart](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/in-georgia-facebooks-changes-brought-back-a-partisan-news-feed#georgians-facebook-feeds-dramatically-shifted-after-facebooks-ban-on-political-ads-changed)).
  - Exposure was not universal: **20 of the 54** recurring panelists saw no election-related content under the keyword definition. The panel also overrepresented Biden voters (**60.3%** versus **49.5%** in the certified result) and underrepresented Hispanic Georgians (**1.7%** versus **9.8%** in the 2019 ACS), material limits on population inference ([limitations](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/in-georgia-facebooks-changes-brought-back-a-partisan-news-feed)).
  - Facebook delivered a David Perdue ad containing a debunked claim that Jon Ossoff was “China-bought”; the campaign spent **$20,000–$25,000** for an estimated **900,000–1 million** impressions. Separately, Facebook's Ad Library showed Warnock for Georgia spent **$1.2 million across 205 ads** during December 2–31 ([ad evidence and spending chart](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/in-georgia-facebooks-changes-brought-back-a-partisan-news-feed#four-of-the-top-five-highest-spending-advertisers-on-facebook-are-democratic-outspending-the-gop-by-three-times-to-one)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Policy-change exposure displacement** — an externally dated platform rule change is followed by a shift in what personalized feeds actually deliver, including which source class loses scarce attention.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Representativeness-seeking paid-panel browser telemetry** — a paid, consented Georgia oversample within Citizen Browser captured the top of logged-in personal feeds repeatedly, preserving HTML, screenshots, and post/link metadata. The national recruitment frame began with invitations to a representative U.S. adult sample, but desktop/Chrome/Facebook requirements and roughly 95% noncompletion produced known demographic skews ([Citizen Browser panel and method](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/how-we-built-a-facebook-inspector#the-citizen-browser-panel)).
  - **Panel demographics and election benchmark** — self-reported race, age, education, location, and vote were compared with ACS and certified election totals to diagnose sample skew ([panel comparison](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/in-georgia-facebooks-changes-brought-back-a-partisan-news-feed)).
  - **Public ad records** — Facebook Ad Library spend and reach ranges linked partisan domains and campaigns in the feed to paid distribution ([spending data](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/in-georgia-facebooks-changes-brought-back-a-partisan-news-feed#four-of-the-top-five-highest-spending-advertisers-on-facebook-are-democratic-outspending-the-gop-by-three-times-to-one)).
  - **Content and fact-check review** — URLs, domains, sponsorship status, reaction totals, and a cited local fact-check distinguished traditional reporting from partisan or misleading campaign material ([story](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/in-georgia-facebooks-changes-brought-back-a-partisan-news-feed)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(d) closed-platform-dependent.** The decisive evidence is what Facebook served inside consenting users' personalized, logged-in feeds. The collector used a private panel, unreleased application/pipeline, private account state, and Netograph's proprietary NGFetch. Facebook began injecting code that disrupted such collectors in September 2021 ([countermeasure](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/09/21/facebook-rolls-out-news-feed-change-that-blocks-watchdogs-from-gathering-data)). The nearest current substitute is [Meta Content Library/API](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/somar/meta-content-library), available by application to qualified researchers and containing public posts, pages, groups, views, and interactions. It loses the crucial unit: which post appeared, at what position, to which individual account and demographic cohort.
- **Detection signature**: **Policy-interruption feed diff** — panel feed links before and after December 16 joined to domain type, sponsorship, and Ad Library advertiser on URL/domain, then compared by frequency and unique-user exposure, revealed partisan paid sites displacing conventional news after the ban changed.
- **Corroboration structure**: Feed captures directly show exposure; the known policy date supplies an interruption; Ad Library records identify the paid campaigns; and public election/ACS data expose the panel's biases. The design is observational, with a small nonrepresentative cohort and election intensity rising near voting day, so it supports a visible panel shift rather than a causal estimate for all Georgia users.
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/how-we-built-a-facebook-inspector) page documents the national panel of more than 1,000 paid participants, the Electron app, one-to-three daily NGFetch captures, automatic identifier redaction, one-month raw-data deletion, and a Trail of Bits security audit. [`citizen-browser-georgia`](https://github.com/the-markup/citizen-browser-georgia) releases only domain-aggregated CSVs; despite its README's reference to code, its file tree contains data and a README, not the core collector or row-level panel captures. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**; the published aggregate chart can be checked, but the feed extraction, keyword filter, and user-level before/after computation cannot be reconstructed.
- **Official impact**: No specific Facebook remedy was attributed to this story. Facebook argued that political content naturally rises near election day; the broader Citizen Browser project later received the Murrow innovation award ([response](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/in-georgia-facebooks-changes-brought-back-a-partisan-news-feed)).
- **Generalization**: When a platform changes a rule, recruit a consented cohort before the effective date, retain exposure rather than engagement, and join observed items to public advertiser/owner records. The pattern fits app-store policy changes, ad moratoria, recommender updates, and procurement portals that reorder opportunities.

### Split Screen: How Different Are Americans’ Facebook Feeds? (2021) — Cohort exposure rates made personalized feed differences comparable without inventing “typical” accounts

- **URL**: [Interactive story](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/03/11/split-screen)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; part of the Murrow-winning Citizen Browser project ([award notice](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2022/10/25/the-markup-receives-honors-from-adweek-and-the-national-edward-r-murrow-awards)).
- **What they found**:
  - The interactive compared content exposure for self-reported **Biden versus Trump voters, women versus men, and millennials versus boomers**, using the panel rather than synthetic persona accounts ([interactive](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/03/11/split-screen)).
  - In the first 92 days, the method produced **17,332 content-pair observations**. The median absolute cohort gap was **3 percentage points**; gaps above **17 points** were treated as empirical outliers for the interface, not as a universal significance cutoff ([method distribution](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/03/11/how-we-built-a-facebook-feed-viewer#percentage-point-differences-for-content-shown-across-all-pairings)).
  - Only the top seven posts per capture were analyzed, and news links were **37% of all links** there. The tool measured material Facebook surfaced, not content panelists necessarily noticed, clicked, endorsed, or obtained from the recommender rather than their chosen friends and groups ([scope and limits](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/03/11/how-we-built-a-facebook-feed-viewer#proportion-of-news-to-other-links-in-facebook-posts)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Personalized exposure segmentation** — the same content's reach is computed within real demographic or political cohorts to identify exceptional gaps hidden by a platform-wide average.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **National paid-panel telemetry** — repeated logged-in captures from Citizen Browser's 1,000-plus panel, joined to participants' self-reported demographics and 2020 vote. A survey provider invited a nationally representative sample, but the realized panel was older, more educated, less Hispanic, and less pro-Trump than the target ([panel recruitment and composition](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/how-we-built-a-facebook-inspector#the-citizen-browser-panel)).
  - **Top-of-feed post/link records** — up to seven posts per capture, normalized so the same underlying URL could be compared regardless of whether a friend, group, or publisher account shared it ([viewer method](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/03/11/how-we-built-a-facebook-feed-viewer#how-we-calculate-population-percentages)).
  - **News-domain reference list** — **6,989** “hard news” domains compiled from CrowdTangle lists and a local-news dataset, then manually filtered under a disclosed but subjective definition ([released source list](https://github.com/the-markup/split-screen-news-sources)).
  - **Sparse-data and sample checks** — content required exposure to at least seven participants and a gap of at least 0.1 points; the display disclosed when data were insufficient and described recruitment imbalance ([limitations](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/03/11/how-we-built-a-facebook-feed-viewer#sparse-data)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(d) closed-platform-dependent.** The analysis requires participant-level, logged-in personalized feed exposures and cohort attributes. Meta's current Content Library provides public content and aggregate view/interaction fields, but not a mapping from content to an individual's home-feed exposure or its rank ([current data scope](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/somar/meta-content-library)). Rebuilding Split Screen therefore requires a new consented browser panel; public Meta data can identify popular public material but cannot reproduce demographic exposure gaps.
- **Detection signature**: **Cohort exposure-gap ranking** — normalized content URL joined to panelist cohort on user ID, with unique exposed users divided by eligible cohort size and absolute percentage-point differences compared across paired cohorts, revealed outlier personalization.
- **Corroboration structure**: Repeated observations prevent a single screenshot from standing in for a feed; cohort denominators make unlike panel sizes comparable; the 92-day empirical distribution gives the gaps context; and raw domain provenance defines what counted as news. What remains unidentified is causation: friend networks, follows, user behavior, ad targeting, and Facebook ranking all contribute to the observed feed.
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Split Screen Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/03/11/how-we-built-a-facebook-feed-viewer) page sits on top of the [Citizen Browser system method](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/how-we-built-a-facebook-inspector). The only story-specific repository, [`split-screen-news-sources`](https://github.com/the-markup/split-screen-news-sources), releases the 6,989-domain reference CSV, not the panel observations, calculation code, Electron app, redactors, or NGFetch collector. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no.**
- **Official impact**: No platform change was attributed specifically to Split Screen; Citizen Browser's broader method received the 2022 Murrow innovation award ([award notice](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2022/10/25/the-markup-receives-honors-from-adweek-and-the-national-edward-r-murrow-awards)).
- **Generalization**: For any personalized allocation—credit offers, job ads, public-benefit outreach, marketplace listings—measure `unique exposed people / eligible cohort`, publish denominators, and rank gaps against the instrument's own distribution. Never infer exposure from likes or public post popularity.

### Facebook Isn’t Telling You How Popular Right-Wing Content Is on the Platform (2021) — Reranking the same feed evidence by impressions reversed Meta’s unique-user popularity story

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/11/18/facebook-isnt-telling-you-how-popular-right-wing-content-is-on-the-platform)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; part of the Murrow-winning Citizen Browser project ([award notice](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2022/10/25/the-markup-receives-honors-from-adweek-and-the-national-edward-r-murrow-awards)).
- **What they found**:
  - Meta's Q3 2021 Widely Viewed Content report ranked domains by **unique users**. The Markup reranked Citizen Browser's same-quarter links by **total impressions**. The Daily Wire jumped **135 places to No. 9** with 7,674 impressions, Western Journal jumped **196 to No. 13** with 5,205, and Fox News climbed 34 to No. 16 ([impression table](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/11/18/how-we-investigated-facebooks-most-popular-content#top-20-domains-ranked-by-impressions-and-the-ranking-change-from-unique-users-citizen-browser)).
  - The divergence was repetition: under Meta's method, one person shown the same publisher hundreds of times contributes one. The story documented a panelist shown Newsmax **1,065 times** in the quarter, exposure erased by a unique-user-only ranking ([story](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/11/18/facebook-isnt-telling-you-how-popular-right-wing-content-is-on-the-platform)).
  - The panel was checked against Meta's report before substituting the metric. Nineteen of Meta's 20 top domains, excluding anomalous PayPal, appeared within Citizen Browser's top 39; ordering had Kendall τ=**0.411**, p=**0.0111**, while overlapping unique-user counts had Spearman ρ=**0.568**, p=**0.009** ([validation](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/11/18/how-we-investigated-facebooks-most-popular-content#correlation)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Metric-induced rank reversal** — a platform's public “most popular” list changes materially when repeated exposure is counted instead of collapsing every person to a single occurrence.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Citizen Browser exposure logs** — domain, panelist, capture, and repeated appearance records for July 1–September 30, 2021 ([method](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/11/18/how-we-investigated-facebooks-most-popular-content#our-data)).
  - **Platform transparency report** — Meta's Q3 Widely Viewed Content domain list and unique-user counts supplied a population benchmark and the metric being audited ([report comparison](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/11/18/how-we-investigated-facebooks-most-popular-content#facebooks-data)).
  - **Two ranking constructions** — `COUNT(DISTINCT user)` and total appearance count were calculated over the same domain-by-user evidence, isolating the effect of definition rather than corpus ([ranking method](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/11/18/how-we-investigated-facebooks-most-popular-content#our-analysis)).
  - **Statistical and record-level checks** — overlap, rank correlation, count correlation, simulation-based p-values, and extreme repeated-exposure cases tested whether the small panel tracked Meta's public ordering ([validation](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/11/18/how-we-investigated-facebooks-most-popular-content#correlation)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(d) closed-platform-dependent.** The decisive raw evidence remains individualized Facebook feed exposure collected through the private Citizen Browser system. The released aggregates and notebooks can reproduce the published reranking, but cannot acquire a new quarter. The nearest substitute, [Meta Content Library/API](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/somar/meta-content-library), now includes public-content views and interactions for approved researchers, which could support a public-content frequency rank; it still cannot identify how often a domain was inserted into each person's home feed, so concentration within users and cohort-specific repetition are lost.
- **Detection signature**: **Metric-substitution rank reversal** — the same domain×user exposure table ranked once by distinct user ID and again by total feed appearances on domain, then diffed by ordinal position; right-wing publishers' jumps revealed repetition hidden by the official popularity denominator.
- **Corroboration structure**: Meta's list validates Citizen Browser's unique-user ordering; correlation tests quantify that match; the alternative impression rank changes only the aggregation rule; and extreme user-domain records illustrate what the official metric discards. The panel remains much smaller than Facebook and capture frequency is not identical to continuous exposure, so the exact ranks are panel estimates.
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/11/18/how-we-investigated-facebooks-most-popular-content) page documents aggregation logic, correlations, simulations, and limits. [`citizen-browser-widely-viewed-content`](https://github.com/the-markup/citizen-browser-widely-viewed-content) releases the top-1,000 aggregate rankings; [`facebook-report-verification`](https://github.com/the-markup/facebook-report-verification) releases validation data, Python, and a reproducible notebook (the repository is archived read-only but remains downloadable). **Published ranking detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes; decisive feed acquisition and a new-period detector: no.**
- **Official impact**: No correction or metric change was attributed to the story. Meta defended unique users as the correct measure of reach; the broader Citizen Browser project later received the Murrow award ([platform response](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/11/18/facebook-isnt-telling-you-how-popular-right-wing-content-is-on-the-platform)).
- **Generalization**: Whenever an institution publishes “top” entities, rerank the same events by distinct people, event count, duration, volume, dollars, and concentration per person. Procurement, court dockets, sanctions matches, hospital encounters, and campaign donations can all invert when reach is substituted for intensity.

### Demoted, Deleted, and Denied: There’s More Than Just Shadowbanning on Instagram (2024) — Controlled accounts showed war images disappearing without notice while several popular shadowban theories failed

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/demoted-deleted-and-denied-theres-more-than-just-shadowbanning-on-instagram)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original. The field audit was a [2025 Sigma Award finalist](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2025/05/13/the-markup-named-sigma-award-finalist); Nieuwsuur later replicated its experiments. The story's Arabic version is a translation, not a separate corpus card under the attribution rule.
- **What they found**:
  - After two rounds, only **9 of 29** nongraphic war photos ever appeared under the controlled `#markuptest` hashtag, versus **158 of 172** other posts. War imagery was **8.5 times** as likely to remain hidden; none of the accounts was notified of the visibility restriction ([method results](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/how-we-investigated-shadowbanning-on-instagram#instagram-suppressed-photos-of-war)).
  - Instagram deleted whole captions containing the test hashtag dozens of times without notice, and search could report `#markuptest` had “100+ posts” but open to “No results found.” The control `#safemarkup` was temporarily labeled restricted after spam reports even though all 19 posts had appeared at least once ([story](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/demoted-deleted-and-denied-theres-more-than-just-shadowbanning-on-instagram#deleting-captions-without-notice-hiding-hashtag-posts)).
  - Users' comments, including Israel–Palestine comments, could be labeled spam with no functioning appeal path. In the controlled tests, many spam/bullying comments became visible only to the commenter or moved to “View hidden comments” with no notice ([appeal test](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/demoted-deleted-and-denied-theres-more-than-just-shadowbanning-on-instagram#caught-in-a-spam-filter-with-no-way-out)).
  - The team **did not** find evidence that advocacy history, erroneous community reports, prior guideline violations, or prior banned-hashtag use caused account-level shadowbanning—important negative results that narrowed the causal story ([conclusion](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/how-we-investigated-shadowbanning-on-instagram#conclusion)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Covert moderation state** — content remains visible to its author or exists in metadata while disappearing from discovery surfaces, and the platform supplies no stable notice or remedy.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **User-documentation intake** — hundreds of screenshots/videos, metadata from thousands of accounts and posts, 20 affected-user interviews, and expert interviews generated ten recurrent censorship scenarios ([reporting frame](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/how-we-investigated-shadowbanning-on-instagram#introduction)).
  - **Controlled account field audit** — 18 accounts in early trials, then **70 new accounts in seven ten-account treatment groups plus 14 existing observer accounts**, all manually created and operated ([full-trial design](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/how-we-investigated-shadowbanning-on-instagram#full-trials)).
  - **Matched image/control corpus** — public-domain nongraphic war images and ordinary pets, food, scenery, plants, and memes posted into newly created `#markuptest` and `#safemarkup`; repeated observers logged presence and absence ([hashtag baseline](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/how-we-investigated-shadowbanning-on-instagram#setup-and-baseline-testing)).
  - **Controlled violations and reports** — spam-like and bullying comments, banned hashtags, false reports, and Israel- versus Palestine-following histories probed competing hypotheses and appeal behavior ([experiment groups](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/how-we-investigated-shadowbanning-on-instagram#full-trials)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable (constructed instrument); runnable today.** The new-account, public-image, novel-hashtag, and multiple-observer design can be rebuilt from the public method without privileged API access. Instagram's current Help Center still describes public hashtag posting and hashtag pages ([current hashtag instructions](https://www.facebook.com/help/351460621611097/)). A present-day run would be new evidence, not a reproduction of the 2024 calculation, and account association/anti-abuse systems may change treatment. The path remains operational in principle; Nieuwsuur's subsequent independent replication is evidence that the method transferred ([impact](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2025/05/13/the-markup-named-sigma-award-finalist#impact)).
- **Detection signature**: **Controlled-content visibility ratio** — matched war and ordinary images posted by new accounts joined to repeated multi-account hashtag observations on post ID, then compared by “ever visible” rate; treatment-group actions were separately diffed against controls to reject common history/reporting hypotheses.
- **Corroboration structure**: User records generate hypotheses; controlled accounts test them; two rounds and many observer accounts reveal persistent visibility gaps; negative treatment results prevent overclaiming; interviews show consequences. The Markup did not treat observations as independent or run significance tests because accounts could be linked and experiments overlapped ([limitations](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/how-we-investigated-shadowbanning-on-instagram#limitations)).
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/how-we-investigated-shadowbanning-on-instagram) page is unusually complete about experimental groups, baselines, false starts, and limitations. No GitHub repository, machine-readable observation log, or code/data release was identified in the story, method page, or census repository inventory. **Historical detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no; a fresh detector is rebuildable from the described protocol.**
- **Official impact**: Meta restored one user's mistakenly removed story and removed his strike after The Markup asked, while declining to explain the war-image disparity. The method was later replicated by Nieuwsuur and became a Sigma finalist ([story response](https://themarkup.org/automated-censorship/2024/02/25/demoted-deleted-and-denied-theres-more-than-just-shadowbanning-on-instagram), [impact](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2025/05/13/the-markup-named-sigma-award-finalist#impact)).
- **Generalization**: Audit any silent demotion with novel namespaces, known-content controls, several observer identities, repeated snapshots, and “ever visible” plus time-to-visibility measures. Include deliberately wrong causal treatments so the instrument can falsify attractive explanations.

### Vietnamese YouTuber Is Filling Information Voids with Newsmax and Breitbart (2023) — Community sourcing connected a scarce-language news gap to translated far-right narratives

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart)
- **Partner/awards**: Copublished with [The Oaklandside](https://oaklandside.org/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-newsmax-breitbart-misinformation/), produced through Brown University's Information Futures Lab fellowship. The series won the [2024 AAJA Excellence in Online/Digital Journalism Engagement award](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/05/28/the-markup-wins-aaja-journalism-excellence-award) and the [2024 ONA/Gather Community-Centered Journalism award](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/09/23/online-news-general-excellence-gather-community-award).
- **What they found**:
  - An information-needs assessment with older Vietnamese residents in Oakland found everyone used YouTube for information and almost everyone knew the commentator Ohlala; few used local television or English-language local news, and participants said they lacked usable news about Oakland ([community findings](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart)).
  - Ohlala's public posts and videos repeatedly translated or repackaged Newsmax, The Gateway Pundit, and Breitbart. The reporter examined dozens of videos and traced the source framing, finding missing historical and cultural context rather than treating the Vietnamese-language video as an isolated false claim ([source tracing](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart#news-out-of-context)).
  - One community member donated a viewing history of more than **17,000 videos**; the reporter manually categorized a random **1,000-video** sample, in which news was the most-watched category. That placed Ohlala within a broader consumption ecology rather than assuming a subscriber count equaled influence ([sample analysis](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart), [archive scale](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/09/23/online-news-general-excellence-gather-community-award#community-centered-journalism-award)).
  - A search of Ohlala's corpus found only one Black Lives Matter video; it omitted the police killings that prompted the protests and instead relayed a story about conflict with Black women and advised Vietnamese viewers to avoid Black people ([content gap](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart#news-out-of-context)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **Language-market information void** — a community's unmet local-language need creates distribution power for a translator/aggregator whose source selection and omissions shape the available worldview.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Multilingual community sourcing** — a focus group of 30 older Vietnamese Oakland residents, about two dozen community conversations, and individual interviews established information needs and consumption habits before selecting the influencer as the story ([reporting description](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/09/23/online-news-general-excellence-gather-community-award#community-centered-journalism-award), [story](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart)).
  - **Donated private viewing archive** — one volunteer's complete YouTube history, dating to 2020 and exceeding 17,000 videos, supplied a person-level exposure frame; a random 1,000-video sample was manually categorized with permission ([sample](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart)).
  - **Public multilingual content corpus** — dozens of Ohlala videos and the channel community page were translated/read, searched, and traced to named English-language publishers ([public-source evidence](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart)).
  - **Experts and local-media inventory** — misinformation researchers and the community's description of Vietnamese BBC/RFI, a Sacramento weekly, and sparse Bay Area coverage supplied ecosystem context ([information-void section](https://themarkup.org/languages-of-misinformation/2023/06/09/vietnamese-youtuber-is-filling-information-voids-with-newsmax-and-breitbart)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(b) re-anchoring.** Discovery of Ohlala's importance and the “news is most watched” exposure result depended on a private donated YouTube history and community access. Verification of the content-provenance claim ran on public videos, community posts, named source outlets, and public event reporting; another investigator can reuse that half while the videos remain live or archived. Without the private archive, public substitutes—channel search, upload counts, views, and comments—lose the fact that a real community member repeatedly encountered this material among all her other viewing and cannot measure the information need that made it salient.
- **Detection signature**: **Information-void provenance join** — community-reported needs and a donated watch-history sample joined to public influencer videos on video/channel, then those videos compared with linked or translated English-language sources and missing local/contextual topics, revealed a scarcity-driven translation pipeline.
- **Corroboration structure**: Community interviews establish need; the donated history shows actual consumption; random sampling prevents cherry-picking only political clips; public video/source comparison verifies repackaging; expert and local-news review tests the “void” interpretation. One person's archive cannot estimate community prevalence, and the focus group is purposive rather than representative.
- **Methodology notes**: No separate Show Your Work page and no GitHub repository or released coding sheet were identified. **[inferred]** The decisive method is reconstructed from the story's disclosure of the random 1,000-video categorization and the later ONA account of the 30-person focus group and 17,000-video archive. **Historical detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**; neither the donated history nor sampling/coding table is public.
- **Official impact**: The reporting led to two in-person Vietnamese-language misinformation/deepfake workshops and resources produced with Viet Fact Check; the series then won AAJA engagement and ONA/Gather community-centered awards ([impact account](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/09/23/online-news-general-excellence-gather-community-award#community-centered-journalism-award)).
- **Generalization**: Start with a community information-needs assessment, not a platform keyword. With consent, sample actual consumption; map translator-to-source chains; code context omissions; and inventory what trusted local-language alternatives exist. The same pattern fits diasporas, rural language communities, professional niches, and crisis zones.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### Pattern A — Controlled-Input Black-Box Audit

- **Mechanics**: Hold account history and request settings constant; alter one identity, policy, or content feature; record every output, error, suppression state, count, and timestamp; mutate strings to infer boundary logic.
- **Minimum data**: At least one valid control, one treatment family, raw responses or screenshots, repeated runs, account/session metadata, and a manual validation sample.
- **Recognition cues**: The institution says decisions are automated or personalized; users receive inconsistent results; a formal blocklist is undisclosed; tiny spelling changes alter eligibility; or “no result” may actually mean enforcement.
- **Origin cards**: Gmail, Keyword Planner, both YouTube blocklists, Instagram.

#### Pattern B — Panelized Exposure Differential

- **Mechanics**: Recruit consented real users, capture what the system actually serves, normalize content IDs, compute unique-user exposure within cohorts, and compare cohorts or pre/post policy windows. Treat exposure, engagement, and endorsement as separate events.
- **Minimum data**: Participant ID, cohort attributes, capture time, feed position, normalized content/source ID, sampling/recruitment frame, and a privacy-preserving deletion/redaction plan.
- **Recognition cues**: No two users see the same surface; public APIs expose posts or likes but not impressions; the question concerns who was shown something; a platform rule changed on a known date.
- **Origin cards**: Georgia, Split Screen, Facebook popularity.

#### Pattern C — Metric-Substitution Rank Reversal

- **Mechanics**: Recompute an official league table over the same base events using reach (`distinct subject`), intensity (`event count`), volume (`amount/duration`), and concentration (`events per subject`); diff ranks and inspect the largest movers.
- **Minimum data**: Entity×subject×event rows or aggregates sufficient for both distinct and repeated counts, the official metric definition, and stable entity resolution.
- **Recognition cues**: “Most popular,” “top contractor,” “most sanctioned,” “largest donor,” or “most litigated” is published without explaining whether repeated encounters collapse to one.
- **Origin cards**: Facebook popularity, with the Google surface-share story as a spatial analogue.

#### Pattern D — Surface-Area Self-Preference Assay

- **Mechanics**: Render the interface at a controlled viewport, classify every visible module by owner/control, calculate vertical position and area, and compare “first screen” with the full page. Manually audit parser and geometry errors.
- **Minimum data**: A defensible query frame, raw rendered pages/screenshots, element bounding boxes, module taxonomy, ownership crosswalk, and a validation sample.
- **Recognition cues**: The platform operates the search/ranking layer and competes with entities listed in it; nominal link rank understates cards, answer boxes, maps, or AI summaries; attention depends heavily on above-fold area.
- **Origin card**: Google Search.

#### Pattern E — Information-Void Provenance Chain

- **Mechanics**: Assess a community's information needs; sample consented consumption; map multilingual creator claims or translations to upstream sources; code omissions and contextual transformations; inventory available trusted alternatives.
- **Minimum data**: Community interviews in the relevant language, a consented consumption sample or defensible proxy, public content/transcripts, source URLs, and local-language media inventory.
- **Recognition cues**: One translator/aggregator dominates a scarce-language niche; audiences ask for local information but receive national ideological content; source attribution disappears in translation; platform views are mistaken for need.
- **Origin card**: Vietnamese YouTuber.

### 5. What this platform could run now—and what is missing

**Immediately runnable with current holdings**

- **Metric-substitution rank reversal** is the strongest fit. On USASpending/FPDS, compare contractors by distinct agencies, award count, dollars, and repeated modifications; on CourtListener/RECAP, compare entities by distinct cases, filings, allegations, and counterparties; on 990s and Etherscan/Solscan, compare recipient reach with transaction intensity. The platform already has stable entity and relationship layers for these joins.
- **Ownership-aware surface/provenance joins** are feasible on the underlying records. Resolve names and domains through the 20-plus registries, OpenCorporates, GLEIF, OpenSanctions, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, and local OpenAleph, then ask whether ostensibly independent results, bidders, grantees, patent assignees, aircraft owners, or wallet counterparties share control. A new browser renderer would be needed only for actual screen-area measurement.
- **Policy–enforcement mismatch** can be adapted without social data: compare sanctions lists with awards/payments, registry status with contracting activity, debarment/eligibility rules with observed recipients, or court orders with later corporate/property transfers. The pattern needs dated policy lists plus event records, both already represented in the holdings.
- **Information-void provenance** can partially run as a document/source-chain detector: use entity resolution to trace publishers, nonprofits, companies, addresses, and funders behind a content ecosystem. The public-record side is strong; the audience-needs and consumption side is not.

**Missing or requiring new acquisition**

- A consented, demographically characterized browser panel; a secure client; feed-position capture; privacy redactors; participant operations; and independent security review. Meta Content Library access is useful but is not a substitute for individualized exposure.
- Maintained browser instruments for modern Google Search/AI surfaces and logged-out/logged-in comparisons, plus paid Google Ads test accounts for placement-planner audits.
- A current hidden-state oracle for YouTube ad blocking. Because Google intentionally collapsed block and empty results, rebuilding the old script does not recover the missing signal.
- Multilingual researchers, trusted community partnerships, consented viewing histories, transcript/translation pipelines, and culturally competent coding. Corporate ownership data cannot infer an information void or what a community actually consumes.
- Raw story-level Instagram observations if the goal is to reproduce 2024 rather than run a new test. The written protocol is reusable, but there is no released observation table against which to validate a reimplementation.
