### Suckers List: How Allstate's Secret Auto Insurance Algorithm Squeezes Big Spenders (2020) — Customer-level rate records exposed a retention algorithm that withheld deserved discounts and pushed the largest increases onto existing big spenders

- **URL**: [The Markup, Feb. 25, 2020](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/car-insurance-suckers-list)
- **Partner/awards**: Joint investigation with **Consumer Reports**; its statisticians Andrew Cohen and Dina Haner are credited for data/statistical analysis in the [story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/car-insurance-suckers-list) and [methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list). No project-specific award was verified on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - Allstate's withdrawn Maryland filing contained individual current, “indicated” risk-based, and proposed transition prices for 92,792 policies. After excluding 70 records without a Census match, the analysis covered **92,722 policies** ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list)).
  - Policyholders already paying roughly **$1,900 or more per six months** could receive increases up to **20 percent**, while lower-paying customers with equally large indicated risk increases were generally capped at **5 percent**. The large-increase group contained 1,831 policies, just 1.97 percent of the cohort ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/car-insurance-suckers-list); [methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list)).
  - Allstate's own risk model said 48,700 customers deserved a decrease, but no transition discount exceeded 0.5 percent. The foregone discounts totaled **more than $10.5 million in the first six months**; customers aged 63 or older who deserved discounts were owed a median $163.79 but would have received a median $1.64 ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/car-insurance-suckers-list); [methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list)).
  - Zip codes at least 75 percent “nonwhite” were overrepresented in the large-increase group, as were middle-aged drivers and men. The method labels zip-level race an ecological proxy, not an individual policyholder attribute ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `personalized-price-extraction` — price is adjusted by inferred willingness to remain or pay, not only by cost/risk; `proxy-concentrated-burden` — a formally nonracial pricing rule produces a burden concentrated in demographically identifiable places.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Regulatory rate filing / customer microdata** — withdrawn Maryland SERFF filing ALSE-129270805, including a 1,101-page customer table and Allstate's data dictionary; identified after reviewing hundreds of filings and then retrieved from Maryland's public SERFF database ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list)).
  - **Government demographic data** — 2011–2015 ACS zip-code-tabulation-area race and income tables joined to Allstate territory/zip fields ([repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm)).
  - **Regulatory correspondence** — Maryland's disapproval letter and Allstate letters to multiple states explaining Complementary Group Rating and retention models, linked in the [methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list).
  - **Company and industry records** — Allstate SEC/investor statements, NAIC price-optimization materials, and regulator interviews used to establish the cost-based-rating norm and cross-state deployment ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/car-insurance-suckers-list)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive evidence was a public regulatory filing, and the complete historical PDFs, cleaned CSVs, Census extracts, schema, and analysis code are preserved in the [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm). **Today:** exact analysis is runnable from released artifacts alone. Fresh automated SERFF acquisition is degraded: the Maryland interface returned HTTP 403 to a direct automated request on July 29, 2026, although [SERFF Filing Access](https://www.serff.com/serff_filing_access.htm) remains a public-facing interactive service.
- **Detection signature**: **`document-to-outcome reconstruction / risk-price residual segmentation`** — Allstate's current premium was compared with its own indicated risk premium and proposed selected premium on policy ID; residual plots exposed three discontinuous transition groups, and current price joined to the group label revealed that high existing spend—not merely the indicated risk change—predicted the 9.5–20 percent increase group.
- **Corroboration structure**: The Markup used a linear-model residual plot to discover the group boundary, then a depth-three CART model with 80/20 train-test split and fivefold cross-validation reproduced the categories with **99.69 percent test accuracy**. Proportion tests examined age, gender, and zip composition; Maryland's regulator independently called CGR unfairly discriminatory; Consumer Reports statisticians and outside experts reviewed the work; Allstate received the data, code, and methodology before publication and did not identify a specific statistical error, while disputing the story's characterization ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list)).
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [“How We Analyzed Allstate's Car Insurance Algorithm”](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list) says the team tested whether the model treated customers differently on non-risk factors. The [GitHub repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm) releases source PDFs, cleaned data, the PDF extractor, and R analysis. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes**, with path edits and an older R/Python environment; raw-to-clean extraction is optional because cleaned CSVs are included.
- **Official impact**: A Texas class action cited the investigation, and Michigan regulators later asked Allstate the same equal-risk/equal-price questions ([Texas follow-up](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/05/05/texas-drivers-sue-allstate-over-secret-suckers-list); [Michigan follow-up](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing)).
- **Generalization**: Audit any transition, loyalty, retention, or “revenue-neutral” pricing system by obtaining current price, provider-estimated cost/risk price, and final offered price. Plot `offered − current` against `cost/risk − current`; discontinuities or asymmetric caps reveal willingness-to-pay extraction. Applicable to telecom renewals, subscription discounts, utility arrears plans, bank fees, travel pricing, and employer compensation adjustments.

### Texas Drivers Sue Allstate over Secret “Suckers List” (2020) — A class complaint alleged that open and closed books charged materially different prices for the same coverage and cited The Markup's Maryland reconstruction

- **URL**: [The Markup, May 5, 2020](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/05/05/texas-drivers-sue-allstate-over-secret-suckers-list)
- **Partner/awards**: The follow-up is a Markup original; the analysis it tracks was jointly produced with **Consumer Reports**. No story-specific award was found on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - Current and former policyholders filed a federal class complaint in the Western District of Texas on April 28, 2020, alleging Allstate used projected price tolerance to charge longer-tenured customers more than otherwise similarly situated customers ([story and complaint](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/05/05/texas-drivers-sue-allstate-over-secret-suckers-list)).
  - The complaint alleged that Allstate's Texas “closed books,” serving existing customers, charged significantly more than “open books” recruiting new customers for the same or materially similar coverage ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/05/05/texas-drivers-sue-allstate-over-secret-suckers-list)).
  - A 2015 letter from the Texas Office of Public Insurance Counsel had told Allstate that the proposed retention model violated multiple Texas laws; Allstate denied merit in the complaint. These were allegations, not adjudicated findings ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/05/05/texas-drivers-sue-allstate-over-secret-suckers-list)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `closed-book-loyalty-penalty` — incumbent customers are segmented into a legacy book where equivalent service allegedly costs more; `investigation-to-litigation uptake` — a reproducible journalistic detector becomes pleaded evidence in a formal legal action.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Federal litigation record** — filed complaint with class allegations, quoted and linked through DocumentCloud from the [story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/05/05/texas-drivers-sue-allstate-over-secret-suckers-list).
  - **State regulatory letter** — Texas Office of Public Insurance Counsel's 2015 warning, attached to the complaint and linked from the [story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/05/05/texas-drivers-sue-allstate-over-secret-suckers-list).
  - **Independent analytic anchor** — Maryland customer-level reconstruction and code from the [underlying investigation](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/car-insurance-suckers-list) and [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The complaint and regulator letter are public court/agency records, and the cited Maryland analysis is preserved. **Today:** runnable; [PACER](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/) still provides federal dockets and the story preserves the complaint link. No platform closure prevents repeating the documentary comparison.
- **Detection signature**: **`document-to-outcome reconstruction / book-of-business price diff`** — the complaint's open/closed-book allegations were compared with the Texas regulator's earlier retention-model warning and joined to the Maryland policy-level residual analysis on Allstate's retention/CGR mechanism, revealing the same loyalty-penalty hypothesis in a second state.
- **Corroboration structure**: Pleaded claims were clearly attributed as allegations; the OPIC letter predated the suit; the independent Maryland analysis supplied a tested mechanism rather than treating complaint rhetoric as proof; Allstate's denial was included ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/05/05/texas-drivers-sue-allstate-over-secret-suckers-list)).
- **Methodology notes**: No separate Show Your Work page exists for this documentary follow-up. **[inferred]** The method is a litigation-record crosswalk because the story links the complaint and agency letter and explicitly reuses the underlying [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list) and [GitHub repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes** for the published documentary comparison; independently testing Texas policy prices would require discovery or regulator microdata not released with this story.
- **Official impact**: The investigation was cited in a filed federal class action; a 2022 Markup correction said the lawsuit remained ongoing at that time ([2022 update](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)).
- **Generalization**: Look for “open/closed,” “new/legacy,” or “acquisition/renewal” customer books. Join product terms and risk attributes across books; test whether tenure predicts price after matching on coverage and risk. Litigation can supply the otherwise hidden vocabulary needed to find the same segmentation in regulatory filings.

### Michigan Regulators Question Allstate's Car Insurance Pricing (2021) — Regulator letters turned the investigation's equal-risk test into formal questions about Allstate's model

- **URL**: [The Markup, Feb. 9, 2021](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing)
- **Partner/awards**: Markup original impact follow-up; the underlying investigation was with **Consumer Reports**. No story-specific award was found on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - Michigan's Department of Insurance and Financial Services asked whether two customers with the same risk profile could pay different rates and how Allstate assigned policyholders to subgroups—the same fairness test used in the Maryland investigation ([story and letter](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing)).
  - Allstate responded that its systems would later align identical risks but said it had found no identical risks in Michigan; a consumer advocate argued that defining near-identical customers as different could make the assurance meaningless ([story and response](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing)).
  - Michigan had hired independent actuaries after Governor Gretchen Whitmer ordered DIFS to investigate non-risk factors in auto pricing; the agency described the exchange as part of ordinary filing review ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `regulator-model-visibility-gap` — a regulator cannot readily determine whether a complex model gives equal prices to equal risks; `definition-based-evasion-risk` — a provider can make an equality guarantee unfalsifiable by defining almost no cases as identical.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Regulatory correspondence** — DIFS objection letter and Allstate's written response, both linked from the [story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing).
  - **Executive/regulatory records** — governor's order and agency statements about independent actuarial review ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing)).
  - **Prior customer-level analysis** — Maryland filing, methodology, and code used to interpret why subgroup assignment mattered ([underlying methodology](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive evidence is public agency-company correspondence. **Today:** runnable from the linked letters and preserved underlying repo; no fresh bulk platform is required.
- **Detection signature**: **`document-to-outcome reconstruction / regulator-question echo`** — The Markup's previously stated fairness test (“same risk, same rate”) was compared with the regulator's questions and Allstate's answer on the shared concepts of risk profile and subgroup, revealing institutional uptake and the “no identical risks” escape hatch.
- **Corroboration structure**: Primary letters established what DIFS asked and what Allstate answered; the Maryland policy analysis established why the question was material; the story included the agency's “standard review” characterization and Allstate's position rather than inferring causation from timing ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing)).
- **Methodology notes**: No separate Show Your Work page or new repository. **[inferred]** Documentary diff based on the story's direct links to the objection and response, with interpretation anchored in [the original Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list) and [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes.**
- **Official impact**: DIFS put equal-risk pricing and subgroup assignment into the formal filing-review record and hired independent actuaries to assess complex insurer filings ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2021/02/09/michigan-regulators-question-allstates-car-insurance-pricing)).
- **Generalization**: Convert an audit's empirical fairness test into regulator-language questions, then compare vendor answers with the original detector. Watch for assurances that cannot be falsified because “same,” “comparable,” or “similarly situated” is defined too narrowly.

### Newly Public Documents Allege Allstate Overcharged Loyal California Customers $1 Billion (2022) — Administrative testimony independently described loyalty-based discount suppression at much larger scale

- **URL**: [The Markup, Feb. 1, 2022](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)
- **Partner/awards**: Markup original; it re-anchors the Consumer Reports partnership's 2020 findings. No story-specific award was found on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - Two public submissions to a California administrative law judge contained expert allegations that Allstate systematically overcharged loyal customers by about **$1 billion over a decade** ([story and testimony](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)).
  - A California Department of Insurance actuary said internal documents, state-filed pricing materials, and employee depositions showed smaller discounts for clean drivers with multiple policies or more than **39 years of driving experience**, groups considered less likely to shop around ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)).
  - Consumer Watchdog's actuarial consultant estimated the $1 billion amount; Allstate denied using price optimization and noted regulators had approved its 2011 plan. The article consistently describes the public submissions as allegations, not a final adjudication ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `actuarial-discount-withholding` — customers whose modeled risk supports a discount allegedly receive less because they are price-insensitive; `cross-jurisdiction-mechanism recurrence` — the same behavioral-pricing mechanism appears in independently produced records from another state.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Administrative testimony** — written submissions by a California Department of Insurance actuary and Consumer Watchdog's actuarial consultant, linked via DocumentCloud from the [story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion).
  - **Litigation/administrative record** — class action filings, referral to the insurance commissioner, and mediation notice ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)).
  - **Underlying company evidence** — internal Allstate records, submitted rate-plan materials, and employee depositions reviewed and summarized by the testifying experts; some are public only through the proceeding ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)).
  - **Independent analytic anchor** — Maryland customer-level model reconstruction and [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive public evidence is the administrative/court record, not a private leak. **Today:** runnable from linked testimony and filings; the Maryland mechanism can be rerun from the repo. Some underlying internal records are mediated through sworn expert testimony, so a new independent dollar estimate beyond the public exhibits would require the case record or discovery.
- **Detection signature**: **`document-to-outcome reconstruction / cross-state mechanism match`** — California expert testimony about discount size was compared with Maryland's current-versus-indicated-versus-selected premium reconstruction on the key mechanism “smaller discount for less price-sensitive customers,” revealing recurrence and a scale estimate in a second jurisdiction.
- **Corroboration structure**: Two experts with different institutional sponsors described the same mechanism; the state actuary cited company materials and depositions; the earlier Maryland dataset independently demonstrated asymmetric discount caps; Allstate's denial and prior regulatory approval were reported ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)).
- **Methodology notes**: No separate Show Your Work page for this follow-up. **[inferred]** Primary-record comparison, based on the linked testimony and court filings. The earlier mechanism is documented in [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/show-your-work-car-insurance-suckers-list) and the [GitHub repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes** for the published cross-record match; independently validating every component of the $1 billion estimate would require the experts' underlying record set.
- **Official impact**: The federal court had referred the dispute to California's insurance commissioner, generating an administrative proceeding; the parties entered voluntary mediation in January 2022 ([story](https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2022/02/01/newly-public-documents-allege-allstate-overcharged-loyal-california-customers-1-billion)).
- **Generalization**: When one jurisdiction exposes a hidden pricing mechanism, search administrative expert testimony and discovery disputes in other jurisdictions for the same operational terms. A public expert report can re-anchor a detector even when the original company data remains protected.

### Access Denied: Faulty Automated Background Checks Freeze Out Renters (2020) — A constructed corpus of tenant-screening lawsuits revealed routine identity and record-matching errors that cost people homes

- **URL**: [The Markup, May 28, 2020](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/05/28/access-denied-faulty-automated-background-checks-freeze-out-renters)
- **Partner/awards**: Joint investigation with **The New York Times**; Matthew Goldstein is co-bylined as a Times reporter ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/05/28/access-denied-faulty-automated-background-checks-freeze-out-renters)). No story-specific prize was found on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards); its [2021 annual report](https://themarkup.org/2021-annual-report) highlights the series' policy impact.
- **What they found**:
  - Automated reports were often produced in seconds from partial names, incomplete birth dates, and wildcard searches, with no human review. Samantha Johnson's report attached charges from multiple states that belonged to other people; Davone Jackson was denied low-income housing after records for Eric Jackson and James Jackson were attributed to him ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/05/28/access-denied-faulty-automated-background-checks-freeze-out-renters)).
  - Glenn Patrick Thompson Sr. and Jr. alleged they became homeless after a report attributed an eviction belonging to an unrelated Patricia Thompson to them. The story documented other mismatches involving dismissed cases, duplicate charges, and sex-offender records ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/05/28/access-denied-faulty-automated-background-checks-freeze-out-renters)).
  - The team requested contracts from **88 public housing authorities**, received 67 responses, and found 46 used for-profit screeners. It programmatically filtered hundreds of PACER cases and manually reviewed the complaints, leaving an explicitly qualitative corpus of about **150 relevant cases**, not a prevalence estimate ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry)).
  - Nearly every resolved case the team reviewed ended in settlement, and many settlement agreements prevented plaintiffs from discussing the case—systematically suppressing public learning about error modes ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `identity-resolution-failure` — a screening system assigns another person's record to the applicant; `automation-accountability-gap` — a rapid automated recommendation controls access to housing while vendor, landlord, and source agency each disclaim responsibility; `settlement-silence` — repeat defects remain hard to observe because resolution is confidential.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Attorney tip / discovery lead** — the investigation began with an attorney's tip that tenant screeners generated risk scores ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry)).
  - **Federal litigation corpus** — PACER cases since 2010 against about two dozen screeners, limited by nature-of-suit codes 480 and 890; dockets and complaints downloaded, OCR/text-converted, keyword-filtered, and manually reviewed ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry)).
  - **FOIA/public-record responses** — housing-authority screening contracts and accepted/rejected sample reports from 67 responding authorities; preserved in DocumentCloud and described in the [methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry).
  - **Screening reports versus authoritative records** — consumer reports, leases, signatures, eviction filings, criminal dockets, and identifiers compared case by case ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/05/28/access-denied-faulty-automated-background-checks-freeze-out-renters)).
  - **Constructed interviews** — applicants, lawyers, advocates, experts, housing officials, landlords, and screening-company employees ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(b) re-anchoring.** **Discovery/non-public half:** an attorney tip identified risk scoring as a tractable target, and interviews supplied cases and operational context. **Public verification half:** PACER complaints/dockets, housing-authority contracts and sample reports obtained under public-records laws, and underlying court/eviction records verified the error patterns. The nearest reusable path is the released case list plus PACER/RECAP and new housing-authority requests. [PACER remains live](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/), so public verification still works today.
- **Detection signature**: **`record-resolution mismatch / complaint-to-source-record mismatch corpus`** — a screener's report was joined to the authoritative court/eviction/source record and applicant identifiers on name, date of birth, address, signature, and case number; mismatched people, dispositions, offense levels, and case multiplicity revealed recurring false positives.
- **Corroboration structure**: The complaint corpus generated hypotheses but was not treated as adjudicated truth. The Markup checked disputed reports against source records, contacted plaintiffs and counsel, sought company responses, used housing-authority contracts to establish market penetration, and labeled the 150-case set qualitative rather than representative ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/05/28/access-denied-faulty-automated-background-checks-freeze-out-renters); [methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry)).
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [“How We Investigated the Tenant Screening Industry”](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry) specifies company selection, PACER codes, keyword filtering, manual review, and FOIA sampling. The [GitHub repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-tenant-screening/tree/data) publishes `courtcases.csv`; its data-branch README links the complaint and FOIA DocumentCloud projects. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: partially.** The published case set, complaint links, and allegation/resolution coding make the qualitative mismatch detector auditable, but the PACER downloader/parser code, discarded candidate universe, and interviews were not released, so the original selection pipeline cannot be fully reconstructed from artifacts alone.
- **Official impact**: Senators questioned regulators, the CFPB recommended that screeners stop an error-prone practice, and a D.C. discriminatory-algorithms proposal cited Locked Out; The Markup summarized those outcomes in its [annual report](https://themarkup.org/2021-annual-report) and [series index](https://themarkup.org/series/locked-out).
- **Generalization**: For any background, sanctions, fraud, identity, or eligibility screen, build a complaint corpus by vendor and join each adverse report to the authoritative source. Code mismatch class, identifier quality, disposition, correction time, decision consequence, and settlement secrecy.

### When Zombie Data Costs You a Home (2020) — Comparing expungement and source records with screening reports showed how obsolete or misclassified records survive correction

- **URL**: [The Markup, Oct. 6, 2020](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)
- **Partner/awards**: Markup original within Locked Out; the foundational corpus was built with The New York Times. No story-specific award was found on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - Rafaela Aldaco was denied transitional housing when a RentGrow report surfaced a nearly 20-year-old matter that had been dismissed after community service and probation; courts later held her guilty plea reportable under federal law, illustrating that harmful “zombie” data can be technically lawful as well as inaccurate ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
  - Brian Miller had three convictions expunged, yet a report showed those plus **26 supposed felonies**. His lawsuit alleged the vendor had treated prison disciplinary infractions from an Arizona corrections lookup as felony convictions ([story and linked records](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
  - Other records turned one charge into five convictions, counted probation violations as new convictions, rendered a speeding ticket as an unspecified criminal offense, and described a false burglar-alarm fine as a misdemeanor ([story and linked records](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
  - Pennsylvania's court system offered a counterexample: its “lifecycle file” sends free weekly updates such as expungements to data buyers, contractually requires incorporation, audits compliance, and can cut off noncompliant vendors ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `zombie-record-propagation` — deleted, sealed, dismissed, or superseded data survives in downstream copies; `event-to-conviction-collapse` — arrests, docket events, probation violations, tickets, or prison discipline are flattened into convictions; `update-obligation-gap` — a vendor ingests the initial adverse event without a reliable correction feed.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Litigation records** — complaints, declarations, rulings, and settlement histories drawn from the Locked Out federal-case corpus ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
  - **Consumer reports and source documents** — screening reports compared with expungement orders, court dockets, corrections records, traffic records, and municipal fines, linked through DocumentCloud ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
  - **Administrative process record** — Pennsylvania AOPC lifecycle-file practices and Clean Slate implementation, corroborated by an AOPC official and legal-services attorney ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
  - **Interviews** — affected renters, lawyers, researchers, and industry response ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive evidence is litigation and linked court/administrative documentation, supplemented by screening reports made public in those cases. **Today:** runnable; PACER remains active, the released [case corpus](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-tenant-screening/tree/data) persists, and no platform shutdown blocks the documentary diff. Cases resolved only through confidential settlement remain a visibility limit, not a platform closure.
- **Detection signature**: **`record-resolution mismatch / deletion-propagation diff`** — the current authoritative court disposition or expungement was compared with the downstream screening report on person and case number; records present downstream but absent, corrected, or differently classified upstream revealed stale-copy and event-flattening failures.
- **Corroboration structure**: Each vignette paired the applicant's report with a complaint and underlying source record; adverse court holdings were included even when they cut against the applicant; multiple error types across independent vendors supported a system pattern; Pennsylvania's audited update feed supplied a falsifying counterexample showing the problem is not inevitable ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
- **Methodology notes**: No story-specific Show Your Work page. **[inferred]** This story recodes the already constructed litigation corpus by update/disposition failure, based on its links to complaints, reports, and source records. The corpus method is documented in [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry), and the case list is in the [tenant-screening repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-tenant-screening/tree/data). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: largely yes** for the published examples; interview-only context is not.
- **Official impact**: The story documented an existing operational remedy rather than claiming a direct new policy result: Pennsylvania's lifecycle file and audits had already created a formal correction channel and the state had automatically sealed more than 35 million cases under Clean Slate ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/10/06/zombie-criminal-records-housing-background-checks)).
- **Generalization**: Any replicated data system needs a deletion/correction propagation audit. Sample records with later expungement, reversal, correction, debarment lift, sanctions delisting, or corporate dissolution; query downstream brokers; measure survival rate and lag. A lifecycle feed plus buyer audit is a transferable remedy.

### Can Algorithms Violate Fair Housing Laws? (2020) — Court records exposed a liability gap between the landlord making the decision and the vendor marketing a recommendation as decision-ready

- **URL**: [The Markup, Sept. 24, 2020; updated Jan. 27, 2021](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)
- **Partner/awards**: Markup original within Locked Out; the underlying market finding came from the joint New York Times investigation. No story-specific award was found on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - A Connecticut federal judge allowed Carmen Arroyo's disparate-impact claim against CoreLogic to proceed, described as the first such case aimed at a screening company rather than only a landlord. CoreLogic argued that landlords make the decisions; the judge noted it marketed CrimSAFE as a decision product and let landlords hide the record detail ([story and linked order](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
  - Arroyo's report merely said “criminal court action” without the detail needed for individualized assessment; her lawyers argued criminal-record screening disproportionately excluded Black and Latino applicants ([story and linked report/brief](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
  - HUD's 2020 disparate-impact rule raised plaintiffs' pleading burden and offered defenses involving predictive models. More than **45,000 public comments** opposed or addressed the change; housing groups sued, a federal judge paused the rule, and President Biden ordered HUD to examine its effects ([story and update](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `liability-shell-game` — each actor points to another node in the automated decision chain as the actual decision-maker; `detail-suppression-by-design` — a vendor converts a source record into a categorical recommendation while withholding facts needed to contest or contextualize it; `doctrine-implementation-conflict` — an agency rule narrows a protection recognized by judicial precedent.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Federal litigation record** — Arroyo complaint/briefs, CoreLogic defense, district order, CrimSAFE marketing material, and the screening report ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
  - **Regulatory record** — HUD final rule, 2016 HUD criminal-record guidance, Federal Register entry, and public comments ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
  - **Judicial precedent** — the 2015 Supreme Court disparate-impact decision compared with HUD's new defenses ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
  - **Prior market evidence** — Locked Out's finding that roughly nine in ten landlords use tenant-screening reports ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** Court opinions, briefs, agency rules, guidance, comments, and executive orders are public. **Today:** runnable; PACER/RECAP and government rulemaking archives still support the same chain-of-responsibility comparison. The vendor's full scoring logic remains proprietary, but the decisive legal detector does not require it.
- **Detection signature**: **`rule-to-practice loophole diff / vendor-decision-liability split`** — CoreLogic's marketing and categorical output were compared with its litigation claim that only landlords decide, while HUD's predictive-model defenses were compared with Supreme Court disparate-impact doctrine; the mismatches revealed responsibility and enforcement gaps.
- **Corroboration structure**: Adversarial filings supplied both plaintiff and vendor theories; the judge's order provided an independent procedural ruling without being overstated as final liability; the agency rule was tested against prior guidance and Supreme Court precedent; the 2021 update recorded intervening suits, injunction, and executive order ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
- **Methodology notes**: No dedicated Show Your Work page or new code. **[inferred]** A legal-record chain reconstruction, based on the linked order, briefs, report, rule, and guidance. The broader corpus method remains [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/05/28/how-we-investigated-the-tenant-screening-industry), with cases in the [tenant-screening repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-tenant-screening/tree/data). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes** as a documentary legal comparison.
- **Official impact**: Housing groups challenged the rule, a federal judge paused it, and President Biden directed HUD to examine its effects; the story's update records those formal actions without claiming they were caused by the article ([story](https://themarkup.org/locked-out/2020/09/24/fair-housing-laws-algorithms-tenant-screenings)).
- **Generalization**: Map every actor in an automated decision chain—data source, scorer, recommender, purchaser, human approver, and regulator—and collect each actor's public claim about who “decides.” Contradictory responsibility claims are a detector for accountability gaps in hiring, fraud screening, health utilization review, sanctions compliance, and content moderation.

### The Secret Bias Hidden in Mortgage-Approval Algorithms (2021) — Regression on more than two million public applications found 40–80 percent higher denial odds for applicants of color after extensive controls

- **URL**: [The Markup, Aug. 25, 2021](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/the-secret-bias-hidden-in-mortgage-approval-algorithms)
- **Partner/awards**: Co-published with **Associated Press**, as The Markup states in its [2022 impact follow-up](https://themarkup.org/denied/2022/02/04/markup-mortgage-industry-investigation-cited-in-support-of-algorithmic-accountability-bill). Under the domain rule, this report counts only the Markup copy. The [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards) names Denied among work honored in the 2021/22 season but does not bind a particular listed prize to this individual URL.
- **What they found**:
  - A binary logistic regression on **more than two million** 2019 first-lien conventional home-purchase applications held 17 factors constant. Relative to similar White applicants, lenders were **40 percent more likely to deny Latino applicants, 50 percent Asian/Pacific Islander applicants, 70 percent Native American applicants, and 80 percent Black applicants** ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/the-secret-bias-hidden-in-mortgage-approval-algorithms)).
  - The controls included income, loan amount, property value, debt-to-income, combined loan-to-value, mortgage term, age, sex, co-applicant, credit model used, automated underwriting system used, tract and metro context, and lender type/size. Credit score itself was not public; a separate CFPB analysis with scores still found disparities ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/08/25/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-federal-mortgage-data); [story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/the-secret-bias-hidden-in-mortgage-approval-algorithms)).
  - Meaningful disparities remained in **89 metro areas**. Examples included Black applicants 150 percent more likely to be denied in Chicago, Latino applicants more than 200 percent more likely in Waco, and Native American applicants 100 percent more likely in Minneapolis than comparable White applicants ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/the-secret-bias-hidden-in-mortgage-approval-algorithms)).
  - The analysis does **not** identify which proprietary underwriting algorithm caused any individual denial: public HMDA removes credit scores, exact addresses, decision dates, and the automated underwriting system's recommendation. It measures lender outcomes after controls, not intentional discrimination or a black-box model's internal rule ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/08/25/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-federal-mortgage-data)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `controlled-outcome-disparity` — protected-class outcome gaps persist after observable qualification and context controls; `proxy-entrenchment` — facially neutral financial variables and legacy credit infrastructure can reproduce historical inequality; `transparency-ceiling` — public data supports a disparity finding but withholds the fields needed to attribute mechanism.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Federal application microdata** — 2019 HMDA Loan/Application Register maintained by CFPB; filtered to made-or-denied owner-occupied first-lien conventional purchase loans for one-to-four-unit properties ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/08/25/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-federal-mortgage-data)).
  - **Government demographic and crosswalk data** — 2019 ACS race, property value, metro population, and county-to-metro files, plus FHFA lender metadata ([repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-redlining)).
  - **Government benchmark** — CFPB's restricted-data analysis including credit scores, used as an external robustness check ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/the-secret-bias-hidden-in-mortgage-approval-algorithms)).
  - **Regulatory/company records and interviews** — Fannie/Freddie/FHFA materials, lender and trade-group responses, loan officers, applicants, fair-housing experts, and government officials ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/the-secret-bias-hidden-in-mortgage-approval-algorithms)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** HMDA, Census, and FHFA inputs are public and the code is open. **Today:** detector runnable, but **exact historical reproduction is degraded**. The [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-redlining) omits the very large raw LAR and warns that CFPB later added 11,609 records; today's [HMDA download portal](https://ffiec.cfpb.gov/data-publication/) works but may not yield the publication's exact counts.
- **Detection signature**: **`controlled disparity test / protected-class coefficient after controls`** — HMDA decisions were joined to Census geography and lender metadata on tract/county/lender identifiers; denial was regressed on race plus 16 qualification/context variables, and statistically significant race odds ratios revealed disparities that raw denial rates alone could not isolate.
- **Corroboration structure**: One national model and derivative metro/lender models; explicit exclusion of small, poorly fit, collinear, or statistically unreliable subsets; standardized odds displays; pair illustrations of observably similar applicants; a CFPB score-inclusive analysis pointing in the same direction; industry critiques quoted and answered with limitations rather than suppressed ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/08/25/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-federal-mortgage-data)).
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/08/25/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-federal-mortgage-data) publishes cohort definition, 17 variables, model exclusions, coding rules, limitations, and industry response. The [GitHub repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-redlining) contains notebooks and cleaning/categorization/regression utilities plus supplemental data. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no.** It also requires the external 2019 CFPB LAR download, and today's revised file can shift results.
- **Official impact**: CFPB director Rohit Chopra cited the 80 percent Black-applicant finding when DOJ, CFPB, and other agencies announced the Combating Redlining Initiative; the Algorithmic Accountability Act of 2022 announcement also cited the work ([enforcement follow-up](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/10/22/markup-investigation-cited-by-officials-in-announcing-new-mortgage-discrimination-enforcement); [bill follow-up](https://themarkup.org/denied/2022/02/04/markup-mortgage-industry-investigation-cited-in-support-of-algorithmic-accountability-bill)).
- **Generalization**: The minimum reusable detector is a decision table with outcome, protected class, qualification variables, decision-maker, time, and geography. Run one pooled model, then nested geographic and institution models; publish sample-size, fit, collinearity, and missing-variable limits. Applies to grants, licensing, procurement bids, school admission, claims approval, and parole.

### Dozens of Mortgage Lenders Showed Significant Disparities. Here Are the Worst (2021) — Hierarchical slicing identified seven large lenders with at least 100 percent Black or Latino denial gaps

- **URL**: [The Markup, Aug. 25, 2021; corrected Nov. 2, 2021](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst)
- **Partner/awards**: Part of the Associated Press co-publication of Denied; The Markup-domain copy is counted. Denied is highlighted on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards) as 2021/22 honored work, without a one-prize/one-story mapping.
- **What they found**:
  - Of more than 5,500 reporting lenders, only institutions with more than 5,000 conventional purchase applications had enough volume for lender-level regression. After fit, collinearity, and significance exclusions, **26 lenders** showed statistically significant race results; seven met the story's highest-disparity and minimum-group-volume thresholds ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/08/25/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-federal-mortgage-data); [story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst)).
  - DHI Mortgage was **160 percent more likely to deny Black** and **100 percent more likely to deny Latino** applicants than comparable White applicants. Lennar Mortgage's gaps were 130 and 110 percent, respectively ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst)).
  - Mortgage affiliates of the nation's three largest home builders—DHI/D.R. Horton, Lennar Mortgage/Lennar, and Pulte Mortgage/PulteGroup—appeared among the worst. All six independent mortgage companies in the seven-lender set made at least 80 percent of their conventional loans in upper- and middle-class communities ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst)).
  - Every listed institution had faced at least one recent government criticism or enforcement action, but the story correctly did not treat unrelated settlements as proof that the measured race disparity was unlawful ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `institution-level-disparity` — a systemwide controlled gap concentrates in identifiable decision-makers; `ownership-cluster-risk` — multiple outlier decision-makers share a parent business model; `weak-regulatory-perimeter` — independent mortgage companies and credit unions face fewer community-lending obligations than banks.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **HMDA/FHFA/Census structured data** — the same public application universe and supplemental lender/geographic data as the national analysis ([repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-redlining)).
  - **Corporate ownership records** — lender-parent relationships and builder rankings checked against corporate sources and industry records ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst)).
  - **Government enforcement records** — CFPB, DOJ, HUD, state regulator, and attorney-general orders/settlements linked lender by lender ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst)).
  - **Company responses** — written responses were published, and several firms disputed completeness without identifying a calculation error ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** Inputs and model code are public. **Today:** runnable but **exact historical reproduction is degraded** for the same reason as the national card: the large raw 2019 HMDA file is external and was revised after publication ([repository note](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-redlining); [live HMDA portal](https://ffiec.cfpb.gov/data-publication/)).
- **Detection signature**: **`controlled disparity test / hierarchical institution outlier`** — the national HMDA cohort was partitioned by lender identifier; the adjusted denial model was rerun per lender after an N≥5,000 screen and model-quality exclusions; statistically significant protected-class odds ratios were ranked, then lender IDs joined to parent companies and enforcement records revealed the builder-affiliate cluster.
- **Corroboration structure**: Thresholds prevented ranking tiny institutions; unreliable, poor-fit, and collinear models were discarded; the story required more than 1,000 Black and Latino applications for its highest-disparity list; ownership was separately verified; enforcement histories were contextual rather than used as causal proof; a ranking correction was transparently appended ([story](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/08/25/dozens-of-mortgage-lenders-showed-significant-disparities-here-are-the-worst); [methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/08/25/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-federal-mortgage-data)).
- **Methodology notes**: Shares the dedicated [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2021/08/25/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-federal-mortgage-data) and [investigation-redlining repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-redlining). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**; it requires the external raw HMDA LAR. With that file, notebooks implement lender-level regressions and exclusion checks.
- **Official impact**: DOJ/CFPB's redlining initiative and later calls from legislators and regulators referenced the broader Denied findings; Minnesota's attorney general warned lenders not to be surprised by investigations ([federal follow-up](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/10/22/markup-investigation-cited-by-officials-in-announcing-new-mortgage-discrimination-enforcement); [state/legislative follow-up](https://themarkup.org/denied/2021/12/09/legislators-regulators-call-for-additional-scrutiny-of-mortgage-lending-industry-after-markup-investigation-finds-disparities)).
- **Generalization**: Always rerun a pooled disparity model at the decision-maker level, then join outliers to ownership, regulator, business model, and prior sanctions. System averages can conceal concentrated harm; shared parents can reveal a policy or incentive operating across nominally separate entities.

### Michigan's “Fair and Reasonable” Reforms Allowed Car Insurers to Charge More in Black Neighborhoods (2024) — Reconstructed insurance territories showed that banning zip codes led to finer geographic proxies concentrated in Black communities

- **URL**: [The Markup, July 18, 2024](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/michigans-fair-and-reasonable-reforms-allowed-car-insurers-to-charge-more-in-black-neighborhoods)
- **Partner/awards**: Coreported with Detroit nonprofit **Outlier Media**; Koby Levin and Sarah Alvarez are co-bylined for Outlier ([story](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/michigans-fair-and-reasonable-reforms-allowed-car-insurers-to-charge-more-in-black-neighborhoods)). This post-merger Markup-domain article is counted; no separate CalMatters copy is added. No project-specific award was found on The Markup's [Awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - The team reviewed **more than 52,000 pages** of approved rate filings for seven insurers representing **83 percent** of Michigan's private-passenger-auto market and manually cataloged hundreds of rating factors ([story](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/michigans-fair-and-reasonable-reforms-allowed-car-insurers-to-charge-more-in-black-neighborhoods); [methodology](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan)).
  - Michigan's 2019 law banned “postal zone” rating but expressly allowed risks to be grouped by “territory.” Insurers replaced roughly 1,000 zip codes with units ranging from Liberty Mutual's 83 counties to State Farm's approximately **176,700 one-square-mile territories**, enabling much finer geographic differentiation ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan)).
  - Across insurers, an average **64 percent of Black Michiganders** lived in each insurer's highest quintile of location effects, where the location component was 10–360 percent above that insurer's state median. For Auto Club and Citizens, about **7 percent of White residents** but more than half of Black residents lived in the top quintile ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan); [story](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/michigans-fair-and-reasonable-reforms-allowed-car-insurers-to-charge-more-in-black-neighborhoods)).
  - State Farm's highest location effect was in Detroit's 97-percent-Black Morningside neighborhood and was nearly **seven times** its lowest Michigan territory. Along 8 Mile Road, 80 percent of compared territories south of the road had higher location effects than nearest neighbors to the north, and half were at least 50 percent higher. A controlled quote exercise showed one Detroit participant around $3,500 per year versus under $1,800 at a mostly White suburban address 13 miles away; the filing analysis isolates address effects but does not estimate a person's final premium or prove intentional racial treatment ([story](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/michigans-fair-and-reasonable-reforms-allowed-car-insurers-to-charge-more-in-black-neighborhoods); [methodology](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan)).
- **Finding type(s)**: `geographic-proxy-redlining` — geography functions as a high-resolution proxy that concentrates costs in racialized places; `loophole-substitution` — a prohibited field is replaced with a legally permitted, more granular equivalent; `boundary-price-cliff` — nearby residents receive sharply different location adjustments across an administrative or racial boundary.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Regulatory rate filings** — approved May 2022–August 2023 private-auto filings from Michigan SERFF, plus pre-reform Allstate and Liberty Mutual filings; seven insurers chosen from NAIC market-share data ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan)).
  - **Constructed rate tables/geographies** — PDF territory tables extracted; base rates and location relativities combined into an insurer-specific “location effect” indexed to that insurer's median; custom maps reconstructed or matched to best-fitting Census boundaries ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan)).
  - **Government demographic/geospatial data** — 2022 five-year ACS, TIGER/Line boundaries, and NHGIS crosswalks to interpolate current counts into 2010 census geographies ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan)).
  - **Constructed instrument / controlled quote test** — three Black Detroit women and a cooperating insurance agent compared current addresses with predominantly White suburban addresses; anonymity protected the agent from professional repercussions ([story](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/michigans-fair-and-reasonable-reforms-allowed-car-insurers-to-charge-more-in-black-neighborhoods)).
  - **Legal/policy records** — 2019 reform language, insurer filings before and after reform, and lawmaker/industry responses ([story](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/michigans-fair-and-reasonable-reforms-allowed-car-insurers-to-charge-more-in-black-neighborhoods)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** Public rate filings and Census data drive the decisive detector, and extracted filings, clean outputs, notebooks, Docker configuration, and insurer-specific analysis are released. **Today:** exact published analysis is runnable from the [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigate-mi-insurance-territories). Fresh automated SERFF collection is degraded by a site countermeasure (Michigan URL returned HTTP 403 to direct automation on July 29, 2026), although [SERFF Filing Access](https://www.serff.com/serff_filing_access.htm) remains public-facing. The anonymous-agent quote instrument is **not** reproducible from released artifacts alone and would require a cooperating licensed agent, but it is corroboration, not the decisive detector.
- **Detection signature**: **`spatial proxy join / territory-demography adjacency audit`** — each insurer's base rate and location relativities were reconstructed into a median-indexed location effect, territory polygons/points were joined to ACS race and income on the best-fitting geography, and each territory was compared with demographic quintiles and nearest neighbors—especially across 8 Mile—revealing racial concentration and border cliffs.
- **Corroboration structure**: Convergent statewide quintile, Spearman rank-correlation, within-county, and nearest-neighbor views; seven different insurer systems; pre/post-law comparison where filings supported it; conservative choice of Citizens' lower location factor; quote test; explicit negative findings for inconclusive insurer-level correlations. The team rejected ordinary regression because spatial dependence violated the independence assumption and used Spearman rather than Pearson because Detroit outliers could dominate ([methodology](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan)).
- **Methodology notes**: The dedicated [“How We Investigated Car Insurance Loopholes in Michigan”](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2024/07/18/how-we-investigated-car-insurance-loopholes-in-michigan) documents selection, extraction, formula reconstruction, geography choice, adjacency tests, quintiles, statistics, exclusions, and limits. The BSD-licensed [GitHub repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigate-mi-insurance-territories) says its insurer notebooks extract tables and “replicate and fact check all the data figures and findings.” **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes.**
- **Official impact**: In 2025 Rep. Rashida Tlaib reintroduced federal legislation that would ban census-tract and credit-based insurance pricing; the impact story explicitly linked the reintroduction to the Markup/Outlier findings ([follow-up](https://themarkup.org/premium-penalty/2025/06/11/house-bill-targets-loopholes-that-let-car-insurance-companies-charge-more-in-black-neighborhoods)).
- **Generalization**: When law bans an input, compare pre/post implementation and search for substitute variables with equal or greater resolution. Spatial systems demand adjacency/border tests, not only statewide correlation. Applies to school zones, service territories, environmental permitting, delivery fees, policing grids, hospital catchments, and political districting.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### Pattern A — Cost/Decision Two-Books Diff

- **Mechanics**: Build three columns for the same unit: current treatment, provider-claimed benchmark treatment, and final offered treatment. Compute both residuals and look for asymmetric caps, discontinuities, or segments tied to tenure/spend rather than the benchmark.
- **Minimum data**: Stable unit ID; current outcome/price; provider's own cost, risk, or eligibility benchmark; final decision; time or cohort.
- **Recognition cues**: “Transition,” “retention,” “loyalty,” “revenue neutral,” “grandfathered,” “closed book,” “manual override,” or a large gap between deserved decrease and delivered decrease.
- **Domains**: Insurance, salaries, loan modifications, grant scoring, utility arrears, procurement negotiations, subscription renewal, and claims settlement.

#### Pattern B — Adverse Report / Authoritative Record Reconciliation

- **Mechanics**: Treat a screening report as a derived database record. Join it back to the authoritative source on person/entity identifiers and case/event ID; code wrong-person, wrong-disposition, duplication, stale deletion, and severity inflation.
- **Minimum data**: Adverse report or complaint describing it; authoritative source record; identifiers; decision consequence; correction date.
- **Recognition cues**: Partial-name or wildcard search, vague “record found” labels, missing dates of birth, expunged events, multiple rows from one case, vendor disclaimer that purchaser should verify.
- **Domains**: Tenant/employment screening, sanctions/AML, vendor debarment, beneficial ownership, medical credentialing, fraud lists, and content matching.

#### Pattern C — Protected-Class Residual + Hierarchical Outlier

- **Mechanics**: Model a binary or continuous outcome with observable qualification controls and a protected-class variable; repeat the model by geography and decision-maker after preregistered N/fit thresholds; join outliers to ownership and regulator histories.
- **Minimum data**: Outcome; protected class or defensible geographic proxy; qualification covariates; decision-maker; time; geography; adequate group sample.
- **Recognition cues**: Industry claims that raw gaps are explained by “risk,” “qualifications,” or “credit”; newly disclosed variables; a pooled gap that may hide institutional concentration.
- **Domains**: Lending, contracting, permits, benefits, school admission, medical approvals, insurance claims, and parole.

#### Pattern D — Boundary Cliff / Proxy Territory Audit

- **Mechanics**: Reconstruct every location-based factor, index within provider, overlay demographic and income geography, then compare adjacent or nearest-neighbor units across salient borders. Use spatially appropriate tests and do not treat ordinary regression independence as given.
- **Minimum data**: Territory points/polygons; provider factor or price; demographic geography; adjacency or distance graph; provider/state median.
- **Recognition cues**: Thousands of custom microterritories, coordinate grids, sudden changes at city/road/county lines, or a nominal ban on zip codes followed by finer units.
- **Domains**: Insurance, delivery/service fees, school zones, policing grids, environmental exposure, healthcare networks, political districts, and utility service.

#### Pattern E — Rule-to-Implementation Substitution Audit

- **Mechanics**: Extract a reform's prohibited fields/functions; compare pre- and post-rule operational documents; identify replacement variables and test whether they reproduce the forbidden effect. Diff later remedial bills against the original rule to see which loopholes entered the policy feedback loop.
- **Minimum data**: Rule text; pre/post implementation manuals or filings; operational outputs; effective dates; outcome data.
- **Recognition cues**: Narrowly enumerated prohibitions, “except,” “territory,” “proxy,” “alternative model,” or a banned variable replaced with a more granular one.
- **Domains**: Discrimination rules, sanctions evasion, procurement thresholds, tax treatment, privacy consent, school filtering, and benefit eligibility.

### 5. What this platform can run now, and what is missing

**Runnable with current holdings**

- **Litigation-to-entity patterning**: CourtListener/RECAP plus 20+ corporate registries, OpenCorporates, GLEIF, OpenSanctions, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, and local OpenAleph can reproduce the tenant-screening strategy at the company/network level: find repeated FCRA or fair-housing defendants, resolve parents/subsidiaries, code allegations and dispositions, and identify shared vendors or counsel.
- **Regulatory/litigation two-books diff**: Public complaints, expert reports, and administrative exhibits in RECAP/OpenAleph can be joined to corporate identities and prior enforcement. This can surface recurring retention pricing, hidden segmentation, or accountability defenses even without raw customer data.
- **Property-owner accountability joins**: Property/deed tools plus registries and court records can connect adverse housing practices to actual property owners, managers, screening vendors, and portfolio structures—especially where nominal landlords hide behind LLCs.
- **Public-contract screening-vendor audit**: USASpending/FPDS plus registries and court records can identify agencies buying screening, scoring, identity, or fraud products and compare vendor litigation/enforcement histories with award timing.
- **Rule-to-practice ownership analysis**: Corporate registries, court records, patents, and government contracts support substitution and diffusion questions: which vendors sell the same model through affiliates, which agencies adopted it, and whether litigation or reform changed the ownership/deployment network.

**Missing or only partial**

- **HMDA/FHFA/Census analytic stack**: The listed holdings do not include HMDA application microdata, ACS/TIGER/NHGIS geographies, or a maintained lender crosswalk. Without them, the platform cannot run the mortgage residual and metro/lender disparity tests.
- **SERFF/insurance filing corpus**: No nationwide rate-filing ingestion, territory extractor, actuarial schema, or filing-version history is listed. The platform cannot refresh Allstate or Michigan insurance detectors at scale, and SERFF's anti-automation behavior raises an acquisition problem.
- **Consumer decision/output data**: No bulk tenant reports, mortgage underwriting recommendations, credit scores, insurance quotes, policy-level premiums, or final eligibility outputs. Court records reveal failures selectively, not prevalence.
- **Complete federal docket coverage**: CourtListener/RECAP is invaluable but incomplete relative to PACER. A nationwide complaint corpus still needs PACER access, careful nature-of-suit/vendor queries, OCR, and a documented candidate denominator.
- **Constructed instruments and field partners**: The platform lacks a maintained controlled-submission protocol and access to licensed insurance agents or cooperating applicants. Those are needed to validate what consumers actually receive after documentary models predict a disparity.

The immediate best fit is **Pattern B (adverse-report reconciliation)** and the entity/ownership half of **Patterns A and E**. Pattern C and Pattern D become runnable after adding HMDA plus Census geographies and a SERFF filing/territory pipeline. The highest-value infrastructure additions are therefore: (1) versioned HMDA/ACS/FHFA ingestion, (2) SERFF filing acquisition with a lawful human-in-the-loop fallback and artifact cache, and (3) a RECAP/PACER complaint-corpus builder that preserves the complete candidate denominator.
