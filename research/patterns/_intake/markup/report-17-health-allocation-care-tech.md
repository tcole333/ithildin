# The Markup Evidence Ontology — Cluster C8: Health Allocation & Care Technology

Compiled July 29, 2026. Nine substantive editorial units from the census's 11-page C8 cluster, covering the bounded series [Organ Failure](https://themarkup.org/series/organ-failure) and [On Borrowed Time](https://themarkup.org/series/on-borrowed-time). The other two census pages are the Show Your Work methods attached to the liver investigations.

## Scope and attribution

The nine units comprise six Organ Failure investigations or official-impact updates, one patient-centered investigation of a race-adjusted kidney calculation, one patient-navigation guide, and one organ-donation explainer. The last two are retained because they contain the cluster's distinctive **patient-navigation evidence** and a constructed community survey; they are identified as service evidence rather than silently treated as original data investigations.

The 2023 liver package says it was reported in partnership with **The Washington Post**, and the 2024 racial-disparity story is explicitly labeled coreported with the Post. The three 2025 pages are Markup-domain copies explicitly copublished with **Word In Black**. Although The Markup merged with CalMatters in 2024, the census's domain-structural attribution rule keeps those three `themarkup.org` pages in The Markup corpus; Word In Black's six other installments are not added. The full nine-part Word In Black series won the [2026 NAACP Image Award for Outstanding Literary Work—Journalism](https://wordinblack.com/2026/02/anissa-durham-on-borrowed-time-naacp-image-award/). No Organ Failure-specific prize was identified on The Markup's [awards page](https://themarkup.org/awards).

“Runnable today” means an outside investigator can obtain fresh decisive inputs through the same core public path, or rerun the released historical package, as of July 29, 2026. It does not mean a 2026 rerun will reproduce a 2019–2021 outcome. “Released-artifact rerunnable” is stricter: the exact decisive analysis can be recomputed from the files and code The Markup or its partner released, without filing a new records request or rebuilding an undocumented collector.

One census descriptor needs refinement. The liver work did not simulate the proprietary allocation algorithm or counterfactually assign each organ under a different rule. It **reconstructed realized donor-to-recipient flows**, compared pre/post-policy periods, approximated travel distance, and normalized outcomes by population, mortality, donations, and income. That distinction is explicit in the [2023 methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy).

### Poorer States Suffer Under New Organ Donation Rules, As Livers Go to Waste (2023) — Reconstructing actual liver flows showed that acuity circles shifted transplants toward richer states while travel and discards rose

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/poorer-states-suffer-under-new-organ-donation-rules-as-livers-go-to-waste); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup and **The Washington Post** reporting partnership; no story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Between 2019 and 2021, adult liver transplants fell by more than 5 percent and more than five procedures in Alabama, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, and Puerto Rico, even as the national total rose 5 percent. Alabama fell from **128 to 72 transplants (−44%)** and Louisiana from **239 to 174 (−27%)**. New York rose from **457 to 602 (+32%)**, while California rose from 903 to 959; both recorded decade highs. ([state analysis](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
  - The share of livers transplanted outside the donor's state rose from **32 percent to 55 percent**. The number of states or territories exporting a majority of their donated livers rose from 22 to 42, while majority importers rose from nine to 31. ([flow analysis](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
  - Approximate median donor-to-recipient distance nearly doubled, from **85 miles to 163 miles**; the share traveling more than 200 miles rose from 30 to 44 percent. The investigators deliberately used straight-line distance, which likely understates actual travel. ([distance method and result](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
  - **949 recovered livers—one in ten—were discarded in 2021**, the highest count in the analyzed decade. The team correctly noted that discards had already begun rising before the February 2020 policy change, so this is temporal concurrence plus a plausible mechanism, not proof that acuity circles caused every discard. ([discard analysis](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
  - Need normalization sharpened the geography finding: states with worse liver-disease mortality and lower incomes often lost transplant capacity, while lower-mortality states gained. Recipients also traveled across state lines for 19 percent of analyzed transplants, a caveat to treating transplant-hospital state as patient-residence state. ([per-capita analysis and caveat](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
- **Finding type(s)**: **scarce-resource redistribution** (new tag: a rule change moves a finite, life-affecting resource between populations rather than merely changing an average); **need-allocation mismatch** (new tag: realized allocation is inversely or weakly aligned with an independent measure of underlying need); **logistics-externality** (new tag: a fairness or priority rule creates travel, delay, spoilage, or cost outside the decision score); **policy-beneficiary concentration** (new tag: institutions that advocated for a rule are concentrated among its measurable beneficiaries).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **FOIA administrative microdata** — several HRSA requests produced 192,982 transplant records, 250,941 deceased-donor records, 341,208 candidate records, 1,048,575 organ-disposition records, facility tables, donor-hospital crosswalks, and coordinates maintained by the Scientific Registry of Transplant Recipients. The analysis narrowed these to adult, policy-relevant periods. ([data inventory](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
  - **Constructed linkage and flow table** — demographic fields recovered redacted donor IDs for all 34,526 adult transplants in the narrowed file; facility IDs, ZIP codes, and coordinates located 32,737 deceased-donor transplants; donor-state × recipient-state pairs became an origin-destination matrix. ([linkage method](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
  - **Need and socioeconomic denominators** — CDC WONDER chronic-liver-disease mortality (ICD-10 K70, K73–K74), population, 2021 American Community Survey household income/poverty, state area, and transplant-hospital counts. ([external datasets](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
  - **Policy and validation records** — OPTN aggregated counts and monitoring reports, allocation rules, public comments, litigation, and UNOS responses; the team reran its analysis using UNOS's own 731-day windows and retained the same major conclusions. ([validation and response](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy))
  - **Affected-person and expert reporting** — waitlisted patients, caregivers, transplant surgeons, advocates, UNOS, and federal officials put the geographic measurements into the actual prerequisites for listing: insurance, travel, dental clearance, regular appointments, and post-operative medication. ([main story](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/poorer-states-suffer-under-new-organ-donation-rules-as-livers-go-to-waste))
  - **Released package** — [`the-markup/investigation-organs`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-organs) includes redacted raw inputs, cleaned R data, ten R/Jupyter analysis notebooks, outputs, and a BSD-3-Clause license.
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The historical detector is fully rerunnable from the released repository: its README says raw and cleaned data, analysis notebooks, and committed outputs are included. A fresh update still has a public route: HRSA maintains [OPTN reports and a data-request path](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/data-calculators/about-optn-data), and CDC WONDER and Census denominators remain available. The system is being moved to a federal multi-vendor and cloud structure, but HRSA says data reliability and public dashboards are being expanded, not shut down. ([June 2026 modernization status](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn-modernization/learn-more-about-modernization))
- **Detection signature**: **policy-shock flow reconstruction + need normalization** — 2019 donor/recovery records were compared with 2021 transplant/disposition records on `donor ID → donor facility/state → recipient facility/state`, revealing changed origin-destination flows; those state outcomes were joined to CDC mortality, population, donations, and ACS income on `state/year`, revealing that several higher-need, poorer states lost procedures while lower-need beneficiaries gained.
- **Corroboration structure**: Three outcome views—absolute transplants, per-capita rates, and transplants per donated liver—pointed in the same direction; the origin-destination matrix showed where organs moved; coordinates measured the logistical mechanism; disposition codes measured discards; official OPTN counts bounded record discrepancies to less than 0.01 percent; UNOS's alternative time window produced the same major conclusions. Patient and clinician accounts supplied consequences but did not substitute for the data.
- **Methodology notes**: The [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy) states the inputs, exclusions, joins, time-window sensitivity tests, and limitations. The [`investigation-organs`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-organs) repository is unusually complete: the decisive historical analysis **is re-runnable from released artifacts alone** after decompressing inputs and installing the documented R packages. It reconstructs observed allocation outcomes; it does not rerun UNOS's match engine.
- **Official impact**: One day after publication, HHS announced the contract-unbundling plan covered below; Congress later enacted [Public Law 118-14](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/2544), authorizing multiple OPTN awards. The timing and later reform are documented, but neither source establishes that this story alone caused them.
- **Generalization**: Use on emergency dispatch, school seats, housing vouchers, court assignments, water, disaster aid, grants, or procurement. Generic detector: build before/after origin-destination matrices; normalize recipients by need, population, and local contribution; measure distance, delay, spoilage, and cost; then identify whether rule advocates are concentrated among beneficiaries.

### How a Group of Health Executives Transformed the Liver Transplant System (2023) — Litigation, money, committee membership, and internal emails showed beneficiaries manufacturing the procedural pressure for a rule change

- **URL**: [Investigation](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system); related [data methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup and **The Washington Post**; no story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - A coalition founded by the Greater New York Hospital Association, LiveOnNY, and OneLegacy expanded to more than a dozen mostly New York, California, and Massachusetts institutions. Reporters found that its members recruited at least four of the six patients in the 2018 suit that became the lever for broader liver sharing; five plaintiffs received transplants before the policy change. ([coalition and plaintiff reconstruction](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
  - The Greater New York Hospital Association acknowledged funding some of the lawsuit, while public tax records showed about **$200,000** paid to the plaintiffs' law firm. It also donated **$57,200** to the 2018 campaigns of six New York sponsors of the related Fairness in Liver Allocation bill; 26 of the bill's 27 cosponsors came from the three beneficiary states. ([money and legislative trail](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
  - UNOS executives used the 2017 lung lawsuit to create an Ad Hoc Geography Committee. Emails showed a board member taking credit for “coaching” a liver-committee member on talking points, while an eventual committee member helped edit recommendations resembling the later acuity-circles framework. ([email and committee sequence](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
  - A five-year expert process had produced a 150-mile compromise, but after the lawsuit UNOS volunteered a six-month rewrite. The liver committee recommended the alternative with less travel and cost; the board selected acuity circles despite committee warnings about efficiency and discards. ([process compression](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
  - A non-public 2019 Gift of Life Institute audit found that LiveOnNY—the procurement organization serving a principal beneficiary—had poor training, undefined standards, missed donation opportunities, and staff who left possible donors before completing assessment. Its then-CEO received **$768,000** in 2019 compensation including a $300,000 bonus, while the organization remained in the lowest federal performance tier. ([audit findings and compensation](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
- **Finding type(s)**: **policy-process capture** (new tag: beneficiaries shape the nominally neutral procedure, committee, evidence frame, or urgency that selects a rule); **litigation-as-policy-lever** (new tag: a lawsuit is organized or financed to create administrative pressure beyond its named plaintiffs' remedy); **procurement-failure externalization** (new tag: an institution responds to its own weak local supply or performance by changing rules to draw resources from other jurisdictions); **governance-conflict network** (new tag: board, committee, employer, funder, and beneficiary ties overlap around a public decision).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Court records and discovery emails** — complaints, orders, litigation correspondence, and nearly 600 public pages of discovery emails supplied dates, actors, talking points, and admissions. ([court-email trail](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
  - **Coalition, committee, and policy records** — CODE membership, OPTN liver-committee and Ad Hoc Geography Committee rosters/reports, board actions, public comments, HHS correspondence, and the compressed rulemaking timeline. ([policy sequence](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
  - **Financial records** — GNYHA tax filings, FEC campaign contributions, bill sponsorship, press releases, and OPO executive compensation connected advocacy to money and beneficiaries. ([financial trail](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
  - **Non-public performance audit** — The Markup obtained the March 2019 LiveOnNY audit; it later published a retyped, redacted version to protect its source. ([published audit description](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
  - **Interviews** — three plaintiffs confirmed hospital recruitment, another was recruited through OneLegacy, and lawyers, transplant executives, surgeons, experts, and institutions were questioned about the process. ([plaintiff sourcing](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system))
  - **Outcome anchor** — the separate FOIA microdata analysis showed who gained, who lost, and how flows changed. The [`investigation-organs`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-organs) repository covers this quantitative outcome layer, not the documentary capture investigation.
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(b) re-anchoring.** The non-public LiveOnNY audit was needed to expose the procurement failure that advocacy omitted. The central policy-capture sequence is nevertheless verifiable through public litigation records and discovery emails, OPTN committee records, tax filings, FEC records, bill sponsorship, and named-plaintiff interviews. Reusable public half: beneficiary/funder/committee/lawsuit/timeline and outcome. Non-public half: the audit's granular workflow failures and the provenance of the copy The Markup obtained.
- **Detection signature**: **beneficiary-to-policy sequence reconstruction** — coalition membership, plaintiff treatment centers, law-firm payments, campaign contributions, committee rosters, emails, and policy events were joined on `organization/person/date`, revealing that measurable beneficiaries recruited plaintiffs, funded legal and legislative pressure, shaped the committee frame, and then gained under the selected rule.
- **Corroboration structure**: Public money records established payments; plaintiffs established recruitment; court discovery established private strategy; official rosters and reports established formal roles; the microdata established beneficiary outcomes; the confidential audit established the omitted local-performance problem. Each channel proves a different link, avoiding the error of treating shared membership alone as proof of coordination.
- **Methodology notes**: No dedicated Show Your Work page or documentary repository was released. **[inferred]** method: construct a master actor-event timeline from dockets, emails, committee records, financial filings, policy drafts, and interviews; then overlay measured state outcomes. Basis: the source trail and chronological organization of the article. The related [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2023/03/21/how-we-investigated-unoss-liver-allocation-policy) and [`investigation-organs`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-organs) package reproduce only the outcome analysis. The decisive documentary detector is **not re-runnable from released artifacts alone**, although much of it can be rebuilt from public records.
- **Official impact**: HRSA formally admonished then-UNOS CEO Brian Shepard over the released emails and required a corrective plan; Shepard left in 2022. Systemically, Congress later authorized multiple contractors and HRSA separated the OPTN board from UNOS. ([regulator response reported in the story](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/21/how-a-group-of-health-executives-transformed-the-liver-transplant-system); [independent-board announcement](https://www.hrsa.gov/about/news/press-releases/historic-overhaul-optn))
- **Generalization**: Apply to zoning, rate-setting, procurement standards, school formulas, utility territories, professional regulation, or sanctions exemptions. Generic detector: list beneficiaries; resolve their board, employer, funder, counsel, plaintiff, lobbyist, and committee ties; align communications and payments to procedural events; compare the stated trigger with evidence that planning predated it; finally test whether beneficiaries gained.

### HHS Plans Breakup of Organ Transplant Monopoly (2023) — An official announcement converted a four-decade single-vendor system into a contract-unbundling reform target

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/22/hhs-plans-breakup-of-organ-transplant-monopoly); [contemporaneous HRSA announcement](https://www.hrsa.gov/about/news/press-releases/organ-procurement-transplantation-network-modernization-initiative)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup follow-up; the plan was first reported by **The Washington Post**. No story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - HRSA announced that it would split the all-encompassing OPTN contract into multiple awards, threatening UNOS's nearly 40-year control over policy support, oversight, matching technology, and system operations. ([announcement coverage](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/22/hhs-plans-breakup-of-organ-transplant-monopoly))
  - The incumbent contract cited in the story was worth **$6.5 million**, but contract value understated UNOS's structural power because the contractor simultaneously supported governance, technology, data, membership, safety, and rulemaking. ([contract scope](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/22/hhs-plans-breakup-of-organ-transplant-monopoly))
  - The announcement came one day after the initial Organ Failure package, amid preexisting congressional investigations and official criticism. Sequence is evidence of contemporaneous reform, not enough to attribute the plan solely to the reporting. ([timing and context](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/22/hhs-plans-breakup-of-organ-transplant-monopoly))
- **Finding type(s)**: **contractor-monopoly unbundling** (new tag: a government breaks one vertically integrated public-function contract into separable, competitively awardable functions); **governance-operations separation** (new tag: policymaking or oversight is structurally separated from the contractor implementing or benefiting from it); **official-impact uptake** (new tag: a documented government action directly addresses the institutional mechanism an investigation examined, without necessarily proving causation).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Official release and testimony** — HRSA's modernization announcement and HHS Secretary Xavier Becerra's Senate Finance Committee description. ([HRSA release](https://www.hrsa.gov/about/news/press-releases/organ-procurement-transplantation-network-modernization-initiative); [story](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/22/hhs-plans-breakup-of-organ-transplant-monopoly))
  - **Federal contract record** — incumbent award value, scope, duration, and HRSA's plan for multiple solicitations. ([story and linked records](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/03/22/hhs-plans-breakup-of-organ-transplant-monopoly))
  - **Underlying investigations** — the state-flow analysis, policy-capture reconstruction, and prior congressional oversight established why bundling mattered. ([Organ Failure series](https://themarkup.org/series/organ-failure))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The decisive evidence is a public agency announcement, contract structure, legislation, and subsequent awards. No leak or platform access is needed. The path has improved: HRSA now publishes modernization updates and a vendor/function table. ([current structure](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn-modernization/learn-more-about-modernization))
- **Detection signature**: **contract-scope unbundling diff** — the incumbent contract's `vendor → functions` map was compared with HRSA's proposed and later awarded `function → vendor` map, revealing which governance, safety, policy, data, and operations roles were separated from UNOS.
- **Corroboration structure**: The agency release establishes intent; the enacted law establishes authority; solicitations and awards establish implementation; the historical contract establishes the before-state. Reporting about policy failures explains significance but is not needed to prove the procurement change.
- **Methodology notes**: No Show Your Work page or repository exists. **[inferred]** method: monitor the official announcement, obtain the incumbent contract scope, compare promised lots with actual solicitations/awards, and maintain a function-vendor timeline. Basis: the story's linked agency and contract records. The detector is **fully re-runnable from public artifacts alone**.
- **Official impact**: By June 2026 HRSA listed separate vendors for board support, meetings, transplant-data infrastructure, SRTR, patient safety/compliance, safety standards, and matching/data; UNOS retained the matching system and data role but no longer held the whole system. ([HRSA multi-vendor table](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn-modernization/learn-more-about-modernization))
- **Generalization**: Apply when one vendor operates a registry, writes rules, evaluates compliance, adjudicates disputes, and owns the software. Generic detector: decompose the contract into functions; mark conflicts and single points of failure; compare nominal contract price with fees and operational control; track whether reform changes vendors, governance, data custody, or merely labels.

### A Crucial Link in the Nation's Organ Donation Process Is Failing (2023) — Objective federal benchmarks and local trigger rules exposed widespread procurement underperformance hidden by years of self-reporting

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing); [published retyped LiveOnNY audit](https://mrkp-static-production.themarkup.org/uploads/2023/04/LiveOnNY-audit-2019-retyped.pdf)
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original follow-up; no co-publisher or story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - The United States had **56 OPOs when the story ran**, but CMS's cited 2021 performance cohort contained 57 organizations, before a January 2023 merger reduced the roster to 56. In that cohort, 15 were Tier 1, 18 were Tier 2, and **24—42 percent—were Tier 3**, two more than in the preceding report. This reconciles the otherwise confusing 15 + 18 + 24 arithmetic. ([The Markup finding](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing); [CMS cohort and merger note](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/opo-annual-public-performance-report-2023.pdf))
  - Before the 2021 standards, OPOs largely self-reported performance, making comparison unreliable. Despite chronic performance problems, no OPO had lost its multimillion-dollar CMS certification contract for poor performance. Advocates estimated that underperformance cost as many as **28,000 organs a year**; the article labels that as an advocate estimate, not an agency count. ([oversight gap](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
  - More than a dozen reviewed “clinical trigger” policies differed on whether one or two absent brainstem reflexes should prompt a referral, whether and how to use the Glasgow Coma Scale, and whether hospitals could wait one hour or four. These are upstream process choices capable of making potential donors disappear before the official outcome denominator. ([trigger-rule comparison](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
  - The obtained LiveOnNY audit documented inconsistent training, lack of competency checks, and staff delaying or abandoning family approaches because they feared refusal—failures auditors said caused lost opportunities. ([audit evidence](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
  - OPOs already submitted death-notification records containing referral time, consent, demographic, cause-of-death, and disposition fields, while a richer voluntary research dataset tracked arrival, family approach, response, and allocation timing. The reporting identified an **unused-regulatory-data gap**: the detailed process data existed but was not routinely used for public accountability. ([available process data](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
- **Finding type(s)**: **benchmark accountability failure** (new tag: an objective peer benchmark reveals chronic underperformance that the oversight regime tolerated); **self-report replacement shock** (new tag: rankings materially change when self-reported denominators are replaced by common external measures); **upstream workflow variance** (new tag: local intake or trigger rules change who enters a supposedly uniform national process); **unused-process-data gap** (new tag: regulators possess granular event data but evaluate only coarse outcomes).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **CMS administrative performance reports** — common donation and transplant outcome measures, tier assignments, and year-over-year movement for all OPOs. ([CMS's transparent-measure design](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/organ-procurement-organization-opo-conditions-coverage-final-rule-revisions-outcome-measures-opos))
  - **Clinical-trigger rule corpus** — more than a dozen hospital/OPO referral policies found online or obtained from other sources and manually compared. ([story method](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
  - **Internal audit** — a source-protected, retyped 2019 Gift of Life Institute assessment of LiveOnNY; the public version omits original appendices. ([provenance note](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
  - **Regulatory forms and process descriptions** — death-notification registration fields, federal OPO rules, UNOS's oversight role, and descriptions of richer MIT and industry datasets. ([article source trail](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
  - **Interviews** — OPO leaders, industry association officials, trainers, regulators, lawyers, and experts explained where procurement can fail and what data is missing from oversight. ([story](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2023/04/28/a-crucial-link-in-the-nations-organ-donation-process-is-failing))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The decisive national finding—the number and share of low-tier OPOs—comes from public CMS data, and a fresh trigger-rule audit can be rebuilt from public/obtainable policies. The internal audit strengthens the mechanism for one OPO but is not necessary to recompute the national benchmark. CMS continues to publish performance reports and said it would publish 2026 reports, conduct recertification reviews, and pursue recertification or decertification after its new rule. ([2026 CMS status](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-strengthens-patient-protections-accountability-organ-donation-system))
- **Detection signature**: **benchmark-to-workflow gap audit** — CMS `possible-donor denominator → donations/transplants → tier` outcomes were compared across all 56 OPOs on `service area/year`; local clinical-trigger and family-approach rules were then compared on `referral condition/timing`, revealing both national underperformance and upstream procedural variance hidden by outcome-only review.
- **Corroboration structure**: CMS establishes common rankings; rule texts show process heterogeneity; the audit observes failure inside a low-tier OPO; leaders at a top-tier OPO show better performance is feasible; existing federal forms show regulators could inspect process. The confidential audit is a case study, not a random sample of all low-tier organizations.
- **Methodology notes**: No Show Your Work page or code repository exists. **[inferred]** method: rank the full CMS OPO roster, compare annual tiers, hand-code referral-rule predicates, and use the audit to trace a low score into workflow. Basis: the article's explicit source inventory. ### A Death Sentence: Native Americans Shut Out of the Nation's Liver Transplant System (2024) — Normalizing waitlist entry by liver-disease mortality located the racial disparity before the transplant list

- **URL**: [Main investigation](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2024/02/08/a-death-sentence-native-americans-shut-out-of-the-nations-liver-transplant-system); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/02/08/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-liver-transplants)
- **Partner/awards**: Explicitly coreported with **The Washington Post**; no story-specific award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - From 2018 through 2021, for every 100 people who died from chronic liver disease, approximately **68 Asian people, 26 White people, 23 Black people, and only nine Native American people** were accepted to the liver waitlist. White patients therefore entered at almost three times the Native rate relative to measured need. ([need-normalized finding](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/02/08/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-liver-transplants))
  - Had the Native acceptance ratio equaled the White ratio, the investigators estimated that **nearly 1,000 additional Native people** would have received liver transplants during the four-year period; an analogous equal-rate estimate yielded **430 additional Black people accepted to the waitlist**. ([counterfactual counts](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2024/02/08/a-death-sentence-native-americans-shut-out-of-the-nations-liver-transplant-system))
  - Once listed, racial groups advanced to transplant at roughly similar rates. The main disparity therefore arose **before or at referral/evaluation/listing**, not primarily in the final allocation among already-listed candidates. ([pipeline-stage finding](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2024/02/08/a-death-sentence-native-americans-shut-out-of-the-nations-liver-transplant-system))
  - IHS paid for inpatient substance-use treatment for an average of only **18 patients a year** and hepatology care for 226 a year from 2018–2022. It recorded nearly **113,000 deferred specialist requests**, including 6,196 gastroenterology requests; it helped fund six liver transplants while deferring 105 requests for all organ transplants. ([IHS access analysis](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/02/08/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-liver-transplants))
  - IHS contributed an average **$11,200 per funded transplant patient**, compared with a cited $650,000 average bill. The method warns that 39 percent of tribes manage their own care and need not report to the Purchased/Referred Care program, so these records understate total services and unmet need rather than measuring the entire tribal system. ([financing comparison and limitation](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/02/08/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-liver-transplants))
- **Finding type(s)**: **need-normalized access disparity** (new tag: group entry or receipt is divided by an independent measure of how many people need the resource, rather than by population alone); **pipeline-stage localization** (new tag: successive funnel ratios identify the stage where disparity first becomes large); **care-financing bottleneck** (new tag: nominal eligibility exists but specialty, travel, addiction-care, or payment approvals prevent reaching the formal allocation system); **administrative-undercoverage** (new tag: the responsible program's records omit a large delegated population, making measured scarcity a lower bound).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **CDC WONDER mortality denominator** — 2018–2021 deaths for chronic liver disease and cirrhosis (K70, K73–K74), population, state, and racial group. ([mortality source](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/02/08/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-liver-transplants))
  - **SRTR candidate and transplant microdata** — 57,076 adult liver candidates and 34,526 adult transplants, including race and residence, obtained in the earlier HRSA FOIA production. ([candidate/transplant sources](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/02/08/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-liver-transplants))
  - **IHS claims and unmet-needs records** — FOIA records for 58 procedure codes covering addiction treatment, gastroenterology/hepatology, and liver transplantation, plus 2016–2022 Purchased/Referred Care deferral reports. ([IHS sources](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/02/08/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-liver-transplants))
  - **Affected-community reporting** — Lee Yaiva and other Native sources, tribal officials, IHS leadership, addiction and transplant experts, and patient-access advocates connected the data to diagnosis, transport, information, substance-use treatment, and referral barriers. ([main story](https://themarkup.org/organ-failure/2024/02/08/a-death-sentence-native-americans-shut-out-of-the-nations-liver-transplant-system))
  - **Released package** — the updated [`investigation-organs`](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-organs) added racial-discrepancy and IHS unmet-needs notebooks and inputs to the earlier repository.
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The decisive race × need × pipeline analysis is rerunnable from the released repository, and fresh OPTN/SRTR request and CDC routes remain public. The repository releases IHS unmet-needs inputs; exact claims-level updates still require a new IHS records request. No API closure or site countermeasure has degraded the core path. - **Detection signature**: **need-to-entry ratio + pipeline-stage localization** — CDC `liver-disease deaths` were joined to SRTR `waitlist candidates` and `transplants` on `race/year (and state where supportable)`, revealing a 9-vs-26 Native/White acceptance ratio; `transplants ÷ waitlisted` was then compared across the same groups, revealing that the large disparity formed before listing. - **Corroboration structure**: Mortality supplies a need proxy; candidate records measure entry; transplant records measure progression; IHS claims/deferrals supply a possible institutional mechanism; interviews establish lived access barriers. The method openly treats mortality as an imperfect proxy because not everyone who dies is transplant-eligible and age structure differs by race.
- **Methodology notes**: The [Show Your Work page](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2024/02/08/how-we-investigated-racial-disparities-in-liver-transplants) states the race harmonization, ratios, counterfactual estimates, IHS code list, and limitations. The broader OPTN modernization program now promises improved public dashboards and equity monitoring, but that is not evidence of causal response to this story. ([HRSA modernization status](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn-modernization/learn-more-about-modernization))
- **Generalization**: Population shares can conceal unequal need. For benefits, inspections, disaster aid, health care, school support, or enforcement relief, divide entry by an independent need proxy; compare entry-to-decision and decision-to-delivery ratios; then inspect the first stage where disparity jumps and the local gatekeepers responsible.

### Is the Patient Black? Check This Box for Yes (2025) — A race coefficient made the same kidney function look healthier for Black patients and delayed waitlist credit

- **URL**: [Markup copy](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/10/14/is-the-patient-black-check-this-box-for-yes); [Word In Black partner copy](https://wordinblack.com/2025/10/is-the-patient-black-check-this-box-for-yes/)

([mechanism and policy sequence](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/10/14/is-the-patient-black-check-this-box-for-yes); [official policy](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/policies-bylaws/policy-issues/waiting-time-modifications-race-inclusive-egfr-calculations))

  - **Affected-patient interviews and correspondence** — detailed histories from Evans and Merritt, including dialysis timelines, hospital/OPTN letters, restored wait time, and transplant dates.   - **Official algorithm and remedy records** — OPTN's 2022 race-neutral rule, 2023 backdating policy, program review requirements, and ongoing monitoring. ([current official policy page](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/policies-bylaws/policy-issues/waiting-time-modifications-race-inclusive-egfr-calculations))
  - **Hospital cohort evidence** — Jefferson's 405 corrections and 122 subsequent transplants, supplied by its nephrology director. ([cohort account](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/10/14/is-the-patient-black-check-this-box-for-yes))
  - **Medical literature and expert history** — eGFR task-force recommendations, studies of race corrections across clinical tools, clinician interviews, and the history of creatinine/muscle-mass assumptions. ([article source trail](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/10/14/is-the-patient-black-check-this-box-for-yes))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(b) re-anchoring.** Patient records and interviews were needed to discover and narrate who lost time and how correction changed named lives. ([current patient FAQ](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/policies-bylaws/policy-issues/waiting-time-modifications-race-inclusive-egfr-calculations/faqs-for-patients); [August 2023 committee summary](https://www.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/hrsa/optn/20230815-pac-meeting-summary.pdf); [monitoring record](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/policies-bylaws/public-comment/monitor-ongoing-egfr-modification-policy-requirements))

  - The FAQ exposed a donor/recipient asymmetry: poor or uninsured people can donate organs without coverage, but people without insurance rarely reach the waitlist; cited out-of-pocket prices ranged from **$409,000 for a pancreas to $1.6 million for a heart**. ([insurance barrier](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/11/14/ten-answers-that-could-save-a-life-organ-donation-explained))
  - It also reported that only about 1 percent of deaths occur under conditions permitting donation and that roughly **28,000 donated organs are discarded annually**, with hospitals—not OPOs—making final acceptance decisions. ([donation and discard mechanics](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/11/14/ten-answers-that-could-save-a-life-organ-donation-explained))
- **Finding type(s)**: **registry-demographic blind spot** (new tag: a system tracks transactions or status but omits the demographic field needed to assess participation or disparity); **community-knowledge gap** (new tag: recurring questions, myths, and uncertainty obstruct participation in a public system); **donor-recipient eligibility asymmetry** (new tag: a person can supply a resource under far weaker financial or administrative conditions than are required to receive it); **service-journalism measurement bridge** (new tag: an explainer uses a bounded original instrument to replace an official missing denominator).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Constructed community survey** — online mixed-methods questionnaire, informed consent, attention checks, duplicate-IP and quality filters, publisher-network recruitment, and subgroup chi-square comparisons. CalMatters investigators consulted on the questionnaire. ([technical appendix](https://wordinblack.com/2026/01/technical-appendix-wib-organ-donation-report/))
  - **Official administrative and legal sources** — OPTN waitlist/allocation material, CMS OPO rules, Donate Life registration, the Uniform Anatomical Gift Act, EMTALA, National Organ Transplant Act, Medicare coverage, and oversight structures. ([article source trail](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/11/14/ten-answers-that-could-save-a-life-organ-donation-explained))
  - **Expert interviews** — transplant surgeon Joseph Keith Melancon, New England Donor Services CEO Alexandra Glazier, American Kidney Fund president LaVarne Burton, and other practitioners explained allocation, bias, discards, living donation, and oversight. ([article source trail](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/11/14/ten-answers-that-could-save-a-life-organ-donation-explained))
  - **Affected-person case** — Micah Clayborne's ten-month wait and heart transplant anchored the FAQ in a patient's timeline. ([case narrative](https://themarkup.org/on-borrowed-time/2025/11/14/ten-answers-that-could-save-a-life-organ-donation-explained))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The official FAQ/count/law detector uses public sources. The constructed survey also qualifies as class (a): Word In Black later released recruitment, filtering, analysis, limitations, and the full questionnaire, so an outside investigator can field a new wave through an equivalent publisher/community network. The respondent-level microdata were not released, so the exact 2025 percentages are auditable only at the published-method level, not independently recomputable. That incompleteness existed at release; no platform API closure or later countermeasure degraded the instrument.
- **Detection signature**: **missing-demographic survey substitution + FAQ source ladder** — the national donor registry's absent `race/ethnicity` field was compared with a constructed 1,588-person Black-reader survey on `registration status`, supplying a descriptive substitute; ten recurring questions were each joined to the highest-available `law / agency count / policy / expert / patient` source, revealing where public understanding, eligibility, and official data were incomplete.
- **Corroboration structure**: Statutes and agency data answer formal-process questions; experts interpret workflow; a recipient case tests comprehensibility against lived experience; the survey supplies community attitudes the registry cannot. The voluntary unweighted sample is not a population estimate, and expert explanation is not a substitute for outcome data.
- **Methodology notes**: No Markup Show Your Work page or GitHub repository exists. Word In Black's later [technical appendix](https://wordinblack.com/2026/01/technical-appendix-wib-organ-donation-report/) is the relevant methods release and links the full instrument. The survey **can be re-fielded from released artifacts**, but the reported percentages cannot be exactly recomputed without raw responses. The FAQ's statement that UNOS “manages” the OPTN and its July 2026 OPO endpoint are now partly stale: HRSA's 2026 multi-vendor structure leaves UNOS with matching/data while other functions moved, and CMS now points to January 2027 agreement expiration. ([current HRSA structure](https://www.hrsa.gov/optn-modernization/learn-more-about-modernization); [current CMS timeline](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-strengthens-patient-protections-accountability-organ-donation-system))
- **Official impact**: The nine-part Word In Black project won the inaugural NAACP Image Award journalism category. No government action is attributed to this FAQ itself. ([award announcement](https://wordinblack.com/2026/02/anissa-durham-on-borrowed-time-naacp-image-award/))
- **Generalization**: When an official registry lacks the demographic or experience variable needed for accountability, publish the missingness first; field a transparent bounded survey; distinguish participant description from population inference; and build every FAQ answer as a source ladder that can be date-checked as governance changes.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### P1. Scarce-resource flow audit

- **Mechanics**: Reconstruct each resource's origin, destination, time, and disposition before and after a rule change; build an origin-destination matrix; normalize delivery by local need, local contribution, and population; measure distance, delay, cost, and waste.
- **Minimum data**: Event ID, origin, destination, event/disposition date, delivered/discarded status, rule-change date, and at least one independent need denominator.
- **Recognition cues**: “Serve the highest priority regardless of location”; sudden growth in exports/imports; richer hubs gaining volume; travel distance or spoilage rising; rule advocates among destination winners.
- **Any-domain applications**: Disaster supplies, ambulances, school seats, public defenders, housing vouchers, water transfers, grid dispatch, grant allocation, procurement set-asides.

#### P2. Need-normalized funnel localization

- **Mechanics**: Choose a defensible need proxy; calculate `entered ÷ need`, `approved ÷ entered`, and `delivered ÷ approved` by group and geography; locate the first ratio where disparity widens. Add same-input counterfactuals when a formula contains a protected-class coefficient.
- **Minimum data**: Need denominator, successive funnel-stage records, group/geography, dates, and rule/formula definitions.
- **Recognition cues**: Equal outcomes only after admission; a high-burden group absent from the formal queue; eligibility rules using race, ZIP, language, insurance, or income; remedial corrections only for current participants.
- **Any-domain applications**: Benefits enrollment, school gifted programs, inspections, loan origination, parole, disability adjudication, clinical trials, disaster claims.

#### P3. Policy-pretext and beneficiary orchestration trace

- **Mechanics**: Resolve advocates, beneficiaries, plaintiffs, counsel, funders, lobbyists, committee members, and decision-makers; join money and communications to a dated policy/litigation sequence; compare the official precipitating event with earlier planning.
- **Minimum data**: Dockets, emails/minutes, committee rosters, organizational affiliations, payments or contributions, policy drafts/actions, and measured beneficiary outcomes.
- **Recognition cues**: The same counsel across “spontaneous” suits; beneficiaries recruiting plaintiffs; prewritten agency language; committee creation before the cited emergency; pilot precedent in an easier domain; compressed process replacing years of study.
- **Any-domain applications**: Zoning, rate cases, procurement rules, tax credits, licensing, environmental exceptions, platform regulation, sanctions.

#### P4. Contractor benchmark-to-workflow gap

- **Mechanics**: Rank every contractor or local unit with a common external denominator; obtain intake triggers and SOPs for top and bottom performers; compare who enters the measured process; trace whether regulators already collect but ignore event-level process data.
- **Minimum data**: Complete unit roster, external denominator, outcome counts, tier/contract status, local trigger rules, and preferably process timestamps.
- **Recognition cues**: Years of self-reporting; no contract termination despite outliers; “possible cases” defined locally; large tier shifts after denominator reform; detailed forms that never appear in public oversight.
- **Any-domain applications**: Child welfare, emergency response, laboratories, fraud review, health plans, employment programs, nonprofit grantees, private prison services.

#### P5. Patient-need-to-remedy coverage map

- **Mechanics**: Code lived needs by journey stage and geography; inventory public and nonprofit remedies; parse each remedy's eligibility, timing, cost, referral, and documentation; test whether the purported solution is usable before the harm occurs.
- **Minimum data**: Structured interviews or complaints, service directories, eligibility rules, geography, and application/outcome data where available.
- **Recognition cues**: Core eligibility depends on transport, insurance, documents, legal help, food, housing, caregiving, or mental health; remedies are post-event-only or intermediary-gated; directories lack outcome and freshness fields.
- **Any-domain applications**: Immigration, housing assistance, veterans' benefits, disaster recovery, disability, unemployment, legal aid, student aid.

### 5. What this platform can run now, and what is missing

**Runnable now with current holdings**

- **Policy-pretext and beneficiary orchestration trace (P3)** is the strongest fit. CourtListener/RECAP can supply dockets and unsealed exhibits; the 990 stack can connect nonprofits, boards, compensation, and payments; USASpending/FPDS can trace federal contracts; corporate registries, OpenCorporates, GLEIF, OpenSanctions, ICIJ, and OpenAleph can resolve affiliated entities and cross-border interests. The generic detector can align those networks to policy dates and beneficiary outcomes.
- **Contract-scope unbundling and partial benchmark audit (P4)** can run on USASpending/FPDS plus the 990 and entity stacks: decompose bundled awards, identify renewals despite adverse findings, measure contractor concentration, and compare governance roles with operating roles.
- **Cross-domain scarce-resource traces (P1)** can run where USASpending/FPDS or court records contain origin/destination or award/disposition data. Property/deed, FAA, patent, blockchain, and corporate tools can add asset movement or beneficiary-control layers, though none substitutes for health allocation events.
- **Impact/citation monitoring** can run through CourtListener/RECAP, federal procurement records, and official documents: trace an investigation, metric, contractor, or allegation into hearings, cases, solicitations, awards, and judgments.

**Missing for a faithful health-cluster rerun**

- Patient/candidate/donor/disposition microdata from SRTR/OPTN, facility crosswalks and coordinates, OPO death-notification/process records, and versioned allocation-policy inputs.
- CDC WONDER cause-of-death extracts and Census denominators ingested as first-class analytical tables; IHS claims and Purchased/Referred Care deferral records.
- A health-rule registry containing eGFR and other clinical calculators, their versions, protected-class coefficients, thresholds, and adoption dates.
- A reproducible FOIA/public-record acquisition layer for large health datasets and confidential-data handling; the existing listed holdings do not provide this.
- Structured patient-navigation interviews/complaints, a versioned service-directory crawler, eligibility parsing, and application-success outcomes.

The immediate platform opportunity is therefore documentary and organizational: use court, 990, contract, and entity holdings to detect **who shaped a health rule or contract and who benefited**. The quantitative allocation and care-funnel patterns require a new health-administrative data layer before this platform can reproduce The Markup's most distinctive detectors.
