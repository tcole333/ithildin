### Postmates Drivers Have Become Easy Prey for Scammers (2021) — App-mediated isolation let phishers impersonate management and steal entire weekly payouts

- **URL**: [Original investigation, Feb. 18, 2021](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/18/postmates-drivers-have-become-easy-prey-for-scammers-and-drivers-say-the-companys-not-helping); [impact follow-up, Feb. 25, 2021](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/25/postmates-workers-scammed-out-of-their-earnings-are-reimbursed-following-the-markups-report).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no copublisher or award is identified on the story or impact page.
- **What they found**:
  - A scammer placed a trivial order, called the assigned courier while impersonating Postmates, obtained account credentials, changed the payout card, and stole Benjamin Safer's entire **$346.73** weekly balance; Postmates did not reimburse him during nearly 11 months of requests before publication ([original](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/18/postmates-drivers-have-become-easy-prey-for-scammers-and-drivers-say-the-companys-not-helping)).
  - The Markup interviewed three targeted couriers—one targeted three times—and found hundreds of substantially similar complaints on Reddit, Twitter, YouTube, and Glassdoor. The common pattern was a small order used to open a phone channel, a fake deactivation or fraud warning, credential capture, and instant cash-out near the end of the weekly pay cycle ([original](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/18/postmates-drivers-have-become-easy-prey-for-scammers-and-drivers-say-the-companys-not-helping)).
  - Postmates declined to disclose its complaint or reimbursement counts. Couriers said they had not seen the company's buried support-page warning, and Safer said no automated alert reached him when the scammer changed his account and payout information ([original](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/18/postmates-drivers-have-become-easy-prey-for-scammers-and-drivers-say-the-companys-not-helping)).
  - One day after publication, Postmates credited Safer 100% of the stolen earnings, contacted the other named couriers, and had recently added two-factor authentication; the company described Safer's payment as a one-time courtesy ([impact follow-up](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/25/postmates-workers-scammed-out-of-their-earnings-are-reimbursed-following-the-markups-report)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **algorithmic-isolation exploit** (defined here: a platform removes ordinary human-management cues and a criminal weaponizes that vacuum to impersonate authority); **recourse asymmetry** (defined here: the platform can instantly move, freeze, or deny a worker's income while the worker cannot reach a responsible decision-maker).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Worker-held account records** — payout amount, support correspondence, threat messages, and screenshots supplied by named couriers; obtained directly from affected workers ([original](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/18/postmates-drivers-have-become-easy-prey-for-scammers-and-drivers-say-the-companys-not-helping)).
  - **Affected-worker interviews** — three Postmates couriers supplied independent descriptions of the same order-call-credential-cash-out sequence ([original](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/18/postmates-drivers-have-become-easy-prey-for-scammers-and-drivers-say-the-companys-not-helping)).
  - **Open complaint traces** — hundreds of posts across worker forums and social/video platforms established breadth but were not treated as a denominator or verified incident census ([original](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/18/postmates-drivers-have-become-easy-prey-for-scammers-and-drivers-say-the-companys-not-helping)).
  - **Company and police records** — Postmates' support page and statements, plus a Rancho Cucamonga Police warning, established what the company publicly told workers and that police had encountered the scheme ([original](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/18/postmates-drivers-have-become-easy-prey-for-scammers-and-drivers-say-the-companys-not-helping)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(c) leak-dependent.** The decisive proof—the actual balance loss, account changes, support history, and repeated script—came from workers' non-public account records and testimony. The nearest public substitute is a corpus of forum complaints plus police scam alerts. It preserves the recurring script but loses authenticated loss amounts, the affected population denominator, company-response timing, and the ability to distinguish genuine incidents from copied complaints.
- **Detection signature**: **payday-timed exploit triangulation** — worker payout and support records joined to the platform's weekly-transfer schedule on account and event date, then compared across three independently targeted couriers on scam-step sequence, revealed that the exploit was engineered around both algorithmic authority and predictable maximum account balances.
- **Corroboration structure**: Each named account supplied a private record trail; open complaints established repetition; a police warning confirmed that the operational script existed outside the interview cohort; the company's reimbursement immediately after publication verified the loss for the named workers without establishing the total incidence.
- **Official impact/response**: Postmates reimbursed the named couriers and added two-factor authentication, although the company did not publish a comprehensive remediation or victim count ([impact follow-up](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/02/25/postmates-workers-scammed-out-of-their-earnings-are-reimbursed-following-the-markups-report)).
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone page appears in [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/series/show-your-work), and no first-party repository was released. **[Inferred]** The detector is reconstructed from the article's explicit three-interview scope, linked complaint platforms, screenshots, support messages, and chronology. **Re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**—there is no released incident table, collection protocol, or source-record bundle.
- **Generalization**: Apply to payroll portals, creator marketplaces, benefits apps, and contractor platforms. A generic detector clusters complaints by social-engineering steps, aligns theft dates to payout cycles, and tests whether sensitive account changes trigger holds, out-of-band alerts, or human review.

### Shadow Bans, Dopamine Hits, and Viral Videos (2021) — Creator livelihoods changed abruptly when an opaque recommendation system appeared to shift

- **URL**: [Story, Apr. 22, 2021](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no copublisher or award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Mason McClellan reported reaching one million followers within days and earning about **$500 a week**, then losing roughly **200,000 followers** after Jan. 18, 2021 even though he said his output had not materially changed ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators)).
  - Multiple creators independently identified Jan. 18 as an inflection point and exchanged theories about suppression in creator forums. TikTok would not answer how often the recommendation system changed or whether creators were notified ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators)).
  - The story documented income and attention volatility, weak appeal channels, and creators' continual behavioral adaptation to an unseen ranking system; it did **not** prove that any named account was deliberately "shadow-banned," and an expert quoted in the story said proving that mechanism remained unresolved ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **opaque-livelihood volatility** (defined here: unexplained platform-distribution changes rapidly alter a dependent worker's income or prospects); **unverifiable moderation suspicion** (defined here: users observe an outcome consistent with suppression but cannot see the treatment, rule, or counterfactual needed to prove it).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Creator interviews and account histories** — named creators described follower, view, work, and income changes from their own accounts ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators)).
  - **Open creator-forum traces** — Reddit threads and other public discussion showed same-date complaints and the folk theories creators used to adapt ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators)).
  - **Platform policy statements** — TikTok's explanation of the For You system, community guidelines, and transparency-center announcements established its public rule layer; its spokesperson declined questions about the observed change ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators)).
  - **Domain experts** — researchers in digital labor, content moderation, and algorithmic transparency bounded what the observations could and could not establish ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(d) closed-platform-dependent.** Creator-side histories expose outcomes, but the treatment log, recommendation impressions, model version, and eligibility decisions remain inside TikTok. The nearest public substitute today is visible account/content history plus TikTok's gated [Research API](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/research-api/), available only to qualifying researchers and limited to public data. It loses historical impression allocation, internal moderation/ranking flags, model-version changes, and a credible no-change counterfactual.
- **Detection signature**: **creator-cohort inflection alignment** — multiple creator performance histories compared on calendar date, content continuity, and follower/view direction revealed a shared Jan. 18 inflection while platform policy statements supplied no matching disclosed rule change. This is a strong anomaly detector but not a causal shadow-ban test.
- **Corroboration structure**: Named longitudinal accounts supplied the core observations; same-date forum reports reduced the chance of a wholly idiosyncratic account event; platform documentation and expert caution prevented the anomaly from being mislabeled as proved intentional suppression.
- **Official impact/response**: No official policy change or enforcement action attributable to this story was documented.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/series/show-your-work) page and no GitHub release. **[Inferred]** The method is a qualitative cohort/change-point comparison based on interviews, visible accounts, and public complaint traces. **Re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**—no account-level time series, collection code, or reproducible sample frame was released.
- **Generalization**: The same pattern appears in seller search rank, freelancer lead allocation, app-store discovery, ad delivery, and automated scheduling. A stronger generic detector recruits a panel, captures daily impressions/referrals and content attributes, marks platform-change dates, and uses untreated or less-exposed accounts as controls.

### More Than 350 Gig Workers Carjacked, 28 Killed (2021–2022) — A cross-jurisdiction incident database exposed a safety crisis absent from company reports

- **URL**: [Initial 124-case investigation, July 22, 2021](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/07/22/uber-and-lyft-drivers-are-being-carjacked-at-alarming-rates); [361-case update, July 28, 2022](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/07/28/more-than-350-gig-workers-carjacked-28-killed-over-the-last-five-years); [data repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-gig-carjacking); [NDA follow-up](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/08/03/uber-requires-nondisclosure-agreement-before-helping-carjacked-driver).
- **Partner/awards**: Publishing partner **The Verge**; the attack and police-response reporting won 2022 SABEW Best in Business awards in Features and Travel/Transportation, with The Markup highlighting the series' nationwide database as the only such tracker ([award announcement](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2023/03/10/the-markup-wins-four-sabew-awards-for-business-journalism)).
- **What they found**:
  - The first census confirmed **124** carjackings or attempts in about 18 months; **75** (more than 60%) followed an app match, **11** workers died, and Chicago accounted for at least 44 known incidents ([2021 story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/07/22/uber-and-lyft-drivers-are-being-carjacked-at-alarming-rates)).
  - By July 2022 the database contained **361** victims since 2017: **28 killed**, 121 identified as Uber drivers, 101 as Lyft drivers, and at least **182 ride-hail cases (56%)** in which the app reportedly paired the driver with the would-be attacker ([2022 story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/07/28/more-than-350-gig-workers-carjacked-28-killed-over-the-last-five-years)).
  - A public motion in a federal wrongful-death suit disclosed that Uber had produced at least **24,000 alleged physical-assault or threatened-assault reports against drivers for 2017–2020** and more than 70,000 pages of reports under seal; Uber's public safety reports omitted nonfatal shootings, robberies, carjackings, and attempted assaults ([2022 story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/07/28/more-than-350-gig-workers-carjacked-28-killed-over-the-last-five-years)).
  - The database reached **430** carjackings or attempts and **31 deaths** by December 2022, still with a majority reportedly initiated after an app match ([police-response follow-up](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/12/09/when-drivers-are-attacked-uber-leaves-police-waiting-for-help)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **orphaned-harm census** (defined here: investigators build the missing denominator for a recurring harm that no responsible institution counts publicly); **platform-created exposure** (defined here: the matching system supplies offender access, timing, location, or victim selection that materially structures the risk); **official reporting aperture** (defined here: a safety report's inclusion rules systematically omit major harm categories).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Constructed incident database** — news and police reports were normalized by state, city, date, company, app-lure status, and fatality; every row includes a primary and optional additional source in the [released CSV and data dictionary](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-gig-carjacking).
  - **Police and charging records** — incident reports, probable-cause statements, and charging papers tied individual victims to ride requests, fake accounts, injuries, deaths, and app-mediated lures ([2021 documents and method description](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/07/22/uber-and-lyft-drivers-are-being-carjacked-at-alarming-rates); [2022 story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/07/28/more-than-350-gig-workers-carjacked-28-killed-over-the-last-five-years)).
  - **Affected-worker and family interviews** — supplied app screenshots, loss and injury details, assistance requests, and cases that local-news searches alone would not fully explain ([2021 story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/07/22/uber-and-lyft-drivers-are-being-carjacked-at-alarming-rates)).
  - **Litigation disclosures** — a public motion and Uber response exposed the 24,000-report internal universe while the underlying reports remained sealed ([2022 story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/07/28/more-than-350-gig-workers-carjacked-28-killed-over-the-last-five-years)).
  - **Company safety reports and statements** — furnished the comparison ledger and documented exclusions, disputes over particular rows, payment/identity policies, and claimed safety measures ([2022 story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/07/28/more-than-350-gig-workers-carjacked-28-killed-over-the-last-five-years)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** News archives, police reports, charging records, court motions, company safety reports, and interviews can be collected by an outside investigator; no platform API or internal leak is required for the public-case census. **Path today: runnable.** Local-record friction and underreporting remain, but police/court/news acquisition has not been shut down, and the released source-linked CSV remains public. The internal 24,000-report contents are not public, so the open detector can prove a floor and reporting gap, not a complete incidence rate.
- **Detection signature**: **jurisdiction-fan-out incident census + app-lure coding** — local news and police records joined on victim, city, and incident date, deduplicated into one national table, then compared to company safety-report inclusion rules and coded on whether the assailant used the app, revealed both the scale of harm and the platform's role in victim access.
- **Corroboration structure**: Each row required at least one attributable public source, stronger cases had police/charging records and interviews, company disputes were preserved in the company field, and sealed litigation supplied an independent internal scale check without being treated as row-level corroboration.
- **Official impact/response**: After The Markup contacted Uber about one victim, Uber offered his $1,000 deductible only if he signed a broad nondisclosure/nondisparagement agreement; he declined ([NDA follow-up](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/08/03/uber-requires-nondisclosure-agreement-before-helping-carjacked-driver)). Uber later said it would freeze obviously fake-name accounts and require ID when a new U.S. account used anonymous payment, while Lyft described a narrower ten-city rule; those changes are documented, but the stories do not establish exclusive causation ([2022 update](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/07/28/more-than-350-gig-workers-carjacked-28-killed-over-the-last-five-years)).
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/series/show-your-work) page. The [investigation-gig-carjacking repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-gig-carjacking) releases the BSD-3-Clause CSV and data dictionary, but no discovery scraper, deduplication code, verification log, or frozen source documents. **[Inferred]** Inclusion decisions were made by manually reconciling news, police, interviews, and company disputes. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: partially**—counts, company distribution, fatality totals, and app-lure shares can be recomputed from the CSV; discovery coverage and row verification cannot be regenerated from the repository alone.
- **Generalization**: Build missing incident ledgers for heat deaths of delivery workers, violence against home-health staff, contractor fatalities, eviction-service assaults, cyber-fraud victims, or product failures. Search many local jurisdictions, normalize event/person/entity identifiers, retain source-level provenance, code causal access mechanisms, and compare the resulting taxonomy with the institution's public reporting aperture.

### Amazon Is Rolling Back COVID Protocols in Its Warehouses (2021) — Public outbreak records showed the two longest active Oregon workplace outbreaks at Amazon while protections receded

- **URL**: [Story, Dec. 21, 2021](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature); [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-covid).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no copublisher or award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Oregon Health Authority reports placed Amazon's PDX7 and PDX9 at the top of the state's active workplace-outbreak duration ranking: **566 and 565 days** respectively, longer than the listed hospitals, prisons, and other warehouses ([story and ranking](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature)).
  - PDX7 had **233 cases** against an OSHA average annual workforce of about 1,600; PDX9 had **453 cases** against nearly 4,300 workers as of mid-December 2021 ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature)).
  - Amazon removed on-site testing, shortened paid breaks from 20 to 15 minutes, relaxed masking, and disabled distancing monitors while Oregon cases rose during fall 2021 ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature)).
  - Public records showed **382** COVID complaints about Amazon facilities, **33** in-depth inspections, and seven fines totaling **$63,120** through Oct. 31, 2021. Cal/OSHA separately cited the Rialto warehouse for failing to record **217** infections and imposed a $41,000 penalty that Amazon appealed ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **protection-outcome divergence** (defined here: an institution relaxes a safeguard while its own or official harm indicators remain elevated); **duration outlier** (defined here: event persistence, rather than raw count, makes an institution exceptional against peers).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Public-health time series** — weekly Oregon Health Authority outbreak PDFs, scraped and converted to facility-level longitudinal data ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature); [repository README](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-covid)).
  - **OSHA administrative records** — complaints and inspections obtained by public-records request, plus public closed-complaint and establishment-specific workforce data ([repository README](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-covid)).
  - **Worker-held notifications and interviews** — app messages established local case-notice frequency and dated the shortened-break and other policy changes ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature)).
  - **Legal and enforcement records** — attorney-general pleadings, settlement material, and Cal/OSHA citations independently documented alleged concealment and safety/recordkeeping failures ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature)).
  - **Company policy announcements and earnings statements** — supplied rollback dates and Amazon's stated rationale and spending claims ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive ranking and complaint/inspection counts came from public-health and OSHA records available to outsiders; worker messages corroborated policy implementation but were not required to reproduce the duration outlier. **Path today: degraded.** The exact historical analysis remains possible from the repository and [OHA's archived reports](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ERD/Pages/covid-19-news.aspx), but OHA says it will no longer revise those reports, and OSHA's dedicated [COVID complaint files](https://www.osha.gov/foia/archived-covid-19-data) end in 2022. A new equivalent nationwide workplace-COVID detector therefore lacks the same live inputs.
- **Detection signature**: **policy-harm event alignment + outbreak-duration ranking** — weekly facility outbreaks joined to OSHA workforce identifiers and compared to dated policy rollback events revealed that protections were removed while the two longest active workplace outbreaks in the state persisted.
- **Corroboration structure**: OHA supplied the outcome ledger; OSHA supplied establishment scale, complaints, inspections, and fines; worker messages independently fixed local policy dates; court and enforcement records tested Amazon's safety and disclosure claims.
- **Official impact/response**: California's $500,000 settlement requiring exact worker notifications and the cited Cal/OSHA enforcement were already underway before this story, so no additional official action is attributed to publication ([story chronology](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/12/21/amazon-is-rolling-back-covid-protocols-in-its-warehouses-workers-say-its-premature)).
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/series/show-your-work) page. The BSD-3-Clause [investigation-amazon-covid](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-covid) repository releases notebooks for downloading/converting OHA PDFs, extracting tables, ranking duration, and filtering OSHA records, plus requested spreadsheets and derived outputs. **[Inferred]** The article's rollback alignment was editorially assembled from worker/app and company dates. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: not fully**—the bundled outputs reproduce published rankings, but a clean end-to-end run requires external historical OSHA/OHA downloads and legacy PDF/Tika dependencies.
- **Generalization**: For any workplace hazard, rank duration and recurrence—not only incident totals—then align policy relaxations, staffing surges, or cost cuts. Suitable inputs include OSHA logs, infection reports, environmental exceedances, injury records, and dated internal policy notices.

### Data Provided by Amazon Workers Offers Rare Glimpse into COVID Cases (2022) — Daily employee alerts became a warehouse-level infection ledger

- **URL**: [Story, Feb. 10, 2022](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/02/10/data-provided-by-amazon-workers-offers-rare-glimpse-into-covid-cases-in-california-warehouses); [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-covid-notifications).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no copublisher or award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Screenshots sent by a Rialto worker showed **1,038 cases** at LGB7 from Nov. 12, 2021 through Jan. 29, 2022; OSHA listed an average annual workforce of about 4,400 ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/02/10/data-provided-by-amazon-workers-offers-rare-glimpse-into-covid-cases-in-california-warehouses)).
  - A second worker's notices showed **832 cases** at Stockton's SMF3 against an OSHA workforce of about 2,700. One notice reported 58 cases on Jan. 20 ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/02/10/data-provided-by-amazon-workers-offers-rare-glimpse-into-covid-cases-in-california-warehouses)).
  - At Rialto the daily average rose from about four cases in early December to **45 by mid-January**, with **61** newly confirmed cases in the Jan. 17 notice ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/02/10/data-provided-by-amazon-workers-offers-rare-glimpse-into-covid-cases-in-california-warehouses)).
  - California notices gave exact counts after a state settlement, while a same-day Minnesota notice merely said that employees had tested positive; the format comparison exposed how geography determined worker visibility into risk ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/02/10/data-provided-by-amazon-workers-offers-rare-glimpse-into-covid-cases-in-california-warehouses)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **worker-record truth serum** (defined here: routine records held by affected workers reveal an operational fact the institution does not aggregate publicly); **geographic transparency split** (defined here: the same employer discloses materially different risk information because one jurisdiction compels more detail).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Worker-supplied private notifications** — daily screenshots from LGB7 and SMF3 were manually converted into dated case counts; a Minnesota notice supplied the format control ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/02/10/data-provided-by-amazon-workers-offers-rare-glimpse-into-covid-cases-in-california-warehouses)).
  - **Released derived data** — one CSV per warehouse records notification date and total cases in that notice ([repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-amazon-covid-notifications)).
  - **OSHA establishment data** — average annual workforce counts gave facility-scale context but not a person-time infection rate ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/02/10/data-provided-by-amazon-workers-offers-rare-glimpse-into-covid-cases-in-california-warehouses)).

- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(c) leak-dependent.** The decisive facility-day records were visible only inside workers' A to Z accounts and were supplied privately. - **Official impact/response**: The $500,000 California judgment and exact-count requirement caused the data stream but predated the investigation; the story itself solicited additional worker screenshots, and no later official action is attributed to publication ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/02/10/data-provided-by-amazon-workers-offers-rare-glimpse-into-covid-cases-in-california-warehouses)).

### Secretive Algorithm Will Now Determine Uber Driver Pay (2022) — A fixed time-and-mileage rate card disappeared behind real-time offers

- **URL**: [Story, Mar. 1, 2022](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no copublisher or award identified.
- **What they found**:
  - Uber replaced a driver-pay rule based primarily on time and distance with "Upfront Fares," an offer calculated from several incompletely disclosed factors, while presenting pickup, destination, and pay before acceptance ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities)).
  - The feature had expanded to **24 U.S. cities** by publication. Uber said longer trips would pay less and shorter trips more under a balancing approach; interviewed drivers reported lower or less predictable earnings ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities)).
  - Driver Sam Vance supplied paired trip screenshots: on a **$30**, 20.9-mile fare he received about **$14** while Uber received about **$13**; on a **$22**, 8.8-mile airport trip he received **$6** while Uber received **$9**, with the remainder attributed to tax/fees ([story and screenshots](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities)).
  - The story explicitly did **not** find evidence that Uber personalized fares to each driver's minimum willingness to accept; it identified secrecy as the condition that prevented testing that hypothesis ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **rate-card disappearance** (defined here: a checkable compensation formula is replaced by individualized-looking offers without an auditable conversion rule); **price-accounting opacity** (defined here: worker, customer, platform, tax, and fee shares cannot be predicted or independently reconciled from a published schedule).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Worker-held paired receipts** — driver earnings, customer payment, mileage, and Uber share for two completed trips, supplied as screenshots ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities)).
  - **Driver interviews** — compared expected pay under the previous approximate dollar-per-mile regime with observed offers and described predictability loss ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities)).
  - **Company product pages and response** — confirmed the rollout, the "several factors" language, and the shorter/longer trip balancing claim ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities)).
  - **Trade/press reporting and experts** — The Rideshare Guy and Reuters supplied rollout geography; labor and algorithm experts framed falsifiable risks without asserting unproved personalization ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/03/01/secretive-algorithm-will-now-determine-uber-driver-pay-in-many-cities)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(d) closed-platform-dependent.** Actual offers, rejected-trip counterfactuals, worker histories, and rider/driver splits exist behind authenticated accounts. The nearest public substitute is Uber's current [pricing explanation](https://www.uber.com/us/en/marketplace/pricing/) and [Upfront Fares documentation](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/all-new-upfront-fares/), which now names more factor categories but still does not expose trip-level weights or all offers. A worker-volunteer panel could measure the system, but that remains closed-account collection, not open-record reproduction.
- **Detection signature**: **rate-card-to-black-box diff + paired-receipt decomposition** — the old time/distance rule compared to the new offer terms, then rider payment joined to driver receipt on trip ID, revealed both lost predictability and large trip-specific variation in the platform share.
- **Corroboration structure**: Product documents proved the rule change; two paired receipts proved concrete allocations; multiple drivers supplied consistent qualitative direction; the story appropriately stopped short of population estimates or claims about personalized offer suppression.
- **Official impact/response**: Uber expanded Upfront Fares to most U.S. markets later in 2022 and continues to use them; its 2025 documentation discloses several factor categories but says drivers cannot switch back to fixed time-and-distance rates ([current product page](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/all-new-upfront-fares/)). This is product evolution, not a documented remedial impact of the story.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/series/show-your-work) page and no GitHub release. **[Inferred]** The analysis is a rule diff plus two-trip accounting reconstruction, not a statistical audit. **Re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**—images permit hand checking of two examples, but there is no trip table, panel protocol, code, or sampling frame.
- **Generalization**: Audit any move from published fee schedules to real-time offers—freight brokerage, nursing shifts, ad bids, insurance repair rates, freelance jobs, or marketplace commissions. Capture every offer (accepted and rejected), link the same transaction's buyer and seller receipts, reconstruct all fees, and test whether observably similar jobs receive materially different offers.

### More Than 50 U.S. Gig Workers Murdered on the Job (2022) — An independently verified fatality census filled a company and government reporting void

- **URL**: [Story, Apr. 6, 2022](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/04/06/more-than-50-u-s-gig-workers-murdered-on-the-job-in-five-years); [data repository](https://github.com/the-markup/data-gig-worker-deaths).
- **Partner/awards**: Data originated with **Gig Workers Rising**, with PowerSwitch Action contributing to its report; The Markup independently verified and published the row-level data. No copublisher or specific award is identified on this page.
- **What they found**:
  - More than **50 U.S. gig workers** were identified as murdered while working from 2017 through early 2022, filling a gap left by companies and government occupational-fatality systems ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/04/06/more-than-50-u-s-gig-workers-murdered-on-the-job-in-five-years)).
  - The supplied spreadsheet attributed 22 deaths to Uber driving, four to Uber Eats, 17 to Lyft (which disputed three), eight to DoorDash, two to Instacart, one to Grubhub, and one to Postmates; The Markup preserved disputes and independently checked the incidents ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/04/06/more-than-50-u-s-gig-workers-murdered-on-the-job-in-five-years)).
  - **Twenty-eight** of the reported homicides occurred in 2021 and seven in the first two months of 2022; causes included carjackings, robberies, road rage, and drive-by shootings ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/04/06/more-than-50-u-s-gig-workers-murdered-on-the-job-in-five-years)).
  - The tally was explicitly a floor: app status can be disputed, some workers use another person's account, and no official system consistently identifies a decedent as an active platform worker ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/04/06/more-than-50-u-s-gig-workers-murdered-on-the-job-in-five-years)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **employment-status blind spot** (defined here: official death or injury systems cannot reliably identify work mediated through an app); **distributed fatality reconstruction** (defined here: a death census is assembled from traces scattered across police, courts, local news, families, and fundraisers).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Advocacy-constructed census** — Gig Workers Rising searched news, police documents, legal filings, family accounts, GoFundMe, Google, Reddit, LexisNexis, and The Markup's carjacking database ([repository README](https://github.com/the-markup/data-gig-worker-deaths)).
  - **Independent row verification** — The Markup checked the supplied incidents and reported company disputes rather than silently deleting contested rows ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/04/06/more-than-50-u-s-gig-workers-murdered-on-the-job-in-five-years)).
  - **Police and court records** — probable-cause affidavits, charging documents, and wrongful-death suits established identity, circumstances, and alleged platform connection for stronger cases ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/04/06/more-than-50-u-s-gig-workers-murdered-on-the-job-in-five-years)).
  - **Family and fundraiser records** — interviews and GoFundMe pages supplied identity and work context when institutional records were incomplete ([repository README](https://github.com/the-markup/data-gig-worker-deaths)).
  - **Company responses** — verified, denied, or qualified platform status for named workers and exposed the absence of a public company census ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/04/06/more-than-50-u-s-gig-workers-murdered-on-the-job-in-five-years)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The census uses public news, police/court records, fundraisers, and family sourcing; no confidential platform dataset is necessary to reproduce a defensible lower bound. **Path today: runnable.** Search and public-record acquisition remain available, although precise on-app status often cannot be resolved without company cooperation. The released data is a starting corpus, not a maintained current feed.
- **Detection signature**: **fatality census from orphaned traces + adversarial row verification** — names found across local news, police, courts, fundraisers, and a related incident database joined on victim/date/location, then checked against company disputes on platform status, revealed a national minimum that no official ledger supplied.
- **Corroboration structure**: The advocacy list served as leads, not final proof; The Markup rechecked each row; police/court records outranked family and fundraiser descriptions where available; company denials were retained as uncertainty labels.
- **Official impact/response**: In June 2022, five U.S. senators requested five years of worker-safety data from Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, and Grubhub; later responses still did not produce a comparable public all-harm census ([documented in the carjacking update](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2022/07/28/more-than-350-gig-workers-carjacked-28-killed-over-the-last-five-years)). The cited page does not claim the letters were caused solely by this story.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/series/show-your-work) page. The BSD-3-Clause [data-gig-worker-deaths](https://github.com/the-markup/data-gig-worker-deaths) repository releases the CSV, data dictionary, and up to four source URLs per row, but no search, deduplication, or verification code. **[Inferred]** The decisive step was manual entity/event resolution. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: partially**—totals and company/year distributions are reproducible; independent source validation and missing-case discovery are not.
- **Generalization**: Reconstruct deaths or severe harms among populations that disappear between classification systems: day laborers, temporary health staff, informal waste workers, overseas contractors, tenants displaced by shell landlords, or victims of unregulated products. Use public traces as leads, require row-level provenance, and publish disputed-status fields rather than forcing false certainty.

- **Methodology notes**: No standalone [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/series/show-your-work) page. The [carjacking repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-gig-carjacking) covers incident context but does not release subpoenas, response timestamps, rate calculations, or analysis code. **[Inferred]** The decisive method was manual event-sequence reconstruction across police, court, company, and interview records. **Re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**—the story links many documents, but there is no joined timeline table or complete request corpus.
- **Generalization**: Audit any private intermediary that holds evidence needed by public responders—telecom providers, banks, cloud hosts, payment processors, building-access vendors, hospitals, or social platforms. Lyft said 85% of California drivers had received at least one wage top-up, without supplying the driver denominator ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2024/09/04/california-companies-wrote-their-own-gig-worker-law-now-no-one-is-enforcing-it)).
- **Finding type(s)**: **statutory enforcement vacuum** (defined here: a law creates benefits but no agency accepts operational responsibility for individual compliance); **claim-aging accountability gap** (defined here: unresolved administrative claims accumulate while each responsible actor points elsewhere); **benefit-without-ledger** (defined here: an institution claims aggregate compliance but withholds the recipient and expenditure counts needed to test it).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **California Public Records Act claim corpus** — wage claims and agency emails obtained from the Department of Industrial Relations, coded by company, amount, benefit type, date, and resolution status ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2024/09/04/california-companies-wrote-their-own-gig-worker-law-now-no-one-is-enforcing-it)).
  - **Statutory and court records** — Proposition 22, the California Supreme Court ruling, and pre-Prop. 22 wage suits established the promised benefits and jurisdictional boundary ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2024/09/04/california-companies-wrote-their-own-gig-worker-law-now-no-one-is-enforcing-it)).
  - **Agency jurisdiction statements** — claim emails and on-record responses from DIR and the attorney general showed that neither would adjudicate individual Prop. 22 benefit claims ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2024/09/04/california-companies-wrote-their-own-gig-worker-law-now-no-one-is-enforcing-it)).
  - **Worker interviews and claim narratives** — documented unpaid adjustments, missing health stipends, occupational-accident insurance disputes, deactivation before final pay, and support delays ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2024/09/04/california-companies-wrote-their-own-gig-worker-law-now-no-one-is-enforcing-it)).
  - **Structured company questionnaire and public filings** — asked the same four questions about spending, recipient counts, consumer fees, and complaint handling; missing answers became a documented accounting gap ([story](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2024/09/04/california-companies-wrote-their-own-gig-worker-law-now-no-one-is-enforcing-it)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive evidence is the public statute, court decisions, wage-claim corpus, agency correspondence, and company disclosures/non-disclosures. **Path today: runnable.** California DIR continues to accept public-record requests online, by email, mail, phone, fax, or in person ([current Labor Commissioner's records page](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/DLSE-PRARs.htm)). Exemptions and incomplete production can still hide claims—as the unexplained DoorDash absence demonstrates—but the detector itself remains available.
- **Detection signature**: **statutory promise–claim outcome–jurisdiction join** — each promised Prop. 22 benefit compared to claimant allegation and resolution status, then joined to agency statements about legal authority and aged from filing date, revealed that benefits existed on paper while no state actor accepted individual enforcement.
- **Corroboration structure**: Claim documents established concrete disputes; the statutory text defined what was promised; DIR and attorney-general statements established the jurisdiction gap; company responses provided claimed delivery without the detailed ledger needed to reconcile it.
- **Official impact/response**: In October 2024 the U.S. Supreme Court declined Uber and Lyft's appeal, allowing California and city **pre-Prop. 22** wage-theft suits covering tens of thousands of drivers to proceed; the follow-up explicitly says this did not cure the post-Prop. 22 enforcement vacuum ([follow-up](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2024/10/16/california-uber-and-lyft-drivers-could-get-back-pay)).
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/series/show-your-work) page and no GitHub release. **[Inferred]** The method is described in the article: request all claims involving gig companies since December 2020, isolate 54 Prop. 22 claims, code resolution/amount/company, and compare agency jurisdiction and company benefit-accounting responses. The extraction rules and row-level dataset were not released. **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**—selected claims are linked, but the complete production and coding table are absent.
- **Generalization**: Apply to industry-written safe harbors, settlement-monitoring programs, environmental offsets, consumer rebates, grant conditions, privacy rights, and insurance mandates. Turn statutory promises into a field checklist; acquire every complaint/claim; compute age and disposition; and map which agency claims authority over each promised remedy.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### P1. Orphaned Incident Census

**Mechanics.** Fan out across local news, police files, charging papers, litigation, fundraisers, and family accounts; resolve duplicate events on person/date/location; code the mechanism that connected institution and victim; publish disputes and source provenance; compare the census with the institution's reporting aperture.

**Minimum data.** One attributable source per incident, event date/location, affected person or stable event identifier, institutional relationship, outcome severity, and inclusion-confidence/dispute fields.

**Recognition cues.** Officials say "we do not track that"; company reports cover only a narrow set of outcomes; local articles repeatedly use the same occupational/product descriptor; advocates maintain informal spreadsheets; families use fundraisers to fill institutional support gaps.

#### P2. Worker-Held Shadow Ledger

**Mechanics.** Recruit workers to contribute routine notifications, earnings screens, deactivation messages, schedules, or buyer/seller receipts; version templates; transcribe to event rows; deduplicate; join to public establishment identifiers, rate rules, headcounts, or regulatory thresholds. The notification reconstruction and paired-receipt pay audit are two forms of the same detector.

**Minimum data.** Timestamp, worker/facility/account pseudonymous key, unaltered source image or export, event amount/count, platform template/version, and an independent rule or denominator.

**Recognition cues.** The platform says it "notifies affected users" but publishes no aggregate; workers compare screenshots to understand pay; geographically different templates exist; a fixed fee schedule disappears; support messages are the only audit trail.

#### P3. Request-to-Response Harm Timeline

**Mechanics.** Obtain the requester's submission, the recipient's acknowledgment and production, the returned fields, and the case outcome; calculate latency and completeness; join repeated events on suspect account, device, location, or other hard identifier; compare across recipients and request types.

**Minimum data.** Request/case ID, submit/acknowledge/produce timestamps, legal-process type, fields requested/returned, severity/emergency marker, and downstream event chronology.

**Recognition cues.** Agencies describe a backlog; request portals have no public service level; officials avoid submitting because the process is cumbersome; repeat harms share a platform identifier; company transparency reports show materially different compliance rates.

#### P4. Statutory Promise-to-Enforcement Graph

**Mechanics.** Convert every statutory or contractual promise into a testable field; acquire all claims and their dispositions; age unresolved cases; map each remedy to the agency that accepts or rejects jurisdiction; compare company aggregate claims with recipient/payment ledgers.

**Minimum data.** Rule text, effective date, obligated entity, promised benefit, claimant/company, filing date, amount, status, disposition reason, and agency jurisdiction statement.

**Recognition cues.** Industry wrote the rule; regulated people are classified outside ordinary labor/consumer protections; one agency refers complainants to another; promised benefits have no public recipient count; claims remain pending across years.

#### P5. Hidden Vendor in Adverse-Event Narratives

**Mechanics.** Search full-text inspection, complaint, court, and incident narratives for intermediary/vendor names and aliases; extract the event and facility; join to contracts, licenses, corporate ownership, and vendor claims about its legal role; test whether operational control and legal responsibility diverge.

**Minimum data.** Narrative corpus, vendor alias list, facility/event IDs, deficiency or harm code, licensing/contract rule, and corporate identity resolution.

**Recognition cues.** The regulated facility blames "agency staff"; the vendor calls itself a marketplace; the same intermediary appears across unrelated facilities; training or credential verification is assumed to have happened elsewhere.

### 5. What this platform can run now, and what is missing

**Runnable or partially runnable with current holdings**

- **Court-led incident and accountability seeds.** CourtListener/RECAP can identify wrongful-death, wage-theft, misclassification, subpoena-compliance, and platform-safety cases; government-release corpora can add charged incident clusters. This can seed P1 and P3, but it will systematically miss incidents that never reach federal/public litigation.
- **Corporate/vendor resolution.** The registry stack (OpenCorporates, Companies House and other registries, GLEIF, OpenSanctions, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, and local OpenAleph) can resolve staffing apps, platform subsidiaries, insurers, contractors, directors, and shared addresses. This directly supports P5 and the obligated-entity layer of P4.
- **Government-money and institutional-customer joins.** USASpending/FPDS can identify public agencies purchasing staffing, safety, cloud, or platform services; the 990 stack can map worker-advocacy organizations and funders. Those joins can prioritize which vendors and jurisdictions to audit.
- **Litigation-to-sanction or litigation-to-contract follow-up.** Existing court, registry, procurement, property, patent, and network holdings can test whether a platform or staffing vendor facing repeated allegations continues to receive contracts, reorganizes through affiliates, patents the disputed mechanism, or shifts assets.

**Missing for full-strength detectors**

- A maintained **local-news/police incident ingestion and entity-resolution layer**, including public-record request tracking, source snapshots, duplicate-event review, and dispute fields. Without it, P1 is a court-biased lower bound.
- A **worker-record intake instrument** with consent, secure upload, redaction, template versioning, OCR, pseudonymous longitudinal keys, and paired rider/worker or buyer/seller transaction capture. Without it, P2 and both closed-platform studies remain unavailable.
- A normalized **subpoena/request-response ledger** spanning agencies and private recipients. CourtListener may expose litigation about delay, but it does not capture the government's ordinary request, acknowledgment, and production timestamps needed for P3.
- Direct ingestion of **state labor claims and jurisdiction correspondence**, plus a rule-to-benefit schema. The California public-record route works, but the platform does not currently hold a reusable national administrative-claim corpus for P4.
- A **CMS nursing-home inspection/complaint narrative corpus** with historical snapshots and vendor-name extraction. The current platform holdings named in the brief do not include Care Compare deficiency text, so P5 cannot yet run at national depth.
- Closed-platform observation capacity: authenticated volunteer panels, accepted/rejected offer capture, recommendation impression histories, and model/policy version logs. Public corporate, court, procurement, and registry holdings cannot substitute for these missing treatment and outcome records.

**Bottom line.** The platform can immediately perform strong entity, litigation, procurement, and corporate follow-through once a worker harm or vendor is known. Its main gap is earlier in the funnel: acquiring and maintaining the worker-held and jurisdiction-fragmented event ledgers that made this Markup cluster distinctive.
