# The Markup evidence ontology: digital access and public-service infrastructure

## Scope and attribution

This review covers 11 self-canonical, dated pages on The Markup domain from the [Coronavirus series](https://themarkup.org/series/coronavirus) and [Still Loading series](https://themarkup.org/series/still-loading). It follows the census's domain-structural attribution rule: a Markup-domain copy is in corpus even when it is co-published or appeared after the 2024 CalMatters merger; a separate collaborator, translation, or syndication copy is not counted as another story. Method and tool pages are included only when they expose a distinctive detector used by the reporting.

“Re-runnable from released artifacts” below means an outside investigator can reproduce the decisive historical comparison, not merely read the published result. “Runnable today” is stricter: the acquisition or testing instrument can still be applied to a current analogue. “Degraded today” means that the historical program/source has shut down, or that a fresh collection requires missing collectors, rediscovered private endpoints, or now-retired emergency data.

### Can You Get a Coronavirus Test? It May Depend on Where You Live (2020) — identical patient scenarios qualified for state-lab testing in some jurisdictions and not others

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/16/can-you-get-a-coronavirus-test); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/04/16/how-we-analyzed-states-coronavirus-testing-plans); [released data](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-covid19-testing-guidelines).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no reporting partner or story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - The Markup sent the same records request to all 50 states, New York City, and Washington, D.C. In the first published snapshot it had responses from 16 states, public guidance from four more jurisdictions, and denials or emergency postponements from four states and D.C. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/16/can-you-get-a-coronavirus-test).
  - A symptomatic health-care worker with fever but no lower-respiratory symptoms would not have met Wisconsin's state-lab priority rule, while Massachusetts put health-care workers with fever or even mild respiratory symptoms in its highest priority category. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/16/can-you-get-a-coronavirus-test).
  - Idaho's guidance could admit a worker exposed to a confirmed colleague, while Nevada's could admit a patient with an underlying respiratory condition; other states treated homelessness, incarceration, age, and non-hospitalized illness differently. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/16/can-you-get-a-coronavirus-test).
- **Finding type(s)**: `rule-fragmentation` — materially different eligibility rules for the same public service across jurisdictions; `snapshot-policy inequality` — unequal outcomes visible only after freezing fast-changing guidance at a common time.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Open-record policy documents** — identical public-records requests to health departments; full memos were linked through DocumentCloud and indexed in the method page. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/04/16/how-we-analyzed-states-coronavirus-testing-plans).
  - **Public agency guidance** — already-public jurisdictional rules were added to the requested records and marked with document dates. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/04/16/how-we-analyzed-states-coronavirus-testing-plans).
  - **Constructed case instrument** — five matched hypothetical patients (health-care worker, unhoused person, sick but not hospitalized person, 65-year-old with fever, incarcerated person) were evaluated against each rule set. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/16/can-you-get-a-coronavirus-test).
  - **Released structured data** — the repository records state, document link/date, priority group, additional criteria, fever, respiratory requirements, influenza-test requirements, and notes. [GitHub](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-covid19-testing-guidelines).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today.** The historical evidence is public and downloadable today from the released dataset and linked documents. An investigator can still file identical state records requests and compare current health rules, but the decisive COVID-19 scarcity protocols were an emergency snapshot and are no longer a live program in many jurisdictions. The exact historical acquisition cannot be freshly replayed; the released snapshot is now the reusable input.
- **Detection signature**: `matched-hypothetical rule diff` — state testing memos **compared to** five identical patient scenarios **on keys** symptoms, occupation, age, exposure, and congregate setting **revealed** geography-dependent eligibility.
- **Corroboration structure**: Direct text from dated agency memos established the rules; a real Massachusetts household illustrated the consequences; cross-state repetition showed the variation was systemic rather than a single bad document. The story explicitly limited the conclusion to government-operated testing and a dated snapshot. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/16/can-you-get-a-coronavirus-test).
- **Methodology notes**: The method page says the requests were “identical” and explains the categorization of recurring priorities. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/04/16/how-we-analyzed-states-coronavirus-testing-plans). The [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-covid19-testing-guidelines) releases the categorized inputs and document links, but no analysis notebook. **Historical decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes**, because the scenario-to-rule comparison can be reconstructed from the released fields; the original records-request campaign cannot.
- **Impact**: No story-attributable official change located. The reporting did document that some governments had suspended or indefinitely postponed public-record access during the emergency. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/16/can-you-get-a-coronavirus-test).
- **Generalization**: Apply the same detector to Medicaid eligibility, disaster aid, voting rules, professional licensing, school discipline, or parole. Build matched applicant profiles, normalize each jurisdiction's required predicates, and flag outcome changes caused only by location.

### Blind Users Struggle with State Coronavirus Websites (2020) — most state emergency sites contained machine-detectable barriers, while manual screen-reader use exposed additional failures

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites); current [WAVE tool](https://wave.webaim.org/).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original. WebAIM conducted the WAVE survey for The Markup and supplied accessibility expertise; it was a testing collaborator, not a co-publisher. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
- **What they found**:
  - Of 50 state COVID-19 homepages, 41 contained low-contrast text, 31 contained empty links or buttons, 15 lacked input labels, and 12 lacked alternative text. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
  - WAVE flagged an average 28.5 errors per homepage. The tool catches fewer than 40% of possible accessibility issues, so the automated count was explicitly a lower-bound screen rather than a complete compliance judgment. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
  - Maine and New Mexico were the only pages with zero WAVE errors, while manual NVDA use found a Washington infographic and chatbot that the automated scan missed. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
- **Finding type(s)**: `digital-accessibility failure` — public information is technically present but unusable through assistive technology; `automation-blind-spot` — a standards scanner materially understates lived barriers.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Constructed target census** — one page per state, selected from the Google information module returned for state-name-plus-“covid” searches. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
  - **Automated accessibility tests** — WebAIM ran WAVE on all 50 homepages on April 15, 2020 and classified common error types. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
  - **Human usability tests and interviews** — two accessibility experts and two screen-reader users evaluated failures WAVE could not see, including image semantics and chatbot operation. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
  - **Agency responses and before/after retest** — states described their standards and remediation; Iowa's page was rescanned after an update. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** State sites are public and WAVE remains available as a web tool, browser extension, API, and stand-alone testing engine in 2026. [WAVE](https://wave.webaim.org/). The 2020 COVID pages and Google's then-current module have changed or disappeared, so the historical page census cannot be reacquired exactly, but the instrument path works on today's emergency, benefits, election, and agency sites.
- **Detection signature**: `automated-standard audit with human override` — a 50-state public-site census **tested against** WAVE/WCAG error rules and **compared to** manual screen-reader behavior **on key** page controls and content objects **revealed** both widespread machine-detectable faults and high-impact false negatives.
- **Corroboration structure**: Automated counts established breadth; manual users supplied functional validation; two zero-error states acted as positive controls; agency responses and Iowa's retest demonstrated that at least some errors were remediable rather than intrinsic to emergency publishing. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
- **Methodology notes**: No story-specific Show Your Work page or GitHub repository was located. **[inferred]** The reproducible method is stated in the article: select one state URL through a common discovery rule, run WAVE on the homepage, aggregate error presence/counts, then manually test a purposive sample. The present [WAVE service](https://wave.webaim.org/) confirms both automation and human evaluation remain supported. **Historical decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**; The Markup did not release the 50-row scan results, target snapshot, or manual-test protocol.
- **Impact**: Iowa updated one COVID site after The Markup's email and cut its WAVE-detectable errors in half; Washington said it would update its site that week. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites).
- **Generalization**: Audit any high-stakes public transaction—benefit applications, court e-filing, utility shutoff notices, school enrollment—with automated conformance tests followed by task-based use by people relying on assistive technology.

### How Unemployment Systems Are Failing Workers Around the Nation (2020) — state benefit systems stacked incompatible technical, timing, and identity barriers in front of applicants

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no reporting partner or story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - An Economic Policy Institute survey estimated that for every 10 people trying to claim unemployment, three to four could not get through and two more found the process too complicated to try. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
  - New York required some applicants to complete claims by phone; Florida and Tennessee took filing systems offline to process payments; Illinois assigned filing days by surname; and New Jersey assigned windows by Social Security number and hour. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
  - Nevada's password reset initially required staff action, Pennsylvania mailed PINs that could take more than a month, and Oklahoma reported six-to-eight-hour call waits before a staffing surge. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
- **Finding type(s)**: `benefit-friction stack` — several individually survivable requirements combine into practical exclusion; `capacity-gated entitlement` — legal eligibility does not produce access when the transaction channel cannot admit the claimant.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Constructed public-workflow audit** — reporters catalogued required steps, downtime notices, gating calendars, reset procedures, and call requirements across state systems. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
  - **User-contributed transaction traces** — applicants supplied screenshots and video of crashes, offline notices, and repeated attempts, including a Florida claimant's screen recording. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
  - **Interviews and agency statements** — applicants described end-to-end failure; agencies confirmed capacity constraints and announced changes. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
  - **External survey and reported statistics** — EPI's access survey and agency call-wait figures supplied a scale check on the nonrepresentative case collection. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The decisive inputs were public transaction paths, public notices, voluntary user submissions, and agency statements rather than a leak or authenticated platform dataset. State unemployment portals still permit a present-day transaction-path audit, although the pandemic load spike and many specific throttles are historical. The method should use test accounts only when lawful and authorized; observation of public pre-login steps, notices, and voluntary claimant traces is sufficient for much of the detector.
- **Detection signature**: `transaction-path friction census` — state claim workflows **compared to** a common end-to-end claimant task **on keys** identity, timing, channel switching, reset, downtime, and human intervention **revealed** where accumulated steps converted nominal eligibility into exclusion.
- **Corroboration structure**: Applicant artifacts proved individual failures; the cross-state inventory established recurring barrier classes; agency admissions explained capacity and design causes; the EPI estimate bounded likely prevalence. The story did not claim its cases were a representative sample. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
- **Methodology notes**: No story-specific Show Your Work page or GitHub repository was located. **[inferred]** The article exposes a qualitative “mystery claimant” protocol: follow or document the public application journey, code each stopping point, request agency explanation, and organize failures by mechanism. **Historical decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**; the underlying URL list, screenshots, videos, and coding sheet were not released.
- **Impact**: The story records contemporaneous fixes—including New York callbacks, Nevada's self-service password reset, and Oklahoma staffing—but does not establish that The Markup caused them. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/05/07/how-unemployment-systems-are-failing-workers-around-the-nation).
- **Generalization**: Treat public services as transactions rather than pages. Enumerate required channel changes and time gates, then test whether one failure forces a loop, reset, mailed credential, or unavailable human intervention.

### Need Help with an Unemployment Claim in Florida? Good Luck (2020) — one contracted call center answered only one-quarter of calls during a three-week period

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck); [released data repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-unemployment-wait-times).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no reporting partner or story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - From May 18 through June 8, Titan Technologies answered 696,795 calls (25%), callers abandoned 128,763 (5%), and 1,951,301 (70%) were blocked with a busy signal. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck).
  - On April 6, callers seeking PIN resets waited an average of almost 3.5 hours; the 40% who abandoned waited nearly two hours on average, and the longest recorded wait was four seconds short of 12 hours. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck).
  - Florida expected Titan to receive nearly $80 million, yet the state supplied no comparable data from its other call-center contractors, making the released operational view consequential but incomplete. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck).
- **Finding type(s)**: `service-capacity collapse` — demand is rejected before it can enter a public-service queue; `contract-performance opacity` — a government outsources service delivery without a complete, comparable public performance ledger.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Administrative operations data** — Florida Department of Economic Opportunity records obtained through a public-records request, generated by contractor Titan Technologies for April through early June. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck).
  - **Public contract and payment records** — state financial records supplied Titan's expected compensation and defined a “blocked” call. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck).
  - **Interviews and official responses** — legal-aid attorneys explained why claim errors required scarce human correction; DEO and Titan supplied staffing and improvement claims. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck).
  - **Released source tables** — The Markup published the records in its [unemployment wait-times repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-unemployment-wait-times).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The historical inputs are public records and a public contract, and the released files remain a direct substitute for a fresh 2020 request. A current version could request call-detail aggregates, queue outcomes, staffing, and vendor invoices from FloridaCommerce and its present vendors. Agency names, contractors, and fields may have changed, but the records path itself remains available.
- **Detection signature**: `service-funnel reconstruction` — daily call-center records **joined to** contractor terms and staffing **on keys** date, queue, answered, abandoned, blocked, wait, and agent count **revealed** that most demand never reached service despite substantial outsourcing spend.
- **Corroboration structure**: Machine-generated operational counts supplied scale; the contract fixed terminology and financial stakes; attorney and claimant experience supplied consequence; vendor/state responses supplied rival explanations and later-period performance. The story foregrounded missing contractors and changing schemas as limitations. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck).
- **Methodology notes**: No separate Show Your Work page was located. **[inferred]** The article and [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-unemployment-wait-times) show a records-to-funnel calculation: normalize daily vendor categories, compute outcome shares, inspect time extrema, and compare with contract definitions and staffing. **Historical decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: substantially yes**; the source data permit the published aggregates, although no story-specific analysis notebook was located and the state withheld other vendors' data.
- **Impact**: No story-attributable official change located. Titan said its June 16 average wait had fallen to 31 minutes and Florida cited 6,000 new customer-service representatives, changes already underway when the story appeared. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/23/need-help-with-an-unemployment-claim-in-florida-good-luck).
- **Generalization**: Request the raw funnel for any outsourced service—311, Medicaid, tax help, prison calls, utility assistance—and calculate accepted, queued, abandoned, rejected, resolved, and repeat-contact rates against invoices and staffing.

### Millions of Americans Depend on Libraries for Internet. Now They're Closed (2020) — shutdowns removed the free connectivity layer on which underconnected communities relied

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no reporting partner or story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - Cherokee Public Library's director estimated that about 40% of local school-age children lacked home internet; after closure, children used the library's 24-hour Wi-Fi from the parking lot. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
  - Pew data showed fewer than two-thirds of rural Americans had home broadband; among households earning under $30,000, four in 10 lacked a computer and three in 10 lacked a smartphone. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
  - A New York City Bar survey found internet in only 6% of city homeless shelters while more than 20,000 school-age children lived in shelters on the first day of remote learning. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
  - A majority of libraries in a Public Library Association survey had boosted Wi-Fi or left it on around the clock; Seattle had 1,000 lendable hotspots and raised its allocation for people in need from 250 to 325 devices. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
- **Finding type(s)**: `substitute-infrastructure dependency` — a public facility quietly compensates for missing household infrastructure; `closure-amplified exclusion` — closing that facility converts an existing access gap into loss of school, benefits, work, and information.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **National and local surveys** — Pew connectivity/device surveys, the New York City Bar shelter survey, and Public Library Association response data. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
  - **Distributed service-provider interviews** — librarians in small rural systems and large cities described demand, workarounds, hotspot stocks, and digital-literacy help. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
  - **Program records and contemporaneous observation** — device counts, lending changes, outdoor labs, bookmobile Wi-Fi, and parking-lot use demonstrated actual substitution. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The evidence came from public surveys, public-library program information, and interviews. The lockdown closure is historical, but the instrument—compare household access against the capacity and interruption of substitute public infrastructure—still works for library closures, transit cuts, disaster outages, and benefit-office consolidation.
- **Detection signature**: `substitute-infrastructure removal triangulation` — household connectivity surveys **compared to** library closure and hotspot-capacity evidence **on keys** geography, income, device ownership, and service availability **revealed** the hidden access supplied by libraries and the unmet demand after removal.
- **Corroboration structure**: National surveys supplied prevalence; shelter and rural figures exposed different high-risk populations; library interviews supplied mechanisms; quantified hotspot and Wi-Fi adaptations served as revealed-demand evidence. No single anecdote carried the national conclusion. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
- **Methodology notes**: No story-specific Show Your Work page or GitHub repository was located. **[inferred]** The detector is a triangulation rather than a released analysis: establish home-access deficits, identify the free substitute, document its interruption, and measure emergency replacement capacity. **Historical decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**; the interview set and facility-level observations were not released.
- **Impact**: The story documents service adaptations—24/7 Wi-Fi, bookmobile and fire-station routers, and expanded hotspot lending—but does not attribute those actions to publication. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/06/25/millions-of-americans-depend-on-libraries-for-internet-now-theyre-closed).
- **Generalization**: Look for systems whose real capacity includes an informal fallback: libraries for internet, emergency rooms for primary care, schools for meals, buses for shelter, or nonprofits for government forms. Compare the fallback's closure/capacity to the population that lacks the primary service.

### Remote Learning During the Pandemic Has Hit Vulnerable Students the Hardest (2020) — attendance losses concentrated in schools already burdened by poverty and low achievement

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools); [code and data](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-florida-online-attendance).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no reporting partner or story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - Records covering more than 800 schools in Broward, Miami-Dade, and Hillsborough showed that schools with more low-income and minority students and weaker prior achievement generally fared worst after instruction moved online. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).
  - McArthur High fell from 91.7% prior-year attendance to 88.2% during the April remote window; low-poverty Cypress Bay stayed nearly flat, 95.7% to 95.4%. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).
  - In Hillsborough, all 2,414 Sickles High students participated during one week, while six miles away 922 Gaither students—45% of enrollment—never interacted with distance learning. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).
  - Miami-Dade's high-poverty schools averaged a nearly 10% attendance drop, versus less than 1% at the wealthiest schools; Broward schools on improvement plans had the largest decline. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).
- **Finding type(s)**: `preexisting-inequality amplification` — a system shock widens gaps already visible in baseline status; `engagement-proxy disparity` — platform interaction or login becomes a measurable but imperfect proxy for access to a public service.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Open-record school microdata** — requests to Florida's 10 largest county districts yielded usable school-level remote participation data from three districts; Broward supplied daily attendance and enrollment. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).
  - **Historical baseline and school attributes** — 2018–19 attendance, Florida school grades/improvement-plan status, economic disadvantage, and racial composition. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).
  - **Interviews and agency responses** — teachers, parents, advocates, district officials, and researchers interpreted equipment, disability, language, caregiving, and measurement limitations. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).
  - **Released reproducibility package** — raw district/education data, processing notebooks, requirements, and analytical notebooks. [GitHub](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-florida-online-attendance).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today.** The 2020 inputs are released and ordinary school-attendance records remain requestable. But widespread lockdown-era “online interaction” was a temporary modality; most districts stopped collecting that specific measure when in-person schooling resumed, so a fresh acquisition cannot recreate the same treatment. The detector remains usable for other modality changes if comparable pre/post service-engagement data exist.
- **Detection signature**: `pre/post participation-demography join` — school-level remote participation **joined to** prior attendance, poverty, race, and achievement **on key** school identifier and **compared across** pre-shock and lockdown periods **revealed** concentrated engagement loss at already disadvantaged schools.
- **Corroboration structure**: Broward's detailed panel supplied the principal comparison; Miami-Dade and Hillsborough supplied external within-state replication using different measures; school-pair contrasts made the aggregate gap concrete; interviews supplied causal hypotheses without converting them into proved causes. The article warned that attendance does not equal learning and that districts measured participation differently. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).
- **Methodology notes**: No separate Show Your Work page was located. **[inferred]** The article states the join and comparison, while the [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-florida-online-attendance) releases data and notebooks explicitly “to reproduce findings.” **Historical decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes.**
- **Impact**: The story reports that Broward sent Wi-Fi hotspots after noticing students had not logged in and that Florida temporarily decoupled funding from attendance, but neither action is established as a response to The Markup. [Story](https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/08/13/remote-learning-attendance-inequity-florida-schools).

### Dollars to Megabits, You May Be Paying 400 Times As Much As Your Neighbor for Internet Service (2022) — four ISPs offered poorer and less-White neighborhoods slower service at the same base price

- **URL**: [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2022/10/19/dollars-to-megabits-you-may-be-paying-400-times-as-much-as-your-neighbor-for-internet-service); [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/10/19/how-we-uncovered-disparities-in-internet-deals); [Story Recipe](https://themarkup.org/story-recipes/2022/10/19/journalists-investigate-which-neighborhoods-in-your-city-are-offered-the-worst-internet-deals); [code and data](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-isp).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original, widely republished through its Associated Press partnership; local outlets used the data, but AP was distribution rather than joint reporting. [Sigma impact note](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2023/03/17/the-markup-wins-sigma-award-for-series-on-internet-disparities). Still Loading won a [2023 Sigma Award](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2023/03/17/the-markup-wins-sigma-award-for-series-on-internet-disparities) and first place in the [2024 Philip Meyer Journalism Award](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/01/17/the-markup-wins-philip-meyer-journalism-award).
- **What they found**:
  - The socioeconomic analysis covered 848,618 address-level offers across 38 cities from AT&T, Verizon, EarthLink, and CenturyLink. The worst-deal neighborhoods were lower-income in 92% of cities and least-White in two-thirds of cities with sufficient variation. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/10/19/how-we-uncovered-disparities-in-internet-deals).
  - In every one of 22 analyzable cities with digitized historical maps, formerly redlined areas disproportionately received the worst deals. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2022/10/19/dollars-to-megabits-you-may-be-paying-400-times-as-much-as-your-neighbor-for-internet-service).
  - CenturyLink offered some customers 200 Mbps at $0.25 per Mbps and others 0.5 Mbps at $100 per Mbps—a 400-fold unit-price difference inside a city. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2022/10/19/dollars-to-megabits-you-may-be-paying-400-times-as-much-as-your-neighbor-for-internet-service).
  - In Kansas City, two AT&T addresses 0.4 miles apart were offered 300 Mbps and 5 Mbps for the same $55; the surrounding block groups differed sharply in income and White population. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2022/10/19/dollars-to-megabits-you-may-be-paying-400-times-as-much-as-your-neighbor-for-internet-service).
  - AT&T's lower-income disparity remained in three-quarters of 20 cities after controlling for population density. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2022/10/19/dollars-to-megabits-you-may-be-paying-400-times-as-much-as-your-neighbor-for-internet-service).
- **Finding type(s)**: `geographic price-quality discrimination` — equal nominal prices purchase radically unequal service by neighborhood; `legacy-infrastructure penalty` — customers bear worse unit value where a provider has not upgraded its network; `historical-disinvestment persistence` — contemporary service gaps align with former redlining geography.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Constructed address-level offer collection** — more than 12 million OpenAddresses/NYC Open Data addresses were geocoded; a random 10% sample within each Census block group was queried through ISP availability-tool endpoints. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/10/19/how-we-uncovered-disparities-in-internet-deals).
  - **Instrumented public-product tests** — four custom collectors made 1,606,964 delayed queries through residential IPs; 455,534 unserved results left 1,151,430 offers, from which the 848,618-offer socioeconomic subset was built. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/10/19/how-we-uncovered-disparities-in-internet-deals).
  - **Public socioeconomic and historical geodata** — 2019 five-year ACS income/race data, Census geography, FCC Form 477, and University of Richmond Mapping Inequality HOLC polygons. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/10/19/how-we-uncovered-disparities-in-internet-deals).
  - **Company and resident corroboration** — household cases confirmed practical consequences; providers acknowledged equal base prices across different speeds while disputing discrimination or the analysis. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2022/10/19/dollars-to-megabits-you-may-be-paying-400-times-as-much-as-your-neighbor-for-internet-service).
  - **Released analysis package and reporting recipe** — address-level offers, processed inputs/outputs, notebooks, localized tables/maps, methodology, and a step-by-step [Story Recipe](https://themarkup.org/story-recipes/2022/10/19/journalists-investigate-which-neighborhoods-in-your-city-are-offered-the-worst-internet-deals). [GitHub](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-isp).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today.** Provider lookup pages were public, unauthenticated product instruments, so this is not closed-platform-dependent. Historical analysis is reproducible from released offer files and notebooks. **Fresh collection is degraded**: the repository contains processing/analysis code and collected API responses, but not the four acquisition collectors; provider endpoints and anti-automation behavior are mutable, and the residential-proxy arrangement was external. A small manual collection remains possible using the published recipe, while a national refresh requires endpoint rediscovery, rate-limit testing, and new compliant collection infrastructure.
- **Detection signature**: `address-offer-demography spatial join` — provider price/speed offers **joined to** ACS demographics and HOLC polygons **on key** address-to-block-group coordinates, then **compared within** provider-city pairs **revealed** equal-price/unequal-quality burdens concentrated in poorer, less-White, formerly redlined areas.
- **Corroboration structure**: Four providers and 38 cities supplied replication; within-city/provider comparisons controlled away different local price schedules; stratification reduced place-selection bias; resident pairs made the mechanism inspectable; density-adjusted models tested a central provider alternative; company responses and stated limitations bounded interpretation. The authors did not test actual delivered speed and avoided p-values because address offers were not independent. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/10/19/how-we-uncovered-disparities-in-internet-deals).
- **Methodology notes**: The method page says the team gathered “1.1 million actual service offers” and documents address sourcing, 10% block-group stratification, endpoint discovery, delays/proxies, categorization, spatial joins, five-percentage-point disparity threshold, regressions, and limitations. [Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/10/19/how-we-uncovered-disparities-in-internet-deals). The [repository](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-isp) releases offer data and processing/analysis notebooks; the [recipe](https://themarkup.org/story-recipes/2022/10/19/journalists-investigate-which-neighborhoods-in-your-city-are-offered-the-worst-internet-deals) shows local reuse. **Historical decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes. Fresh acquisition re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no.**
- **Impact**: Los Angeles later became the first U.S. city to ban digital discrimination locally; the council record and advocacy letter cited disparity studies including The Markup, and the city's complaint process requires demographic trend reporting. [Impact story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2024/02/01/los-angeles-becomes-first-us-city-to-outlaw-digital-discrimination). The Markup also briefed local, state, and federal lawmakers and regulators, and at least seven local newsrooms used the data to localize findings. [Sigma note](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2023/03/17/the-markup-wins-sigma-award-for-series-on-internet-disparities).
- **Generalization**: Sample actual offers at real addresses, normalize value per unit, and spatially join terms to neighborhood characteristics and historical investment boundaries. The generic detector applies to insurance quotes, delivery availability, utility hookups, ride-hail wait times, lending, and health-provider appointment supply.

### Introducing the United States Place Sampler (USPS) (2023) — the reporting team turned representative address selection into reusable public infrastructure

- **URL**: [Method/tool story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps); [live tool](https://usps.biglocalnews.org/); [source repository](https://github.com/biglocalnews/usps).
- **Partner/awards**: Built with Big Local News at Stanford; Joe Nudell is credited for Big Local News. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps). Judges for the first-place [2024 Philip Meyer Journalism Award](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2024/01/17/the-markup-wins-philip-meyer-journalism-award) specifically praised the public bespoke mapping tool and partnership.
- **What they found**:
  - The team identified address sampling—not later statistical analysis—as a hidden bottleneck: public address files were scarce or incomplete, and selecting points by clicking Google Maps would create a biased sample. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps).
  - The beta indexed more than 200 million OpenAddresses records and could return a fixed number or percentage of random addresses by place, county, tract, or block group, with FIPS codes down to block-group level. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps).
  - At least seven newsrooms in previously analyzed cities localized the ISP findings; the sampler was designed to let investigators outside those 38 cities build less-biased address frames for the same or other place-based tests. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps).
- **Finding type(s)**: `measurement-frame repair` — the tool fixes bias before outcome collection begins; `method-as-public-infrastructure` — a one-off investigative dependency becomes a reusable instrument for others.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Open address corpus** — OpenAddresses records ingested nationwide during February 19–23, 2023. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps).
  - **Official geographic boundaries and identifiers** — 2022 Census TIGER shapefiles, TIGER Geocoder, and FIPS geography. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps).
  - **Constructed sampling service** — PostGIS spatial indexes align address coordinates to block groups, places, counties, and other designations before random selection. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps).
  - **Open-source implementation** — database, ingestion, application, and deployment materials in the Big Local News [USPS repository](https://github.com/biglocalnews/usps).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The [public service](https://usps.biglocalnews.org/) is live, and the [source repository](https://github.com/biglocalnews/usps) remains available. An outside investigator can use the hosted tool or deploy the stack. The main limitations are source completeness/freshness and the substantial storage/compute needed for nationwide ingestion, not a platform closure.
- **Detection signature**: `representative-frame construction` — OpenAddresses points **spatially joined to** Census TIGER boundaries **on key** coordinates and geographic IDs, then randomly sampled within declared places **revealed** a reproducible, less-biased address frame for downstream disparity measurements.
- **Corroboration structure**: Statisticians' rejection of convenience clicking supplied the bias rationale; the prior ISP investigation supplied an empirical use case; FIPS attachment made independent demographic joins possible; local newsroom reuse demonstrated demand. [Story](https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps).
- **Methodology notes**: There is no separate Show Your Work page because the article itself is the build/method account. It states that preprocessing replaced per-address Census API calls, describes CRS alignment and spatial indices, and links the [GitHub repository](https://github.com/biglocalnews/usps). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes**, subject to reacquiring current OpenAddresses/TIGER inputs and provisioning PostGIS.
- **Impact**: The tool remains publicly usable and made address-frame construction available beyond The Markup's original 38-city corpus. [Live USPS](https://usps.biglocalnews.org/).
- **Generalization**: Before measuring geographic inequality, audit the sampling frame. Prefer random or stratified selection inside official boundaries, record coverage gaps, and attach stable geographic IDs so outcomes can be joined without post hoc place selection.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### Pattern candidate A: Matched-Applicant Jurisdiction Diff

- **Mechanics**: Express eligibility as predicates; create several fixed applicant profiles; evaluate every jurisdiction's dated rule against each profile; flag outcome changes caused only by place.
- **Minimum data**: Dated rule text for at least two jurisdictions, a shared eligibility schema, and one or more matched profiles.
- **Recognition cues**: “May qualify,” “priority group,” local discretion, different forms for the same program, or complaints that moving across a boundary changes access.
- **Source stories**: testing-plan comparison and unemployment workflow census.

#### Pattern candidate B: High-Stakes Transaction Audit with Human Override

- **Mechanics**: Census official service URLs; run accessibility, privacy, performance, and workflow checks under common conditions; then have affected users attempt the decisive task and code failures automation misses.
- **Minimum data**: Canonical URL list, automated test outputs, a fixed task script, and a small manual validation sample.
- **Recognition cues**: emergency microsites, third-party widgets, unlabeled controls, redirects among vendors, high abandonment, or officials claiming standards compliance without task evidence.
- **Source stories**: blind-user audit, unemployment system audit, and vaccine website audit.

#### Pattern candidate C: Public-Service Funnel Capacity Ledger

- **Mechanics**: Reconstruct every transition from attempted contact to resolution; join channel outcomes to staffing, vendor contracts, invoices, and service-level terms; calculate rejection and abandonment before the official queue.
- **Minimum data**: Timestamped channel logs with outcome states, queue/wait fields, vendor identity, and contract/payment records.
- **Recognition cues**: busy signals excluded from “wait time,” contractor success claims without denominator, large spend plus persistent complaints, or changing category definitions.
- **Source stories**: Florida unemployment call-center reconstruction and the national benefit-friction census.

#### Pattern candidate D: Address-Level Terms-to-Place Join

- **Mechanics**: Draw a representative address sample inside stable boundaries; collect the actual offer or availability result at each address; normalize value; spatially join to demographics, investment history, and official coverage.
- **Minimum data**: Address frame with coordinates, timestamped offer/availability result, geographic crosswalk, and at least one neighborhood attribute.
- **Recognition cues**: “Prices start at,” service dependent on address, maps claiming entire areas are served, legacy infrastructure, or neighboring households reporting different terms.
- **Source stories**: ISP price/speed investigation, USPS, and rural served-on-paper reporting.

#### Pattern candidate E: Program-Cliff Exposure Map

- **Mechanics**: Project exhaustion from appropriation and monthly claims; join recipients to demographic/geographic vulnerability; compare remaining runway with legislative, procurement, and notification lead time.
- **Minimum data**: Total funding, monthly burn, enrollment by place/group, statutory end conditions, and pending extension status.
- **Recognition cues**: one-time appropriation funding a recurring service, enrollment freeze, “wind-down,” partial final benefit, or contractors/providers warning recipients before lawmakers act.
- **Source stories**: ACP termination, with the library closure as a physical-infrastructure analogue.

### 5. What this platform could run today

**Runnable or substantially runnable with current holdings**

- **Vendor-to-failure enrichment**: Once a public-service breakdown identifies a contractor, USASpending/FPDS can measure awards and modifications; corporate registries, OpenCorporates, GLEIF, OpenSanctions, ICIJ, and OpenAleph can resolve ownership/officers; CourtListener/RECAP can find performance litigation. This supplies the contract/accountability half of the Public-Service Funnel pattern.
- **Public-investment network analysis**: USASpending/FPDS can identify broadband, call-center, benefits-technology, and accessibility vendors, then connect them across agencies and jurisdictions. Registries and 990 data can expose nonprofit/provider partnerships, while property tools can locate facilities and infrastructure owners.
- **After-the-fact consequence checks**: CourtListener/RECAP and corporate/property sources can test whether service failures produced lawsuits, consent decrees, bankruptcies, ownership changes, or asset transfers.
- **Program runway, partially**: Federal spending and award data can identify finite grants and contract periods. Where an agency also publishes enrollment/claims, the platform can calculate a cliff, but those program-specific administrative tables are not among the stated native holdings.

**Missing for full execution**

- A maintained census of public-benefit and emergency-service URLs, plus browser automation, WAVE/Lighthouse/Blacklight execution, screenshots, and human task-test protocols.
- A public-records acquisition layer for call logs, school participation, agency rules, and service-level metrics; current holdings contain dockets and spending, not the decisive operational microdata.
- A native nationwide address frame/geocoder, ACS/TIGER/HOLC/FCC broadband loaders, and compliant provider-offer collectors. The live [United States Place Sampler](https://usps.biglocalnews.org/) can supply addresses externally, but it does not collect outcomes.
- Time-versioned capture of dynamic public pages and product offers. Without snapshots, a public instrument can be open yet evidentially irreproducible after a page or program changes.
- Structured beneficiary enrollment and monthly burn data for subsidy-cliff monitoring.

The strongest immediate platform-native adaptation is therefore **contract-first**: use USASpending/FPDS to find technology vendors behind public services, enrich their corporate and litigation networks, and then seek the missing operational funnel records. The strongest currently missing capability is **measurement-first**: repeatable public-site and address-level instruments that create the outcome data before the platform's existing entity/network tools take over.
