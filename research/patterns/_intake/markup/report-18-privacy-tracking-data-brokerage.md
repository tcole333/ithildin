# The Markup Evidence Ontology — Cluster 18: Privacy, Tracking & Data Brokerage

Compiled July 29, 2026. Web-verified against The Markup’s live stories, methodology pages, GitHub releases, official impact posts, and the current status of the underlying instruments. Coverage: **11 original investigations** (three Blacklight, eight Pixel Hunt), 2020–2025.

## Scope and attribution

- This report follows the census’s **domain-structural attribution rule**: a dated editorial URL exposed on `themarkup.org` is counted once, including post-merger work published there; a separately self-canonical CalMatters copy, CalMatters-only story, or syndication/translation is not added. The rule and merger reasoning are documented in [the Markup corpus census](report-10-census.md#1-method-and-sources).
- Methodology and impact posts are cited under the investigation they support, not counted as additional stories. The selected investigations are the method-bearing core of [Blacklight](https://themarkup.org/series/blacklight) and [Pixel Hunt](https://themarkup.org/series/pixel-hunt), rather than a sample of the series’ many award, service, and impact updates.
- **Finding-type tags are free-form and defined on first use.** **Latent-surveillance infrastructure** means code embedded in an outward-facing service silently creates a second data flow to an outside recipient. **Sensitive-context inference** means an otherwise ordinary URL, page title, button label, search term, or form answer discloses an intimate condition or intent. **Operator-blind vendor cascade** means the organization intended to install one tool, but that tool or its vendor chain loaded additional recipients the operator did not understand. **Policy–packet contradiction** means observed network traffic conflicts with a privacy statement, platform rule, consent choice, or governing legal expectation. **Identity-relinkable hashing** means a one-way hash is presented as anonymization even though the recipient uses it to match the subject to an existing identity. **Authenticated-space exfiltration** means telemetry carries data out of a logged-in or otherwise nonpublic service area. **Closed-platform receipt validation** means the reporter proves not merely that a packet was sent, but that the receiving platform retained, associated, or operationalized it.

### The High Privacy Cost of a “Free” Website (2020) — Blacklight turned hidden web surveillance into a measurable request graph

- **URL**: [The High Privacy Cost of a “Free” Website](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/blacklight-tracking-advertisers-digital-privacy-sensitive-websites) (Aaron Sankin and Surya Mattu, Sept. 22, 2020).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - Blacklight successfully captured **81,617** of the Tranco top 100,000 sites: 6% used canvas fingerprinting, 15% loaded known session recorders, 4% logged keystrokes, 74% loaded Google tracking technology, and 33% loaded Facebook tracking technology. Only 13% loaded neither third-party cookies nor tracking requests. ([Methodology and survey](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector#2020-investigation-and-survey))
  - SPART*A’s operator believed its site contained three trackers, but Blacklight observed **21 additional advertising companies** arriving through the free Disqus plug-in, including businesses associated with Google, Amazon, and Oracle/BlueKai. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/blacklight-tracking-advertisers-digital-privacy-sensitive-websites))
  - More than 200 popular sites captured information typed into forms before submission. SunTrust’s site sent The Markup’s sentinel username and password to Jornaya; the transmission stopped after the newsroom contacted the bank. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/blacklight-tracking-advertisers-digital-privacy-sensitive-websites))
  - The sensitive-site audit found trackers on more than 100 sites serving undocumented immigrants, abuse survivors, sex workers, and LGBTQ people; 80 abortion-provider sites loaded third-party trackers; and the Arizona child-abuse-reporting page contradicted its no-cookie statement until the agency added a new disclosure. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/blacklight-tracking-advertisers-digital-privacy-sensitive-websites))
- **Finding type(s)**: latent-surveillance infrastructure; operator-blind vendor cascade; sensitive-context inference; policy–packet contradiction.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Instrumented browser telemetry — constructed**: a fresh-profile Puppeteer/Chromium browser recorded JavaScript API calls, network requests, headers, cookies, pixel parameters, and text egress while loading a homepage plus a randomly chosen internal page. ([Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector))
  - **Tracker and ownership lists — open public**: request domains were compared with DuckDuckGo Tracker Radar, EasyList/EasyPrivacy, the Public Suffix List, and research-maintained lists of session-recording and fingerprinting behavior. ([Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector))
  - **Popularity frame — open public**: the Tranco top-100,000 list supplied the denominator; 81,617 successful captures were analyzed and failures were explicitly excluded. ([Survey](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector#2020-investigation-and-survey))
  - **Controlled sentinel inputs — constructed**: Blacklight typed predetermined names, credentials, addresses, and payment-field values without submitting and searched requests for plain, Base64, MD5, SHA-256, and SHA-512 representations. ([Appendix](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector#appendix))
  - **Operator interviews and policies — constructed/open public**: site owners’ stated expectations and privacy policies were compared with observed traffic. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/blacklight-tracking-advertisers-digital-privacy-sensitive-websites))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The acquisition instrument is rebuildable and remains operational: [Blacklight Collector](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-collector) can save HARs and run locally, and [Blacklight Query](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-query) accepts newline-delimited URL cohorts. The Markup reported in February 2026 that Blacklight had passed **18 million scans** and had added TikTok and X pixel detection. ([2026 update](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2026/02/09/blacklight-update-tiktok-x-twitter)) Exact 2020 prevalence numbers are historical, however: tracker lists, Chromium behavior, consent banners, and websites have all changed.
- **Detection signature**: **instrumented request-graph census + operator expectation diff** — a controlled browser’s JavaScript/network trace joined to tracker-owner lists on third-party domain, then compared with the operator’s stated three intended integrations, revealed both population prevalence and the unrecognized 21-company cascade. A sub-test joined unique form sentinels to outbound payload values across several encodings, revealing pre-submit exfiltration.
- **Corroboration structure**: convergence across a large bounded cohort, precise packet-level examples, local-versus-AWS validation on a random 1,000-site sample, preserved code, operator responses, and before/after scans. The methodology explicitly warns that automated browsing can trigger different behavior, canvas heuristics can produce false positives, and detection does not establish recipient intent. ([Limitations](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector#limitations))
- **Methodology notes**: Quoted methodology is [“How We Built a Real-time Privacy Inspector”](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector). Released artifacts: [blacklight-collector](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-collector), [blacklight-query](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-query), [blacklight-reporter](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-reporter), and [investigation-blacklight-the-high-cost-of-free](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-blacklight-the-high-cost-of-free). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: substantially yes for a current scan**, because Collector and Query run locally; **not as a bit-for-bit 2020 reproduction**, because the repo notes that Collector is only part of the production application and web dependencies are time-varying.
- **Official impact**: SunTrust stopped the Jornaya credential transmission; SPART*A and the National Sexual Violence Resource Center removed the offending plug-ins; Arkansas and Louisiana removed stale AddThis code; the Arizona agency changed its disclosure; and the U.S. Mint stopped the fingerprinting behavior after inquiry. ([Story and updates](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/blacklight-tracking-advertisers-digital-privacy-sensitive-websites))
- **Generalization**: Any public web service can be treated as a data-flow system rather than a page. A generic detector instruments script calls and request/response traffic, resolves recipients to owners, plants unique sentinel values, and diffs the observed graph against the site’s policy and operator inventory.

### We Ran Tests on Every State’s COVID-19 Vaccine Website (2021) — a complete public-service cohort exposed privacy and access outliers

- **URL**: [We Ran Tests on Every State’s COVID-19 Vaccine Website](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/03/24/we-ran-tests-on-every-states-covid-19-vaccine-website) (Jon Keegan and Colin Lecher, Mar. 24, 2021).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - Across all 50 states, Puerto Rico, and Washington, D.C., the mean was **2.8 ad trackers and 3.4 third-party cookies**; Texas, New York, Kentucky, West Virginia, New Hampshire, and Puerto Rico had neither in the test. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/03/24/we-ran-tests-on-every-states-covid-19-vaccine-website))
  - Nevada was the extreme privacy outlier with **24 ad trackers and 45 third-party cookies—more cookies than the lowest 46 states combined**; Hawaii had 17 trackers/37 cookies, Utah 10/22, and New Jersey 12/11. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/03/24/we-ran-tests-on-every-states-covid-19-vaccine-website))
  - Pennsylvania scored **17/100** for performance and was second-lowest for accessibility; 84% of sites nevertheless scored at least 85 on Lighthouse accessibility, while New Mexico was lowest at 66. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/03/24/we-ran-tests-on-every-states-covid-19-vaccine-website))
  - Performance was not a one-shot observation: The Markup averaged 30 Lighthouse tests per state over two days from New York, Texas, and California. ([Story methodology](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/03/24/we-ran-tests-on-every-states-covid-19-vaccine-website))
- **Finding type(s)**: latent-surveillance infrastructure; **public-service outlier** (a complete or nearly complete government-service cohort contains an extreme implementation); **access–surveillance coupling** (the same third-party stack that observes users can also burden loading and accessibility).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Official target list — open public**: Department of Health and Human Services vaccine-finder links, refined to the page where users could locate vaccination. ([Data README](https://github.com/the-markup/state_covid-19-vaccine_websites_audit))
  - **Blacklight scans — constructed**: tracker, cookie, and third-party-request counts for every jurisdiction. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/03/24/we-ran-tests-on-every-states-covid-19-vaccine-website))
  - **Lighthouse audits — constructed/open-source instrument**: repeated mobile performance and accessibility tests from three regions. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/03/24/we-ran-tests-on-every-states-covid-19-vaccine-website))
  - **Agency responses and re-tests — constructed**: explanations from state/nonprofit operators and later Blacklight/Lighthouse remeasurement. ([Impact follow-up](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/05/18/nevada-lawmakers-introduce-privacy-legislation-after-markup-investigation-into-vaccine-websites))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded today.** The Blacklight and Lighthouse instruments still work and the full 2021 input/data package is public, but the decisive source population—emergency-era state vaccine portals—is historical. Many URLs and registration architectures have been retired, redirected, or repurposed; a 2026 run measures successor pages, not the March 2021 system.
- **Detection signature**: **bounded-service cohort outlier scan** — HHS’s jurisdiction list joined to Blacklight and 30-run Lighthouse measurements on jurisdiction URL revealed Nevada’s surveillance outlier and Pennsylvania/New Mexico access outliers; comparison against the cohort distribution made configuration problems legible without needing an internal leak.
- **Corroboration structure**: complete-jurisdiction coverage, repeated performance trials from three geographic origins, two independent open-source instruments, named outlier details, operator responses, and a later before/after audit.
- **Methodology notes**: No separate story-specific Show Your Work page was located. **[Inferred workflow synthesis]** from the article’s test description, its link to the foundational [Blacklight methodology](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector), and the released [state_covid-19-vaccine_websites_audit](https://github.com/the-markup/state_covid-19-vaccine_websites_audit) inputs/outputs; reusable collectors are [blacklight-collector](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-collector) and [blacklight-query](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-query). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes for the archived analysis and for a successor-site audit; no for recreating the vanished 2021 live state.**
- **Official impact**: Nevada’s operator audited the site, disabled cookies by default, removed three-quarters of its ad trackers, published a new privacy policy, and improved performance; lawmakers introduced emergency privacy legislation after the investigation and AP follow-up. ([Impact](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/05/18/nevada-lawmakers-introduce-privacy-legislation-after-markup-investigation-into-vaccine-websites))
- **Generalization**: Enumerate every state/local implementation of a federally mandated service, run the same privacy and usability tests, and rank against the cohort rather than an arbitrary benchmark. Suitable targets include benefits enrollment, court e-filing, tax portals, procurement registration, licensing, and public-health scheduling.

### Nonprofit Websites Are Riddled With Ad Trackers (2021) — tax-status records became a 23,856-site surveillance census

- **URL**: [Nonprofit Websites Are Riddled With Ad Trackers](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers) (Alfred Ng and Maddy Varner, Oct. 21, 2021).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup original; no story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - The Markup joined the IRS nonprofit master file to GuideStar website fields and scanned **23,856** nonprofit domains in sensitive categories; about **86%** loaded a third-party cookie or tracking request, 11% used a Facebook pixel, and 18% used Google Analytics’ remarketing feature. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
  - **439** sites loaded session-recording scripts, including **89** organizations the IRS classified under mental health and crisis intervention. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
  - Planned Parenthood’s main site carried **28 ad trackers, 40 third-party cookies, and session recorders**, with observed recipients including Oracle, Verizon, LiveRamp, TowerData, and Quantcast. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
  - High-count examples included an immigration legal clinic (26 trackers/50 cookies), Save the Children (26/49), March of Dimes (more than 29/58), and City of Hope (25/47). ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
- **Finding type(s)**: latent-surveillance infrastructure; sensitive-context inference; **regulatory-carveout exposure** (an institution class holds sensitive data but is excluded from otherwise relevant privacy duties); operator-blind vendor cascade.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **IRS nonprofit master file — open public**: organization universe, recent-return status, and NTEE-like subject categorization. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
  - **GuideStar website fields — public/commercial interface**: nonprofit-to-domain resolution. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
  - **Blacklight scan corpus — constructed**: request, cookie, pixel, and session-recorder observations for 23,856 sites. ([Released analysis](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-nonprofit-privacy))
  - **State privacy laws, data-broker records, and vendor materials — open public**: California/Virginia nonprofit exemptions and examples such as Blackbaud’s donor-data co-op. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
  - **Organization and expert interviews — constructed**: operators explained fundraising/analytics incentives and, in some cases, promised review. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; runnable today.** The released cohort and analysis remain public, the IRS/990 backbone remains obtainable, and Blacklight’s local collector/query are active. GuideStar’s access model can complicate rebuilding the nonprofit→domain join from scratch, but the historical domains and outputs are released; current scans will measure contemporary configurations.
- **Detection signature**: **registry-to-domain-to-egress join** — IRS nonprofit identity/category rows joined to GuideStar on organization, then to Blacklight on domain, revealed surveillance prevalence specifically inside mental-health, civil-rights, reproductive-health, and medical nonprofits; the sector label supplied the sensitivity that a tracker count alone could not.
- **Corroboration structure**: population-scale scan, public tax classifications, named examples with exact recipients, comparisons to the 2020 general-web baseline, and responses from large and small nonprofits.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone Show Your Work page was located. **[Inferred workflow synthesis]** from the story and [investigation-nonprofit-privacy](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-nonprofit-privacy), which explicitly describe the IRS→GuideStar→Blacklight sequence, plus the foundational [Blacklight methodology](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector). Other released repos: [blacklight-reporter](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-reporter) and [investigation-blacklight-the-high-cost-of-free](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-blacklight-the-high-cost-of-free). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes for the released cohort and current web state; exact historical responses survive only in the archive.**
- **Official impact**: No separate formal impact post was located. The immediate official responses included an immigration clinic’s promise to address the findings and Save the Children’s disclosure that it was revising retention policies and had hired a data lead. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2021/10/21/nonprofit-websites-are-riddled-with-ad-trackers))
- **Generalization**: Join an institution registry to public domains, scan at scale, and stratify by mission or client sensitivity. The same detector works for schools, clinics, unions, churches, domestic-violence services, government contractors, and licensed professionals.

### Applied for Student Aid Online? Facebook Saw You (2022) — volunteer browser telemetry exposed Meta Advanced Matching inside FAFSA

- **URL**: [Applied for Student Aid Online? Facebook Saw You](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/04/28/applied-for-student-aid-online-facebook-saw-you) (Surya Mattu and Colin Lecher, Apr. 28, 2022).
- **Partner/awards**: Copublished with **Chalkbeat**; **Mozilla Rally** was the study partner. The story, methodology, hospital, tax, and telehealth investigations jointly won the [2023 National Press Club Consumer Journalism Award](https://themarkup.org/inside-the-markup/2023/07/26/the-markup-wins-national-press-club-award).
- **What they found**:
  - StudentAid.gov sent first and last names, email addresses, phone numbers, country, and ZIP codes to Facebook through the Meta Pixel, including before login and regardless of whether the visitor had a Facebook account. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/04/28/applied-for-student-aid-online-facebook-saw-you))
  - The sharing continued on demographic, financial, parent-information, and high-school pages; names entered by a reporter appeared as SHA-256 parameters in the outbound request. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/04/28/applied-for-student-aid-online-facebook-saw-you))
  - The observed data predated the Education Department’s stated March 2022 campaign: Pixel Hunt showed first name, last name, country, phone, and email transmission as early as January 2022. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/04/28/applied-for-student-aid-online-facebook-saw-you))
  - At methodology publication, **5,447** panelists had produced **2,635,130** pixel events; 80% had encountered the Meta Pixel, providing the discovery layer for sensitive logged-in pages that a homepage scanner could miss. ([Show Your Work](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/04/28/how-we-built-a-meta-pixel-inspector))
- **Finding type(s)**: latent-surveillance infrastructure; authenticated-space exfiltration; identity-relinkable hashing; policy–packet contradiction; **panel-reach discovery** (consenting users’ real browsing reveals behavior behind individualized or logged-in surfaces).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Mozilla Rally browser-panel telemetry — participant-contributed**: the add-on collected copies of Meta Pixel network requests encountered during real browsing. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/04/28/applied-for-student-aid-online-facebook-saw-you); [methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/04/28/how-we-built-a-meta-pixel-inspector))
  - **Reporter-controlled replication — constructed**: a reporter entered known values and inspected their hashed representation in the Meta request. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/04/28/applied-for-student-aid-online-facebook-saw-you))
  - **Pixel schema and Meta documentation — open public/archived**: standard, custom, automatic Advanced Matching, and identifier parameter formats supplied the parser. ([Methodology](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/04/28/how-we-built-a-meta-pixel-inspector#how-we-analyze-the-pixel-data))
  - **Department policy and response — open public/constructed**: the agency’s denial and privacy statement were checked against packets and dates. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/04/28/applied-for-student-aid-online-facebook-saw-you))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-instrument-runnable; degraded today.** Pixel Hunt was a constructed, open-source browser instrument rather than a leak. An outside investigator can still inspect their own network traffic, and the [archived Facebook Pixel Hunt extension](https://github.com/mozilla-rally/facebook-pixel-hunt) preserves study code. But the exact path is degraded twice: the Department disabled the identifying-data configuration after questions, and the [Mozilla Rally organization was archived in July 2024](https://github.com/mozilla-rally), so there is no live public panel/back end for population discovery.
- **Detection signature**: **authenticated sentinel-to-egress join** — volunteer Meta requests surfaced a FAFSA path; a reporter’s known form values were normalized and hashed, then joined to `udff[...]`/Advanced Matching fields in requests to Meta, revealing identity exfiltration behind a mandatory public-service workflow. A date diff between panel observations and the agency’s claimed campaign start revealed the timeline contradiction.
- **Corroboration structure**: panel discovery, controlled reporter replication, parameter-level interpretation from Meta’s own schema, an agency admission after initial denial, and a released URL list stripped of participant PII.
- **Methodology notes**: Quoted method: [“How We Built a Meta Pixel Inspector”](https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/04/28/how-we-built-a-meta-pixel-inspector). Released repos: [meta-pixel-student-aid](https://github.com/the-markup/meta-pixel-student-aid) and Mozilla’s archived [facebook-pixel-hunt](https://github.com/mozilla-rally/facebook-pixel-hunt). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no at original conditions.** The release preserves affected URLs and study code but not sensitive participant payloads or a live Rally deployment; an investigator can reimplement the local packet test on a service they are authorized to use.
- **Official impact**: The Department turned off identifying-data sharing; Sens. Richard Burr and Rep. Virginia Foxx demanded answers; later advocacy groups sued for records about the incident. ([May 2022 impact](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/05/11/lawmakers-question-education-department-about-facebook-student-aid-tracking-after-markup-investigation); [2024 lawsuit update](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2024/07/26/department-of-education-sued-following-markup-investigation-into-fafsa-data-shared-with-facebook))
- **Generalization**: A consenting user panel can surface otherwise invisible transmissions from tax, benefits, school, bank, and patient portals; investigators then reproduce with their own authorized test account and sentinel data before publication.

### Facebook and Anti-Abortion Clinics Are Collecting Highly Sensitive Info on Would-Be Patients (2022) — a clean account proved transmission became retained targeting data

- **URL**: [Facebook and Anti-Abortion Clinics Are Collecting Highly Sensitive Info on Would-Be Patients](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients) (Grace Oldham and Dhruv Mehrotra, June 15, 2022).
- **Partner/awards**: Collaboration with **Reveal from the Center for Investigative Reporting**; no story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - Reveal scanned nearly **2,500** crisis-pregnancy-center sites from a University of Georgia dataset and found at least **294** sharing visitor information with Facebook, sometimes exposing whether a visitor considered abortion, pregnancy testing, or emergency contraception. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients))
  - A reporter used a fresh Facebook identity and clean browser to visit those 294 sites and complete available appointment forms; Meta’s account export retained interactions with **88%** of them, including a recorded appointment request in Owasso, Oklahoma. ([Story methodology](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients#how-we-tracked-the-data))
  - At least **120 centers** in states then expected to ban most or all abortions sent visitor data to Facebook; 11 were in Tennessee. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients))
  - Anti-abortion marketing companies that the reporter never visited gained access to some pixel data and added the clean account to custom audiences capable of targeting the reporter and similar users. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients))
- **Finding type(s)**: sensitive-context inference; closed-platform receipt validation; policy–packet contradiction; **downstream audience activation** (transmitted behavior is made usable by an advertiser or intermediary for targeting).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **University of Georgia crisis-center map — open public/academic**: target universe of roughly 2,500 sites. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients))
  - **Blacklight cohort scan — constructed**: identified the 294 pixel-bearing sites. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients))
  - **Clean-account browsing and controlled forms — constructed**: a new Facebook profile, cleared cache, and repeated February/May tests isolated the investigation’s actions. ([Story method](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients#how-we-tracked-the-data))
  - **Meta Privacy Center export and audience state — closed-platform/user-accessible**: established retention, association with the reporter’s identity, appointment awareness, and custom-audience placement. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2022/06/15/facebook-and-anti-abortion-clinics-are-collecting-highly-sensitive-info-on-would-be-patients))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(d) closed-platform-dependent.** Blacklight discovery and outbound packet capture are public/rebuildable, but the decisive claim that Meta **retained and operationalized** the data depended on Meta’s own account export and audience interfaces. Nearest public substitute: scan the sites and preserve `/tr/` requests/HARs. That proves transmission and payload content but loses proof of retention, profile linkage, custom-audience admission, and later use.

- **Methodology notes**: No standalone Show Your Work page or dedicated Markup/Reveal GitHub release was located. **[Inferred workflow synthesis]** from the article’s explicit clean-account/export description, supplemented by Blacklight’s quoted [method](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector) and [collector code](https://github.com/the-markup/blacklight-collector). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: no**—the platform-side receipt evidence and custom-audience state are not in a released package and remain subject to Meta interface changes.

- **Generalization**: When a platform offers a personal activity export, join a clean test account’s controlled behavior to that export to distinguish “packet left the site” from “platform accepted and associated the packet.” The same loop can audit ad networks, recommendation systems, credit dashboards, loyalty profiles, and data-broker access portals.

([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/04/28/how-california-sent-residents-personal-health-data-to-linkedin); [shared methodology/data repo](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-healthcare-exchange-tracking))

Covered California removed the advertising trackers during reporting and confirmed most trackers/cookies were gone by April 21, 2025, so the historical leak cannot be recaptured live.

- **Generalization**: Use a fast, shallow scan over hundreds of government domains, then spend human testing time on extreme tracker/cookie outliers. The pattern applies to procurement portals, benefits calculators, licensing tools, and outsourced municipal services.

### We Caught 4 More States Sharing Personal Health Data with Big Tech (2025) — a full state-exchange audit showed the California leak was systemic

- **URL**: [We Caught 4 More States Sharing Personal Health Data with Big Tech](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech) (Colin Lecher and Tomas Apodaca, June 17, 2025).
- **Partner/awards**: The Markup/CalMatters integrated-newsroom investigation, represented by the Markup-domain copy under the census attribution rule; no story-specific award located.
- **What they found**:
  - The Markup and CalMatters audited **all 19** independently operated state health exchanges; 18 used some tracker and four—Nevada, Maine, Rhode Island, and Massachusetts—sent sensitive health facts to Google, LinkedIn, or Snapchat. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech))
  - Nevada sent prescription names/doses—including fluoxetine, birth control, and hormone therapies—to LinkedIn and drug names to Snapchat; Maine sent drug/dose and doctor/hospital information to Google; Rhode Island sent drug/dose and doctor names to Google. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech); [released evidence](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-healthcare-exchange-tracking))
  - Massachusetts told LinkedIn whether a visitor selected pregnant, blind, or disabled. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech))
  - The outsourced Maine/Rhode Island tools lived on vendor domains but carried the exchanges’ logos and were linked prominently enough that an ordinary user was unlikely to notice leaving the state domain. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech))
- **Finding type(s)**: latent-surveillance infrastructure; sensitive-context inference; policy–packet contradiction; **branded-vendor boundary blur** (an outsourced service looks official while shifting data handling to another operator/domain); public-service outlier.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Complete state-exchange universe — open public**: 19 independent state exchanges, with Washington, D.C. also examined during the post-California phase. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech))
  - **Blacklight screening — constructed**: 18 of 19 state exchanges had trackers; the scan prioritized above-average sites for manual work. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech))
  - **Controlled fictional household/form data — constructed**: reporters entered made-up health/person data while watching recipient traffic. ([Repo methodology](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-healthcare-exchange-tracking))
  - **Browser network panel, HARs, screenshots, and scans — preserved primary technical evidence**: state-by-state packet archives, with explicit redaction and dummy-data handling. ([Repo methodology/data](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-healthcare-exchange-tracking))
  - **Platform documentation, vendor boundaries, and agency statements — open/constructed**: established identity correlation, terms violations, outsourcing, and official explanations. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable; degraded/partially remediated today.** The full cohort, public shopping flows, Blacklight code, HAR procedure, and BSD-licensed evidence release remain available. But Nevada stopped LinkedIn/Snap transmission and Massachusetts stopped LinkedIn transmission during reporting; a current run will not reproduce all four historical findings.
- **Detection signature**: **seed-to-system expansion + synthetic-household egress join** — the California anomaly generated a hypothesis; all peer state exchanges were Blacklight-scanned, high-count sites were prioritized, and dummy prescription/doctor/disability choices were joined to Google/LinkedIn/Snap requests, revealing a multi-state implementation pattern and an outsourced vendor boundary.
- **Corroboration structure**: census of all state-run exchanges, shared controlled inputs, packet-level evidence for four states, public release of positive and negative Blacklight scans, platform rules, agency confirmations, and remediation during testing.
- **Methodology notes**: No separate Show Your Work page was located. **[Inferred workflow synthesis]** from the [investigation-healthcare-exchange-tracking README](https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-healthcare-exchange-tracking), which documents the method and releases initial scans (including negatives), HARs, and annotated screenshots, plus the quoted [Blacklight methodology](https://themarkup.org/blacklight/2020/09/22/how-we-built-a-real-time-privacy-inspector). **Decisive detector re-runnable from released artifacts alone: yes as a fresh audit and as historical verification; not as a live reproduction of remediated Nevada/Massachusetts transfers.**
- **Official impact**: Nevada disabled tracking pending an audit; Massachusetts stopped LinkedIn sharing; Nevada also stopped LinkedIn and Snapchat transmission during the investigation. ([Story](https://themarkup.org/pixel-hunt/2025/06/17/we-caught-4-more-states-sharing-personal-health-data-with-big-tech))
- **Generalization**: Treat one strong case as a seed, enumerate every peer implementation, and publish both positives and negatives. Preserve branding/domain transitions, because an “official” service can silently hand users into a vendor-controlled data environment.

The dominant acquisition mode is therefore not FOIA, leaks, or interviews. It is **constructed observation**: enumerate a target class, cause a controlled browser action, capture what leaves the browser, resolve the recipient, and preserve the packet.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### A. Sentinel-Value Egress Audit

- **Mechanics**: create a fictional or reporter-controlled identity; enter unique values into every stage of a form; capture all requests; search exact, normalized, rounded, Base64, MD5, SHA-256, and common field-name variants; map each hit to the recipient owner and triggering UI action.
- **Minimum data**: authorized test flow, controlled values, HAR/network log, recipient-domain map, and the vendor’s public event schema where available.
- **Recognition cues in any domain**: a multistep intake, quote, application, cart, or appeal contains sensitive facts while analytics/ad scripts are loaded.
- **Where else**: mortgage prequalification, benefits eligibility, insurance quotes, legal intake, genetic testing, school enrollment, government grants, whistleblower portals.

#### B. Cohort First, Forensics Second

- **Mechanics**: enumerate the complete target class from a registry/directory; run a cheap uniform scanner; rank tracker/cookie/request outliers; perform expensive controlled-form and manual packet analysis only on the outliers plus a negative control sample.
- **Minimum data**: entity/domain cohort, bulk scanner, comparable score, and a manual deep-inspection protocol.
- **Recognition cues**: many peers provide the same service through locally selected vendors; some have dramatically denser third-party stacks.
- **Where else**: state procurement portals, county court systems, nonprofit service providers, hospital networks, public-benefit contractors, campaign sites.

#### C. Context-to-Packet Semantic Join

- **Mechanics**: join page URL/title, search term, selected provider, button label, and form choice to the time-adjacent outbound event. Treat context as data even when the packet lacks a conventional name.
- **Minimum data**: timestamped UI/action log and outbound requests.
- **Recognition cues**: generic pixel events such as `PageView`, `SubscribedButtonClick`, `Lead`, or `CompleteRegistration` occur on pages whose labels disclose the user’s intent.
- **Where else**: “report abuse,” “seek shelter,” “HIV test,” “bankruptcy,” “veteran disability,” “unionize,” or “appeal deportation” actions.

#### D. Vendor-Piggyback Accountability Diff

- **Mechanics**: build the observed request graph, resolve recipients to corporate owners, and compare it with the operator’s intended integration inventory, vendor contract, privacy policy, and consent UI.
- **Minimum data**: HAR/request graph, domain-owner resolution, stated vendor/policy list, and ideally a clean scan after removing or disabling one plug-in.
- **Recognition cues**: free widgets, tag managers, social buttons, embedded forms, branded vendor subdomains, or operators who say “our agency installed it.”
- **Where else**: payment processors, applicant-tracking systems, donor platforms, court e-filing vendors, school ed-tech, property-management portals.

#### E. Closed-Platform Receipt Loop

- **Mechanics**: use a clean account for controlled off-platform actions, then inspect the platform’s activity export, transparency dashboard, custom-audience membership, recommendations, or ad state; join on account, domain, event, and time.
- **Minimum data**: a lawful test account, controlled action log, platform-side export/state, and outbound packet capture.
- **Recognition cues**: the sender can prove a request left, but the recipient claims filtering or refuses to say whether it retained/used the data.
- **Where else**: social ad platforms, loyalty programs, data-broker access portals, credit dashboards, app-store advertising IDs. This pattern is especially vulnerable to UI/API closure and must preserve screenshots/exports contemporaneously.

### 5. What this platform could run today

**Runnable with current holdings:**

- Use the **990 stack** to reproduce the nonprofit target-universe step, then resolve organizations and web vendors through the corporate registries, OpenCorporates, GLEIF, and SEC holdings.
- Use **USASpending/FPDS** to identify government website, enrollment, analytics, and marketing contractors; join observed tracker recipients or vendor domains to awards and corporate parents.
- Use corporate registries, OpenSanctions, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, and local OpenAleph to enrich a request-recipient graph after domains/companies are supplied.
- Use CourtListener/RECAP and government releases to trace lawsuits, consent orders, breach notices, and regulatory impact following a tracker finding.
- Use property, nonprofit, and corporate data to build high-value cohorts: domestic-violence providers, addiction services, clinics, schools, government contractors, or politically sensitive nonprofits.

**Missing for native end-to-end execution:**

- A maintained **headless browser instrumentation layer** equivalent to Blacklight Collector/Query, with JavaScript API hooks, request/response capture, consent-state control, and deterministic browsing profiles.
- A **HAR parser and sensitive-value matcher** that searches exact, normalized, rounded, encoded, and hashed sentinel forms while redacting secrets by default.
- A safe **controlled-account/form runner** supporting synthetic identities, multistep workflows, screenshot/HAR preservation, and explicit authorization boundaries.
- A **tracker-domain/owner knowledge base** with versioned EasyPrivacy/Tracker Radar/platform event schemas and corporate-parent resolution.
- A replacement for the archived **Mozilla Rally consent/panel infrastructure** if authenticated-space discovery is required.
- A recipient-side **platform export/audience audit harness** for the closed-platform receipt loop.

The platform is therefore already strong at **cohort construction, corporate ownership, vendor/procurement linkage, and post-finding legal impact**, but it lacks the browser-level acquisition layer that made The Markup’s privacy work distinctive. The highest-value build is not another web search wrapper; it is a privacy-preserving, reproducible **sentinel-to-HAR detector** feeding the existing entity and contract graph.
