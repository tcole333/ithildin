# ICIJ conflict, repression, and transnational-rights reporting: structured evidence ontology

**Research date:** 2026-07-29  
**Scope:** 12 stories/analytical units from the seven census-identified ICIJ project pages. The sample deliberately spans every seeded project and emphasizes distinct evidence systems rather than taking many stories from one collaboration. All story discovery began at the supplied project hubs: [China Cables](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/), [China Targets](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/), [Solitary Voices](https://www.icij.org/investigations/solitary-voices/), [Interpol's Red Flag](https://www.icij.org/investigations/interpols-red-flag/), [Damascus Dossier](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/), [Making a Killing](https://www.icij.org/investigations/makingkilling/), and [Inside the Kidnapping and Murder of Daniel Pearl](https://www.icij.org/investigations/daniel-pearl/).

**Reading rule:** “Detection signature” is an analytical reconstruction of the decisive comparison or join, not necessarily ICIJ's own name for its method. Method descriptions are labeled **stated** when ICIJ describes them and **[inferred]** when reconstructed from the published evidence trail. “Official impact” is included only where a public authority, legislature, prosecutor, or international body took or announced an action; timing alone is not treated as proof that publication caused it.

## Story-level ontology

### Exposed: China's Operating Manuals for Mass Internment and Arrest by Algorithm (2019) — Secret directives showed that Xinjiang's “training centers” were coercive camps supplied with detainees by a predictive-policing system

- **URL:** [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/exposed-chinas-operating-manuals-for-mass-internment-and-arrest-by-algorithm/); [document collection](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/read-the-china-cables-documents/); [project methodology/about page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/).
- **Partner/awards:** This was an **ICIJ-coordinated** investigation, not a member-outlet story merely republished under the banner. Lead reporting was by Bethany Allen-Ebrahimian; ICIJ says more than 75 journalists at 17 media partners in 14 countries worked on the documents, with The Guardian among the publication partners that sought the Chinese government's response. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/)
- **What they found:**
  - A classified nine-page “telegram” prescribed double-locked doors, round-the-clock video surveillance, secrecy, escape prevention, tightly controlled toilet and family visits, behavioral scoring, forced ideological study, and at least a year of confinement—operational facts inconsistent with the government's description of voluntary vocational schools. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/exposed-chinas-operating-manuals-for-mass-internment-and-arrest-by-algorithm/)
  - Four Integrated Joint Operations Platform (IJOP) bulletins described a data-fusion system that flagged 24,412 “suspicious” people in one week in June 2017; 15,683 were sent to internment and 706 were formally arrested. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/exposed-chinas-operating-manuals-for-mass-internment-and-arrest-by-algorithm/)
  - Other orders called for screening nearly two million Zapya users and examining a list of 4,341 people who had applied for visas or consular documents, including 1,535 people then abroad, exposing both behavior-based and cross-border targeting. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/exposed-chinas-operating-manuals-for-mass-internment-and-arrest-by-algorithm/)
- **Finding type(s):**
  - **coercive-rulebook exposure** — internal operating instructions disclose coercion hidden by an institution's public label.
  - **algorithm-to-custody pipeline** — an automated risk or selector system is tied to a concrete deprivation of liberty rather than described only as surveillance.
  - **euphemism-record contradiction** — authoritative internal records conflict with the institution's benign public account of the same program.
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Leaked state directives — non-public primary records:** a source chain beginning with exiled Uyghurs supplied the nine-page camp telegram and four IJOP bulletins; ICIJ published Chinese originals and English translations. [ICIJ documents](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/read-the-china-cables-documents/)
  - **Document-authentication panel — expert verification:** linguists, Xinjiang specialists, document expert James Mulvenon, researcher Adrian Zenz, and intelligence sources examined language, bureaucratic formatting, distribution markings, chronology, and Zhu Hailun's signature. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/)
  - **Former-detainee and diaspora interviews — firsthand corroboration:** the collaboration interviewed more than 40 Uyghurs in roughly 10 countries and compared reported camp routines and police phone searches with the directives. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/)
  - **Prior open reporting and official responses — contextual checks:** human-rights research on IJOP, satellite-based camp reporting, and China's own evolving descriptions supplied external anchors but not the internal orders or system-generated detention counts. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/exposed-chinas-operating-manuals-for-mass-internment-and-arrest-by-algorithm/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED): (c) leak-dependent.** The decisive link from secret rules and IJOP flags to confinement is inert without the non-public directives. The nearest public substitute is a bundle of satellite imagery, construction/procurement records, official notices, survivor testimony, and published rights reports. It can establish camp expansion and recurring treatment, but it loses the authoritative operating rules, internal one-week counts, command chain, and explicit algorithm-to-internment workflow.
- **Detection signature:** **Rulebook-to-practice conformance match** — the camp telegram and IJOP bulletins were **compared to** former-detainee accounts, public descriptions, and observed camp features **on keys** facility routine, prohibited conduct, decision rule, date, and responsible authority, **revealing** that “vocational” sites were secured detention facilities fed by algorithmic selection.
- **Corroboration structure:** Internal directive → independent technical/document authentication → witness accounts matching prescribed routines → contemporary public reporting and official denials. The IJOP counts and commands are direct-document findings; conclusions about systemwide implementation are a synthesis across those layers. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/)
- **Methodology notes:** **Stated.** ICIJ describes secure cross-border collaboration, translation, document-forensics checks, expert review, interviews, and a structured effort to compare the documents with already known facts. The precise conformance join above is **[inferred]** from that stated workflow. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/)
- **Official impact:** A 2026 peer-reviewed study reported by ICIJ concluded that the project and parallel coverage helped force a public policy shift; days after publication, Xinjiang officials claimed all “trainees” had “graduated,” and the study associated the scrutiny with partial dismantling of the camp system. This is a retrospective research finding about contribution, not proof that ICIJ alone caused the change. [ICIJ impact report](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/beijings-backtrack-on-xinjiang-detention-camps-spurred-by-icij-investigation-research-finds/)
- **Generalization:** [Inferred] The detector applies wherever a government or company publishes a therapeutic, educational, safety, or compliance label while internal SOPs specify restraint and punishment. Seek internal rule verbs (“prevent,” “screen,” “transfer,” “score”), then test whether witness accounts and facility traces instantiate the same fields.

### How China Targets Uighurs “One by One” for Using a Mobile App (2019) — An IJOP bulletin made ordinary religious file-sharing a selector for individual investigation and possible internment

- **URL:** [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/); [China Cables methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/).
- **Partner/awards:** This is an **ICIJ-authored story within the ICIJ-coordinated China Cables project**, by Scilla Alecci. ICIJ shared the leak with 17 partners; Le Monde contributed a documented individual case, while The Guardian and ABC supplied related reporting and response-seeking cited in the story. Those are contributions, not evidence that the item originated as a member-outlet story. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
- **What they found:**
  - Since at least July 2016, authorities had treated use of Zapya—an offline file-sharing app promoted for sharing Quranic material—as grounds for further investigation; the leaked instruction called for users to be checked “one by one” and sent to “concentrated training” when suspicion could not be eliminated. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
  - A June 2017 bulletin counted more than 1.8 million Uyghur Zapya users in Xinjiang, including nearly 4,000 people it termed “unauthorized imams.” The documents did **not** indicate that developer DewMobile cooperated with the authorities. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
  - Former detainee Zumrat Dawut said police searched phones for Zapya and religious words, while Le Monde documented a computer scientist detained twice for 30 days after downloading the app; those cases matched the bulletin's selection logic. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
- **Finding type(s):**
  - **identity-proxy targeting** — a mundane behavior or technology is used as a proxy for a protected religious, ethnic, or political identity.
  - **selector-scale repression** — a broad digital selector turns a population-scale dataset into person-by-person coercive review.
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Leaked IJOP bulletin — non-public primary record:** the June 2017 party document supplied the user denominator, targeted category, decision rule, and instruction to review each person. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
  - **Detainee/refugee testimony — firsthand corroboration:** interviewees described police seizures and phone inspections for Zapya and religious content. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
  - **Contemporaneous case reporting — external corroboration:** later 2017 reports, a Le Monde case, and another Chinese government notice associating Zapya with religious offenses showed implementation beyond the single bulletin. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
  - **Technical and human-rights research — system context:** Human Rights Watch's prior reconstruction of IJOP data inputs explained how phone, identity, checkpoint, camera, Wi-Fi, and delivery data could be fused; it did not itself expose this Zapya order. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED): (c) leak-dependent.** The internal instruction is what converts anecdotal phone searches into an official selector-and-disposition system. A public substitute would combine app-store data, survivor accounts, criminal judgments, police notices, procurement records for mobile forensics, and rights-group reporting. That substitute loses the full user denominator, the internal “one by one” order, the nearly 4,000-imam subcategory, and the rule sending unresolved cases to internment.
- **Detection signature:** **Selector-to-person funnel reconstruction** — the IJOP bulletin was **compared to** individual detention accounts and later implementation reports **on keys** app name, protected-content indicator, date, locality, and custody outcome, **revealing** that use of Zapya functioned as a population-scale religious proxy feeding individual detention decisions.
- **Corroboration structure:** Internal selector and population count → witness description of the same phone search → named individual detention example → prior independent model of IJOP's data ingestion. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/how-china-targets-uighurs-one-by-one-for-using-a-mobile-app/)
- **Methodology notes:** **Stated at project level.** The China Cables methodology describes authentication, translation, expert review, and more than 40 Uyghur interviews. The app-specific selector funnel is **[inferred]** from the bulletin-to-case structure of this story. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/)
- **Official impact:** No app-specific official change is documented. Project-wide, a later study credited the broader China Cables reporting with helping force Beijing to change its public position and partly dismantle the mass-camp system. [ICIJ impact report](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/beijings-backtrack-on-xinjiang-detention-camps-spurred-by-icij-investigation-research-finds/)
- **Generalization:** [Inferred] A generic detector identifies a protected-trait proxy in a rule or model, estimates the exposed population, and traces the funnel from flag → review → sanction. Comparable proxies include language, neighborhood, device software, travel, donations, or participation in otherwise lawful associations.

### Inside China's Machinery of Repression—and How It Crushes Dissent Around the World (2025) — A standardized case matrix showed the same state playbook recurring against 105 critics in 23 countries

- **URL:** [ICIJ overview](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/china-transnational-repression-dissent-around-world/); [methods/data-footprints story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/); [project about page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/about-china-targets-investigation/).
- **Partner/awards:** **ICIJ led** the 10-month collaboration with 42 media partners. The overview was an ICIJ network product led by Scilla Alecci; country reporting came from, among others, CBC News, The Irish Times, paper trail media/ZDF/Der Spiegel, Yle, Knack, Göteborgs-Posten, Radio France/Le Monde, ABC, Stuff, and The Guardian. [ICIJ about page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/about-china-targets-investigation/)
- **What they found:**
  - Interviews with 105 targeted people in 23 countries showed a coordinated repertoire: roughly half said relatives in China were intimidated or interrogated, 60 reported physical or electronic surveillance, 22 described threats or physical assaults, and 19 reported suspicious emails or hacking attempts. [ICIJ overview](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/china-transnational-repression-dissent-around-world/)
  - Internal Chinese security documents spanning 2001–2020 prescribed pressure on relatives, border controls, surveillance, co-optation, and coerced returns; the described tactics recurred in targets' accounts rather than appearing as unrelated local incidents. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
  - Secret recordings and message traffic connected the institutional playbook to actors: ICIJ says it reviewed communications involving 11 Chinese security officers and nine overseas targets, as well as videos or audio of interrogations. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
- **Finding type(s):**
  - **repression-playbook replication** — the same documented tactic sequence recurs across nominally separate cases and jurisdictions.
  - **family-hostage coercion** — pressure on relatives inside the origin country is used to control a person abroad.
  - **host-state protection gap** — target accounts and official handling show a repeated mismatch between cross-border threat and domestic protective capacity.
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Standardized victim cohort — original interviews:** reporters interviewed 105 people and coded tactics into a shared spreadsheet developed from initial interviews and confidential documents. Interviewees' supporting records were checked for consistency. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
  - **Internal police/security doctrine — confidential primary records:** textbooks, training presentations, security guidelines, and other government material supplied the expected tactic set and command logic. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
  - **Recorded operations — private digital evidence:** secretly recorded interrogations, calls, texts, emails, images, and videos linked tactics to identified security officers and targets. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
  - **Public legal and policy records — verification/context:** court records, police complaints, parliamentary material, rights reports, and host-country investigations anchored individual episodes and institutional response. [ICIJ overview](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/china-transnational-repression-dissent-around-world/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED): (c) leak-dependent.** Public sources can recover many individual episodes, but the decisive finding—a documented official playbook replicated across a standardized 105-person cohort—depends on confidential government material, private victim evidence, and covert recordings. The nearest public substitute is a normalized corpus of court filings, police reports, parliamentary inquiries, cyber-threat advisories, NGO case reports, and public testimony. It loses the denominator, underreported incidents, direct officer-to-target communications, and the strong playbook-to-case comparison.
- **Detection signature:** **Playbook-to-case matrix** — internal Chinese tactic guides were **compared to** a coded cohort of 105 target histories **on keys** tactic, actor role, target affiliation, relative relationship, jurisdiction, and event sequence, **revealing** a systematic cross-border program rather than a collection of idiosyncratic disputes.
- **Corroboration structure:** Internal doctrine defines expected behavior; private communications observe officers performing it; target documentation and interviews show repetition; public legal/police records anchor a subset independently. The aggregation claim is a cross-case synthesis, not a claim that every allegation received identical documentary support. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
- **Methodology notes:** **Stated.** ICIJ explains how reporters built the spreadsheet, defined tactics iteratively, reviewed documentation for consistency, and compared internal instructions with interviews and recorded interactions. The “playbook-to-case matrix” label is **[inferred]**, but its fields come directly from that account. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
- **Official impact:** Canadian lawmakers explicitly invoked the ICIJ/CBC reporting while urging implementation of the Countering Foreign Interference Act and stronger action against transnational repression. The law predated publication, so the documented impact is renewed implementation pressure, not passage of the statute. [ICIJ impact story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/canadian-lawmakers-urge-action-against-transnational-repression-in-wake-of-china-targets-probe/)
- **Generalization:** [Inferred] The method generalizes to any dispersed abuse program: derive a tactic vocabulary from doctrine and early cases, code a cohort without forcing every case into every field, and test whether tactic combinations and sequences recur above a plausible background rate.

### Case Involving Alibaba's Jack Ma Shows How China Weaponizes Interpol (2025) — A Red Notice's stated charge diverged from recorded demands that its subject help an unrelated corruption case

- **URL:** [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/); [China Targets methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/).
- **Partner/awards:** The article is an **ICIJ-authored China Targets story** by Scilla Alecci with network reporting. Radio France and Le Monde were lead outlets for French court material; paper trail media/ZDF/Der Spiegel, ABC, Göteborgs-Posten, Oštro, Stuff, and others contributed cases or country reporting. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
- **What they found:**
  - A Singapore-based businessman called “H.” was arrested in France on a Chinese Red Notice, but recorded calls showed officials pressing him to provide information in the unrelated case of former public-security official Sun Lijun and offering relief if he returned or cooperated. Jack Ma acted as an intermediary in some contacts. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
  - The pressure extended beyond the notice: authorities froze assets and detained H.'s sister, according to records and recordings reviewed by the collaboration. A French appeals court rejected extradition in July 2021, and Interpol's Commission for the Control of Files deleted the notice in January 2022. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
  - ICIJ interviewed eight targets and reviewed nearly 50 post-2016 cases. Public Red Notices rose from 10,718 in 2014 to 12,260 in 2023, while requests to correct or delete data grew by more than 350% over a decade; the CCF deleted data in nearly 300 cases in 2022 for rule violations. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
- **Finding type(s):**
  - **international-warrant weaponization** — a legitimate cross-border police channel is used to impose political or bargaining pressure.
  - **accusation-purpose mismatch** — the stated offense and the operational demand made of the target concern different matters.
  - **remedy-capacity gap** — review mechanisms correct some abusive notices but cannot prevent the arrest, travel restriction, or reputational harm that precedes review.
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Extradition and court files — legal records:** French proceedings supplied the formal allegations, chronology, evidence offered by China, and judicial disposition. Radio France and Le Monde led this reporting. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
  - **Recorded calls/messages — private direct evidence:** communications captured what Chinese officers and intermediaries actually wanted from H., creating the critical mismatch with the Red Notice's stated case. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
  - **Confidential CCF decisions and target interviews — non-public case records:** these expanded the review beyond H. and showed how subjects challenged notices after the 2016 screening reforms. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
  - **Interpol annual statistics and public reports — open trend data:** notice volumes, complaint/correction counts, and published rules established system scale and remedial load. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED): (b) re-anchoring.** Confidential CCF material, private recordings, and access to H. were needed to discover and explain the exemplar. The **public and reusable verification half** is the join among court/extradition files, Interpol rules and annual reports, public notice data, asylum decisions, and judicial outcomes. That open half can flag accusation-purpose and rights-risk anomalies, but it cannot reproduce the recorded quid pro quo or observe notices never made public.
- **Detection signature:** **Accusation-purpose mismatch reconstruction** — the Red Notice and extradition allegation were **compared to** recorded pressure calls and the Sun Lijun case **on keys** subject, requested act, alleged offense, intermediary, and date, **revealing** that the notice was being used to compel cooperation in a different investigation.
- **Corroboration structure:** Formal notice/extradition record → contemporaneous recorded demands → asset/family pressure records → independent French judicial rejection and CCF deletion → broader complaint/removal trend. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
- **Methodology notes:** **Stated.** ICIJ says it reviewed human-rights reports, public and confidential CCF decisions, court and extradition files, Interpol reports, and interviews, with special attention to cases after the 2016 reforms. The mismatch signature is **[inferred]** from the H. chronology. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
- **Official impact:** The French court's 2021 refusal and CCF's 2022 deletion are official outcomes in the reconstructed case, but both predate the 2025 story and therefore are **not publication impact**. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/)
- **Generalization:** [Inferred] For any sanctions, watchlist, warrant, or deplatforming regime, compare the formal predicate to communications specifying the relief sought. A different demanded act, unrelated third-party case, or pressure on relatives is a high-value misuse signal.

### At the UN, China Is Deploying a Growing Army of Puppet Organizations to Monitor and Intimidate Human Rights Activists (2025) — Open records exposed state-linked groups occupying the privileges and speaking space reserved for independent civil society

- **URL:** [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/united-nations-ngo-gongo-intimidate-human-rights/); [China Targets methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/).
- **Partner/awards:** This is an **ICIJ-authored network story** by Tamsin Lee-Smith and Jelena Cosic within the ICIJ-led China Targets collaboration. Tamedia, paper trail media/ZDF/Der Spiegel, Stuff, The Washington Post, Politiken, Göteborgs-Posten, Radio Free Asia, ABC, profil, SVT, and Le Monde were among contributing outlets; the classification analysis is presented as ICIJ's. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/united-nations-ngo-gongo-intimidate-human-rights/)
- **What they found:**
  - Of 106 organizations from mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan holding U.N. consultative status, ICIJ classified 59 as closely connected to the Chinese government or Communist Party. At least 46 had senior staff who also held government or party roles, 10 received more than half their funding from the state, and 53 publicly pledged party loyalty. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/united-nations-ngo-gongo-intimidate-human-rights/)
  - Chinese NGO participation surged more than twentyfold from 2018 through 2024; one organization appeared more than 300 times over the seven-year speaker-list dataset, and none of the reviewed Chinese NGO statements fell into ICIJ's “criticizing China” category. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
  - Fifteen activists and lawyers described surveillance or harassment in Geneva. In a March 2024 episode, representatives linked to Chinese organizations appeared at a private meeting site, after which activists reported being photographed; the U.N. raised the episode with China but did not include it in its annual reprisals report because it said the evidence did not meet the reporting threshold. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/united-nations-ngo-gongo-intimidate-human-rights/)
- **Finding type(s):**
  - **state-proxy civil society** — an organization formally presented as nongovernmental has leadership, funding, governance, or speech ties showing material state direction.
  - **speaking-slot capture** — nominally independent entities accumulate scarce participation opportunities and use them to amplify a sponsor's position.
  - **accreditation-integrity gap** — formal eligibility rules do not test or surface the operational dependency relevant to the privilege granted.
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **U.N. accreditation and speaker data — open administrative records:** the ECOSOC NGO database defined the universe, while Human Rights Council speaker lists and an International Service for Human Rights dataset measured participation over time. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
  - **Organizational records — open web and filed documents:** accreditation applications, charters, financial statements, annual reports, organization websites, and archived pages supplied funding, governance, and mission evidence. [ICIJ methods](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/inside-china-targets-the-data-footprints-of-chinas-transnational-repression/)
  - **Person-role records — open official sources:** CCP/government biographies, agency and university pages, Chinese-language searches, and public profiles tied NGO officers to state or party positions. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/united-nations-ngo-gongo-intimidate-human-rights/)

[ICIJ about page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/about-damascus-dossier-syria-investigation/)

[ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/inside-the-damascus-dossier-from-leaked-images-to-verified-data/)

[ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/inside-the-damascus-dossier-from-leaked-images-to-verified-data/)

[ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/inside-the-damascus-dossier-from-leaked-images-to-verified-data/)
  - **Forensic/pathology experts, survivors, families, and rights groups — independent corroboration:** specialists interpreted visible signs and former detainees or relatives checked identities and institutional context. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/inside-the-damascus-dossier-from-leaked-images-to-verified-data/)
- **Methodology notes:** **Stated in unusual detail.** ICIJ specifies corpus size, preservation and organization, sample design, reviewer count, coding protocol, disagreement resolution, OCR, and entity-resolution process. The compact signature name is **[inferred]**. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/inside-the-damascus-dossier-from-leaked-images-to-verified-data/)
- **Official impact:** ICIJ/NDR shared name lists with the U.N. International, Impartial and Independent Mechanism for Syria and Syrian documentation groups; German and Swedish prosecutors said the material could support existing investigations. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/syria-assad-mass-murder-photos-evidence/)

- **URL:** [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/makingkilling/privatizing-combat-new-world-order/); [project/about page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/makingkilling/about-project/).

  - **revolving-door war commerce** — personnel circulate between government decision-making and firms selling related services.

[ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/makingkilling/privatizing-combat-new-world-order/)

[ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/makingkilling/privatizing-combat-new-world-order/)
- **Generalization:** [Inferred] Start with a complete-enough vendor universe, normalize parents and successor entities, join awards to places and functions, and then enrich people with prior public roles. The same method applies to border security, intelligence contractors, prisons, disaster response, cyber operations, and public-health outsourcing.

[ICIJ ebook page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/daniel-pearl/ebook-truth-left-behind/)
- **What they found:**
  - The project identified 27 men involved across the kidnapping and murder, from at least three militant groups. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)

[Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)
  - Omar Sheikh and the three other men convicted in Pakistan participated in the kidnapping but were not present at the killing; the project concluded that Pakistani prosecutors knowingly relied on perjured testimony and did not charge the men alleged to have carried out the murder. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/daniel-pearl/truth-left-behind-inside-kidnapping-and-murder-daniel-pearl/)
- **Finding type(s):**
  - **conviction-perpetrator divergence** — the people convicted do not match the people assigned the decisive acts by the fuller evidence graph.
  - **unprosecuted-conspiracy graph** — a reconstructed network identifies operational participants absent from the charging and conviction record.
  - **investigative-silo failure** — separate agencies possess complementary facts but do not combine them into the case presented in court.
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Pakistani court and police records — legal primary records:** about 2,400 pages supplied the official chronology, testimony, charges, defendants, and contradictions. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)
  - **Hundreds of interviews in five countries — original reporting:** investigators, officials, militants' associates, witnesses, and family/context sources supplied identities, aliases, roles, and alternative sequences. [ICIJ ebook page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/daniel-pearl/ebook-truth-left-behind/)
  - **FBI reports, State Department cables, and Pearl's notes/emails — government and victim records:** these connected the journalist's reporting trail, U.S. intelligence, and the Pakistani case. Some were non-public or heavily redacted. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)
  - **Murder video and biometric comparison — visual forensic evidence:** analysts compared the perpetrator's hand to Khalid Sheikh Mohammed reference imagery. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)
  - **FOIA litigation and releases — public-record acquisition:** litigation against eight U.S. agencies generated thousands of pages, many heavily redacted or consisting of prior media, and documented the limits of the public record. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)
  - **Palantir and i2 relationship analysis — analytical tooling:** the team used link-analysis software and a shared chronology/wiki to reconcile people, groups, aliases, calls, and events. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED): (c) leak-dependent.** The core attribution of the killing and the complete 27-person role graph depend on non-public FBI/police material, confidential interviews, and access to reference imagery/analysis. The nearest public substitute is the Pakistani judgments and trial record, public military-commission material and confessions, FOIA releases, archived reporting, and the public murder video. It loses the hand-comparison foundation, confidential source reconciliation, and many links needed to distinguish kidnapping, custody, killing, and cover-up roles.
- **Detection signature:** **Role-event graph reconciliation** — the official trial narrative was **joined to** interview, police, FBI, communication, and video evidence **on keys** person/alias, militant group, phone/contact, location, act, and time, **revealing** a 27-person conspiracy in which the convicted kidnappers and alleged killer occupied different roles.

A specific edge required multiple sources where possible; the published status table openly distinguished convicted, dead, detained/missing, and free participants. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)

[Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)

The FOIA lawsuit did compel document production during reporting; that is an acquisition outcome, not a criminal-justice remedy. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)

**Ontology-level observation:** The strongest work rarely treats “documents” as one evidence class. It separates (1) a rule or representation, (2) an event ledger or corpus, (3) actor/entity resolution, and (4) outcome or witness evidence. That separation makes the analytic move auditable and shows which claims survive if a leak disappears.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### Pattern candidate A — Mandate–event conformance audit

- **Mechanics:** Extract a machine-readable rule or institutional promise; construct the corresponding event ledger; derive tests for prohibited duration, protected/vulnerable status, predicate, disposition, reviewer, and exception; report both breach counts and the facilities/actors producing the tail.
- **Minimum data:** A rule/manual/charter with explicit conditions; events with subject, actor, dates, reason, outcome, and organizational unit; enough coverage to define the denominator.
- **Recognition cues in any domain:** Benign public label; exception fields used more often than the base rule; vulnerability recorded as an adverse-action reason; long-duration tail; high inter-unit variation; repeated “temporary” measures.
- **Source exemplars:** China Cables' manual-to-camp practice, Solitary Voices' ICE-log audit, and the U.N. NGO independence test. [China Cables methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/about-the-china-cables-investigation/) [Solitary Voices story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/solitary-voices/thousands-of-immigrants-suffer-in-us-solitary-confinement/) [U.N. GONGO story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/united-nations-ngo-gongo-intimidate-human-rights/)

#### Pattern candidate B — Predicate–purpose mismatch detector

- **Mechanics:** Represent the formal basis for an action separately from the behavior demanded in communications and the outcome actually sought; compare offense, target, third party, requested act, and relief offered.
- **Minimum data:** A formal notice/charge/sanction/debarment; a court, appeal, complaint, or communication record revealing operational demands; a shared subject and timeline.
- **Recognition cues in any domain:** Relief conditioned on unrelated cooperation; relatives or assets pressured outside the alleged offense; asylum/acquittal conflicts; notice removed without merits resolution; accusation text describes protected political conduct.
- **Source exemplars:** H.'s Red Notice and the 2011 asylum/notice contradictions. [China Targets/Interpol story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/interpol-red-notice-police-warrant-jack-ma/) [Interpol's Red Flag story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/interpols-red-flag/interpols-red-notices-used-some-pursue-political-dissenters-opponents/)

#### Pattern candidate C — Proxy-entity privilege capture

- **Mechanics:** Start from entities receiving a privilege—accreditation, grant, contract, license, speaking slot, tax status—then resolve funders, controllers, officers, bylaws, and output behavior. Measure how much of the scarce privilege a linked bloc occupies.
- **Minimum data:** Privileged-entity roster; person-role and funder/controller records; dated utilization or output records; an independence or eligibility rule.
- **Recognition cues in any domain:** Shared officials across nominally independent bodies; funder approval of leaders or large donations; uniform messaging; sudden cohort growth after a political event; repeated occupation of limited agenda slots.
- **Source exemplars:** Chinese GONGOs at the U.N., Shorouk as a nominally private contractor, and private military firms carrying state functions. [U.N. GONGO story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-targets/united-nations-ngo-gongo-intimidate-human-rights/) [Shorouk story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/damascus-dossier/assad-intelligence-security-united-nations-aid/) [PMC story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/makingkilling/privatizing-combat-new-world-order/)

#### Pattern candidate D — Asset-continuity evasion graph

- **Mechanics:** Treat legal entities as mutable labels and operational assets/identifiers as continuity anchors; construct time-aware ownership/operator edges, then overlay sanctions, embargoes, conflicts, contract awards, and transfers.
- **Minimum data:** Corporate histories; stable or semi-stable asset IDs; controller/officer/address/phone fields; dated external-risk events.
- **Recognition cues in any domain:** Reflagging or renaming near sanctions; same tail number/ship/domain/property/account across successor companies; recycled phones and addresses; transfers among associates at nominal value; legitimate business coexisting with prohibited routes.
- **Source exemplar:** Victor Bout's aircraft-company network. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/makingkilling/merchant-death/)

#### Pattern candidate E — Adjudicated-story versus evidence-graph reconciliation

- **Mechanics:** Parse the official judgment or case narrative into person–role–event assertions; build an independent graph from records, communications, and interviews; diff the people, acts, and sequence, preserving uncertainty and source provenance per edge.
- **Minimum data:** Charging/judgment record; at least one independent documentary corpus; identity/alias resolution; an event chronology.
- **Recognition cues in any domain:** Convicted actors absent at the decisive event; operational actors never charged; perjured or recanted testimony; agencies holding disconnected fragments; official narrative collapsing distinct phases into one conspiracy role.
- **Source exemplar:** The Pearl Project's 27-person kidnapping/murder graph. [Pearl Project report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/documents/pdfs/The_Pearl_Project.pdf)

### 5. What this platform can run now—and what is missing

#### Runnable now with current holdings

- **Proxy-entity privilege and payment capture:** OpenCorporates, 20+ corporate registries including UK Companies House, GLEIF, OpenSanctions, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, local OpenAleph, 990 data, and USASpending/FPDS can join privileged recipients to officers, parents, funders, offshore vehicles, sanctions, grants, and contracts. This directly supports Pattern C and the public half of the Shorouk detector.
- **Asset-continuity evasion:** The FAA registry plus corporate registries, OpenSanctions, property/deed tools, OpenAleph, and ICIJ Offshore Leaks can resolve U.S.-registered aircraft, companies, controllers, and property. This supports a domestic/US-touchpoint version of Pattern D.
- **Adjudicated-story reconciliation:** CourtListener/RECAP and OpenAleph can extract defendants, charges, findings, exhibits, aliases, dates, and organizations, then compare judgments with other public document corpora. This supports Pattern E where U.S. federal dockets or imported records are sufficiently complete.
- **Contractor census and revolving-door screening:** USASpending/FPDS joined to registries, GLEIF, OpenCorporates, OpenSanctions, 990s, USPTO, and property records can detect recipient concentration, successor entities, related nonprofits, patents, and post-office employment. This reproduces much of the Making a Killing procurement logic with current data.
- **Sanction/list contradiction screens:** OpenSanctions plus CourtListener/RECAP can identify list entries whose subjects also have acquittals, dismissals, asylum-related litigation, or inconsistent entity resolution. This is a partial public analog of the Interpol detectors; public Interpol notices themselves would still need a preserved crawl.
- **Crypto-enabled network extensions:** Etherscan and Solscan can add wallet flows to controller/entity graphs where public identifiers connect an actor or contractor to an address. This is not present in the reviewed ICIJ stories but fits the same controller-to-flow join without changing its ontology.

#### Missing or materially incomplete

- **Leak/insider intake:** Nothing in the listed holdings substitutes for secret camp directives, security-service ownership letters, private officer communications, or the Damascus image archive. These are the decisive inputs in the five class-(c) entries.
- **Administrative microdata/FOIA pipeline:** The platform lacks a general collection of person- or event-level agency logs comparable to ICE's solitary-confinement records. A repeatable FOIA-request, release-preservation, schema-normalization, and limitation-tracking workflow is needed for Pattern A.
- **Historical Interpol and CCF corpus:** OpenSanctions is not a substitute for dated complete snapshots of public Red Notices, non-public diffusions, or confidential CCF decisions. A lawful historical crawl/Wayback corpus plus asylum/court joins would recover the open portion; diffusions and many review decisions will remain unavailable.
- **International aviation and controlled-cargo depth:** FAA data covers only part of an international fleet history. The Bout detector needs global aircraft-register history, operator/lessor transitions, flight and cargo records, export licenses, end-user certificates, and U.N. embargo-panel extraction.
- **Multilingual organization and foreign-court coverage:** The GONGO and China Targets methods depended on Chinese-language official pages and numerous non-U.S. legal systems. OpenAleph can hold acquired documents, but the listed holdings do not themselves provide a comprehensive multilingual NGO, foreign-court, or police-complaint corpus.
- **Forensic image/audio workflow:** Damascus and Pearl required native-file preservation, metadata/path retention, OCR, statistically valid sampling, double-blind or double-review coding, adjudication, biometric/forensic expert review, and victim-safe access controls. The current listed source holdings do not establish that workflow.
- **Cohort intake with protected provenance:** The 105-person China Targets matrix depended on interviews and private supporting records. A generic detector would need a secure, consent-aware case schema that stores tactic, actor, date, relative relationship, evidence type, confidence, and publicability separately; public sources alone will systematically undercount repression.

**Bottom line:** With its current registries, sanctions, contract, court, asset, nonprofit, offshore, and blockchain holdings, the platform is strongest on **controller/entity joins, public-money flows, asset continuity, and adjudicated-record diffs**. It can run four sampled patterns end to end and the public verification half of three more. Its largest gaps are not graph analytics but primary-input acquisition: administrative event ledgers, historical international-alert data, multilingual foreign records, and securely handled leaked or forensic corpora.
