**Free-form tagging rule.** All finding-type and detection-signature labels below are coined from this sample, not imported from a prior taxonomy. Each finding-type tag is defined at first use. Methodology is marked **stated** when ICIJ describes the acquisition or analysis itself; otherwise it is marked **[inferred]** and the inference basis is given.

**Input-dependency rule.** The required classes describe whether another investigator could reproduce the decisive analytic result, not whether every interview, photograph, or colorful detail could be repeated:

- **(a) open-record-runnable** — decisive inputs remain obtainable from public records or openly accessible archives.
- **(b) re-anchoring** — a leak/insider was needed to discover the target or hidden event, but verification can be rerun against specified public records.
- **(c) leak-dependent** — the decisive proposition is inert without non-public material.
- **(d) closed-platform-dependent** — the decisive proposition requires a restricted commercial or technical platform.

## Big Tobacco Smuggling

### Exposed: How billions of cigarettes end up on black markets (2000) — BAT’s own planning records treated contraband as a managed market channel

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ/Center for Public Integrity original, by Maud Beelman, Duncan Campbell, María Teresa Ronderos, Erik J. Schelzig, and Kathryn Wallace. No separate lead partner or story-specific award is identified on the seeded ICIJ pages. [Byline and provenance](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
- **What they found**:
  - ICIJ reviewed more than **11,000 pages** from BAT and subsidiaries over six months, selected from a public litigation depository of roughly **8 million pages**. The reviewed files, mostly from 1990–1995, showed executives controlled contraband-channel volumes, brands, marketing, launch timing, and prices even though BAT publicly disclaimed control after sale to distributors. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
  - BAT’s internal vocabulary separated legal **“Duty Paid”** trade from “DNP,” “transit,” “GT,” “parallel market,” and “second channel.” The terms were not isolated euphemisms: they appeared in the same memos, charts, and sales plans as legal-market categories, allowing ICIJ to infer their operational meaning from repeated contrasts. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
  - A restricted 1994 committee note targeted annual Latin American sales of **50 billion cigarettes, including duty-not-paid sales**; planning instructions told managers to cover brand variants in both paid and unpaid markets. A separate “umbrella” strategy used a small legal import stream to make advertising possible while the larger market was supplied through the unpaid channel. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
  - The records connected strategy to particular executives and routes: BAT managers discussed Brazilian-made cigarettes entering Argentina through DNP channels, Venezuelan cigarettes exported to Aruba and re-entered as “transit,” and the movement of sensitive DNP files from Bogotá to Caracas with a safe and shredder. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
- **Finding type(s)**: `coded-channel-governance` (a company uses stable euphemisms to plan, measure, and govern a channel it publicly treats as external); `illicit-channel-as-market-strategy` (unlawful or untaxed distribution is integrated into ordinary brand, price, and market-share planning rather than treated as leakage).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Litigation-released internal corporate records — public archive**: BAT/Brown & Williamson memos, country files, executive correspondence, committee notes, sales graphs, and market plans, made public after U.S. tobacco litigation and the 1998 state settlement; ICIJ selected by region/subject and sometimes reviewed complete person or country files. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
  - **Court and enforcement records — public legal records**: Canadian and U.S. RICO litigation, smuggling convictions, and the Hong Kong bribery conviction supplied adjudicated anchors for the decoded vocabulary and route descriptions. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
  - **Corporate financial disclosures — public filings**: BAT and Philip Morris profit and regional-sales figures established the commercial stakes. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
  - **Insider/expert interviews — reporter-obtained**: a convicted RJR manager and a former state antitrust official explained how the industry used DNP; Colombian officials and lawyers described lost-tax litigation; companies received detailed questions and supplied denials. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive files were once internal, but by the investigation they were public through the litigation document depository and remain available through the UCSF Industry Documents Library. The court records, annual reports, and government cases used to validate meaning are also public. A modern investigator can rerun the semantic and operational joins without a new leak; interviews improve interpretation but are not the sole anchor. [Acquisition description](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/) | [Current UCSF tobacco archive](https://www.industrydocuments.ucsf.edu/tobacco/)
- **Detection signature**: **`corporate-dialect-to-operations join`** — coded terms in internal correspondence joined to route, volume, price, advertising, and launch fields on **country + brand + date + channel** revealed that “DNP/GT/transit” was not loose slang but a centrally managed contraband sales ledger.
- **Corroboration structure**: The archive establishes intent and operational control; criminal/civil cases independently establish that the named routes and duty-not-paid practices had real legal consequences; insider interpretation tests the codebook; company denials are preserved. Thousands of documents from one depository are **documentary depth, not independent corroboration**—the court records and admissions provide the independent legs. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/)
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in the story and project page.** ICIJ gives corpus size, review duration, selection logic, date coverage, and the use of complete country/person files. The project page adds that a six-country team combined thousands of internal documents with insider interviews. [Story methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/exposed-how-billions-cigarettes-end-black-markets/) | [Project methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/about-project-big-tobacco-smuggling/)
- **Official impact**: ICIJ says this series helped prompt lawsuits and government inquiries and led to promises of a global crackdown on illicit tobacco trade; it does not attribute a discrete action solely to this story. [ICIJ project impact statement](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/about-project-big-tobacco-smuggling/)
- **Generalization**: Search internal or litigation-released corpora for stable unexplained abbreviations that appear beside ordinary KPIs. Build a contextual codebook from oppositions (“paid” versus coded category), then join each code to shipments, pricing, customer, geography, and executive approval. The pattern applies to sanctions evasion (“special market”), off-book labor (“contractor optimization”), corrupt sales (“facilitation”), unsafe-product channels (“secondary distribution”), and tax structures (“nonstandard flows”).

### Global reach of tobacco company’s involvement in cigarette smuggling exposed in company papers (2000) — Asian “general trade” plans reconstructed a multinational route portfolio

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
- **Partner/awards**: By ICIJ, produced through the Center for Public Integrity. No member outlet or story-specific award is credited on the seeded pages. [ICIJ byline](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
- **What they found**:
  - The same 11,000-document review showed BAT’s Asian records divided sales into legal and **“GT” (general trade)** channels. Country plans treated GT as a measurable substitute when legal markets fell or borders closed. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
  - BAT identified regional hubs—Singapura United Trading Ltd. (SUTL), “Unit 1,” Dubai, and Hong Kong—and budgeted **£3.8 million** to grow GT markets. A 1993 study assigned SUTL four duty-paid, 20 duty-free, and 11 GT markets; BAT personnel oversaw or were seconded into the distributor. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
  - Evidence from Hong Kong indicated more than **£490 million ($810 million)** in BAT cigarettes were smuggled into China during the 1990s. A BAT manager was convicted in a related bribery case, while the company itself was not charged. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
  - The files showed route adaptation: after Vietnam’s import ban, a Cambodia business plan treated Cambodia as the service route into Vietnam; smuggled State Express 555 sales were estimated at about **1 billion cigarettes and £10 million a year**. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
- **Finding type(s)**: `route-portfolio-governance` (a central company treats legal, duty-free, and illicit routes as interchangeable portfolio channels optimized by market); `intermediary-deniability` (the company retains planning and supervision while a distributor performs the last separable step that supports a public no-control defense).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Internal company plans and correspondence — litigation-released public archive**: Asian country plans, distributor studies, route reports, investment budgets, and executive correspondence from roughly 1990–1994. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
  - **Distributor relationship records — internal corporate records**: SUTL’s assigned market portfolio, staff secondments, and reporting lines established operational proximity without alleging that SUTL itself crossed borders with the cigarettes. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
  - **Anti-corruption prosecution and appeal — public court/enforcement record**: Jerry Lui’s conviction and the judge’s account linked enormous duty-not-paid volumes to a bribery/smuggling syndicate. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
  - **Company response — solicited on record**: BAT disputed context and declined to define GT/DNP/transit, allowing its alternative explanation to be tested against repeated operational usage. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** As in the first entry, the decisive internal files were made public by litigation. The route reconstruction depends on documents now open to an outside investigator plus public court records; it does not require an unpublished source cache. [Project acquisition statement](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/about-project-big-tobacco-smuggling/)
- **Detection signature**: **`channel-portfolio route reconstruction`** — country plans and distributor assignments joined to border status, import bans, sales volumes, and court events on **market + route hub + year + channel label** revealed a managed Asian distribution system that shifted volume between legal, duty-free, and GT paths.
- **Corroboration structure**: Multiple company plans across countries show repeated structure; a criminal case validates the meaning and consequences in one hub; the distributor portfolio connects central strategy to local execution; BAT’s refusal to define terms is reported but not treated as proof. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in the story.** ICIJ specifies the 11,000-document, six-month review and explains that Asian documents were compared across legal and GT categories. **[Inferred]** The final route map required manual normalization of historical country names, distributor aliases, channel labels, and dates; this follows from the story’s cross-country reconstruction but no separate data recipe is published. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/global-reach-tobacco-companys-involvement-cigarette-smuggling-exposed-company-papers/)
- **Official impact**: The project-level impact statement says the 2000–2001 findings helped prompt lawsuits and government inquiries; no Asia-story-only causal claim is made. [ICIJ project impact statement](https://www.icij.org/investigations/big-tobacco-smuggling/about-project-big-tobacco-smuggling/)
- **Generalization**: Build a route-channel matrix from a company’s plans, intermediaries, border regimes, and sales. Look for the same brand or service moving from a blocked legal route into a euphemized alternative with unchanged targets and central oversight. This works for dual-use exports, sanctioned commodities, parallel pharmaceutical trade, waste exports, labor subcontracting, and digital services launched through nominally independent resellers.

### Made To Be Smuggled (2008) — A factory network’s capacity, absent legal market, and customs footprint identified a contraband-only brand

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ-led ten-country story by Roman Shleynov, Stefan Candea, Duncan Campbell, and Vlad Lavrov. Shleynov’s ICIJ profile says the Kaliningrad reporting team exposed the factory chain while he was associated with *Novaya Gazeta*; the project page credits a multinational ICIJ team, PIERS pro bono data, and additional support from *Le Soir*, *Novye Kolesa*, and others, but the story itself does not name a co-publishing lead outlet. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/) | [Project credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/about-project-tobacco-underground/) | [Shleynov profile](https://www.icij.org/journalists/roman-shleynov/)
- **What they found**:
  - Jin Ling had no identified legal retail market in Europe, was not advertised or sold in shops, yet customs officials said it rivaled Marlboro as the most-seized smuggled brand. ICIJ identified **five factories** in a wider Russian/East European network claiming more than **24 billion cigarettes** of annual capacity. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
  - Baltic Tobacco Factory claimed mainly Russian sales, but market data put its Russian retail share at only **0.1%, about 400 million cigarettes**. Its claimed 5% exports implied at least 400 million cigarettes, yet PIERS showed no U.S. waterborne shipments and available Russian trade data showed one small Poland export. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
  - European agencies seized **258 million Jin Ling cigarettes in 2007**, an 87% annual jump. Using customs’ 5%–10% interception expectation, ICIJ estimated that as many as **5 billion** might be entering Europe annually. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
  - Undercover reporters posing as smugglers were offered a container of more than **10 million cigarettes for $102,500**, ready in two weeks; that load could be worth $3.2 million at legal prices in Germany or Sweden and nearly $6 million in Britain or Norway. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
- **Finding type(s)**: `capacity-without-lawful-market` (declared production capacity cannot be reconciled to domestic sales or documented exports); `contraband-brand-by-design` (a product’s packaging, supply, pricing, and geographic footprint make sense only in an illicit channel).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Corporate, ownership, and trademark records — public registries**: Russian EGRUL ownership information, Moldovan state records, and Jin Ling trademark registrations across seven jurisdictions mapped factories, owners, and brand replication. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
  - **Customs seizure records — official administrative data**: country and EU reports supplied brand, quantity, route, concealment, and trend data; ICIJ examined 2007 and 2008 reports. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
  - **Trade records — licensed/commercial and unofficial datasets**: PIERS U.S. waterborne trade data, supplied pro bono, and unofficial Russian trade data tested BTF’s claimed export markets; Russian customs data traced global inputs. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/) | [PIERS credit](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/about-project-tobacco-underground/)
  - **Retail-market data and field checks — commercial estimate plus constructed observation**: Business Analytica estimated domestic share; reporters checked vendors in Russian cities and Kaliningrad for actual retail presence. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
  - **Undercover transaction test — reporter-generated non-public encounter, then published**: concealed video/audio from the Kaliningrad plant tested whether the seller would supply a purported EU smuggling route. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
  - **Supply-chain trade records — official/commercial**: PIERS showed BAT’s Brazilian subsidiary shipped 21 tons of leaf to BTF in 2008; Russian customs data showed tobacco, filters, paper, flavorings, packs, and machinery arriving from multiple countries. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable**, with a field-reporting caveat. The decisive detector—capacity versus lawful domestic sales and recorded exports, corroborated by brand-specific seizures, ownership, trademarks, and supply shipments—can be rebuilt from public or licensable records. The undercover visit powerfully established seller intent but was not needed to calculate the unexplained output. PIERS and Business Analytica improve resolution; customs/registry/retail evidence supplies a weaker public substitute if those commercial feeds are unavailable. [ICIJ’s evidence summary](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
- **Detection signature**: **`capacity-market-export gap triangulation`** — factory capacity and company export claims joined to retail share, recorded exports, brand seizures, and input shipments on **manufacturer + brand + year + destination** revealed industrial output with no plausible lawful end market.
- **Corroboration structure**: The quantitative gap supplies the flag; customs seizures show the missing product in destination markets; registries/trademarks connect distributed plants; input shipments prove production capability; field retail checks show absence from the claimed market; the undercover offer validates intent. Each source answers a different failure mode, so the structure is genuinely convergent rather than multiple reports repeating one seizure. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in the story.** ICIJ identifies the ten-country scope and explicitly lists corporate records, customs data, and undercover reporting. It also discloses the covert role, date, container size and offer, key commercial datasets, and estimation assumption. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
- **Official impact**: No post-publication action is attributed to this story on the seeded ICIJ pages. The article notes that OLAF had already launched Operation Baltic before publication, so that is context, not claimed impact. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/)
- **Generalization**: For any manufacturer, compare nameplate capacity and input purchases with lawful domestic sales plus documented exports. Resolve brand and owner aliases; overlay seizures, retailer presence, and destination price gradients. The pattern is useful for precursor chemicals, counterfeit medicines, weapons components, illegal mining output, waste-processing plants, ghost factories, and sanctioned dual-use goods.

### Ukraine’s “Lost” Cigarettes Flood Europe (2009) — A national supply balance exposed 30 billion cigarettes missing into illicit trade

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ project story by Vlad Lavrov, then a *Kyiv Post* staff writer and OCCRP regional editor. Attribution is therefore member-reporter/local expertise under an ICIJ-coordinated project; the ICIJ page, not *Kyiv Post*, is the credited lead publication in the supplied corpus. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/) | [Lavrov profile](https://www.icij.org/journalists/vlad-lavrov/)
- **What they found**:
  - Ukraine’s lawful consumption plus exports exceeded 100 billion cigarettes in 2008, while production and imports approached **130 billion**—a **30% excess and roughly 30 billion “lost” cigarettes**. ICIJ valued the residual illicit trade conservatively at **$2.1 billion a year**. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
  - National production grew from **96.8 billion in 2003 to 129.8 billion in 2008**. Philip Morris alone made 44 billion in 2008, up more than 85% from 2003; the four multinational producers controlled 99% of Ukraine’s market. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
  - Official seizures were a tiny fraction of the residual: Ukrainian customs seized about **66 million cigarettes in 2008**, less than 1% of the volume officials believed left illicitly. Neighboring countries made many of the large seizures, matching the cross-border price gradient from roughly $1.05 a pack in Ukraine to as much as $10 in the U.K. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
  - BAT’s own 2007 responsibility report acknowledged stopping Ukrainian Pall Mall production after an audit found output disproportionate to local demand; it said the change reduced smuggling by 3 billion cigarettes in 2005–2006. That admission validated the supply-balance logic with a company’s own corrective action. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
- **Finding type(s)**: `supply-demand-black-hole` (production plus imports materially exceed lawful consumption plus exports, leaving a residual that appears in illicit markets); `price-gradient-smuggling` (large cross-border price and tax differences explain where the residual flows and its profit potential).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **National production, import, consumption, and export statistics — government/industry administrative data**: Ukraine Ministry of Health figures, government statistics, and SOVAT tobacco/alcohol industry data supported the national balance and 2003–2008 trend. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
  - **Company-level output and responsibility reports — corporate disclosure**: producer totals and BAT’s audit/corrective-action statement tied the national residual to brands and firms without assuming all excess came from one producer. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
  - **Customs and criminal-case data — official enforcement records**: seizure totals, brand observations, and more than 120 smuggling prosecutions in one border region tested whether the residual appeared along predicted routes. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
  - **Online market observation — open-web constructed evidence**: searches and inquiries to Moldova-based sellers documented Ukrainian tax-stamped cigarettes offered for EU delivery, including minimum loads and claims of customs avoidance. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
  - **Government, company, and expert interviews — on-record reporting**: producers acknowledged outflow but disputed responsibility; health and customs specialists explained wholesaler and pack-date tracing opportunities. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive national balance uses published or obtainable production, import, consumption, and export data. Customs seizures, company reports, prices, and web listings are reusable corroboration. An outside investigator would need to reconstruct historical SOVAT/government series from archives if current links have decayed, but no leak or closed platform is conceptually necessary. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
- **Detection signature**: **`residual supply-balance reconstruction`** — national production plus imports compared with domestic consumption plus legal exports on **country + product + year** produced a 30-billion-unit residual; that residual joined to border price differences, seizure brands, and route cases revealed the likely illicit outflow and its value.
- **Corroboration structure**: The macro residual supplies scale; brand/company production trends allocate investigative attention; seizures and online offers locate the output; BAT’s own local-demand audit validates the causal premise; interviews preserve competing explanations. The story avoids claiming that every residual cigarette was proven smuggled, using “conservatively” and expert attribution for valuation. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in the story.** ICIJ identifies the component datasets, years, arithmetic quantities, corporate report, seizure records, and open-web test. **[Inferred]** The $2.1 billion valuation necessarily applies a price assumption to the residual, but the page does not publish a reproducible calculation sheet. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe/)
- **Official impact**: No post-publication impact specific to this story is stated on the seeded ICIJ pages. [Tobacco Underground project page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/)
- **Generalization**: Construct a material-balance identity for any commodity: opening stock + production + imports − lawful consumption − lawful exports − closing stock. Investigate persistent residuals, especially where adjacent jurisdictions have large price/tax differences and seizures show the same producer or lot. Applies to oil, gold, timber, fish, opioids, pesticides, carbon credits, electronic waste, and public inventory.

## Smoke Screen

### The tobacco lobby goes global (2012) — The same policy-delay toolkit appeared across emerging markets

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ global overview by Ricardo Sandoval Palos, Traver Riggins, Solomon Adebayo, Duncan Campbell, Andreas Harsono, Murali Krishnan, and Claudio Paolillo. Lead outlet: ICIJ/Center for Public Integrity. No story-specific partner or award is listed on the supplied pages. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/) | [Team](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/about-project-smoke-screen/)
- **What they found**:
  - Reporters in six countries documented a common influence toolkit: campaign contributions, gifts and charitable donations, regulators helped to draft rules, threatened or filed litigation, front-group activity, and—in prosecuted cases—bribes. ICIJ traced **millions of dollars** through lobbying firms, foundations, clubs, and even health ministries despite weak disclosure systems. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
  - In Russia, a tobacco-industry website said lobbyists wrote the country’s control rules; a Eurasian delegation included four industry-group executives and two Philip Morris employees, most listed as Agriculture Ministry experts. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
  - In Mexico, a private meeting preceded a bargain that postponed excise taxes in exchange for industry health payments; a legislator whose district received 15 school computers later inserted a smoking-space rule into federal restrictions. Industry-linked lawsuits further weakened public-smoking controls. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
  - In Uruguay, Philip Morris used a World Bank trade-dispute claim while domestic tobacco executives privately sought compromise; in Colombia, a promised **$200 million investment** coincided with debate over new rules; U.S. prosecutions described more than **$500,000** in Thai payoffs and settlements of almost **$30 million** across two tobacco suppliers’ cases. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
- **Finding type(s)**: `campaign-portability` (a corporation repeats the same influence functions across jurisdictions while substituting locally effective intermediaries); `policy-delay-by-multipronged-pressure` (money, access, rule drafting, litigation, research, and public relations act together to dilute or postpone regulation).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Cross-country lobbying and donation disclosures — public records and company lists**: campaign spending, charitable gifts, lobbyist payments, and recipients were traced where disclosure existed. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
  - **Litigation-released internal tobacco documents — public archive**: Project Cerberus and earlier industry strategy documents supplied a historical playbook against which current tactics could be compared. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/) | [Project methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/about-project-smoke-screen/)
  - **Legislation, treaty commitments, committee rosters, and official websites — public government records**: ICIJ compared domestic rules and negotiating delegations with WHO requirements and industry affiliations. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
  - **Court and enforcement files — public legal records**: World Bank trade-dispute materials, U.S. foreign-bribery cases, and local challenges documented the legal-pressure and payoff arms. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
  - **Local government, advocate, expert, and company interviews — reporter-obtained**: sources explained private meetings and implementation effects; companies were asked to defend participation and lobbying. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive cross-jurisdiction pattern can be rebuilt from public litigation archives, donation/lobby disclosures, laws, treaty obligations, committee rosters, court files, and official company/government pages. Confidential-source accounts strengthen particular episodes but the recurring campaign functions do not depend on a leak. [ICIJ project method](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/about-project-smoke-screen/)
- **Detection signature**: **`cross-jurisdiction tactic-outcome matrix`** — lobbying inputs (donation, official access, rule drafting, lawsuit, research sponsorship, investment, payoff) joined to regulatory outputs (delay, exemption, dilution, reversal) on **country + policy + actor + date** revealed the same corporate campaign architecture adapted to local power structures.
- **Corroboration structure**: Each country contributes a different tactic-to-outcome instance; internal strategy documents show that the repetition was consistent with an industry playbook; laws and case files prove actual policy/legal events; payment records ground money claims; local sources explain mechanisms. Cross-country repetition is pattern evidence, but it does not by itself prove central coordination for every episode. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/tobacco-lobby-goes-global/)
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated at project level.** ICIJ says reporters followed money and official connections in eight countries and examined litigation-released tobacco documents to connect past practice to present consequences. **[Inferred]** The story’s synthesis implies a common country worksheet for actors, tactic, amount, target policy, and outcome, but ICIJ does not publish one. [Project methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/about-project-smoke-screen/)
- **Official impact**: No story-specific post-publication action is identified on the supplied Smoke Screen pages. [Smoke Screen project page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/)
- **Generalization**: Represent influence campaigns by function rather than local label. A charity gift, advisory appointment, sponsored paper, threatened arbitration, and strategic investment may all purchase the same output: delay or dilution. Normalize those functions across countries, then align them to policy milestones. This detector transfers to fossil fuels, pharmaceuticals, gambling, mining, food, defense, chemicals, and platform regulation.

### Moscow’s open, revolving door for big tobacco (2012) — Industry personnel occupied official seats and helped write weaker tobacco rules

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
- **Partner/awards**: Member-outlet-led reporting under the ICIJ banner. Roman Anin, identified in the article as a *Novaya Gazeta* investigative reporter, led; Roman Shleynov contributed and was then an ICIJ member with *Vedomosti*/*Novaya Gazeta* experience. ICIJ coordinated and published the eight-month probe. No story-specific award is listed. [Story attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/) | [Shleynov profile](https://www.icij.org/journalists/roman-shleynov/)
- **What they found**:
  - Russia signed the WHO tobacco-control convention in 2008, but had already developed weaker industry-compatible rules. ICIJ found that tobacco lobbyists wrote the principal bill with former deputy prime minister Gennady Kulik; the weaker measure passed seven months after Russia signed the WHO convention. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
  - Official working-group rosters listed Tabakprom and industry-council representatives as Agriculture Ministry experts and included two Philip Morris Russia executives; an Imperial Tobacco executive reportedly sat as a **Kyrgyzstan delegate**. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
  - Internal BAT records showed a coordinated effort to “kill” a tougher Duma bill. A later BAT memo counted the result: the 2001 version had no higher excise tax, ad ban, or nonsmoking rules. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
  - Financial and career ties surrounded the rulemaking: Philip Morris gave **$52,000** to a charity run by the finance minister’s wife; Donskoy Tabak, 87.1% owned by a Duma member’s wife, had won **13 military contracts worth 60 million rubles (more than $2 million)** since 2007; an author of tobacco-funded tax studies became the Finance Ministry official overseeing excise and customs policy. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
- **Finding type(s)**: `regulator-seat-capture` (industry personnel or lobbyists occupy official expert/delegate positions inside the body setting their rules); `revolving-door-rulemaking` (a person moves from industry-funded policy production into public authority over the same subject, or holds overlapping roles).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Official working-group rosters and draft legislation — public government/association records**: rosters established declared official roles; Duma and Tabakprom pages identified the bill, sponsors, and industry authorship claim. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
  - **WHO convention and Russian law — public legal texts**: a provision-level comparison showed weaker warnings, retained “light” descriptors, and timing inconsistent with Russia’s new treaty obligation. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
  - **Litigation-released company records — public archive**: BAT and RJR memos documented target bills, meetings, travel support, lobbying aims, and the company’s own assessment of the diluted outcome. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
  - **State-contract registry and corporate ownership disclosures — public records**: military awards, ownership percentages, and politician-family ties made benefits and conflicts measurable. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
  - **Donation and research-sponsorship records — company/institute disclosures**: Philip Morris’s gift list and the Institute for the Economy in Transition’s project pages connected funded analysis, recipients, and later government roles. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
  - **Interviews and company acknowledgments — on record**: Philip Morris acknowledged participation in the working group; officials, advocates, researchers, and companies explained or contested the relationships. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive identity/role conflicts and rule-text differences are in rosters, corporate and state-contract records, sponsorship pages, laws, and the public tobacco-document archive. Interviews make the Kyrgyzstan-delegate episode vivid but are not the only basis for the broader capture finding. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
- **Detection signature**: **`official-roster conflict join + benchmark rule diff`** — government working-group/delegation rosters joined to corporate employment, association leadership, sponsorship, family ownership, and contract records on **person + organization + role + date**, then the resulting rule text compared with the WHO benchmark, revealed industry personnel inside the regulator and the precise concessions their preferred bill preserved.
- **Corroboration structure**: The roster proves presence; employment and association pages prove conflicting affiliation; historical internal memos prove lobbying intent and target provisions; the before/after law diff proves outcome; money and contract records show adjacent benefits; acknowledgments and denials preserve response. No single donation is treated as proof that a vote was purchased. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in the story/project.** ICIJ identifies an eight-month probe, named document classes, official sources, internal litigation records, and on-record responses. **[Inferred]** The conflict join and WHO-to-Russian-law provision diff are reconstructed from the evidence sequence; no downloadable roster or legislative comparison is published. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/) | [Project methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/about-project-smoke-screen/)
- **Official impact**: No specific post-publication action is claimed on the supplied project/story pages. The story reports contemporaneous pushback from Kazakhstan, Belarus, and Tajikistan, which preceded publication and is therefore context rather than impact. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/smoke-screen/moscows-open-revolving-door-big-tobacco/)
- **Generalization**: Obtain every advisory committee, standards group, public consultation, and negotiating-delegation roster. Entity-resolve members to employers, clients, family companies, donations, grants, and contracts; then diff the adopted rule against the external standard and earlier draft. The same pattern appears in drug formularies, environmental standards, procurement specifications, financial regulation, AI safety panels, building codes, and food-label rules.

### How Uber won access to world leaders, deceived investigators and exploited violence against its drivers (2022) — Launch-first illegality was converted into a global lobbying lever

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-global-rise-lobbying-violence-technology/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ newsroom story by Sydney P. Freedberg, Nicole Sadek, Brenda Medina, Agustin Armendariz, and Karrie Kehoe inside a project **co-led by The Guardian and ICIJ**, with 42 other media partners. *The Guardian* obtained the cache; ICIJ coordinated structured analysis and international distribution. [Story attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-global-rise-lobbying-violence-technology/) | [Project attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/about-uber-files-investigation/)
- **What they found**:
  - The cache revealed **more than 100 meetings** between Uber executives and public officials from 2014–2016, including **12 European Commission meetings not publicly disclosed**. It documented more than a dozen undisclosed communications and at least four meetings with Emmanuel Macron while Uber faced French investigations and suspension. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-global-rise-lobbying-violence-technology/)
  - Uber and an advisory firm compiled more than **1,850 stakeholders**—officials, former officials, think tanks, and citizens groups—across 29 countries and EU institutions. A proposed 2016 global influence budget reached **$90 million**. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-global-rise-lobbying-violence-technology/)
  - Rather than secure licenses first, Uber entered 31 countries in 2014 and let the installed service create a regulatory crisis. Internal guidance summarized the sequence as launch, endure the legal “storm,” then lobby; executives described the posture as asking forgiveness rather than permission. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-global-rise-lobbying-violence-technology/)
  - The company hired former officials to reach former colleagues, mobilized customers through “save Uber” campaigns, paid friendly academics, and recruited politically connected investors. The records also showed a kill switch used during raids in at least six countries and a confidential relationship with former EU commissioner Neelie Kroes before she later joined Uber’s advisory board. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-global-rise-lobbying-violence-technology/)

- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Leaked internal company cache — whistleblower material**: more than 124,000 records, including 83,000 emails plus texts, calendars, presentations, invoices, briefing notes, and other files from 2013–2017; former chief European lobbyist Mark MacGann gave the material to *The Guardian*. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/how-we-unearthed-ubers-controversial-playbook-from-a-cache-of-employee-communications/)
  - **Structured calendar and communications data — derived from leak**: ICIJ extracted three calendar formats, email addresses, names, and domains; thousands of messages were reviewed to confirm that scheduled meetings occurred. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/how-we-unearthed-ubers-controversial-playbook-from-a-cache-of-employee-communications/)
  - **Public lobbying and meeting disclosures — open government records**: EU Transparency Register, U.S. Senate lobbying disclosures, French lobbying registry, and country/institution meeting schedules tested which contacts were disclosed. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/how-we-unearthed-ubers-controversial-playbook-from-a-cache-of-employee-communications/)
  - **Public laws, raids, investigations, speeches, and policy outcomes — official records/open web**: these re-anchored internal messages to observable enforcement and rule changes by place and date. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-global-rise-lobbying-violence-technology/)
  - **SEC filings and archived Uber websites — public records/web archives**: used project-wide to quantify post-2019 growth and reconstruct the company’s country/city footprint. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/how-we-unearthed-ubers-controversial-playbook-from-a-cache-of-employee-communications/)
  - **Company and subject responses — solicited statements**: Uber, Kalanick’s representative, former officials, and executives disputed, contextualized, or acknowledged specific practices. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-global-rise-lobbying-violence-technology/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(b) re-anchoring.** **Discovery half:** the leak supplied hidden calendars, communications, stakeholder lists, internal budgets, and the launch-first strategy. **Public/reusable verification half:** meeting/lobby registers, official schedules, laws, enforcement actions, speeches, SEC filings, archived launch footprints, and later parliamentary records verify who met, what was omitted, and what changed. The precise private language and full stakeholder universe remain non-public, but the central **undisclosed-access diff and launch→enforcement→policy sequence** can be tested once the leaked events identify dates and actors. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/how-we-unearthed-ubers-controversial-playbook-from-a-cache-of-employee-communications/)
- **Detection signature**: **`calendar-register-outcome diff`** — leaked calendars and messages joined to public meeting/lobby registers and official policy/enforcement timelines on **official + company + date + jurisdiction** revealed more than 100 contacts, 12 undisclosed EU meetings, and a repeated launch-first sequence in which enforcement pressure was followed by high-level lobbying and regulatory concessions.
- **Corroboration structure**: Calendar items were checked against thousands of internal messages for occurrence; declared schedules/registers supplied an independent disclosure ledger; country partners checked law and enforcement context; public records and later testimony re-anchored key events; subjects were contacted. ICIJ explicitly warns that the leak is a partial slice ending in 2017 and that lobbying-disclosure quality varies by country. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/how-we-unearthed-ubers-controversial-playbook-from-a-cache-of-employee-communications/)
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in a dedicated ICIJ methods page.** Files were loaded into Datashare; Apache Tika, Python, and pandas extracted identities and structured calendar data; a combined stakeholder sheet was organized by country; leaked events were compared with public registers; all data work was fact-checked. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/how-we-unearthed-ubers-controversial-playbook-from-a-cache-of-employee-communications/)
- **Official impact**: France and Belgium opened parliamentary inquiries and the European Parliament held a responsive session; France’s final inquiry criticized an opaque, privileged Uber–Macron relationship and said its creation responded to the Uber Files. [ICIJ impact: inquiries](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/french-mps-to-investigate-ubers-lobbying-of-public-officials/) | [ICIJ impact: final French report](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/french-uber-files-inquiry-criticizes-macrons-secret-dealings-with-ride-hailing-giant/)
- **Generalization**: Build a launch/enforcement/access/policy timeline for regulated products. Compare disclosed lobbying contacts with executive calendars or FOIA’d schedules; measure meetings during active enforcement; identify user petitions, subsidized adoption, academic sponsorship, revolving-door hires, and strategic investors. The pattern fits crypto exchanges, short-term rentals, delivery platforms, sports betting, AI products, e-cigarettes, telehealth, and autonomous vehicles.

### Uber forged deals with top Putin allies in failed bid to break into Russian market (2022) — Stock and investment terms were used to purchase political intermediation

- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ story by Nicole Sadek and Sydney P. Lead publishing outlet for this version: ICIJ; source outlet and co-lead: *The Guardian*. No story-specific award is listed. [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/uber-deals-russia-putin-allies/)

Project-wide, the Uber Files triggered protests and inquiries across Europe, including French and Belgian parliamentary processes. [ICIJ impact summary](https://www.icij.org/investigations/uber-files/french-mps-to-investigate-ubers-lobbying-of-public-officials/)
- **Generalization**: Treat “strategic investor” as a potential lobbying role. Resolve investors and beneficial owners to political offices, sanctions, state enterprises, advisory firms, and policy targets; compare investment terms with normal financing terms; look for warrants, side letters, board seats, discounts, or consulting contracts whose value rises if regulation changes. This transfers to mining licenses, telecom spectrum, defense procurement, casinos, crypto, foreign market entry, and infrastructure concessions.

### How Merck turned its wonder drug into a blockbuster — and priced out cancer patients worldwide (2026) — Patent families, regulatory shortcuts, pricing secrecy, and influence spending reinforced one monopoly

- **What they found**:
  - Starting from U.S. filings and expanding patent families, ICIJ identified at least **1,212 Keytruda-related patent applications in 53 jurisdictions**. [Project findings](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/about-keytruda-cancer-calculus/)

[ICIJ overview](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/merck-keytruda-cancer-drug-price/)
  - **Commercial/exclusive health data — closed but non-decisive enhancement**: IQVIA sales, Serif Health reimbursement data, and Health Care Cost Institute claims supplied growth and site-of-care comparisons. [ICIJ overview](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/merck-keytruda-cancer-drug-price/) | [Project findings](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/about-keytruda-cancer-calculus/)

[ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/patents-prices-and-court-files-how-icij-used-data-to-investigate-an-industry-that-thrives-on-secrecy/)
- **Detection signature**: **`patent-family exclusivity ladder`** — 180 verified U.S. applications expanded through family links, assignees, legal status, filing dates, and expiry horizons on **invention family + jurisdiction + assignee + priority date**, then compared with original-patent expiry, standardized prices, competitor entry, and regulatory/payment timelines, revealed a layered exclusivity strategy whose commercial effect persisted beyond the core patent.
- **Corroboration structure**: Patent offices establish the filings; expert/manual review controls family and assignee overstatement; explicit exclusions prevent double-counting PCT and duplicated Eurasian filings; prices are normalized to a common dose and affordability denominator; regulatory/payment records show adjacent influence pathways; Argentina’s pre-entry price cut supplies a quasi-experimental competition response; company statements contest the inference that patent count determines entry. [ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/patents-prices-and-court-files-how-icij-used-data-to-investigate-an-industry-that-thrives-on-secrecy/) | [ICIJ overview](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/merck-keytruda-cancer-drug-price/)

- **Official impact**: U.S. Sen. Maggie Hassan cited Cancer Calculus in a letter demanding answers from Merck about secondary patents, product hopping, biosimilar delay, and pricing; she asked for a response by July 20, 2026. [ICIJ impact story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/senator-questions-merck-over-patent-strategy-for-blockbuster-cancer-drug-keytruda/)

This applies to seeds, biologics, medical devices, software standards, chemicals, batteries, telecom, and defense technology.

[ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/cancer-calculus/cancer-patients-legal-battle-keytruda-lifesaving-drug/)

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### P1. Permission-after-entry pressure loop

**Mechanics.** Establish the product/service before authorization; subsidize rapid adoption; allow enforcement and dependency to create a crisis; mobilize users, investors, academics, and former officials; seek a bespoke rule or non-enforcement outcome. Uber provides the cleanest instance, while tobacco’s small legal “umbrella” supporting a much larger DNP market is a physical-goods precursor.

**Minimum data.**

1. Market-entry/launch dates by jurisdiction.
2. Licensing status and governing rule at launch.
3. Enforcement actions, lawsuits, raids, or suspension dates.
4. Lobbying contacts, official calendars, petitions, strategic investors, and paid research.
5. Policy changes, exemptions, settlements, or enforcement reversals.

**Recognition cues.** “Launch now”; sudden user petition after enforcement; steep subsidies before legalization; former officials hired during active investigations; private meetings clustered between suspension and reversal; investors chosen for political reach; the same company asks different jurisdictions for nearly identical exceptions.

**Generic detector.** Build a jurisdiction timeline and test whether **launch precedes legal authority**, then calculate contact density and policy change in the enforcement window. Rank companies by the fraction of markets entered before authorization and by elapsed days from enforcement to high-level meeting.

#### P2. Hidden-channel corporate dialect decoder

**Mechanics.** Extract unexplained internal terms; identify explicit oppositions to known lawful categories; examine nearby quantities, routes, prices, controls, and executive approvals; validate the inferred meaning against court/enforcement outcomes. Big Tobacco’s DNP/GT/transit vocabulary is the canonical case.

**Minimum data.**

1. Searchable internal or litigation-released document corpus.
2. Document metadata and author/recipient identities.
3. A transaction, shipment, sales, or operational ledger.
4. At least one external case or known event that supplies semantic ground truth.

**Recognition cues.** Stable abbreviations absent from public materials; a coded category appears alongside ordinary KPIs; “sensitive” offices, safes, shredders, or file relocation; the code rises when borders close; the company refuses to define a term it uses systematically.

**Generic detector.** Generate candidate code terms by corpus frequency and context; cluster co-occurring metrics and locations; compare term usage to enforcement events and legal/illegal channel labels; require operational consistency across at least three documents before assigning meaning.

#### P3. Capacity without a lawful market

**Mechanics.** Reconcile production capacity and input purchases to domestic sales, recorded exports, and inventory. Follow the residual into seizures, destination markets, or unregistered retailers. Made To Be Smuggled and Ukraine’s national residual show plant-level and country-level versions.

**Minimum data.**

1. Production/nameplate capacity or input quantities.
2. Domestic sell-through/consumption.
3. Imports, exports, and inventory change.
4. Brand/lot-specific seizures or destination evidence.

**Recognition cues.** Capacity multiples above lawful demand; factories with no signage or normal retail presence; large inputs but negligible tax-paid sales; claimed exports missing from destination data; the same brand dominates seizures; production rises while declared demand is flat.

**Generic detector.** Compute residual = production + imports − domestic lawful consumption − exports − inventory growth. Resolve producers/brands across customs and seizure records; estimate bounds rather than treating the full residual as proven diversion.

#### P4. Capture roster-to-rule diff

**Mechanics.** Join official committee, standards, consultation, and delegation rosters to current/former employers, clients, funders, family companies, contracts, and donations. Then compare the adopted rule with the external benchmark and earlier drafts. Moscow shows the complete sequence; the global tobacco overview shows its portability.

**Minimum data.**

1. Official rosters and meeting/consultation records.
2. Corporate registry, employment, lobbying-client, grant, donation, and contract data.
3. Versioned bill/regulation text.
4. An external standard, treaty, or regulator’s initial proposal.

**Recognition cues.** Company staff listed as ministry experts; delegates unable to identify the country they represent; industry association claims authorship; regulated-party funded research cited by the regulator; family ownership near a policy portfolio; weaker domestic text enacted soon after a stronger treaty commitment.

**Generic detector.** Entity-resolve every roster member, score conflicts at the meeting date, and run a provision-level diff from draft/benchmark to final text. Report the exact concession and the conflicted participant with the relevant subject-matter role; do not infer causation from affiliation alone.

#### P5. Exclusivity ladder to access externality

**Mechanics.** Expand a core patent into all families and assignees, calculate the practical exclusion horizon, align follow-on filings with approval/formulation changes and competitor entry, normalize prices and affordability, then count litigation or regulator actions needed to obtain the product. Cancer Calculus connects the upstream moat to a downstream court burden.

**Minimum data.**

1. Core patents and global family/priority/legal-status data.
2. Product approvals and formulation/indication dates.
3. Standardized prices and an earnings or payer denominator.
4. Competitor/biosimilar entry dates.
5. Coverage-denial, court, tribunal, or regulator outcomes.

**Recognition cues.** Many secondary applications near core expiry; new delivery form before biosimilar entry; large price drop immediately before a competitor launch; many successful appeals against the same payer; legal access concentrated among claimants able to hire counsel.

**Generic detector.** Calculate original expiry versus latest plausible family barrier; mark product-hop events; run price and affordability comparisons; build a deduplicated coverage case cohort; test whether high appeal success means adjudication has become a routine access gate.

### 5. What this platform can run today, and what is missing

#### Runnable now or with bounded source acquisition

- **Roster/investor/entity conflict joins — strong partial capability.** UK Companies House, 20+ registries, OpenCorporates, GLEIF, OpenSanctions, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, and local OpenAleph can resolve companies, directors, beneficial-owner clues, sanctioned investors, advisers, and recurring intermediaries. USASpending/FPDS can add public-contract benefits. This supports the entity side of **Capture roster-to-rule diff** and **Political investor conduit**, provided a target roster or deal list is supplied.
- **U.S. patent-moat screening — strong seed capability.** USPTO data can identify assignees, applications, continuations, inventors, dates, and clusters around a product. Corporate registries/GLEIF can resolve assignee families; CourtListener/RECAP can add patent litigation. This can flag candidates for **Exclusivity ladder**, though it cannot reproduce ICIJ’s 53-jurisdiction family count without international patent-family ingestion.
- **U.S. federal litigation-gated-access screening — partial capability.** CourtListener/RECAP can search drug, insurer, agency, benefit, and remedy terms and build federal cohorts. Local OpenAleph can connect discovered cases to documents and entities. This can surface repeat defendants and appeal reversals but will miss most state administrative reviews and the Latin American constitutional-court systems central to ICIJ’s result.
- **Internal-corpus dialect and role mining — runnable when documents exist.** Local OpenAleph can index litigation exhibits, hearing records, FOIA releases, and leaked/public corpora; entity extraction and keyword/context analysis can identify euphemisms, stakeholder lists, and revolving-door names. The detector is ready; the binding constraint is acquisition of a relevant internal or litigation-released corpus.
- **Sanctions/strategic-investor risk mapping — runnable as hypothesis generation.** Corporate registries, GLEIF, OpenSanctions, Offshore Leaks, and court records can join investors to sanctioned actors and state-linked entities. This reproduces the public substitute for Uber Russia: it can flag a suspicious political-access network, but not prove undisclosed intent.

#### Missing or materially incomplete

- **Lobbying contacts and official calendars across jurisdictions.** The holdings listed do not provide a normalized EU/U.S./national lobbying register, ministerial schedule, advisory-committee roster, or consultation-submission corpus. Without those, the platform cannot automate Uber’s calendar-register diff or Moscow’s roster conflict join at scale.
- **Versioned legislation, regulations, treaty implementation, and committee membership.** A bill/regulation diff layer is needed to connect an interested actor to the exact provision weakened, inserted, delayed, or exempted.
- **Customs, production, consumption, price, and seizure ledgers.** Current holdings do not supply the commodity-flow denominators necessary for Ukraine’s residual calculation or BTF’s capacity-market-export gap. Trade feeds, national excise/production data, and brand/lot seizure records are the main missing inputs.
- **Global patent-family and legal-status coverage.** USPTO alone cannot reproduce Espacenet/Google Patents family expansion, WIPO/PCT handling, EPO regional applications, or status across 53 jurisdictions. A normalized family graph with priority, assignee history, status, and expiry is required.
- **Pharmaceutical prices, reimbursement, claims, professional payments, and coverage decisions.** The platform lacks the GÖG/partner price series, ILO-linked affordability workflow, IQVIA/Serif/HCCI-like sales and claims data, national reimbursement decisions, and insurer denial logs. Public list-price acquisition is feasible; paid-price and site-of-care analysis remains data-constrained.
- **State and international court/tribunal corpora.** CourtListener/RECAP is valuable but cannot supply Guatemalan amparos, Mexican partner records, Chilean rulings, Brazilian state cases, or most U.S. state insurance-review decisions. Those systems need jurisdiction-specific connectors and deduplication rules.
- **Non-public intent evidence.** No existing public holding can substitute for the Uber cache’s private warrants, strategy memos, or instructions. The platform should label public-network outputs as risk hypotheses until a contract, message, testimony, or filing establishes purpose.

**Near-term operating order:** (1) run USPTO + corporate-resolution + CourtListener candidate screens for dense follow-on patent portfolios; (2) ingest official advisory/committee rosters and lobbying/meeting disclosures for one jurisdiction; (3) add versioned rule text so roster conflicts can be connected to exact policy concessions; (4) acquire one bounded commodity production/export/seizure series for a capacity-without-lawful-market pilot. These steps exploit current entity and document strengths while filling the smallest decisive gaps exposed by ICIJ’s methods.
