# ICIJ Extractives & Environment: Structured Evidence Ontology

## Scope and selection

This review starts only from the eight census-supplied ICIJ project hubs: [Fatal Extraction](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/), [Deforestation Inc.](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/), [Looting the Seas: The Black Market in Bluefin](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/), [Looting the Seas II: Spain’s $8 Billion Fish](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/), [Looting the Seas III: Plunder in the Pacific](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/), [The Water Barons](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/), [The Illicit Trade of Coltan](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/), and [The Global Climate Change Lobby](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/). Eleven stories were selected across all eight projects to preserve the cluster’s evidentiary diversity: issuer filings and mining licenses; environmental certificates and timber shipments; fisheries quotas, subsidies, and DNA; concession contracts; mineral export documents; and lobbying records.

The finding-type labels below are intentionally free-form and inductive. Each is defined at first use. “Detection signature” isolates the smallest analytic operation that converted records into a publishable finding. Input-dependency classes ask whether that operation can be run by an outside investigator today—not merely whether some supporting material is public now.

## 1. Fatal Extraction

### Australian mining companies digging a deadly footprint in Africa (2015) — Resolving African mines and deaths back to Australian-listed parents exposed a far larger human toll than issuer disclosures conveyed

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/australian-mining-companies-digging-deadly-footprint-africa/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ coordinated the investigation with the African Network of Centers for Investigative Reporting (ANCIR), fielding 13 reporters in 13 African countries; local reporting partners included Mmegi, Nyasa Times, Dépêches du Mali, Daily News, The Post of Zambia, Citi FM, L’Evénement, and Voice of America. This is an ICIJ-coordinated cross-border project, not a member-outlet story merely carried under an ICIJ banner. [Project attribution and partner list](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/about-project-fatal-extraction/)
- **What they found**:
  - ICIJ identified 152 Australian Securities Exchange-listed mining companies operating in 33 African countries at the end of 2014, after reviewing more than 1,000 issuer announcements and reports and resolving roughly 1,500 project and license interests. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
  - Reporters documented more than 380 deaths associated with Australian mining operations in 13 African countries between 2004 and 2015, including workers and people living near mines. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/australian-mining-companies-digging-deadly-footprint-africa/)
  - Aquarius Platinum’s African operations recorded 38 on-site deaths from 2004 through 2014; ICIJ contrasted that toll with Western Australia’s mining sector despite the latter employing roughly five times as many people. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/australian-mining-companies-digging-deadly-footprint-africa/)
  - In Kilwa, Democratic Republic of Congo, Anvil Mining acknowledged supplying vehicles, aircraft, and rations later used by government troops during an operation in which a United Nations investigation found 73 people died, 29 by summary execution; Anvil denied knowing of or assisting abuses, while ICIJ found the written requisition used to justify the assistance was dated eight months after the operation. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/australian-mining-companies-digging-deadly-footprint-africa/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Disclosure-footprint gap** — locally documented licenses, incidents, or harms become visible as a pattern only after sites and subsidiaries are resolved to a remote reporting parent and compared with that parent’s public disclosures.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Issuer disclosures — public securities records, downloaded and reviewed**: more than 1,000 ASX annual reports, quarterly reports, and announcements supplied the parent companies, projects, countries, and disclosed fatalities. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
  - **License and project records — public and commercial reference records, cross-checked manually**: mining-tenement disclosures were compared with SNL Metals & Mining, InfoMine, Investogain, company sites, and country-level license information to distinguish active interests from applications, options, relinquished tenements, and delisted firms. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
  - **Incident and legal records — public/local records, gathered country by country**: court files, government documents, press archives, company reports, and local reporting supplied deaths and allegations that could be associated with a mine or operator. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
  - **Field evidence — first-hand reporting**: the team traveled more than 30,000 kilometers, interviewed workers, families, officials, and company representatives, and inspected mine communities across 13 countries. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive discovery set—ASX issuer filings, company and license records, court/government records, and local incident reporting—can be gathered by an outside investigator. Field interviews improve attribution and human consequences but are not required to rerun the parent/site/fatality reconciliation. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
- **Detection signature**: **Issuer-to-harm reconciliation** — ASX issuer filings and mining-license/project records joined to local court, government, and incident records on **parent/subsidiary + mine/site + event date** revealed deaths and abuses dispersed across local records, including events absent from parent-company disclosures. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
- **Corroboration structure**: Each candidate fatality was rechecked manually and cross-referenced across corporate reports, news and research databases, court or government records, and field interviews; entity resolution connected local operators and sites to ASX parents, while on-the-ground reporting tested what the paper record omitted. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ’s methodology says reporters first used automated searches for fatality terms in issuer documents, then manually checked and rechecked hits; the operating-company census combined ASX quarterly sector lists with searches for African country names and “mining tenements,” followed by annual-report review and commercial-source cross-checks. It also explains important exclusions, including option-only, application-only, relinquished, and delisted interests. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/fatal-extraction/data-and-field-work-combine-reveal-australian-minings-impact-africa/)
- **Generalization**: The detector applies wherever a reporting entity operates through locally named subsidiaries or assets: join the parent’s claimed footprint and disclosures to permit, inspection, court, casualty, environmental, or labor records at the site level, then flag locally evidenced events with no corresponding parent disclosure. **[analysis]**

## 2. Deforestation Inc.

### How auditing firms help companies hide environmental damage and human rights abuses (2023) — Certification periods overlapped hundreds of alleged or adjudicated harms that “sustainable” labels were supposed to screen out

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/auditors-green-labels-sustainability-environmental-harm/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ led a collaboration of 43 media partners in 28 countries, including Der Spiegel, NDR, Süddeutsche Zeitung, Le Monde, Radio France, NRC, Tempo, The Indian Express, CBC/Radio-Canada, and Convoca. The project won the 2024 John B. Oakes Award for Distinguished Environmental Journalism. [Project attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/about-deforestation-inc-investigation/) [Award](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/deforestation-inc-wins-2024-oakes-award-for-environmental-journalism/)
- **What they found**:
  - The team assembled nearly 2,000 cases involving more than 410 companies in at least 50 countries that governments, courts, environmental groups, Indigenous communities, or other organizations had accused of environmental damage or human-rights abuse. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/auditors-green-labels-sustainability-environmental-harm/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
  - At least 340 certified forest-product companies had been accused of environmental crimes or other wrongdoing after receiving certification, and at least 50 had been fined or convicted by a government agency or court. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/auditors-green-labels-sustainability-environmental-harm/)
  - Forty-eight auditing firms had issued certificates to companies accused of wrongdoing during the validity period of the certificate. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/auditors-green-labels-sustainability-environmental-harm/)
  - Official monitoring reports showed that European Union member states inspected fewer than 1% of the estimated companies importing or selling forest products in each year from 2019 through 2021. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/auditors-green-labels-sustainability-environmental-harm/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Assurance-conduct contradiction** — a trust label, audit opinion, or eligibility certification remains active during conduct that the assurance is marketed as preventing or detecting.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Allegation/adjudication case corpus — public records and reports, collected and structured**: court decisions, government sanctions and inspection material, NGO and Indigenous-community reports, and local reporting supplied company, place, conduct, date, and outcome fields. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
  - **Certification records — public certification databases, downloaded and normalized**: Forest Stewardship Council and Programme for the Endorsement of Forest Certification records supplied certificate holder, validity dates, and auditing body. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
  - **Corporate hierarchy — commercial and public company research, reconciled manually**: Orbis and company records linked named operators to parents and corporate groups so allegations and certificates could be matched despite naming differences. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
  - **Regulatory coverage — public and freedom-of-information records**: EU monitoring reports and agency responses supplied inspection counts and estimated regulated populations. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/auditors-green-labels-sustainability-environmental-harm/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** No leak was necessary to build the central contradiction. An outside investigator can collect public allegation/adjudication records, download certification histories, resolve corporate groups, and test date overlap, although global completeness requires substantial multilingual collection and archival work. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
- **Detection signature**: **Certificate-violation temporal overlap** — court, regulator, community, and NGO cases joined to FSC/PEFC certificate records on **resolved corporate group + operating unit + event/certificate dates** revealed alleged or adjudicated harms occurring while certificates issued by 48 auditors were active. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
- **Corroboration structure**: ICIJ retained cases only after matching the accused company to a certificate holder or group and establishing temporal overlap; government fines and court outcomes formed a stronger adjudicated subset, while company and auditor responses tested identity, timing, and disputed allegations. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ says it created a new database because no central record linked forest-sector allegations to certifications. Reporters structured nearly 2,000 cases, resolved names and parents, cross-referenced certification databases, and excluded cases that did not overlap an active certificate. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
- **Generalization**: Join any time-bounded assurance—ESG ratings, safety accreditation, vendor approval, grant eligibility, professional licensing—to dated violations, recalls, sanctions, lawsuits, or physical tests. Rank contradictions by source authority and whether the certifier had an inspection opportunity before renewal. **[analysis]**

### Companies are importing teak from Myanmar despite sanctions and military rule (2023) — Trade and tax records traced restricted timber through intermediaries into certified Western supply chains

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
- **Partner/awards**: This is ICIJ-led reporting by Scilla Alecci and Jelena Cosic within Deforestation Inc.; reporting contributions named in the story include the Miami Herald, NRC, Commonwealth Magazine, New Zealand Herald, L’Espresso/IRPI Media, The Indian Express, Paper Trail Media, and NDR. It is an ICIJ-coordinated story assembled with project partners, not a republished single-outlet investigation. [Story credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/) [Project attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/about-deforestation-inc-investigation/)
- **What they found**:
  - At least 10 traders and retailers held environmental certificates while buying teak from Myanmar suppliers, including trade after the EU’s 2017 restrictions and sanctions imposed after Myanmar’s 2021 military coup. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
  - U.S. importer Global Dynamics imported more than 300 metric tons of Myanmar teak after U.S. sanctions, using suppliers that were not themselves named on the sanctions list. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
  - Gibson McIlvain bought at least 39 metric tons after mid-2021 from Win Enterprise; leaked tax records and corporate research linked a Win director to Forest Products Joint Venture Corp., an entity associated with the state timber monopoly, while the company said its transactions complied with U.S. rules. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
  - German company BBS bought about $106,000 in teak through an intermediary from Htoo Trading, whose founder Tay Za and related interests were subject to U.K. sanctions; BBS said the purchase predated the applicable EU prohibition. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Intermediary sanctions bypass** — a restricted originator or state-linked supplier remains economically connected to a transaction while an unsanctioned intermediary becomes the formal counterparty. **Assurance-conduct contradiction** also applies because environmental certificates remained part of the product’s market representation.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Myanmar tax records — leaked administrative data, obtained through collaborators**: tax records shared by Justice for Myanmar, Finance Uncovered, and Distributed Denial of Secrets supplied domestic sellers, buyers, values, and dates that were not otherwise exposed in a complete public ledger. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
  - **International trade records — licensed and public datasets, queried by company and product**: ImportGenius shipment records and UN Comtrade supplied importer, exporter, product, weight, route, and date. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
  - **Sanctions and corporate records — public government and registry sources**: U.S., U.K., and EU sanctions notices, Myanmar company records, and corporate websites linked suppliers and directors to sanctioned or state-linked actors. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
  - **Certification records — public databases**: FSC and PEFC histories established which importers or traders carried environmental claims during relevant trade periods. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(b) re-anchoring.** The leaked Myanmar tax data was the discovery anchor for exact domestic counterparties, transaction values, and invoice-level links. The reusable public verification half consists of customs/shipping records, UN trade data, sanctions effective dates, public corporate/director records, and certification histories; those sources can verify post-sanctions movements, ownership links, and active green claims once the counterparties are known. Without the leak, a public rerun loses the Myanmar-side tax trail and some invoice-level prices, but can still test the supply-chain and sanctions-window finding. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
- **Detection signature**: **Sanctions-window supply-chain trace** — leaked Myanmar tax transactions and international shipment records joined to sanctions lists, exporter ownership, and certificate validity on **supplier/intermediary/importer + product + transaction date** revealed state-linked teak moving after sanctions through formally unsanctioned counterparties under green labels. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
- **Corroboration structure**: The tax record identified a Myanmar-side transaction; shipment data verified the cross-border movement; registry and sanctions records resolved the people and entities; certificate records established the environmental claim; and responses from importers, certifiers, and sanctions authorities tested competing legal interpretations. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ’s Deforestation Inc. methodology expressly identifies the leaked tax files, ImportGenius, UN Comtrade, sanctions records, certification databases, company records, and interviews used in the Myanmar investigation. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/how-we-used-data-to-expose-flaws-in-hundreds-of-green-claims-by-forest-products-companies/)
- **Official impact**: After ICIJ’s questions, the Forest Stewardship Council said it was investigating certified companies’ Myanmar teak supply chains. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/deforestation-inc/myanmar-teak-trade-sanctions-military-regime/)
- **Generalization**: Starting with a sanctioned or high-risk origin, enumerate first-hop sellers, resolve shared directors and state affiliations, then search shipment records for post-effective-date trade through unsanctioned intermediaries. Overlay any certification or compliance claim active on the importer. **[analysis]**

## 3. Looting the Seas: The Black Market in Bluefin

### The Mediterranean feeding frenzy (2010) — Catch-chain records and quota arithmetic showed that industrial bluefin trade substantially exceeded lawful and sustainable limits

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/part-i-mediterranean-feeding-frenzy/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ coordinated a seven-month investigation by 12 journalists with BBC World News, Le Soir, Stern, The Sunday Times, and Il Fatto Quotidiano; the methodology names ICIJ data editor Kate Willson as the analyst of the confidential bluefin catch-document database. This was an ICIJ-led collaboration rather than a partner story simply hosted by ICIJ. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
- **What they found**:
  - ICIJ estimated that more than one in three bluefin tuna caught from 1998 through 2007 was traded off the books, representing at least $4 billion in illegal catch. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/part-i-mediterranean-feeding-frenzy/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
  - The project estimated annual catch of roughly 50,000 to 60,000 metric tons during much of the period—about twice official quotas and roughly three times what scientists said was sustainable. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/part-i-mediterranean-feeding-frenzy/)
  - In 2008–2009 Bluefin Catch Document records, more than 75% of purse-seine catch reports lacked legally required information needed for verification, and more than a dozen ranches appeared to sell more tuna than their records accounted for. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
  - ICIJ found more than 130 catch records totaling 715 metric tons in which every fish was reported at exactly the minimum permitted average weight, a pattern the team treated as a red flag for underweight fish being legalized on paper. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Paper-chain laundering** — mandatory traceability documents appear complete enough to legalize a commodity even though quantities, biological constraints, or adjacent transaction stages make the documented flow physically implausible.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Bluefin Catch Document database — password-protected regulator data, accessed through a member-state source**: the chain-level database supplied vessel, catch, transfer, ranch, harvest, export, weight, and catch-document identifiers. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
  - **Confidential complete file — non-public source material, used for validation**: ICIJ reports comparing its database access with a full confidential data file and manually validating a random sample after cleaning vessel and company names. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
  - **Quota and scientific estimates — public international-regulator records**: ICCAT quotas and scientists’ catch estimates supplied legal and sustainable baselines for the 1998–2007 market reconstruction. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/part-i-mediterranean-feeding-frenzy/) [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
  - **Market valuation — trade and price research**: Japanese market and Tokyo auction prices supplied conservative values for unreported tonnage. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(d) closed-platform-dependent.** The decisive catch-ID-level mass balance required password-protected ICCAT Bluefin Catch Document access plus a confidential full file. The nearest public substitute is ICCAT’s aggregate catch, quota, compliance, and scientific material combined with national landing data, customs records, and Japanese import/auction data. That substitute can rerun the macro gap and conservative market valuation, but it loses the vessel-to-ranch-to-export joins, missing-field audit, and specific impossible harvest/transfer chains. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
- **Detection signature**: **Regulated-chain mass balance** — Bluefin Catch Document catch, transfer, ranch, harvest, and export rows joined on **catch-document ID + vessel + ranch + date**, then compared with vessel quotas and biological minimum weights, revealed missing verification data, harvest exceeding recorded input, and suspicious exact-threshold weights. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
- **Corroboration structure**: ICIJ cleaned names, manually checked a random database sample against underlying documents, compared password-system data with a confidential full file, checked vessel totals against quotas, and separately bounded the long-run market gap with scientific catch estimates and conservative prices. [Project and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ’s combined project/about page describes both calculations: the 1998–2007 black-market estimate compared scientific catch estimates with legal quotas and priced only the excess, while the 2008–2009 audit queried the chain database for missing fields, harvests above transfers, vessel catches above quota, and repeated minimum-weight values. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/about-project-3/)
- **Generalization**: Treat every regulated commodity chain as conservation-of-mass accounting. Normalize units, join adjacent stages by lot or document ID, estimate plausible conversion loss, and flag output above input, unexplained disappearance, duplicated IDs, exact legal-threshold bunching, and documents with verification-critical fields missing. **[analysis]**

## 4. Looting the Seas II: Spain’s $8 Billion Fish

### Nearly €6 billion in subsidies fuel Spain’s ravenous fleet (2011) — A reconstructed benefits ledger showed public aid continuing to firms and vessels with adverse fishing records

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/nearly-eu6-billion-subsidies-fuel-spains-ravenous-fleet/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ led a two-year investigation with The Sunday Times, El País, EUobserver, HuffPost, and Trouw. The subsidy analysis was an ICIJ project-wide data reconstruction shared with partners, not a single member-outlet story published under the banner. [Project attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/about-project-5/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
- **What they found**:
  - Spain’s fishing industry received more than €5.8 billion in subsidies from 2000 onward, an amount ICIJ calculated as nearly one-third of the sector’s value after combining direct aid, tax advantages, and international fishing-access support. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/nearly-eu6-billion-subsidies-fuel-spains-ravenous-fleet/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
  - More than 80% of the subsidized companies ICIJ identified as having lost appeals of fishing penalties continued to receive public aid. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/nearly-eu6-billion-subsidies-fuel-spains-ravenous-fleet/)
  - Companies associated with the Vidal family received at least €8.2 million despite more than 40 allegations of illegal fishing; Pescanova received more than €175 million; and an Albacora company received €1.8 million after an affiliated vessel incurred a $5 million U.S. penalty. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/nearly-eu6-billion-subsidies-fuel-spains-ravenous-fleet/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Public-aid recidivism** — grants, subsidies, contracts, tax benefits, or other public support continue after an authoritative adverse finding against the beneficiary or a controlled vessel/entity.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Subsidy awards — government administrative records, obtained from multiple levels**: European Commission Directorate-General for Maritime Affairs and Fisheries data, Spain’s agriculture and fisheries ministry, five regional governments, the official state gazette, and Fishsubsidy.org supplied paid awards and file numbers. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
  - **Indirect aid — public budget and policy records, modeled conservatively**: fuel-consumption reports and tax differentials estimated fuel-tax support; EU fishing-partnership agreements and vessel lists attributed access payments to Spanish vessels. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
  - **Adverse records — court and regulator documents, matched to beneficiaries**: final appellate rulings and EU/U.S. enforcement records established the subset in which allegations had survived appeal or penalties were final. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/nearly-eu6-billion-subsidies-fuel-spains-ravenous-fleet/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
  - **Economic denominator — official statistics**: sector gross value added, excluding processing, supplied the denominator for the “nearly one-third” comparison. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The analysis depends on public subsidy, gazette, court, enforcement, partnership, vessel, tax, and sector-value records. Exact historical files may now require archival requests, but no insider leak or closed platform was intrinsic to the join. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
- **Detection signature**: **Subsidy-to-sanction continuation join** — paid subsidy rows joined to final court and enforcement records on **beneficiary/company group + vessel + award/adverse-decision date** revealed firms that kept receiving aid after adverse findings; aggregating all aid categories and comparing them with sector gross value added revealed the scale of dependence. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
- **Corroboration structure**: ICIJ reconciled overlapping national, regional, EU, gazette, and nonprofit award lists by official file number; counted paid rather than merely allocated amounts; excluded uncertain categories; and used final appeal outcomes for the strongest wrongdoing comparison. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ publishes a dedicated methodology explaining source-by-source construction, file-number deduplication, conservative fuel-tax and partnership calculations, the use of paid awards, exclusions, and the sector-value denominator. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/subsidy-methodology/)
- **Official impact**: After publication, EU Fisheries Commissioner Maria Damanaki said the European Commission was investigating possible illegalities and misuse of aid by Spanish fishing companies. [ICIJ impact report](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/top-fisheries-official-says-eu-investigating-illegalities-spanish-companies/)
- **Generalization**: Join any public-benefits ledger to final sanctions, judgments, exclusions, recalls, or license actions at both legal-entity and controlled-asset level; flag awards after the adverse date and calculate recurrence, value, and agency concentration. **[analysis]**

### The hake hoax: cheaper fish sold as premium species in Spanish markets (2011) — Blind DNA tests converted retail labels into falsifiable species claims

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-hoax-spanish-markets/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ commissioned and coordinated the test within Spain’s $8 Billion Fish, working with the University of Oviedo; the parent project’s lead publishing partners were The Sunday Times, El País, EUobserver, HuffPost, and Trouw. This is ICIJ’s original test-based reporting within the collaboration. [Project attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/about-project-5/) [DNA methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/)
- **What they found**:
  - Researchers tested 150 retail samples—100 frozen and 50 fresh—and found 8.6% were mislabeled, with cheaper species such as catfish or grenadier sold as hake. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-hoax-spanish-markets/) [DNA methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/)
  - Samples from eight shops were mislabeled in ICIJ’s snapshot; the project distinguished its 8.6% result from an earlier academic study that reported a much higher rate. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-hoax-spanish-markets/)
  - El Corte Inglés commissioned independent retesting, identified one mislabeled 1.4-metric-ton batch, withdrew it, and said it would begin routine genetic testing. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-hoax-spanish-markets/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Label-substance mismatch** — a seller’s product identity, origin, composition, or quality claim is directly contradicted by a blinded physical or laboratory measurement.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Retail sample corpus — investigator-created evidence**: reporters bought fresh and frozen products, recorded store, chain, label, brand, stated species and origin, retained receipts, and documented purchases with notes and video. [DNA methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/)
  - **Blind laboratory testing — commissioned scientific analysis**: the University of Oviedo laboratory received coded samples, used positive and negative controls, amplified the mitochondrial COI marker, and reran positive aliquots. [DNA methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/)
  - **Reference sequence comparison — public scientific database**: researchers compared sequences with GenBank using BLAST and required matches above 99%; two researchers independently reviewed identifications. [DNA methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/)
  - **Vendor retest — independent commercial corroboration**: El Corte Inglés’s own testing confirmed a mislabeled batch and prompted withdrawal. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-hoax-spanish-markets/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive evidence is constructed from publicly purchasable products, recorded labels, an independent laboratory, and a public genetic reference database. It requires funding and chain-of-custody discipline, not privileged access. [DNA methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/)
- **Detection signature**: **Label-to-genome identity diff** — retail label and purchase metadata joined to blinded COI sequence matches on **coded sample ID**, then compared on **claimed species versus genetic species**, revealed an 8.6% mislabeling rate and the implicated vendors. [DNA methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/)
- **Corroboration structure**: Recorded retail metadata preserved the claim; blinding separated the laboratory from vendor identity; controls and repeat aliquots tested laboratory error; two researchers reviewed sequence matches; and a retailer’s independent retest confirmed a batch-level problem. [DNA methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/) [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-hoax-spanish-markets/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ publishes a protocol-level account covering sampling, metadata, blinding, preservation, extraction, PCR, controls, repeat testing, BLAST thresholds, and independent review. The sample is a market snapshot rather than a probability sample of all Spanish fish sales, so the 8.6% rate should not be generalized beyond the tested corpus. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-dna-testing-how-we-did-it/)
- **Official impact**: El Corte Inglés withdrew the independently confirmed 1.4-ton batch and announced routine genetic testing. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas-ii/hake-hoax-spanish-markets/)
- **Generalization**: Turn a market claim into a blinded test: define the claimed attribute, purchase or sample across vendors, preserve chain of custody, use a validated external reference, and compare claimed with measured identity. The same structure fits pharmaceuticals, fuel composition, food origin, emissions, building materials, and cybersecurity certifications. **[analysis]**

## 5. Looting the Seas III: Plunder in the Pacific

### Lords of the fish (2012) — Rolling nominal quota holders up to merged groups and controlling families revealed that eight groups held 87% of Chile’s jack-mackerel rights

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/lords-fish/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ coordinated Plunder in the Pacific with Chilean lead outlet CIPER, whose Juan Pablo Figueroa Lasch reported this story, alongside partners including Le Monde, the International Herald Tribune, El Mundo, and Trouw. The project received a citation (honorable mention) from the Overseas Press Club’s Whitman Bassow Award judges for international environmental reporting. [Project attribution and award](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/about-project-6/)
- **What they found**:
  - Eight fishing groups controlled 87% of Chile’s jack-mackerel quota, and six of those groups were controlled by families. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/lords-fish/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
  - The Angelini-controlled Orizon and Corpesca group held 29.3% and Blumar held 18.6% after ICIJ consolidated entities that official quota tables presented separately. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
  - In 2009, government scientists recommended a 750,000-metric-ton catch, the government proposed 1.4 million tons, and the National Fishing Council—60% of whose members represented industry—approved the higher figure. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/lords-fish/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Fragmented-rights concentration** — licenses, quotas, contracts, or assets appear dispersed in an official ledger until legal entities are rolled up to merged groups, common controllers, or families.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Quota records — public administrative data**: annual regional and company quota reports supplied nominal holder and allocation. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
  - **Ownership and corporate events — public securities and legal records**: Chilean securities-regulator filings, the official gazette, and company websites supplied parentage, family control, and mergers that quota reports did not consolidate. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
  - **Policy and scientific baselines — public council and government records**: scientific recommendations, government proposals, council composition, and final catch limits established the decision gap. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/lords-fish/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** Official quota reports, securities filings, gazette notices, corporate disclosures, scientific advice, and council records are public. The difficulty is historical entity resolution and merger-aware aggregation, not privileged access. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **Detection signature**: **Beneficial-control quota rollup** — nominal company quota rows joined to securities filings, gazette merger notices, and family ownership on **company identifier/name + effective date + controlling group** revealed eight groups holding 87% of the quota despite more fragmented official presentation. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **Corroboration structure**: Annual quota totals were independently aggregated; entity names were reconciled against regulator and company records; mergers were applied as of the relevant year; and concentration results were checked against the governance structure that set overall catch limits. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/) [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-the-seas/lords-fish/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ explains that three groups had recently merged but the official quota documents continued to list constituent companies separately. The published 87% result therefore depended on a deliberate group-level rollup, not simply summing the largest rows in the government table. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **Official impact**: At the project level, Chile’s Senate voted 26–0 to ratify the South Pacific Regional Fisheries Management Organization treaty after publication; ICIJ reported that Chile, Russia, and South Korea’s ratifications cleared the way for binding regional quotas. [ICIJ impact report](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/key-vote-clears-way-stop-fish-plundering-south-pacific/)
- **Generalization**: Recompute concentration after resolving every nominal rights holder to ultimate parent, common controller, family, investment sponsor, or post-merger group as of the allocation date. Compare nominal and beneficial-owner Herfindahl or top-N shares and flag agencies whose published tables conceal that difference. **[analysis]**

### Peru’s vanishing fish (2012) — Comparing two mandatory measurements of the same landings exposed 630,000 metric tons disappearing between vessel declaration and plant scale

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ coordinated the project; Peruvian lead outlet IDL-Reporteros and reporter Milagros Salazar led this investigation, with the wider Plunder in the Pacific partnership including Le Monde, the International Herald Tribune, El Mundo, and Trouw. This is partner-led local reporting inside an ICIJ-coordinated data project, not a story whose provenance is solely ICIJ staff. [Project attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/about-project-6/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **What they found**:
  - In more than 100,000 anchoveta landing records from 2009 through July 2011, 52% showed a difference greater than 10% between the vessel’s declared catch and the processing plant’s scale weight. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
  - The discrepancy totaled about 630,000 metric tons, which ICIJ valued at roughly $200 million using official fish prices. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/)
  - Ten companies controlled more than 60% of Peru’s anchoveta rights; eight repeatedly recorded large discrepancies, including reported discrepancy rates of 90% for Humacare and 88% for Exalmar in the analyzed records. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/)
  - A 2009 government scale inspection found serious manipulation in 31% of inspected equipment. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Process-stage shrinkage** — a regulated quantity contracts implausibly between two adjacent mandatory measurements, with the loss benefiting the party controlling the second stage through extra quota, lower taxes, lower royalties, or lower revenue sharing.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Landing-level paired measurements — non-public administrative database, obtained from sources**: after repeated freedom-of-information denials, reporters obtained more than 100,000 government records containing vessel-declared and plant-scale weights for the same landings. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
  - **Scale inspections — government records, manually entered**: a 2009 inspection report supplied equipment-level manipulation findings that corroborated the direction of the landing discrepancies. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
  - **Quota and ownership records — public administrative and corporate material**: allocation records established which companies controlled fishing rights and enabled recurrence to be rolled up by company. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/)
  - **Price records — official data**: government fish prices supplied the conservative $200 million valuation. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/)
  - **Field reporting — first-hand inspection and interviews**: reporters observed landings and interviewed fishers, plant personnel, officials, and experts about how the two measurements worked and who benefited from underweighting. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(c) leak-dependent.** The decisive row-level diff required a government landing database that authorities denied under freedom-of-information law and sources supplied. The nearest public substitute is aggregate ministry catch and export data combined with public quota records, inspection reports, AIS/port-call data, plant production, and company accounts. Those sources can identify system-level inconsistencies and risky plants, but they lose the landing ID that pairs the vessel declaration with the plant scale, so they cannot reproduce the 52%, 630,000-ton, or company recurrence calculations precisely. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **Detection signature**: **Two-stage weighing shrinkage** — vessel-declared catch joined to processing-plant scale weight on **landing/vessel + plant + landing date**, then filtered at a greater-than-10% discrepancy and rolled up by company, revealed 630,000 metric tons and roughly $200 million missing between adjacent controls. [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **Corroboration structure**: The paired landing records established scale and recurrence; the independent scale-inspection report supplied a plausible mechanism; quota and corporate records identified beneficiaries; official prices valued the gap; and observations and interviews tested workflow, incentives, and alternative explanations such as ordinary measurement loss. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/perus-vanishing-fish/) [Methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ says it excluded discrepancies of 10% or less, manually entered the 2009 inspection, aggregated larger discrepancies by company, and applied official prices. The methodology also discloses the failed public-record requests and source-obtained database, which is why this is classed as leak-dependent rather than merely hard-to-obtain public data. [ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/looting-seas-iii/methodology-behind-numbers/)
- **Generalization**: Identify processes that measure the same object twice—mine mouth versus mill, manifest versus customs scale, hospital order versus administration, warehouse receipt versus sale, meter input versus billed output. Pair the records, set a domain-justified tolerance, determine who controls each measurement, and rank repeated directional discrepancies by beneficiary. **[analysis]**

## 6. The Water Barons

### Water and politics: The fall of Suharto and Jakarta’s no-bid privatization (2003) — Contracts, political directives, and performance records reconstructed how crony-linked firms acquired the city’s water system while public risk and weak oversight persisted

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
- **Partner/awards**: The Water Barons was produced by ICIJ at the Center for Public Integrity; this story is credited to Andreas Harsono on ICIJ’s project site, and the page does not identify a separate lead member outlet. Attribution is therefore ICIJ/Center original reporting rather than a member-outlet story republished under the project banner. [Project hub](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/) [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
- **What they found**:
  - Thames Water and Suez-linked ventures partnered with a Suharto son and a Suharto associate, respectively, and divided Jakarta’s water system without competitive bidding or public consultation. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
  - A $92 million World Bank loan approved in 1991 financed water-system improvements; World Bank documents reviewed by ICIJ described the program as facilitating later private-sector participation. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
  - The 1997 concessions limited access to the private operators’ finances, contained no clear performance sanctions described by the story, required the public utility PAM Jaya to absorb shortfalls caused by delayed tariff increases, and required bidding only for contracts above $5 million. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
  - By 2001 the operators reported roughly 620,000 connections versus a projected 711,000, while around 70% of Jakarta residents still lacked running water; ICIJ also found that rupiah-denominated investment could meet the nominal local target while equating to only about $100 million against a promised $318 million because of currency depreciation and limited financial visibility. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Concession capture** — politically advantaged private parties obtain monopoly or quasi-monopoly control under a contract that limits public visibility, shifts downside risk to the state or users, or weakens sanctions for missed performance.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Concession contracts — obtained primary documents**: the 1997 agreements supplied exclusivity, procurement, tariff, oversight, investment, and performance terms. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
  - **Political directives and correspondence — obtained government and company documents**: Suharto-era meeting and instruction records traced the selection of foreign firms and local partners outside a competitive process. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
  - **Development-finance records — public institutional documents**: World Bank loan and project reports established the public-finance timeline and the stated transition toward privatization. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
  - **Ownership and financial disclosures — public corporate research**: company annual reports, filings, websites, and local corporate material identified the foreign and local ownership chain. [ICIJ project methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/methodology-water-barons/)
  - **Performance and price records — utility/contract records and interviews**: connection counts, investment values, tariff changes, service coverage, and interviews supplied actual outcomes against contractual promises. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(c) leak-dependent.** The decisive reconstruction relied on obtained political directives, correspondence, and concession documents that exposed the no-bid selection and risk-allocation clauses. The nearest public substitute is World Bank project material, corporate filings and registries, municipal rate/performance reports, and later court or arbitration records. It can reconstruct financing, ownership, rates, and outcomes, but may lose the direct Suharto selection trail and original contract clauses if those agreements are not publicly archived. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
- **Detection signature**: **Concession promise/control-rights reconstruction** — concession clauses and political directives joined to World Bank financing, local-partner ownership, tariffs, investment, and service metrics on **concession + counterparty + obligation + due date** revealed no-bid entry, public assumption of downside risk, limited financial oversight, and missed service promises. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
- **Corroboration structure**: Political records established selection; signed terms established enforceable rights and risk; World Bank files established the public-finance chronology; ownership records resolved the cronies and foreign operators; and performance, price, and interview evidence tested promises against outcomes. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ’s general Water Barons methodology describes using corporate annual reports, securities filings, company websites, paper lobbying records, and a relationship matrix. The Jakarta-specific document assembly is **[inferred]** from the story’s explicit citations to obtained contracts, meeting documents, correspondence, World Bank records, and performance data; ICIJ does not publish a separate Jakarta calculation protocol. [ICIJ project methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/methodology-water-barons/) [Story basis](https://www.icij.org/investigations/waterbarons/water-and-politics-fall-suharto/)
- **Generalization**: Convert a concession into a table of promises, rights, risk transfers, reporting rights, sanctions, and due dates; resolve every local partner and financier; then compare actual rates, investment, coverage, and public payments with the contractual baseline. **[analysis]**

## 7. The Illicit Trade of Coltan

### Colombia’s black-market coltan tied to drug traffickers and paramilitaries (2012) — Permits, export forms, seizures, sanctions, and field reporting showed how mineral from a protected conflict zone could approach the legal supply chain

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ coordinated reporting across six countries; Colombian reporting involved Ignacio Gómez and lead outlet Noticias Uno, with project partners including El Universal, Armando.info, Noticias Uno, and El Espectador. This was an ICIJ-coordinated investigation with named local leads, not an unattributed member-outlet republication. [Project attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/about-project-illicit-trade-coltan/)
- **What they found**:
  - Reporters documented an illegal coltan mine inside Colombia’s Puinawai National Park where Indigenous men, women, and children worked; miners and regional sources alleged that guerrillas controlled extraction and armed or criminal networks controlled routes, allegations the story attributes to those sources rather than presenting as adjudicated fact. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
  - A mine associated with the Cifuentes Villa family was connected in U.S. indictment and Treasury records to the Sinaloa cartel; Colombian authorities had seized 83 metric tons associated with the operation since 2010 and revoked its license. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
  - Tax export forms showed the only local permit holder, César Rodríguez, exporting coltan in 2011 to Traxys in Luxembourg; the military separately seized 17 metric tons from the protected park, and a law-enforcement source said its mineral concentrations resembled material associated with Rodríguez, while Traxys denied buying illegal material and the story did not state that the company was suspected of wrongdoing. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
  - Rodríguez’s license contained the name of public official Mario Jiménez; Colombia’s geological service told ICIJ the appearance of the name was an error. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Conflict-commodity laundering** — material from a prohibited, conflict-affected, or criminally controlled origin is relabeled or aggregated through a permitted actor so origin disappears before export or processing.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Mining licenses and tax export forms — public/government primary records**: permits established the nominal legal source and export forms supplied exporter, foreign buyer, commodity, and date. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
  - **Criminal and sanctions records — public primary records**: U.S. indictments and Treasury kingpin designations linked named actors to alleged or sanctioned trafficking networks. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
  - **Seizure and mineral-composition evidence — law-enforcement records and source account**: seizure totals, origin locations, license actions, and a source’s comparison of mineral concentrations supplied a possible bridge from protected-area ore to a licensed channel; the composition link remained source-attributed rather than a published independent laboratory result. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
  - **Field observation and interviews — first-hand reporting**: reporters visited mining and trading locations and interviewed miners, Indigenous residents, traders, law enforcement, officials, exporters, and buyers. [Project attribution](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/about-project-illicit-trade-coltan/) [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The core legal-channel anomaly can be rerun from licenses, export forms, park boundaries, seizure records, public indictments, Treasury designations, and corporate research, supplemented by lawful field observation. The non-public law-enforcement source strengthens the ore-origin bridge but is not necessary to surface the permitted-exporter/prohibited-origin risk pattern; without it, that particular composition similarity must remain unverified. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
- **Detection signature**: **Origin-legality chain reconstruction** — mining permits and export forms joined to protected-area seizures, license actions, and criminal/sanctions records on **permit holder/exporter + mine origin + shipment date + foreign buyer** revealed how a scarce legal title could serve as an apparent gateway for ore originating outside the licensed chain. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
- **Corroboration structure**: Field observation established active illegal extraction; permits and tax forms established the formal export route; seizures and park locations established illicit supply; U.S. primary records established named criminal links; and responses from authorities and Traxys bounded what could and could not be asserted about the buyer and the license anomaly. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ’s project page says the team spent months reporting in six countries and used government records, court documents, mining permits, export records, field visits, and interviews. No standalone computation page exists; the chain reconstruction above is **[inferred]** from the story’s document sequence and explicitly attributed source types. [ICIJ project methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/about-project-illicit-trade-coltan/) [Story basis](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombias-black-market-coltan-tied-drug-traffickers-paramilitaries/)
- **Official impact**: After the investigation, Colombian President Juan Manuel Santos announced plans to make coltan a strategic mineral reserve and to clean up its mining and trade. [ICIJ impact report](https://www.icij.org/investigations/coltan/colombia-vows-clean-coltan-mining/)
- **Generalization**: For any high-risk commodity, compare permitted output capacity with exports and seizures, map protected or sanctioned origins near the legal channel, resolve traders and buyers, and look for a small number of licenses absorbing implausibly broad supply. **[analysis]**

## 8. The Global Climate Change Lobby

### The climate lobby from soup to nuts (2009) — Lobby registrations and successive bill versions exposed a surge of regulated interests seeking delays, free allowances, and weaker near-term targets

- **URL**: [ICIJ story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/climate-lobby-soup-nuts/)
- **Partner/awards**: ICIJ coordinated reporters in eight major economies; this U.S. analysis was produced by ICIJ/Center reporters Marianne Lavelle, M.B. Pell, David Donald, and Dan Ettinger. The wider project used lead local reporting including The Sydney Morning Herald in Australia, but the U.S. story itself is an ICIJ original rather than a member-outlet banner story. [Project attribution and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/about-project-global-climate-change-lobby/)
- **What they found**:
  - ICIJ counted 1,160 groups lobbying the U.S. federal government on climate legislation, including about 140 that entered the issue during the third quarter of 2009. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/climate-lobby-soup-nuts/)
  - Under ICIJ’s stated scenario assigning 10% of a lobbying registrant’s reported total budget to climate work, third-quarter climate lobbying would equal about $30.5 million, 13% higher than the prior quarter; this is a modeled estimate, not a sum of transaction-level climate invoices. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/climate-lobby-soup-nuts/)
  - The House climate bill grew to 1,428 pages, its 2020 emissions-reduction target fell from 20% to 17%, and many free emission allowances continued until 2026 as lawmakers negotiated with affected industries. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/climate-lobby-soup-nuts/)
  - Across the eight economies, ICIJ found public lobby registries only in Australia, Canada, the European Union, and the United States; it reported important coverage limits, including the EU’s voluntary register and Australia’s exclusion of in-house and issue-specific details. [Transparency analysis](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/global-lack-transparency/)
- **Finding type(s)**: **Policy-delay lobbying** — regulated interests accept or avoid directly contesting a policy’s premise while targeting implementation dates, near-term targets, exemptions, free allocations, and technical clauses that reduce immediate cost or effect.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Lobby registrations — public administrative records**: U.S. federal lobbying disclosures supplied registrant, client, broad issue, quarter, and total lobbying budget; the project also scraped Canada’s public registry for climate terms and named bills from 1996 through August 2009. [Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/climate-lobby-soup-nuts/) [Project attribution and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/about-project-global-climate-change-lobby/)

[Project attribution and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/about-project-global-climate-change-lobby/)

[Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/climate-lobby-soup-nuts/)

[Transparency analysis](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/global-lack-transparency/) [Project attribution and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/about-project-global-climate-change-lobby/)
  - **Interviews — first-hand reporting**: the eight-country team conducted more than 200 interviews with lobbyists, officials, companies, advocates, and experts. [Project attribution and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/about-project-global-climate-change-lobby/)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The U.S. count, newcomer analysis, spending scenario, contribution context, and bill-version comparison use public filings and legislative documents. Reproducing countries without complete mandatory registers remains a coverage problem, but not a leak or closed-platform dependency for this story. [Project attribution and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/about-project-global-climate-change-lobby/) [Transparency analysis](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/global-lack-transparency/)

[Story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/climate-lobby-soup-nuts/) [Project attribution and methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/global-climate-change-lobby/about-project-global-climate-change-lobby/)

### 2. Detection-signature ontology and frequency

These are non-exclusive analytic families; the story-specific signature remains the more precise implementation.

| Detection-signature family | Frequency | Included stories | Core operation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Record-to-reality reconciliation** | 5/11 | Fatal Extraction; Deforestation certification; hake DNA; Peru landings; Jakarta water | Compare a formal disclosure, certificate, label, measurement, or promise with independently observed conduct or outcomes. |
| **Beneficial-control rollup** | 3/11 | Fatal Extraction; Myanmar teak; Chile quotas | Resolve nominal operators, intermediaries, or allocation holders to parents, state links, merged groups, or families before aggregating. |
| **Regulated-chain mass balance** | 3/11 | Bluefin; Myanmar teak; Peru landings | Join adjacent commodity stages by lot, shipment, catch, landing, or date and test whether quantity, origin, or legality remains possible. |
| **Benefit/assurance-to-adverse-record join** | 3/11 | Deforestation certification; Myanmar teak; Spanish subsidies | Overlay a benefit or trust claim with sanctions, judgments, allegations, or prohibited-source exposure during the relevant validity window. |
| **Policy/contract input-output reconstruction** | 2/11 | Jakarta water; climate lobby | Compare financing, ownership, influence inputs, and promised clauses with final contract/policy text and later outcomes. |
| **Physical identity diff** | 1/11 | Hake DNA | Bind a market claim to a coded sample and compare the claim with an external scientific reference. |
| **Origin-legality chain reconstruction** | 1/11 | Colombian coltan | Compare licensed origin and export paperwork with protected-area supply, seizures, criminal links, and buyer routes. |

The repeated operational grammar is:

1. **Resolve identity** across spelling, subsidiary, vessel, site, license, certificate, and beneficial owner.
2. **Normalize time** so an award, certificate, sanction, merger, shipment, or obligation is evaluated under the rules and control structure in force that day.
3. **Join unlike systems** that describe the same asset, commodity, entity, or obligation.
4. **Test an invariant**: mass should balance; a certified actor should satisfy its standard; a legal origin should have adequate lawful supply; a benefit should honor exclusion rules; a promise should match performance.
5. **Roll up recurrence and value** by beneficiary, parent, controller, auditor, plant, agency, or policy provision.
6. **Seek a second mode of proof**—field observation, laboratory result, judgment, inspection, company response, or independent transaction system. **[analysis]**

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### Pattern candidate A — Assurance–conduct contradiction engine

- **Mechanics**: Build a dated ledger of certificates, accreditations, labels, eligibility determinations, audits, and renewals. Resolve holders to corporate groups. Join to dated sanctions, judgments, inspections, recalls, allegations, physical tests, or provenance failures. Separate allegation, administrative finding, final judgment, and direct physical contradiction.
- **Minimum data**: assurance holder and issuer; standard or claimed attribute; validity dates; entity hierarchy; adverse event date, source, and disposition.
- **Recognition cues in any domain**: certificate renewals after repeated incidents; the same auditor across many adverse clients; active approval during an exclusion period; identical boilerplate findings; physical tests contradicting labels; violations clustered just outside sampled sites.
- **ICIJ exemplars**: Deforestation certification, Myanmar teak, and hake DNA.

#### Pattern candidate B — Regulated-chain conservation audit

- **Mechanics**: Model the commodity or payment as a directed chain. Join adjacent stages by lot/document/asset/date, normalize units, apply a defensible conversion-loss range, and test output greater than input, unexplained shrinkage, duplicate identifiers, exact-threshold bunching, missing fields, impossible timing, and origin discontinuity.
- **Minimum data**: stage identifiers; timestamps; quantity and unit; sender/receiver; governing quota or threshold; expected yield/loss.
- **Recognition cues in any domain**: repeated 9.9% losses below a 10% trigger; harvest above transfer; sale above lawful production capacity; every unit exactly at a legal minimum; missing verification fields concentrated by operator; output appearing before input.
- **ICIJ exemplars**: bluefin catch documents, Myanmar teak shipments, and Peru’s paired landing weights.

#### Pattern candidate C — Beneficial-control concentration rollup

- **Mechanics**: Resolve every nominal license, quota, contract, grant, property, patent, aircraft, vessel, or permit holder through parents, mergers, shared directors, family ownership, and offshore vehicles as of the award date. Recalculate top-N share and concentration before and after resolution.
- **Minimum data**: allocation/asset ledger; legal-entity identifiers; dated ownership and merger edges; controller or family links; allocation value or share.
- **Recognition cues in any domain**: many similarly named holders; recent mergers not reflected in agency tables; shared addresses/directors; nominal competitors controlled by one family; rights repeatedly transferred within a group; published concentration far below resolved concentration.
- **ICIJ exemplars**: ASX/local mine resolution, Myanmar intermediary ownership, and Chile’s eight-group quota result.

#### Pattern candidate D — Public benefit after adverse action

- **Mechanics**: Join grants, contracts, subsidies, tax benefits, licenses, or concession payments to final sanctions, judgments, debarments, license actions, recalls, or repeated serious allegations at both recipient and controlled-asset level. Calculate post-adverse count, value, agency, and time-to-next-award.
- **Minimum data**: beneficiary and controlling group; benefit amount/date/program; adverse party/asset/date/status; exclusion rule if one exists.
- **Recognition cues in any domain**: awards immediately after final judgment; penalties attached to a vessel or subsidiary while the parent continues receiving funds; agencies using inconsistent identifiers; repeat awards from the same office; legal eligibility that diverges from stated policy.
- **ICIJ exemplars**: Spain’s fishing subsidies and Deforestation Inc.’s certification overlap.

#### Pattern candidate E — Origin laundering through a legal gateway

- **Mechanics**: Starting from a sanctioned, protected, conflict-affected, or capacity-constrained origin, map permits and lawful output; join exports and downstream imports; resolve intermediaries and shared control; compare post-restriction shipment timing, volume, product, and certification claims with lawful source capacity.
- **Minimum data**: origin restrictions and effective dates; licensed producers and capacity; shipments or exports; importer/exporter identities; corporate/sanctions edges; product codes.
- **Recognition cues in any domain**: one scarce permit supports implausibly large exports; unsanctioned intermediaries appear immediately after restrictions; supplier names change while directors or addresses persist; certificates remain active despite a prohibited source; export volume exceeds legal production.
- **ICIJ exemplars**: Myanmar teak and Colombian coltan.

## Bottom line

The cluster’s transferable asset is not a topic taxonomy but an analytic grammar: resolve control, align effective dates, join two systems that purport to describe the same thing, test a physical/legal/contractual invariant, and roll the discrepancy up to whoever benefits. The seven open-record-runnable stories show that high-impact environmental reporting does not inherently require leaks. The other four show why provenance must be more precise than “public versus secret”: a leak may only anchor discovery, may supply the indispensable paired rows or clauses, or may be a closed regulator platform whose public aggregate cannot reproduce the decisive chain.
