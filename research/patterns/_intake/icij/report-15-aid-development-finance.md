# ICIJ Aid, Development Finance, and Public Contracting: Evidence Ontology

## Scope and reading rules

This report examines ten investigations selected only by following stories and methodology pages from the five census-supplied ICIJ project pages: [Evicted and Abandoned](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/), [Divine Intervention](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/), [U.S. Aid in Latin America](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/), [Collateral Damage](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/), and [Windfalls of War](https://www.icij.org/investigations/windfalls-war/). The sample deliberately spans all five projects and emphasizes distinct evidence mechanics rather than attempting to summarize all 111 census items.

Two date conventions matter. Several old Center for Public Integrity/ICIJ stories were migrated to ICIJ in 2012. Where the original date can be recovered from ICIJ's legacy materials or the original Center page, the heading gives that date and separately notes the migration date. “Partner” distinguishes an ICIJ- or Center-led investigation from a member-outlet story merely carried under a project banner. “Official impact” reports only a documented institutional response; it does not infer that journalism caused a pre-existing prosecution, audit, or reform.

The free-form finding tags below are observational. Each tag is defined when it first appears and then reused without importing an external taxonomy.

## Story evidence ontologies

### How the World Bank Broke Its Promise to Protect the Poor (2015) — A reconstructed project ledger showed that Bank-financed development displaced about 3.4 million people while routinely failing to document what happened to them

- **URL**: [ICIJ, “How the World Bank Broke Its Promise to Protect the Poor”](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/how-world-bank-broke-its-promise-protect-poor/)
- **Partner/awards**: This was an **ICIJ-coordinated** investigation led by ICIJ and HuffPost, with The GroundTruth Project, The Investigative Fund, The Guardian, and more than 20 other news organizations; ICIJ says more than 50 journalists reported in 21 countries ([project credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/about-project-evicted-and-abandoned/)). It won the Online News Association's Al Neuharth Innovation in Investigative Journalism Award ([ICIJ award notice](https://www.icij.org/inside-icij/2015/09/evicted-and-abandoned-investigation-wins-top-award/)).
- **What they found**:
  - World Bank-financed projects physically or economically displaced an estimated **3,350,449 people** in nearly 1,000 projects approved from 2004 through 2013 ([ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/thousands-world-bank-documents-unique-new-database/)).
  - The Bank's own 2014 review found that 60% of a sampled set of projects lacked documentation of what happened after displacement and 70% lacked documentation of complaints or their resolution ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/how-world-bank-broke-its-promise-protect-poor/)).
  - The value of projects carrying the Bank's highest social or environmental risk ratings exceeded **$50 billion from 2009 through 2013**, more than twice the value in the preceding five years ([ICIJ key findings](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/key-findings-10/)).
  - The investigation paired that system-level estimate with field reporting showing homes, livelihoods, and access to land lost in countries including Ethiopia, India, Kenya, and Peru ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/how-world-bank-broke-its-promise-protect-poor/)).
- **Finding type(s)**:
  - `safeguard-performance-gap` — a formal protection or oversight rule exists, but project records and observed outcomes show that execution failed.
  - `administrative-undercount` — fragmented or missing administrative records conceal the aggregate scale of affected people, money, or events.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Project-approval and safeguard documents — public administrative records**: reporters downloaded World Bank project pages and resettlement documents available on the Bank's website; ICIJ says all but a few documents were public, with 43 additional documents supplied directly by the Bank ([ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/thousands-world-bank-documents-unique-new-database/)).
  - **Resettlement Action Plans and completion reports — public implementation records**: the team extracted affected-person counts from planning documents and, for closed projects without an accessible plan, from Implementation Completion and Results reports ([ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/thousands-world-bank-documents-unique-new-database/)).
  - **Internal Bank review — publicly reported accountability record**: a 2014 internal review supplied the percentages of projects missing follow-through and complaint documentation ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/how-world-bank-broke-its-promise-protect-poor/)).
  - **Field interviews and observation — original reporting**: the project interviewed hundreds of people and reported on affected communities in multiple countries ([ICIJ project credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/about-project-evicted-and-abandoned/)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive population ledger was reconstructed from public World Bank project, resettlement, and completion files. An outside investigator could rerun the core method today, although changed URLs, withdrawn attachments, and incomplete follow-through records would require archival capture and explicit missingness handling ([ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/thousands-world-bank-documents-unique-new-database/)).
- **Detection signature**: **Fragmented-ledger denominator reconstruction** — World Bank project approvals were joined to Resettlement Action Plans and completion reports on project identifier; affected-person fields were normalized and summed, and the result was compared with follow-through documentation on the same projects, revealing both the 3.35-million-person scale and the institution's outcome-record gap ([ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/thousands-world-bank-documents-unique-new-database/)).
- **Corroboration structure**: The aggregate rested on project-level Bank documents; the Bank's internal review independently corroborated the recordkeeping failure; interviews and field visits tested whether the documentary safeguards matched lived outcomes. ICIJ distinguished “confirmed” from “possible” displacement—43% versus 57% of the database—rather than treating every planning estimate as a completed eviction ([ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/thousands-world-bank-documents-unique-new-database/)).
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ's methodology says reporters “manually review[ed] more than 4,000 files and 1,500 projects,” then classified projects by status and disclosure quality ([ICIJ data methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/thousands-world-bank-documents-unique-new-database/)). That transparent denominator construction is the methodological center of the story.
- **Official impact**: After the investigation and continued accountability pressure, the Bank adopted a 2020 package that added independent monitoring of action plans, a dispute-resolution service, and a longer complaint window; the source documents the reform but does not isolate ICIJ's causal share ([ICIJ impact report](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/world-bank-amplifies-accountability-to-communities-affected-by-its-projects/)).
- **Generalization**: The same pattern appears wherever an institution publishes plans and approvals but not outcomes—housing clearance, conservation resettlement, hospital closures, school consolidation, disaster rebuilding, or environmental remediation. A generic detector inventories all approved projects, resolves all attached implementation documents, normalizes the promised and affected populations, and measures both outcome variance and missing follow-through by project.

### New Evidence Ties World Bank to Human Rights Abuses in Ethiopia (2015) — Former officials linked Bank-supported local budgets to a coercive resettlement campaign affecting hundreds of thousands

- **URL**: [ICIJ, “New Evidence Ties World Bank to Human Rights Abuses in Ethiopia”](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)
- **Partner/awards**: This was part of the **ICIJ-coordinated Evicted and Abandoned** collaboration led by ICIJ and HuffPost; it was not a separately branded member-outlet story ([project credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/about-project-evicted-and-abandoned/)). The project-level Online News Association award applies to the collaboration ([ICIJ award notice](https://www.icij.org/inside-icij/2015/09/evicted-and-abandoned-investigation-wins-top-award/)).
- **What they found**:
  - Ethiopia's “villagization” campaign contemplated moving **1.9 million people**; the government said **37,883 families** had been relocated in Gambella by the end of 2013 ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
  - Former Gambella governor Omot Obang Olom told reporters that roughly **$10 million over two years** was diverted from the World Bank-supported Protection of Basic Services program to villagization; a former district official separately described the diversion of about **$1,500** intended for a primary school ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
  - Human Rights Watch had documented at least seven credible reports of people dying after beatings during forced relocations, while refugees interviewed by reporters described beatings, rape, and destruction of homes ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
  - The Bank approved **$420 million** for the basic-services program in February 2011, **$600 million** the day after a September 2012 complaint was filed, and another **$350 million** in March 2015; its Inspection Panel found an “operational link” between the program and villagization but stopped short of attributing the abuses to Bank financing ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
- **Finding type(s)**:
  - `fungible-aid-diversion` — nominally earmarked aid supports an adjacent harmful program through shared budgets, personnel, or facilities even when the donor did not expressly fund the harmful line item.
  - `safeguard-performance-gap`.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Former-government-official interviews — insider discovery**: two former Ethiopian officials independently described budget diversion and local implementation; these interviews supplied the non-public discovery anchor ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
  - **World Bank approvals and program documents — public administrative records**: the award dates and values established the scale and timing of continued support ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
  - **Inspection Panel complaint, report, and expert material — lender-accountability files**: the complaint and review established the affected program, geography, safeguards, and the Panel's operational-link finding ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
  - **Human Rights Watch documentation — independently gathered human-rights evidence**: prior interviews and incident documentation corroborated the coercive character and physical harm ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
  - **Refugee interviews — victim testimony**: first-person accounts tested the institutional record against events on the ground ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(b) re-anchoring.** Discovery of the alleged diversion required insider testimony from former Ethiopian officials. Verification could be rerun on public World Bank approval records, the Inspection Panel complaint/report, program geography, and Human Rights Watch's public findings. Those public materials establish contemporaneous funding, operational linkage, and documented abuses, but **do not independently prove the precise $10 million diversion** ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
- **Detection signature**: **Budget-fungibility triangulation** — World Bank program disbursements were compared with villagization place and timing on the key of Gambella/program period, then joined to independent former-official descriptions of budget orders and the Inspection Panel's operational-link finding, revealing a plausible channel from nominal basic-services aid to coercive resettlement ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
- **Corroboration structure**: Two insiders described related diversion mechanics; public award records fixed the money and dates; the Inspection Panel independently found an operational link; Human Rights Watch and refugee interviews corroborated the abuse environment. The limits matter: the Panel did not conclude that Bank money paid for abuses, and the exact diversion amount depends on insider evidence ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)).
- **Methodology notes**: **[inferred]** ICIJ did not publish a story-specific “how we did it” page. The article itself shows a triangulation workflow: identify two officials with direct budget knowledge, check their accounts against each other, reconstruct the public funding chronology, and test the alleged harm against Inspection Panel and Human Rights Watch records ([ICIJ Ethiopia story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/new-evidence-ties-world-bank-human-rights-abuses-ethiopia/)). The project's general credits confirm field reporting and document review across the collaboration ([project credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/about-project-evicted-and-abandoned/)).
- **Official impact**: No story-specific official remedy is identified on the story page. At project level, later World Bank accountability reforms increased monitoring and complaint options, but the available source does not attribute a distinct Ethiopia remedy to this article ([ICIJ impact report](https://www.icij.org/investigations/world-bank/world-bank-amplifies-accountability-to-communities-affected-by-its-projects/)).
- **Generalization**: Fungibility is common in education, humanitarian, climate, policing, and military assistance when the recipient uses a shared treasury, payroll, vehicles, or facilities. A generic detector aligns donor disbursement dates and permitted purposes with recipient budget execution, staffing, procurement, and harmful-program milestones, then looks for geographic and temporal overlap that cannot be explained by the nominal budget label.

### Bush's AIDS Initiative: Too Little Choice, Too Much Ideology (2006) — Grant records and field reporting showed PEPFAR conditions shifting prevention money toward abstinence programs and away from locally measured risk

- **URL**: [ICIJ, “Bush's AIDS Initiative: Too Little Choice, Too Much Ideology”](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)
- **Partner/awards**: This was an **ICIJ-led investigation under its then-parent, the Center for Public Integrity**, involving about a dozen reporters in eight countries; the project page does not name a member outlet as lead ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/behind-scenes-questions-lawsuits-and-eventually-some-answers/)).
- **What they found**:
  - The President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief committed **$15 billion over five years** in 15 focus countries, while Congress required one-third of prevention spending to support abstinence and fidelity activities and recipients faced an anti-prostitution pledge ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)).
  - Prevention's share of the program fell from **34% in 2004 to 22% in 2006** ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)).
  - A **$10 million** award went to the Children's AIDS Fund even though an expert review panel rated its proposal “not suitable” for funding ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)).
  - In Ethiopia's 2006–07 plan, about **$110,000** was assigned to condom promotion along a major transport corridor while abstinence-and-fidelity funding was nearly five times larger; the latter grew 63% from the prior year versus 28% for condoms ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)).
- **Finding type(s)**:
  - `conditionality-misalignment` — statutory, contractual, or ideological grant conditions redirect resources away from the risk distribution or service mix documented in the target population.
  - `administrative-undercount`.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **PEPFAR and USAID grant records — FOIA and litigation-produced administrative data**: ICIJ sought prime and subaward data, received 2004–05 program records under a court schedule, and separately retained usable USAID central-award data ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/behind-scenes-questions-lawsuits-and-eventually-some-answers/); [ICIJ USAID award table](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/funds-awarded-us-agency-international-development/)).
  - **Country Operational Plans — agency planning records**: activity-level allocations supplied the country, year, intervention, and amount needed to compare abstinence, condom, treatment, and care priorities ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/behind-scenes-questions-lawsuits-and-eventually-some-answers/)).
  - **Authorizing law and grant conditions — public legal records**: the prevention earmark and anti-prostitution condition established the policy constraint against which allocations were assessed ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)).
  - **Expert review and government oversight material — program-integrity records**: proposal ratings and Government Accountability Office findings tested whether award selection and program operations matched stated technical criteria ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)).
  - **More than 100 interviews and eight-country field reporting — original reporting**: public-health specialists, grantees, officials, and people in affected communities supplied operational context not visible in the award rows ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/behind-scenes-questions-lawsuits-and-eventually-some-answers/)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive evidence consisted of agency grants, Country Operational Plans, statutes, proposal-review material, and oversight records obtained through public-record requests and litigation. They were difficult and expensive to obtain, but no leak was required. A rerun would need to preserve the original program categories and distinguish prime awards from subawards, a limitation ICIJ explicitly encountered ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/behind-scenes-questions-lawsuits-and-eventually-some-answers/)).
- **Detection signature**: **Conditionality-to-risk mismatch** — PEPFAR/USAID activity amounts were grouped by country, year, and prevention mode, then compared with statutory conditions and local epidemiological or target-risk evidence on the same country-year, revealing allocation growth driven by abstinence requirements rather than the observed exposure profile ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)).
- **Corroboration structure**: Award and operational-plan records established allocation; law established the imposed condition; review and GAO material tested selection quality; public-health data and interviews assessed fit to local need. The field layer turned a budget-category anomaly into evidence about service availability and risk ([ICIJ main story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/bushs-aids-initiative-too-little-choice-too-much-ideology/)).
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ reports filing “about two dozen Freedom of Information Act requests,” suing State, HHS, and USAID, reviewing thousands of pages, and conducting more than 100 interviews ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/behind-scenes-questions-lawsuits-and-eventually-some-answers/)). It also documented material data defects: one release contained errors, some program funding was redacted, and USAID rejected part of the country-level data because subawards outnumbered prime awards by more than 100 to one ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/behind-scenes-questions-lawsuits-and-eventually-some-answers/)).
- **Official impact**: The public-record lawsuits produced records on a court schedule and ended in a settlement, a concrete transparency impact; the cited pages do not establish that the reporting itself changed PEPFAR's substantive policy ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/divine-intervention/behind-scenes-questions-lawsuits-and-eventually-some-answers/)).
- **Generalization**: This detector transfers to school grants, reproductive health, homelessness, climate aid, and workforce programs whenever a mandated spending category can be compared with a measured need distribution. The generic test calculates category shares and growth by place and year, then compares them with prevalence, eligibility, performance, or expert-review scores.

### U.S.-Trained Forces Linked to Human Rights Abuses (2001; ICIJ archive page dated 2012) — Training records connected U.S.-supported Mexican special-forces personnel to a torture-and-killing case

- **URL**: [ICIJ archive, “U.S.-Trained Forces Linked to Human Rights Abuses”](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)
- **Partner/awards**: This was a **Center for Public Integrity/ICIJ-led** story from the original U.S. Aid in Latin America project, not a member-outlet contribution. The project credits name reporters from across the region, including Ignacio Gómez, Ángel Páez, Leonarda Reyes, Fernando Rodrigues, Frank Smyth, Laura Peterson, and André Verlöy ([ICIJ project credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/about-project-us-aid-latin-america/)). The 2012 ICIJ date is a migration date; the reporting appears in the project's July 2001 legacy compilation ([Center/ICIJ legacy report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/legacy_projects/pdf_reports/CROSSINGBORDERS.pdf)).
- **What they found**:
  - The United States trained **416 members** of Mexico's elite Air-mobile Special Forces Groups, known as GAFE, from November 1996 through September 1998, mostly at Fort Bragg ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
  - More than **4,000 Mexican military and police personnel** received U.S. training beginning in 1996, compared with 512 from 1984 through 1992; Mexico received **$152.7 million** in U.S. military and police assistance from 1997 through 2000 ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
  - In the Ocotán case, 20 young men were detained and tortured and Salvador Jiménez died; 27 GAFE personnel were charged, and **six of those implicated had received U.S. training** ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
  - Mexico's national human-rights commission received **2,088 military-related complaints** from 1990 to February 2001 but issued only 24 recommendations for prosecution ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
- **Finding type(s)**:
  - `security-assistance-harm-chain` — training, equipment, or funding records can be resolved to a recipient unit or person who later appears in a documented abuse case.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **U.S. training and aid records — public or agency-supplied security-assistance records**: program counts, dates, locations, equipment, and spending established the support side of the link ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
  - **Mexican criminal and military case records — legal records**: charges, convictions, sentences, and defendant identities established the abuse side of the join ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
  - **Jalisco Human Rights Commission files — accountability records and victim testimony**: case investigation and witness accounts documented detention, torture, and death ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
  - **Pentagon and official responses — on-record confirmation**: U.S. officials confirmed the training relationship and addressed the human-rights-screening problem ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive link is a roster-to-case join between government training records and named legal defendants. It does not require a leak. A present-day rerun would be limited where training rosters identify only units, host-country court files are inaccessible, or names require transliteration and alias resolution ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
- **Detection signature**: **Trainer-to-abuse unit join** — U.S. training records were joined to Mexican case defendants on person and GAFE unit, revealing that six personnel implicated in the Ocotán abuses had received U.S. training ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
- **Corroboration structure**: Training records established exposure; legal and commission files established the incident and personnel; victim accounts established conduct; Pentagon acknowledgment tested the identity match. The broader complaint statistics showed that the named case sat within a larger accountability deficit but did not prove that training caused abuse ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
- **Methodology notes**: **[inferred]** No story-specific methodology page is preserved in the ICIJ project. The article demonstrates a record-linkage method: enumerate trained units/personnel, identify human-rights cases involving the same unit, resolve the defendant names, and seek responses from both governments ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
- **Official impact**: The story describes prosecutions and U.S. screening concerns, but these are evidence in the reported chronology rather than a documented response caused by publication; no post-publication official impact is claimed ([ICIJ GAFE story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-trained-forces-linked-human-rights-abuses/)).
- **Generalization**: The pattern extends to police academies, border forces, private security, export-controlled surveillance tools, and corporate compliance training. A generic detector resolves trainee names and unit lineage, joins them to complaints, sanctions, disciplinary cases, and court dockets, and compares incident dates with training and equipment delivery.

### U.S. Shrugged Off Corruption, Abuse in Service of Drug War (2001; ICIJ archive page dated 2012) — Insider evidence about CIA payments was re-anchored to public warnings showing aid continued after U.S. officials knew of Vladimiro Montesinos's abuses

- **URL**: [ICIJ archive, “U.S. Shrugged Off Corruption, Abuse in Service of Drug War”](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)
- **Partner/awards**: This was a **Center for Public Integrity/ICIJ-led** investigation by Ángel Páez, not a member-outlet banner story. The original July 12, 2001 date appears in the project's legacy compilation; the ICIJ web page was migrated in 2012 ([Center/ICIJ legacy report](https://cloudfront-files-1.publicintegrity.org/legacy_projects/pdf_reports/CROSSINGBORDERS.pdf)).
- **What they found**:
  - Confidential U.S. and Peruvian sources said the CIA supplied intelligence chief Vladimiro Montesinos with at least **$10 million from 1990 through September 2000**, money he controlled and allegedly diverted ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
  - Peruvian prosecutors estimated Montesinos accumulated a personal fortune of **$264 million**; the public record also tied his network to surveillance of political opponents and the diversion of **10,000 AK-47 rifles** from Jordan to Colombia's FARC ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
  - State Department reporting raised concerns by 1997 and a U.S. Senate hearing did so in 1999, yet U.S. aid to Peru was about **$42 million in 2000** and the administration proposed **$49.18 million for 2002**, an 82% increase ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
- **Finding type(s)**:
  - `trusted-intermediary-betrayal` — a recipient or operational partner uses donor support, access, or tolerated power against the donor's stated mission or protected population.
  - `security-assistance-harm-chain`.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Confidential U.S. and Peruvian sources — insider discovery**: sources with knowledge of the relationship supplied the CIA-payment amount and control mechanism ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
  - **Peruvian prosecutorial and court material — public legal records**: asset estimates, charges, testimony, and related proceedings anchored the corruption and arms-trafficking claims ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).

  - **State Department reports, Senate record, and aid budgets — public government records**: these established what U.S. institutions knew and when, and whether support continued afterward ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(b) re-anchoring.** Discovery of the CIA's reported $10 million relationship depended on confidential sources. The reusable public half consists of Peruvian court and prosecutorial records, the videotapes, State Department reporting, Senate proceedings, and U.S. aid budgets. Those records verify corruption, abuse, official warning, and continued aid, but they do **not** independently establish the classified payment's exact amount or terms ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
- **Detection signature**: **Ally-record contradiction timeline** — insider claims about CIA support were re-anchored to public court evidence, videotapes, State Department warnings, Senate scrutiny, and annual aid amounts on actor and date, revealing that support continued and was proposed to increase after serious warning signals were in the public U.S. record ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
- **Corroboration structure**: Confidential sources established the hidden relationship; judicial evidence and videotapes established the recipient's misconduct; government reports and hearings fixed notice; budget records established continued material support. The public record corroborates the contradiction but not the confidential cash figure ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
- **Methodology notes**: **[inferred]** No separate ICIJ methodology page survives. The report's method is a knowledge-and-support chronology: date confidential relationship claims, independently date public misconduct and official warnings, then compare them with aid decisions ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
- **Official impact**: Montesinos's flight, capture, and prosecution arose from the pre-publication collapse of the Fujimori government and public videotapes; the story does not claim those events as its impact ([ICIJ Montesinos story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/us-aid-latin-america/us-shrugged-corruption-abuse-service-drug-war/)).
- **Generalization**: This sequence applies to sanctioned intermediaries, intelligence partners, local anti-corruption bodies, aid implementing partners, and corporate agents. The detector builds a dated ledger of adverse knowledge, decision-maker access, and subsequent funding or renewal, then flags support approved after credible notice.

### U.S. Hands Out Vast Sums of Money While Ignoring Human Rights Records (2007) — A cross-program audit found lightly scrutinized counterterrorism vehicles bypassing restrictions and human-rights vetting

- **URL**: [ICIJ, “U.S. Hands Out Vast Sums of Money While Ignoring Human Rights Records”](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/us-hands-out-vast-sums-money-while-ignoring-human-rights-records/)
- **Partner/awards**: This was an **ICIJ-led Center for Public Integrity project**, reported by a ten-journalist team; the project credits do not name a member outlet as lead ([ICIJ project credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/about-project-collateral-damage/)).
- **What they found**:

- **Finding type(s)**:
  - `program-vehicle-arbitrage` — money or services move through a new, parallel, or differently governed vehicle to evade limits applied to the obvious program.
  - `security-assistance-harm-chain`.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Coalition Support Fund reimbursements — FOIA-obtained procurement/payment records**: ICIJ says it obtained the complete fiscal 2002–06 reimbursement set, enabling claim-level examination ([ICIJ aid story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/us-hands-out-vast-sums-money-while-ignoring-human-rights-records/)).
  - **Training and security-assistance records — government program records**: IMET, FMF, Counterterrorism Fellowship, and Section 1206 data supplied comparable country, vehicle, date, and amount fields ([ICIJ aid story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/us-hands-out-vast-sums-money-while-ignoring-human-rights-records/)).

  - **State Department human-rights reporting and official interviews — public accountability evidence**: these records established the restrictions, recipient-country risks, and agency explanations ([ICIJ aid story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/us-hands-out-vast-sums-money-while-ignoring-human-rights-records/)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY (REQUIRED)**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** FOIA reimbursement files, public budget/program records, and GAO reports are obtainable by an outside investigator. The main practical loss in a rerun would be any roster- or claim-level fields now withheld or poorly preserved, not a dependency on leaked material ([ICIJ aid story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/us-hands-out-vast-sums-money-while-ignoring-human-rights-records/)).
- **Detection signature**: **Parallel-program bypass diff** — countries and units restricted under IMET/FMF were compared with recipients in Counterterrorism Fellowship and Section 1206 records on country, unit, and year, then joined to GAO vetting results, revealing that alternate authorities restored support without equivalent documented screening ([ICIJ aid story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/us-hands-out-vast-sums-money-while-ignoring-human-rights-records/)).
- **Corroboration structure**: Payment and training rows established the flow; statutory/program rules established the expected constraint; GAO independently tested vetting; State Department records established recipient risk; agency responses addressed audit practice. Pakistan's unaudited reimbursement issue and Indonesia's vehicle bypass are distinct manifestations of weak controls, not evidence that every payment was improper ([ICIJ aid story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/us-hands-out-vast-sums-money-while-ignoring-human-rights-records/)).
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ says ten reporters reviewed official reports, human-rights records, and thousands of Justice Department lobbying records and used FOIA to build a comprehensive aid database ([ICIJ project credits](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/about-project-collateral-damage/)). **[Inferred]** The story-specific comparison required normalizing programs with different authorities into a country-year-recipient table before testing whether restrictions in one vehicle persisted in another.
- **Official impact**: The article records contemporary congressional concern and GAO scrutiny, but no post-publication official reform is identified; none is inferred ([ICIJ aid story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/collateraldamage/us-hands-out-vast-sums-money-while-ignoring-human-rights-records/)).
- **Generalization**: Parallel-vehicle bypass appears in grants versus contracts, operating versus capital budgets, emergency powers, special-purpose entities, subcontracting, and “pilot” programs. A generic detector creates an entity-place-year view across all vehicles, overlays restriction and eligibility rules, and flags support that disappears in the regulated channel but reappears elsewhere.

It converts lobbying reports into dated contact events, aligns them with official milestones and money flows, and escalates clusters where adverse oversight findings coexist with access and a favorable decision.

  - **Corporate ownership and subsidiary history — company and business-reference records**: reporters consolidated parent/subsidiary identities and allocated awards according to ownership period rather than assuming today's parent owned the vendor at award time ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/windfalls-war/methodology-windfalls-war/)).

The investigation shows the practical difficulty: contractor lists omitted major firms and agencies gave contradictory answers, so modern replication must preserve provenance and reconcile multiple award systems rather than trust one export ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/windfalls-war/methodology-windfalls-war/)).
- **Detection signature**: **Contractor-influence multiplex join** — award records were resolved through time-aware parent/subsidiary ownership, then joined to FEC contributions, lobbying filings, and government-employment histories on company, person, and date, revealing procurement concentration inside a dense political and revolving-door network ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/windfalls-war/methodology-windfalls-war/)).
- **Corroboration structure**: Award systems and FOIA responses established contracts; ownership research prevented entity double counting; FEC and lobbying filings established monetary influence; biographies established personnel ties; agency and company responses tested disputed or omitted awards. The analysis showed association and concentration, not that any contribution purchased a contract ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/windfalls-war/methodology-windfalls-war/)).
- **Methodology notes**: ICIJ published a detailed methodology describing 73 FOIA requests and appeals, approximately seven million contract actions, nine million FEC records, lobbying filings, and time-aware ownership consolidation ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/windfalls-war/methodology-windfalls-war/)). Especially transferable is its refusal to accept the agency list as a complete denominator: KBR and Bechtel were absent from an early government response despite being the two largest contractors ([ICIJ methodology](https://www.icij.org/investigations/windfalls-war/methodology-windfalls-war/)).
- **Official impact**: The article reports that congressional investigators and the GAO were already examining reconstruction awards; it does not establish those probes as effects of publication. The documented external recognition is the project-level Polk Award ([original Center story](https://publicintegrity.org/national-security/winning-contractors/); [LIU winners archive](https://liu.edu/polk-awards/past-winners)).
- **Generalization**: The same multiplex appears in disaster response, health emergencies, infrastructure, privatization, and regulated-industry grants. A generic detector resolves vendor ownership at award date, calculates concentration and noncompetitive share, and overlays political giving, lobbying issues, former-agency employment, and oversight findings.

## Cluster Synthesis

### 1. Evidence-source types and frequency

Counts are story-level and overlapping: one story can use several evidence types.

| Evidence-source type | Stories (of 10) | What the cluster shows |
|---|---:|---|
| Government/lender funding, award, contract, or project-administration records | 10 | The universal base layer: award, project, disbursement, training, or contract rows establish who received what, when, and under which program. |
| Oversight, accountability, human-rights, legislative, or court records | 9 | Inspection Panel, GAO, human-rights commission, State Department, court, congressional, and military-investigation files supply the rule, warning, complaint, or outcome side of the comparison. |
| Interviews, testimony, or field observation | 10 | Every story uses human reporting to resolve identity, implementation, intent, lived consequence, or agency explanation; the data rarely interprets itself. |
| Laws, grant conditions, program restrictions, policy texts, or manuals | 7 | These define the promised or prohibited state that can be diffed against execution. |
| Explicitly FOIA/litigation-obtained record sets | 4 | PEPFAR data, Coalition Support Fund records, Windfalls contract data, and the Baghdad contractor list depended on persistent public-record acquisition. |
| Corporate ownership, subsidiary, or personnel-history records | 3 | The three Windfalls stories require identity resolution across companies, parents, staff, and time. |

| Epidemiological or population-risk data | 1 | Divine Intervention uniquely compares program categories with measured public-health risk. |

The evidence ontology is therefore not “documents versus interviews.” It is a repeated three-layer stack: **flow record** (money, training, project, contract) + **norm or adverse record** (rule, complaint, audit, case, risk) + **identity/time bridge** (person, unit, vendor, place, program, date). Interviews usually validate the bridge or explain the resulting mismatch.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### Pattern A — Safeguard-to-Outcome Diff

- **Mechanics**: Enumerate every funded project; extract mandatory safeguards, affected populations, mitigation budgets, and required monitoring; join to completion reports, complaints, cases, and observed outcomes on project/location/date; calculate both divergence and missing follow-through.
- **Minimum data**: A project or award table, the governing safeguard or condition, at least one implementation/outcome source, and a durable project/location identifier.
- **Recognition cues**: Many planning documents but few completion records; repeated “anticipated” or “possible” counts; complaints without resolutions; highest-risk projects disproportionately missing outcome files.
- **Domains**: Development resettlement, environmental mitigation, hospital access, consent decrees, disaster recovery, school closures, and grant performance.

#### Pattern B — Aid-Recipient Harm Chain

- **Mechanics**: Resolve aid, training, equipment, or shared-budget support to a recipient person/unit/program; join it to complaints, court cases, sanctions, investigations, or a harmful adjacent program; preserve the distinction between direct funding, fungible support, and mere temporal overlap.
- **Minimum data**: Recipient-level flow records, identity/unit lineage, incident or accountability records, and dates.
- **Recognition cues**: Trained officers named in cases; prohibited units appearing under alternate program vehicles; aid continuing after public warnings; nominal service budgets sharing payroll, vehicles, or facilities with a coercive program.
- **Domains**: Police aid, private security, surveillance exports, humanitarian implementing partners, anti-corruption bodies, and corporate third-party compliance.

#### Pattern C — Emergency Award–Influence Multiplex

- **Mechanics**: Normalize contract actions to parent owner at award date; compute concentration and competition; join vendors and principals to campaign giving, lobbying, former-government employment, board ties, and oversight findings.
- **Minimum data**: Transaction-level awards, time-aware corporate ownership, person-company roles, and at least one political-influence dataset.
- **Recognition cues**: Incumbents dominate emergency awards; agency lists omit known large vendors; the same small vendor set appears across donations, lobbying, and revolving-door roles; awards cluster after access events.
- **Domains**: War, pandemic procurement, disaster relief, infrastructure, public-private partnerships, and privatization.

#### Pattern D — Fragmented-Ledger and Missingness Audit

- **Mechanics**: Acquire every available export, attachment, and responsive list; reconcile totals and entity identities; create explicit unresolved and absent-document categories; rank missingness by money, risk, population, and institutional source.
- **Minimum data**: Two partially overlapping administrative sources or one source with document expectations, plus value/risk fields and provenance.
- **Recognition cues**: Totals disagree; placeholder vendors outrank named vendors; attachments vanish; null identities cluster at high values; source agencies provide inconsistent lists; subawards swamp prime-award records.
- **Domains**: Procurement, grants, property, beneficial ownership, customs, medical billing, campaign finance, blockchain attribution, and litigation dockets.

#### Pattern E — Rule-Writer–Beneficiary Audit

- **Mechanics**: Resolve who drafted or financed a rule; collect prior directives, drafts, comments, and final text; diff consequential clauses; join the author to contracts, licenses, clients, or market exposure governed by the rule.
- **Minimum data**: Document provenance, at least two policy versions or a controlling instruction, and a beneficiary-interest record.
- **Recognition cues**: Outsourced rule drafting; undisclosed client or contract; expert-recommended limits disappear; final language creates a service category sold by the drafter; oversight responsibilities remain undefined.
- **Domains**: Technical standards, model laws, environmental review, drug reimbursement, tax guidance, AI procurement, and self-regulation.

### 5. What this platform can run now, and what is missing

**Runnable now with the stated holdings**

- **Emergency Award–Influence Multiplex, procurement side**: USASpending/FPDS can supply transactions; 20+ registries, UK Companies House, OpenCorporates, and GLEIF can resolve parents and officers; OpenSanctions, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, and local OpenAleph can identify risk and hidden affiliations. CourtListener/RECAP can add contract disputes, fraud cases, and enforcement context.
- **Fragmented-Ledger and Missingness Audit for federal procurement**: compare USASpending/FPDS totals, vendor identities, place-of-performance fields, and contract attachments; rank unknown vendors and inconsistent parent resolution by value. Property/deed, FAA, USPTO, Etherscan, and Solscan records can extend the asset and control graph when vendors or principals own relevant real estate, aircraft, patents, or wallets.
- **Aid-Recipient Harm Chain, partial**: public procurement and court data can join contractors or grantees to cases, sanctions, and offshore entities. The 990 stack enables a nonprofit-grant variant when recipient and downstream-grantee schedules are available.
- **Rule-Writer–Beneficiary Audit, document-dependent**: local OpenAleph can support provenance and text comparison if draft rules, memoranda, contracts, comments, and final versions are first ingested.

**Missing or not identified in the supplied holdings**

- A bulk, versioned World Bank/IFC project corpus covering Resettlement Action Plans, completion reports, Inspection Panel/CAO files, complaints, and attachment disappearance.
- Historical and current PEPFAR/USAID operational plans with activity-level prime/subaward data and machine-readable grant conditions.
- FMF, IMET, Counterterrorism Fellowship, Section 1206/333, Coalition Support Fund, Leahy-vetting, trainee-roster, and unit-lineage data.
- A normalized historical FARA/Lobbying Disclosure Act/FEC layer. Without it, the platform can map procurement and corporate networks but cannot fully run the influence half of the Windfalls or Ethiopia-lobbying signatures.
- Cross-national human-rights complaint, disciplinary, military-justice, and local-court identity data needed to resolve aided people and units to incidents.
- A dedicated FOIA acquisition/versioning workflow and document-OCR pipeline for administrative records that are public in law but not available as stable bulk data.
- Outcome and beneficiary ledgers. Existing award data shows disbursement, not whether promised resettlement, services, training safeguards, or remediation occurred.

The immediate high-value implementation is Pattern D on federal contracting, followed by the procurement half of Pattern C. Patterns A and B require new domain corpora; their analytic logic is ready, but the platform presently lacks the project-outcome and recipient-unit evidence needed to avoid substituting award data for proof of consequence.
