# OCCRP Evidence Ontology — Cluster 14: Illicit Trade, Smuggling, and Trafficking Corridors

Compiled 2026-07-29. Web-verified against OCCRP project and story pages, ICIJ's original *Tobacco Underground* project page, and OCCRP award/impact pages. The sample contains 11 stories drawn only from the six census-specified seeds: [China Tobacco Goes Global](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global), [Making a Killing](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing), [Tobacco Underground](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground), [The Cruel Road North](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north), [War Dog Millionaire](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/war-dog-millionaire), and [Veggie Scam](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam).

**Input-dependency coding rule.** “Open-record-runnable” includes records obtainable through ordinary public-record requests, public dockets, regulator queries, field reporting, and open web research; it does not mean “available as a one-click bulk download.” “Re-anchoring” means non-public material was important to discovery or scale, but the allegation can be tested again against public records. “Leak-dependent” includes other decisive non-public acquisition, such as a covert recording, when the public substitute cannot reproduce the central proof. “Closed-platform-dependent” is reserved for a story that cannot be reconstructed without OCCRP Aleph Pro or equivalent member-gated access.

### Illegal Chinese Cigarettes Flooding Latin America Flow Through Panama (2021) — A free-zone company network shipped China Tobacco brands into countries with no lawful market and sold hundreds of tons to accused smugglers

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP-coordinated joint reporting by Nathan Jaccard and Lilia Saúl (OCCRP), Sol Lauría (**Concolón**, Panama), and David Tarazona and Mateo Yepes (**Cuestión Pública**, Colombia); the [project credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global) also name the Houston Chronicle and other country partners. No story-specific award was located.
- **What they found**:
  - Four Panama-registered firms tied by ownership, officers, or supply relationships to China Tobacco exported cigarettes into Latin American countries where official agencies or market checks found no legal CNTC retail market. Reporters tested 18 Spanish-speaking countries plus Spain; 11 governments said CNTC brands could not legally be sold, Chile said they could, and checks in the remaining six found none in normal legal outlets. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
  - Finta exported more than **632 metric tons** of cigarettes from May 2013 to October 2018; over **599 metric tons in 44 shipments** went to Victor M. Guerra Inc., whose owner was convicted in the United States after a truck with 17 million cigarettes and false manifests was stopped en route to Mexico. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
  - Kinea and Take Roll sent more than **$40 million** of Chinese cigarettes to Colombia from 2012–2018, overwhelmingly to companies in smuggling hub Maicao; Kinea's main customer, José Guillermo Maestre Díaz, had been charged in an alleged large-scale smuggling network. One Kinea shipment worth about **$34,500** went to China's embassy in Ecuador. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
  - Colombia's seizure ledger showed six of the ten most-confiscated brands from 2015 to August 2020 were made by China Tobacco; Chinese-cigarette seizures rose from 300,000 packs in 2016 to more than 6 million in the first seven months of 2020. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
- **Finding type(s)**: `unauthorized-market seeding` (deliberately or recklessly supplying a branded product into a jurisdiction with no verified lawful retail channel); `free-zone route laundering` (using a free-trade/transit zone and formally valid export documents to give an illicit destination chain legitimate cover); `smuggler-customer overlap` (a supplier's trade-ledger counterparties also appear as defendants or suspects in trafficking records).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Legal-market matrix — official/requested**: online regulator records and FOIA responses from health, tax, and tobacco-control bodies in 19 jurisdictions; acquired by country reporters through government websites and records requests. ([OCCRP, “About This Investigation”](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
  - **Customs/trade ledgers — official or commercial trade data, acquisition vendor not disclosed**: Panamanian export records and U.S. import data supplied exporter, buyer, route, weight, value, and shipment count. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
  - **Seizure microdata — records request**: Colombia Tax and Customs Police database and Brazilian seizure data obtained by FOIA; these supplied brand, year, and quantity but not always the companies involved. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
  - **Corporate and litigation records — public/requestable**: Panama registry documents, U.S. court records for Guerra, and Colombian proceedings against Maestre tied legal entities to alleged smugglers. The public ICIJ Offshore Leaks database corroborated common officers; leaked Panama Papers emails added the purpose of the BVI Take Roll vehicle but were not necessary to show the shipment pattern. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
  - **Field verification — constructed**: reporters checked supermarkets, tobacconists, kiosks, and street markets and bought CNTC brands in Panama, Colombia, and Mexico. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive pattern is reproducible with public/requestable brand-authorization records, customs/import-export rows, corporate registries, seizure data, and criminal dockets. Panama Papers emails sharpen the explanation of Take Roll but are not required to detect shipments into unauthorized markets or trade with an accused smuggler.
- **Detection signature**: **legal-market/manifest contradiction join** — destination-country brand authorization and retail-availability records joined to customs shipments on **brand + destination country**, then joined to corporate and criminal records on **buyer legal name/owner**, revealed high-volume exports into countries with no lawful market and repeated sales to accused smugglers.
- **Corroboration structure**: The supply ledger establishes who shipped what and where; independent regulator responses establish whether lawful demand exists; registries resolve the Panama entities and common officers; court and seizure records test the risk of the buyers; street purchases show the goods' real-world endpoint.
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in OCCRP's in-story methodology section.** Reporters “checked online official information and sent FOIA requests,” then used a documented fallback—broad checks of legal retail outlets—where agencies did not answer. The story explicitly limits that fallback: retail absence is evidence of no substantial legal market, not conclusive proof that every sale is illegal. ([OCCRP, “About This Investigation”](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
- **Official impact**: No post-publication enforcement action directly attributed to this story was located. The factory closure and export-permit cancellation described in the article predated publication and are evidence, not impact. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/illegal-chinese-cigarettes-flooding-latin-america-flow-through-panama))
- **Generalization**: Apply the same detector to pharmaceuticals, alcohol, pesticides, dual-use electronics, or sanctioned commodities: construct a jurisdiction-by-product authorization table, join shipment volume to lawful market capacity, and risk-score counterparties against prosecutions, sanctions, and shared-officer networks.

### A Fake Shipping Container Leads to Chinese Cigarettes — and Italy's Camorra Crime Group (2021) — A cloned container and a factory executive's wiretapped coordination exposed a cigarette-for-junk excise switch

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP-coordinated reporting by Alessia Cerantola and Andrei Ciurcanu; the [project credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global) identify Ciurcanu with OCCRP member center **RISE Romania**. This was not an ICIJ- or Forbidden Stories-led project, and no story-specific award was located.
- **What they found**:
  - Italian and Moldovan smugglers, some linked by prosecutors to the Camorra, bought **8.9 million cigarettes for $66,200**, loaded them into a cloned Messina Line container, and prepared an identically numbered container of bricks, mattresses, and cardboard at the same weight for formal export to Libya. Black-market resale could have yielded about **$1 million**. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
  - Wiretaps and court records placed CTIEC executive Adina Ionescu inside the logistics, including customs-procedure advice; Italian prosecutors called her a “central pawn.” CTIEC said it fired her after learning of the case, and Ionescu denied involvement. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
  - A decade of CTIEC trade data showed sales into conflict and smuggling hubs. Devmak, a low-profile Iraqi firm, bought **2.6 billion cigarettes for $20 million** from 2014–2020—almost one-third of CTIEC output—while its corporate stamp was found in Ionescu's apartment. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
  - CTIEC reported sending **350 million cigarettes in 33 shipments** in 2016 to Al Emteyaz in Misurata, Libya; reporters could not find a working address or contact for the company. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
- **Finding type(s)**: `documented-cargo substitution` (preserving a shipment identifier and declared weight while swapping taxable goods for inert cargo); `corporate-insider facilitation` (a supplier employee provides procedural or logistical help to an illicit customer); `recipient-demand mismatch` (declared purchases materially exceed the recipient's observable lawful market); `smuggler-customer overlap` (defined above).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Criminal case file — obtained from authorities/court**: Italian wiretap transcripts, search records, arrest/prosecution documents, and photographs of both containers supplied coordination, container identity, seizure, and corporate-stamp evidence. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
  - **Factory trade/customs ledger — reporter-obtained, access path undisclosed**: ten years of CTIEC exports supplied buyers, destinations, quantities, shipment counts, and values. OCCRP says it “managed to obtain” the dataset but does not identify a public portal or records-request route. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
  - **Corporate/physical-presence checks — public plus field**: Iraqi registration claims, an on-site check at Devmak's listed Duhok address, and interviews with occupants tested whether the high-volume buyer had a real operating presence. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
  - **Illicit-market benchmarks — industry/regulatory**: a confidential tobacco-industry report, KPMG estimates, Italian customs observations, and seizure shares established Regina and Marble's black-market prevalence. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(b) re-anchoring.** Discovery and scale relied on a reporter-only decade of CTIEC trade data. The reusable public half is the Italian criminal case—wiretaps once filed, container/seizure records, search inventory, defendants, and corporate stamp—plus registries and physical-address checks. Those records reproduce the cloned-container conspiracy and insider link; without the private CTIEC ledger, an outside investigator loses the 2.6-billion-cigarette customer ranking and cross-country oversupply map.
- **Detection signature**: **container-identity twin diff** — shipping and court records for two containers compared on **container number + declared route + weight**, then joined to physical seizure inventories and CTIEC buyer records, revealed the same identifier and mass attached to cigarettes in one location and junk formally exported from another, while the purported buyer's stamp sat with the factory insider.
- **Corroboration structure**: The wiretaps explain intent; two physical containers and police photographs prove execution; customs/export rows quantify repetition beyond the one bust; the stamp and failed address check undermine the purported independent buyer; defendant interviews and CTIEC's denial provide adversarial testing.
- **Methodology notes**: **[inferred]** OCCRP publishes no separate methodology page. The article identifies the component sources and says reporters built a timeline from wiretaps and court documents; the analytic method is inferred from the synchronized chronology of calls, container movements, factory loading, port export, and raids. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
- **Official impact**: The prosecutions, arrests, extradition, and CTIEC's dismissal of Ionescu preceded publication; no later official action was located that OCCRP attributes to this story. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/a-fake-shipping-container-leads-to-chinese-cigarettes-and-italys-camorra-crime-group))
- **Generalization**: Search for duplicated container/seal/bill-of-lading identifiers, improbable exact-weight replacements, transit exports whose taxable cargo never exits, and buyer seals or stamps found with sellers. The same signature applies to fuel, alcohol, e-waste, scrap-metal, and sanctions evasion.

### Huge Quantities of Chinese Cigarettes Smuggled Into Ukraine (2021) — Leaked Romanian exports joined to Ukrainian licenses and cases showed half a billion cigarettes flowing to suspect or unlicensed buyers

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine
- **Partner/awards**: Joint OCCRP–**Kyiv Post** investigation reported by Anna Myroniuk; OCCRP coordinated the wider project, whose credits also name **RISE Romania** and other partners. No story-specific award was located. ([Project credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global))
- **What they found**:
  - CTIEC's Romanian factory exported at least **half a billion cigarettes** to 14 Ukrainian companies over seven years even though the State Fiscal Service and major producers said none of the Chinese brands was legally sold in Ukraine. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
  - At least three recipient companies were subjects of a large-scale smuggling investigation. Duty Free Odesa imported 12.5 million Reginas in April 2017, one month before another 12.5-million-cigarette truck was stopped; linked Travel Retail Ukraine had imported 15.5 million in 2015. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
  - Two buyers in Russian-occupied territory purchased more than **300 million cigarettes** without tobacco import/distribution licenses; Crimea-based Rivera Grand alone imported 200 million in 2014. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
  - Vadym Sliusariev, a former senior border official and Zelensky political organizer, later became co-owner of Duty Free Odesa and Travel Retail Ukraine. OCCRP explicitly reported that investigators had no evidence he was personally involved in the companies' alleged smuggling. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
- **Finding type(s)**: `unlicensed-recipient concentration` (large shares of a commodity flow converge on entities lacking the required import/distribution authority); `duty-free diversion` (goods labeled for tax-exempt retail appear in ordinary domestic or onward illicit markets); `regulator-silo failure` (agencies hold complementary ownership, import, and case records but do not connect the same entities).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Romanian export ledger — leaked**: CTIEC declarations supplied shipper, Ukrainian recipient, date, volume, and brand. The story explicitly calls the Romanian data leaked to OCCRP. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
  - **Ukrainian court and investigation records — public/requestable**: search warrants, court orders, the smuggling case, and a judge's order compelling Odesa Customs to release documents supplied the intercepted shipment and falsified-refusal-letter trail. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
  - **Registry/licensing records — public and commercial-public interface**: the official Ukrainian register, YouControl ownership histories, and regulator confirmations supplied officers, ownership changes, common addresses, and absence of licenses. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
  - **Customs/seizure and pack evidence — official plus field**: Italian tax-police data attributed 17 percent of 41 metric tons of seized Regina/D&B/Dubao cigarettes in 2017–2019 to Ukraine; reporters found “duty-free sale only” packs in Ukrainian shops and online. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
  - **Interviews — constructed**: detectives, a health-ministry tobacco-control official, experts, companies, and the state security service tested the documentary interpretation and recorded denials/limitations. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(b) re-anchoring.** The leaked Romanian ledger was necessary for the half-billion scale and complete 14-recipient universe. The public and reusable half is Ukrainian company/ownership history, license status, court documents, intercepted-shipment files, sanctions/occupied-territory status, Italian seizure data obtainable by request, and field verification. Those sources can re-test whether named buyers were licensed, linked, or under investigation; they cannot reconstruct all CTIEC exports without the leak.
- **Detection signature**: **licensed-market/export-recipient join** — leaked exporter rows joined to the destination's company register, license table, and criminal docket on **recipient legal name + registration number + shipment date**, then compared with lawful brand availability, revealed hundreds of millions of cigarettes going to unlicensed, linked, or investigated buyers in a market with no legal sale channel.
- **Corroboration structure**: The leak creates the recipient cohort; official registry and license systems independently classify it; court and seizure records test specific diversion events; pack labels and retail checks show actual market behavior; ownership history prevents a false claim that later owners necessarily controlled earlier shipments.
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated sources, inferred join.** OCCRP identifies the leaked Romanian export data, Ukrainian court documents, YouControl ownership history, licensing confirmations, Italian seizure data, and field checks. **[inferred]** The entity-resolution key and cohort join are not separately documented but are evident from matched legal names, owners, addresses, and shipment dates. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
- **Official impact**: Ukraine's smuggling investigation had been closed in December 2020 and was **reopened on April 29, 2021, one week after journalists asked the prosecutor general's office about it**—a documented pre-publication response to reporting activity. ([OCCRP/Kyiv Post](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/china-tobacco-goes-global/huge-quantities-of-chinese-cigarettes-smuggled-into-ukraine))
- **Generalization**: For any regulated commodity, build an importer cohort from origin-country exports and join it to destination licenses, ownership-at-time, enforcement cases, and permitted sales channels. Flag volume arriving before licensing, after dissolution, at shared residential addresses, or through firms in disputed/sanctioned territory.

### Making a Killing: The €1.2 Billion Arms Pipeline to the Middle East (2016) — Public export ledgers and battlefield markings exposed a post-2012 surge of Balkan weapons diverted into Syria and Yemen

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east
- **Partner/awards**: Joint **Balkan Investigative Reporting Network (BIRN)**–OCCRP project, also published with *The Guardian*; additional reporting included OCCRP member/partner reporters and **RISE Moldova**. The project was a [2017 Global Shining Light Award finalist](https://www.occrp.org/en/announcement/occrp-investigations-named-global-shining-light-award-finalists).
- **What they found**:
  - Eight Central and Eastern European states approved at least **€1.2 billion** in arms and ammunition exports to Saudi Arabia, Jordan, the UAE, and Turkey after 2012; about **€500 million** was known delivered. Several years/countries were missing, so OCCRP described the total as a floor. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
  - The recipient states had little or no pre-2012 history of buying the relevant Soviet/Yugoslav-pattern weapons and did not generally use them, while more than 50 battlefield photographs/videos showed matching arms with Syrian and Yemeni factions, including groups accused of serious abuses. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
  - Reporters identified at least **68 cargo flights in 13 months** and at least three U.S.-military sea shipments carrying an estimated **4,700 tons** from Black Sea ports. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
  - Confidential Serbian defense records showed officials had rejected a Saudi export in 2013 over diversion risk and later reversed course; one Slovak license authorized nearly **€32 million** in launchers, machine guns, and almost a million bullets. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
- **Finding type(s)**: `conflict-supply surge` (a sharp trade increase in conflict-compatible goods to recipients without a normal-use baseline); `end-use diversion` (the observed user or theater differs from the end user certified on export paperwork); `license-despite-diversion-risk` (an authorizing state approves an export after its own records or observable facts identify a material diversion risk).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **National/EU arms-export licenses and reports — public official**: values, commodity classes, exporter states, destination states, approval years, and known deliveries. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
  - **UN trade data and UN reports — public official**: delivered-value checks and sanctions/diversion context. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
  - **Flight and shipping records — public, observed, and requested**: timetables, flight tracking, carrier histories, air-traffic-control sources, port/transport documents, and U.S. procurement records reconstructed air and sea routes. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
  - **Battlefield imagery — open web**: more than 50 social-media photographs/videos; weapon markings supplied model, manufacturer country, and sometimes production year. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
  - **Confidential/leaked deal files — non-public**: Serbian Ministry of Defense papers and meeting minutes, cargo-carrier documents, two end-user certificates, and an export license supplied intent, inventory, and routing detail. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The headline detector—post-2012 export step-change to militaries that do not use the goods, aligned with public battlefield imagery, flight/ship routes, and public procurement—can run without leaks. Confidential minutes establish prior official knowledge and leaked carrier files improve route resolution, but an outside investigator can still reproduce the trade anomaly and probable diversion. The public-only version must not claim officials' internal knowledge.
- **Detection signature**: **trade-step-change/end-use contradiction** — national export approvals and UN delivery values compared with each recipient military's **pre-conflict import baseline + compatible inventory**, then joined to battlefield images on **weapon model/marking + production country/year**, revealed a €1.2 billion surge whose goods appeared with undeclared users in Syria and Yemen.
- **Corroboration structure**: Aggregate official ledgers establish scale; flight and ship movements establish logistics; imagery establishes actual theater/end user; confidential minutes test government knowledge; independent arms-law experts assess legal implications rather than reporters presenting an unqualified legal conclusion.
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in article.** BIRN/OCCRP say they examined arms-export data, UN reports, flight records, weapons contracts, airport timetables, carrier history, flight tracking, air-traffic-control sources, procurement records, and more than 50 battlefield images during a year-long investigation. Missing-year caveats are explicit. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
- **Official impact**: Within days, the EU External Action Service said it was examining the report and gathering data; OCCRP later reported that the EU announced monitoring and several countries reviewed policies. ([Immediate reaction](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/eu-were-tracking-balkan-arms-flow-to-syria); [award/impact summary](https://www.occrp.org/en/announcement/occrp-investigations-named-global-shining-light-award-finalists))
- **Generalization**: Detect conflict diversion in fertilizers/explosive precursors, drones, surveillance gear, armored vehicles, or dual-use electronics by comparing recipient capability and historical demand with sudden commodity growth, then seek product-level identifiers in destination imagery.

### The Middle East Airlift (2016) — Timetables, tracking, carrier history, and official confirmations resolved a weapons flight every six days

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-middle-east-airlift
- **Partner/awards**: Joint **BIRN–OCCRP** reporting by Lawrence Marzouk and Ivan Angelovski, with Atanas Tchobanov and Pavla Holcová; part of the [Global Shining Light finalist](https://www.occrp.org/en/announcement/occrp-investigations-named-global-shining-light-award-finalists) *Making a Killing* project.
- **What they found**:
  - Reporters identified **68 cargo flights over 13 months** from Serbia, Slovakia, Bulgaria, and the Czech Republic to Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Jordan—about one every six days. Eleven carriers appeared; Belarusian Ruby Star Airways operated nearly half. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-middle-east-airlift))
  - Serbia's Civil Aviation Directorate confirmed **49** flights carried arms after reporters presented their evidence, and Bulgarian customs confirmed one more. The remaining 18 were classified as highly likely based on military-airport destination, carrier arms history, or connection to confirmed flights. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-middle-east-airlift))
  - EU statistics separately showed **2,268 tons** of cargo from Bulgaria and Slovakia to the same military bases since summer 2014, equivalent to about 44 fully loaded Il-76 flights. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/making-a-killing-the-eur12-billion-arms-pipeline-to-middle-east))
- **Finding type(s)**: `cargo-route anomaly` (a route/frequency/destination pattern inconsistent with the historical baseline or ordinary commerce); `carrier-risk recurrence` (the same aircraft operator repeatedly appears in arms or sanctions cases); `military-destination concentration` (nominally commercial cargo activity clusters at military bases).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Flight-event ledger — open and archival**: airport timetables, flight-tracking histories, aircraft/carrier history, and plane-spotter observations supplied dates, routes, equipment, stops, and operator. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-middle-east-airlift))
  - **Aviation/customs confirmations — obtained by reporter query**: Serbia's Civil Aviation Directorate and Bulgaria's customs agency confirmed 50 flight contents after reporters presented route evidence and loading photographs. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-middle-east-airlift))
  - **Contracts/end-user certificates — leaked**: arms contracts and certificates connected some flight events to specific cargo and declared users. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-middle-east-airlift))
  - **Loading photographs and destination classification — observed/open**: photographs at Belgrade and public identification of Prince Sultan, Al Dhafra, and Aqaba as military destinations strengthened the route inference. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-middle-east-airlift))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** Public/archival tracking, airport schedules, aircraft/operator histories, destination classification, photographs, and agency confirmations are sufficient for the event detector; leaked contracts resolve contents earlier but are not necessary after 50 official confirmations. A present-day rerun may require paid historical flight archives, but that is commercial access rather than OCCRP member gating.
- **Detection signature**: **route-intent triangulation** — flight schedules and tracks joined to **aircraft registration + timestamp + origin/destination**, compared with carrier arms history and military-airport status, then reconciled to customs/aviation confirmations, revealed a recurring air bridge whose frequency and cargo differed sharply from the pre-2012 baseline.
- **Corroboration structure**: Four independent layers converge: machine/archival flight events, human plane-spotter and loading-photo observation, carrier/destination risk context, and 50 official content confirmations. The 18 unconfirmed events remain explicitly “likely,” not proven.
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in article.** The story lists airport timetables, cargo-carrier history, flight-tracking data, leaked contracts/end-user certificates, air-traffic-control sources, loading photographs, and explicit three-factor rules for classifying the 18 unconfirmed flights. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-middle-east-airlift))
- **Official impact**: Project-level, after the initial *Making a Killing* publication, the EU said it was monitoring the flow and looking at the report; several countries reviewed policy. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/announcement/occrp-investigations-named-global-shining-light-award-finalists))
- **Generalization**: Apply to sanctions-busting aviation, conflict minerals, live-animal transport, rendition flights, or disaster-profiteering logistics: build aircraft-level event histories, classify endpoint function, compare against baseline, and demand official cargo confirmation for the high-scoring cohort.

### The Coyote's Trail: A Machine Gun's Path from Serbia to Syria (2017) — One visible serial number proved a Serbian gun certified for Saudi Arabia reached a Syrian rebel unit

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria
- **Partner/awards**: Joint **BIRN–OCCRP** reporting by Ivan Angelovski, Jelena Cosić, Lawrence Marzouk, and Maria Cheresheva; part of the [Global Shining Light finalist](https://www.occrp.org/en/announcement/occrp-investigations-named-global-shining-light-award-finalists) *Making a Killing* project.
- **What they found**:
  - A pristine M02 Coyote heavy machine gun visible with a Syrian Free Syrian Army fighter carried a serial number that Serbia's Ministry of Defense eventually confirmed was sold by state-owned Zastava Arms to Bulgarian broker BIEM in 2015 for declared export to Saudi Arabia. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
  - Zastava's 2015 accounts and official fragments indicated BIEM paid about **€2.75 million for 205 Coyotes**. The end-user certificate named Saudi security forces, which did not normally use the weapon, yet multiple new Coyotes and manuals appeared across Syrian battlefields. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
  - The fighter said he encountered the Coyote during training in Saudi Arabia and later received it through Turkey; that testimony supplies the intermediate route but remains human-source evidence rather than the serial-number proof itself. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
  - Confidential 2013 Serbian records showed an earlier Saudi license was rejected over diversion risk; in 2015 Serbia approved the Coyote deal and other Saudi arms exports worth **€135 million**. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
- **Finding type(s)**: `serialized-item provenance` (following one uniquely marked physical item through seller, broker, declared user, and observed user); `end-use diversion` (defined above); `risk-warning reversal` (an authority later approves a transaction matching a diversion risk it had previously documented).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Battlefield imagery — open web/direct source**: Facebook, Twitter, and YouTube images showed model, condition, crates/manuals, locations claimed by posters, and one unique serial number. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
  - **Serial lookup — official response obtained by repeated query**: reporters sent the image and serial to Serbia's Ministry of Defense; after follow-ups, it confirmed sale to BIEM in 2015. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
  - **Export/end-user records and company accounts — official/public plus confidential**: the end-user certificate and export-license process named Saudi forces; Zastava accounts supported the 205-gun order and price; confidential ministry minutes showed the earlier risk assessment. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
  - **Fighter and official interviews — constructed**: a Syrian fighter described training and delivery; Serbian officials and BIEM answered or declined specific claims. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive mismatch is a public battlefield serial joined to an official manufacturer/export confirmation and the declared end user. The anonymous fighter interview strengthens the Saudi–Turkey route and confidential minutes show foreknowledge, but neither is required to prove that this gun left the declared Saudi end use and reached Syria.
- **Detection signature**: **serial-number chain-of-custody join** — a battlefield photograph joined to manufacturer and ministry sale records on **weapon serial number**, then compared with the export certificate's **declared end user**, revealed a specific Saudi-certified Serbian weapon in the hands of a Syrian rebel unit.
- **Corroboration structure**: Unique identifier prevents model-level false matching; multiple independent battlefield images establish more than one appearance; the ministry supplies origin/broker confirmation against interest; accounts validate order magnitude; fighter testimony explains, but does not solely prove, the route.
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in article.** BIRN/OCCRP say they spent a year following the trail, identified the model from distinctive imagery, extracted the serial, and repeatedly queried the Serbian ministry until it confirmed the BIEM sale. ([OCCRP/BIRN](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/making-a-killing/the-coyotes-trail-a-machine-guns-path-from-serbia-to-syria))
- **Official impact**: The documented EU monitoring response belongs to the 2016 project launch and should not be attributed specifically to this May 2017 follow-up; no separate official action tied to the Coyote story was located. ([OCCRP impact summary](https://www.occrp.org/en/announcement/occrp-investigations-named-global-shining-light-award-finalists))
- **Generalization**: Serial/lot/batch identifiers are the strongest corridor key. The same move works for ammunition headstamps, drone serials, medicine lots, vehicle VINs, aircraft components, timber tags, electronic IMEIs, and seized product tax stamps.

### Ukraine's “Lost” Cigarettes Flood Europe (2008) — A national production-versus-absorption calculation found 30 billion excess cigarettes feeding a $2.1 billion black market

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe
- **Partner/awards**: **ICIJ-led**, not OCCRP-coordinated. The [OCCRP project page](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground) says ICIJ worked with OCCRP journalists in Bosnia and Herzegovina, Romania, Russia, and Ukraine; this story was reported by Vlad Lavrov. The full project won IRE's [2008 Tom Renner Award](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/icij-wins-tom-renner-award); OCCRP says five of its contributors participated.
- **What they found**:
  - Ukraine's legal consumption plus exports exceeded 100 billion cigarettes, but companies manufactured/imported nearly **130 billion in 2008**, leaving a **30 percent/30-billion-stick surplus** that could not be absorbed by the declared market. ICIJ conservatively valued the illicit trade at **$2.1 billion annually**. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe))
  - Production rose from **96.8 billion in 2003 to 129.8 billion in 2008**; Philip Morris alone produced 44 billion in 2008, up more than 85 percent since 2003, while JTI produced roughly 37 billion. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe))
  - Ukrainian customs seized about **66 million cigarettes in 2008**, less than one percent of what officials believed was smuggled; neighboring EU states made many of the larger seizures. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe))
  - The price gradient—from about **$1.05 per pack** in Ukraine to as much as $5 in Germany or $10 in the U.K.—made small and industrial-scale routes profitable, including vehicles, trains, buses, internet mail orders, and hang gliders. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe))
- **Finding type(s)**: `production-absorption surplus` (domestic output plus imports materially exceeds lawful domestic consumption plus declared exports); `excise-gradient corridor` (a large cross-border tax/price difference predicts the direction and profitability of diversion); `enforcement-capture gap` (seizures represent an implausibly small fraction of the independently estimated illicit flow).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Production/import/consumption/export series — official and industry-public**: Ukraine government statistics, Ministry of Health figures, and SOVAT tobacco/alcohol association data supplied annual national totals. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe))
  - **Customs seizures and criminal cases — official/reporter-obtained**: national and regional case counts, pack quantities, brands, concealment methods, and customs-officer prosecutions tested where the calculated surplus surfaced. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe))
  - **Excise stamps, prices, and corporate reports — public/physical**: marked packs, legal price comparisons, and BAT's published acknowledgment that Pall Mall output exceeded local demand established product origin and commercial incentive. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe))
  - **Online seller test — open web/constructed**: searches found Moldova-based stores and bulk-delivery advertisements; a reporter inquiry obtained a quote for 1,000 cartons delivered to Germany. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/ukraines-lost-cigarettes-flood-europe))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive arithmetic uses official/industry production, import, consumption, and legal-export totals plus public price/tax data. Customs records and market tests corroborate the outflow. No leak or closed research platform is necessary.
- **Detection signature**: **production-absorption gap** — annual cigarette **production + imports** compared with **estimated lawful consumption + recorded exports** on country/year revealed roughly 30 billion unexplained sticks; that residual joined to neighboring-country seizure brands and excise stamps revealed the direction of leakage.
- **Corroboration structure**: Macro residual establishes unexplained supply; product-level seizures establish physical outflow; price/tax differences explain incentive; manufacturer statements and one company's own corrective production cut validate that “local demand” was an operationally meaningful benchmark.
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in article and project materials.** The article names the government and SOVAT series and gives the arithmetic; ICIJ's [project-about page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/about-project-tobacco-underground/) describes a multi-country reporting team, insider interviews, document analysis, and PIERS trade-data support for the wider project. **[inferred]** The exact consumption-estimation formula and uncertainty interval were not published.
- **Official impact**: No story-specific policy response was located. Project-level recognition: IRE awarded *Tobacco Underground* the 2008 Tom Renner Award for organized-crime reporting. ([OCCRP award announcement](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/icij-wins-tom-renner-award))
- **Generalization**: This is the cluster's most portable commodity detector. Apply material-balance accounting to fuel, timber, gold, fertilizer, opioids, fish, or carbon credits: opening stock + production + imports − lawful consumption − exports − closing stock should reconcile within known loss/error bands.

### Going Undercover — Inside Baltic Tobacco's Smuggling Empire (2008) — A covert factory visit showed a brand absent from local shops being offered by the container with route advice

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/going-undercover-inside-baltic-tobaccos-smuggling-empire
- **Partner/awards**: **ICIJ-led** *Tobacco Underground* reporting in cooperation with OCCRP contributors; ICIJ's [staff page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/about-project-tobacco-underground/) names Stefan Candea, Roman Shleynov, Paul Christian Radu, Drew Sullivan, and the wider multinational team. The project won IRE's [2008 Tom Renner Award](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/icij-wins-tom-renner-award).
- **What they found**:
  - Jin Ling cigarettes were unavailable in Kaliningrad's ordinary shops and markets, yet Baltic Tobacco Factory managers offered an undercover reporter posing as a Romanian smuggler a **10-million-cigarette container for $102,500**, ready in two weeks. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/going-undercover-inside-baltic-tobaccos-smuggling-empire))
  - Logistics manager Dmitry Gyrja said the factory could make a container in eight hours and operated continuously; director Vladimir Kazakov advised on Kaliningrad/Constanța and Lviv/Odesa routes and offered company trucks. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/going-undercover-inside-baltic-tobaccos-smuggling-empire))
  - Reporters saw every machine in the 14,000-square-meter plant producing Jin Ling, one line at **400 packs a minute**. A manager claimed production of 120 containers per month, or **1.2 billion cigarettes**. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/going-undercover-inside-baltic-tobaccos-smuggling-empire))
  - At the Bagrationovsk crossing, reporters observed cars and individuals openly loading or wearing Jin Lings while customs officers looked on; a $102.50 factory case could be worth at least ten times more in Western markets. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/going-undercover-inside-baltic-tobaccos-smuggling-empire))
- **Finding type(s)**: `covert supplier intent` (a supplier directly states willingness and operational readiness to serve an illicit channel under a reporter's assumed identity); `smuggler-only product` (large production and seizure volume coexists with near-total absence from the producer's lawful local market); `border-tolerance observation` (enforcement personnel visibly permit repeated suspicious activity).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Undercover audio/video — covertly constructed**: in June 2008 a reporter posed as a Romanian smuggler and carried concealed recording equipment through meetings and a factory tour. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/going-undercover-inside-baltic-tobaccos-smuggling-empire))
  - **Direct factory observation — constructed**: machinery, brand mix, operating speed, trucks, loading activity, physical scale, and manager price/route quotes tested whether the offer was operationally plausible. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/going-undercover-inside-baltic-tobaccos-smuggling-empire))
  - **Retail and border observation — constructed**: local-market checks established absence; route-following to Bagrationovsk established availability, repacking, price uplift, and visible customs tolerance. ([OCCRP/ICIJ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/tobacco-underground/going-undercover-inside-baltic-tobaccos-smuggling-empire))
  - **Wider project trade/customs data — public/commercial/requested**: ICIJ's project page identifies PIERS trade-data assistance, while the companion Jin Ling work used Russian and Polish customs records to reconstruct inputs and European routes. ([ICIJ about page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/about-project-tobacco-underground/); [ICIJ companion story](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/made-be-smuggled/))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(c) leak-dependent/non-public-dependent.** The story's decisive proof is the covert factory recording and access obtained under an assumed smuggler identity; public records alone do not reproduce managers' route advice, indifference to end use, or the observed all-Jin-Ling production line. **Nearest public substitute:** Russian import data for factory inputs, Polish/EU customs seizures, corporate records, and a local retail-availability survey. **What it loses:** direct supplier intent, factory capacity/brand observation, price quote, route offer, and willingness to provide trucks.
- **Detection signature**: **market-absence/supplier-offer contradiction** — lawful retail checks compared with factory output observation and EU seizure volumes on **brand + producer**, then linked to the supplier's covert route/price offer, revealed a product manufactured at industrial scale for customers who could not be found in its stated home market.
- **Corroboration structure**: Concealed recording captures admissions; physical factory observation tests boasts; local-market absence undercuts the domestic-sales explanation; border observation follows the offered corridor; customs data and seizures independently show the same brand at destination.
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated in article.** OCCRP/ICIJ disclose the assumed identity, June 2008 date, concealed video/audio, factory tour, and route-following observation. ICIJ's [project-about page](https://www.icij.org/investigations/tobacco-underground/about-project-tobacco-underground/) identifies the multinational reporting team and supporting trade-data source.
- **Official impact**: No official enforcement response caused by this specific undercover story was located; the wider project received the Tom Renner Award. ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/icij-wins-tom-renner-award))
- **Generalization**: A public-only platform can flag a smuggler-only product through production, local availability, and seizure geography, but direct intent usually requires subpoena, whistleblower, or controlled/undercover access. Suitable domains include counterfeit medicines, “export only” pesticides, ghost guns, and unlicensed gambling devices.

### Latin America's Lucrative People-Smuggling Networks (2020) — Police files, migrant itineraries, and national encounter statistics stitched local facilitators into a $150–350 million corridor

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP and **Centro Latinoamericano de Investigación Periodística (CLIP)** coordinated the project “along with 16 media outlets in 14 countries.” The [full credit page](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/the-collaboration) separately describes 18 partner organizations and names Animal Político, Periodistas de a Pie, Chiapas Paralelo, Voz Alterna, Univision Noticias, FACTum, La Prensa, El Universo, Anfibia, La Voz de Guanacaste, Profissão Repórter, Confluence Media, Efecto Cocuyo, The Museba Project, Bellingcat, The Record, and Semana. No award was located.
- **What they found**:
  - Reporters mapped two major networks moving African and Asian migrants through Brazil and as many as ten Latin American countries toward the United States, using airlines, buses, taxis, boats, false visas, seamen's books, local “coyotes,” and corrupt officials. ([OCCRP/CLIP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks))
  - Saifullah Al-Mamun's alleged network charged about **$7,600** from Bangladesh to Brazil and **$14,200** to the United States; Brazilian and U.S. authorities arrested Al-Mamun and six associates in 2019. ([OCCRP/CLIP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks))
  - Official Costa Rican, Mexican, and U.S. statistics plus migrant payment accounts yielded a conservative estimate of **$150 million to $350 million annually**. A separate Guatemalan case attributed about **$4 million** in 2009–2016 receipts to one group. ([OCCRP/CLIP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks))
  - Reporters collated at least **94 deaths in the Darién Gap since 2016** and documented corrupt-fee nodes, including alleged per-migrant exit-permit payments and officials or intermediaries who moved people without logging them. ([OCCRP/CLIP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks))
- **Finding type(s)**: `corridor-as-marketplace` (a route functions as a distributed service market whose local actors sell successive transport, document, lodging, protection, and bribery steps); `policy-friction exploitation` (smugglers arbitrage divergent visa, asylum, and transit rules across jurisdictions); `distributed-fee extraction` (the total price is composed of recurrent payments to semi-independent route nodes rather than one vertically integrated organization).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Police/investigative files — official/requested**: Brazilian, Honduran, Colombian, Guatemalan, Mexican, and U.S. cases supplied phone extractions, intercepted calls, money-transfer records, fake-visa evidence, route roles, arrests, and alleged official corruption. ([OCCRP/CLIP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks))
  - **Court and DOJ records — public**: U.S. indictments/pleas and Latin American prosecutions supplied defendants, aliases, charged conduct, convictions, and case status. ([OCCRP/CLIP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks))
  - **National encounter/immigration statistics — public official**: Costa Rica, Mexico, and U.S. counts supplied route-volume bounds and temporal growth. ([OCCRP/CLIP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks))
  - **Migrant itinerary/payment interviews — constructed**: dozens of migrants supplied origin, legs, transport mode, facilitators, prices, extortion, documents, and harms; country partners checked the route locally. ([OCCRP project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north))
  - **OSINT/field verification — open and constructed**: Bellingcat investigators and local outlets geolocated or checked route claims, documents, border sites, shelters, camps, and facilitator identities. ([Collaboration credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/the-collaboration))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The route and economics were reconstructed from public/requestable police and court records, official aggregate statistics, and replicable interviews/fieldwork. Some phone extractions and wiretaps require case-file access, but the story does not identify a leak or closed OCCRP platform as necessary.
- **Detection signature**: **cross-border corridor stitching + fee-volume model** — country-level apprehension events and police cases joined to migrant itineraries on **alias/person + border node + time window + transport leg**, then route volume multiplied by reported low/high package prices, revealed recurring handoff nodes and a bounded $150–350 million annual market.
- **Corroboration structure**: Police files identify alleged organizers and communications; court records discipline allegation language; many migrant accounts reconstruct the same handoffs from the customer side; official counts provide denominators; local reporters physically validate route nodes; low/high estimates expose uncertainty rather than a false point estimate.
- **Methodology notes**: **Stated at project level and in article.** OCCRP says the team spent nine months mapping routes with police documents, official statistics, and dozens of migrant accounts; the [collaboration page](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/the-collaboration) publishes all 18 partner roles, including CLIP data/web engineering and Bellingcat open-source investigation. **[inferred]** No reproducible calculation sheet or entity-matching protocol was published.
- **Official impact**: No post-publication policy or prosecution directly attributed to the project was located. Arrests and convictions in the story largely predated publication and form part of the evidence. ([OCCRP/CLIP](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-cruel-road-north/latin-americas-lucrative-people-smuggling-networks))
- **Generalization**: Treat any clandestine corridor as a sequence of paid services rather than a single edge. A generic detector extracts origin, destination, border node, transport mode, facilitator alias, price, transfer service, and timestamp from case files and testimonies, then clusters repeated handoffs and prices.

- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP collaboration with **BIRN Albania**, the **Czech Center for Investigative Journalism** (an OCCRP member center), Serbia's **Crime and Corruption Reporting Network (KRIK)** (OCCRP member center), and **NOVA TV** in North Macedonia. ### Building a Highway to Crime From the Middle East Into Europe (2014) — A stolen passport and repeat straw owners linked 80-company tax debt to a migrant-smuggling infrastructure

- **URL**: https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe
- **Partner/awards**: Presented jointly by OCCRP and Romanian member center **RISE Project (RISE Romania)**; reported by RISE co-founder Romana Puiuleț with Daniel Bojin and Cristi Ciupercă. No story-specific award was located. ([Project page](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam))
- **What they found**:
  - A Swedish citizen of Iranian origin identified as AB appeared in Romanian records as owner of at least **80 companies** and was tried for **€16 million** in tax evasion, although his passport had been stolen and canceled in Sweden and he had never been to Romania; the court acquitted him in December 2013. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))
  - The wider network transferred debt-ridden Romanian companies to people whose identities were stolen or who did not understand the documents, leaving false targets for tax investigators; OCCRP estimated at least **€50 million** defrauded in 2008–2009. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))
  - Morad Ahmed appeared as co-owner of at least **150 Romanian companies**; a Romanian intelligence letter in the tax file said his cousin Omar Farid Ahmed used AB's passport to take over companies. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))
  - Convicted migrant smuggler Mohamad Al Dulaimi's network allegedly brought more than **250 Iraqis** to Romania on business visas; many became straw owners after signing papers they believed related to residence. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))
- **Finding type(s)**: `liability dumping` (moving an indebted or offending company to a person selected to absorb enforcement); `identity-reuse fan-out` (one stolen or controlled identity appears across an implausibly large company portfolio); `straw-person victimization` (migrants, workers, or identity-theft victims are made nominal owners without informed control).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Romanian company/tax records — public/requestable**: ownership, company count, transfer timing, and tax debts supplied the portfolio and liability pattern. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))
  - **Court/prosecution files — public/requestable**: Ilfov tax-evasion proceedings, terrorism/migrant-smuggling files, witness statements, and a Romanian intelligence letter attached to the case supplied allegations, case outcomes, and the stolen-passport mechanism. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))
  - **Passport cancellation and foreign correspondence — official/personal record**: Swedish cancellation timing and AB's notices to Romanian authorities established that the identity document was invalid before the company takeovers. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))
  - **Victim/defendant/prison interviews — constructed**: AB, Morad Ahmed, and Al Dulaimi supplied competing accounts of identity use, document signing, migration assistance, and knowledge. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive evidence is in corporate ownership/tax histories, court files, official passport cancellation, and replicable interviews. The intelligence letter was attached to a court file, not described as an OCCRP leak. No Aleph Pro dependency is identified.
- **Detection signature**: **debt-dump identity reuse join** — corporate ownership events and tax balances joined to passport-validity and court records on **person identity + transfer date + company**, revealed one canceled stolen identity attached to at least 80 companies and repeat migrant straw owners taking control after liabilities had accumulated.
- **Corroboration structure**: Registry data shows the formal ownership fan-out; tax files show liabilities predated or clustered around transfers; Swedish records disprove AB's valid participation; the Romanian court's acquittal independently validates the identity-theft defense; interviews explain how migrants signed documents without informed control.
- **Methodology notes**: **[inferred]** OCCRP published no methods page. The story says the team investigated for more than a year and identifies company records, tax/court files, the attached intelligence letter, foreign passport evidence, and interviews; the cross-company entity resolution and transfer-timeline audit are inferred from the resulting portfolio. ([OCCRP/RISE](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/veggie-scam/building-a-highway-to-crime-from-the-middle-east-into-europe))

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

**P1. Destination Legitimacy Mismatch.** Build a matrix of commodity/brand authorization and lawful market capacity by jurisdiction; join shipment rows to that matrix and to importer license status; compare volume with plausible consumption and score recipients against criminal, sanctions, dissolution, and shared-address records. *Minimum data*: shipper, consignee, product/HS code or brand, quantity/value, origin/destination/date; destination license/authorization table; rough demand denominator. *Recognition cues*: repeated exports where no legal brand registration exists; “duty free only” product outside duty-free channels; imports before licensing; conflict/free-zone destination; buyer lacks premises; the same buyer appears in seizures.

**P2. Serialized Corridor Chain.** Extract a unique physical identifier from seizure or destination evidence and walk it backward through every available ledger. *Minimum data*: serial/container/seal/lot/passport/VIN; timestamp/location; seller/manufacturer or registry lookup; declared recipient/end user. *Recognition cues*: duplicated container ID; serial found outside certified end use; company stamp held by seller; canceled passport reused after cancellation; tax-stamp country differs from sale country. The detector should preserve uncertainty at each edge and distinguish item-level proof from model-level resemblance.

**P3. Commodity Material-Balance Gap.** Reconcile production and imports against consumption, exports, stock changes, and expected loss; investigate the residual by brand and border seizure. *Minimum data*: annual production/import/export/consumption or sales; inventory where material; price/excise by adjacent jurisdiction; seizure origin marks. *Recognition cues*: persistent positive residual, output rising while domestic consumption falls, border-adjacent wholesale concentration, manufacturer product dominating foreign seizures, and enforcement capturing only a tiny fraction of the modeled residual.

**P4. Route-Event Triangulation.** Create an event table for flights, vessels, trucks, border encounters, or migrant legs and resolve recurrent conveyances, operators, endpoints, and handoffs. *Minimum data*: timestamp, origin, destination, conveyance identifier/operator, endpoint type, and at least one cargo/person classification source. *Recognition cues*: new route after conflict/policy change; repeated multi-stop paths; military or free-zone endpoint; high-risk carrier; return leg inconsistent with declared cargo; local handoff actors recurring across cases. Confirm contents separately—route anomaly alone is not cargo proof.

**P5. Liability-Dump Ownership Transition.** Time-align debt, tax, environmental, or criminal exposure with officer/shareholder transfers; test whether the incoming owner is real, informed, present, and capable. *Minimum data*: full officer/ownership history with dates; liabilities/filings by date; person identifiers/addresses; dissolution and court outcomes. *Recognition cues*: one person acquires dozens of distressed firms; transfer follows audit or debt accrual; foreign low-income worker/refugee becomes owner; shared passport/address/translator/notary; no operating premises; invalid identity document; prior controller remains economically active.
