# OCCRP Evidence Ontology — Cluster 16: Human Harms and Public-Service Failures

Compiled 2026-07-29. Web-verified against OCCRP project and story pages, OCCRP impact and network pages, and the European Court of Human Rights. Ten article units were sampled from all six census-specified project pages. Tags below are free-form descriptions of observed reporting moves, not an imported taxonomy.

## Sampling and attribution notes

- The sample is restricted to stories reached from [Bad Practice](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice), [The Steward Files](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-steward-files), [Slaves to Progress](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress), [Birth and Death in Venezuela's Time of Hunger](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger), [Battered Justice](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice), and [Crime, Corruption, and Coronavirus](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus).
- Attribution follows the census rule: the project-page coordination credit controls project ownership; the article byline controls story ownership; an OCCRP host page does not turn a partner story into an OCCRP original. None of the ten sampled stories belongs to an ICIJ- or Forbidden Stories-led project.
- “Member center” is used only for outlets appearing on OCCRP's current [global-network roster](https://www.occrp.org/en/about-us/our-global-network). Current status is not backdated. An outlet credited by a historical project but absent from that roster is called a publishing partner, not a member center.
- Input-dependency classes answer whether an outside platform can reproduce the decisive evidence: **(a) open-record-runnable**, including ordinary public-record requests; **(b) re-anchoring**, where a leak or insider opened the path but public records can verify the core; **(c) leak-dependent**; and **(d) closed-platform-dependent**. Interviews, test purchases, and site checks are treated as constructible reporting, not “leaks.”

### System Failure: How Banned Doctors Move Across Europe, Leaving Patients Vulnerable (2025) — More than 100 doctors banned in one jurisdiction remained licensed elsewhere

- **URL**: [OCCRP investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/system-failure-banned-doctors-can-easily-move-across-europe-leaving-patients-vulnerable); [project page](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice); [methods feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/bad-practice-how-we-built-a-database-of-delicensed-doctors)
- **Partner/awards**: A joint project coordinated by Kira Zalan of OCCRP, Eiliv Frich Flydal of Norway's VG, and George Greenwood of *The Times*, with reporters at roughly 50 outlets. Current OCCRP member centers in the credited team include Public Record, RISE Moldova, Re:Baltica, Hetq, KRIK, Investigace.cz, Oštro, and the Investigative Center of Ján Kuciak; Public Record did the Romanian reporting on Iuliu Stan. It was not ICIJ- or Forbidden Stories-led. [Bad Practice won the 2026 Nonprofit News Award for Best Investigative Journalism and placed third in the 2025 Awards for Excellence in Health Care Journalism's investigative category](https://www.occrp.org/en/about-us/awards).
- **What they found**:
  - OCCRP and its partners confirmed **more than 100** physicians who were banned or suspended for serious wrongdoing in at least one jurisdiction but licensed in another; reporters confirmed through calls, appointments, or visits that a majority were actively practicing, not merely listed in stale registers. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/system-failure-banned-doctors-can-easily-move-across-europe-leaving-patients-vulnerable)
  - British regulators struck orthopedic doctor Iuliu Stan from the register after a tribunal found that he had administered rectal medication **278 times** to patients, mostly male, for sexual gratification. He remained licensed and employed at a Romanian public hospital after the British General Medical Council said it had notified Romania. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/system-failure-banned-doctors-can-easily-move-across-europe-leaving-patients-vulnerable)
  - The research database contained **more than 2.5 million records**. An initial sanctions-to-active-license comparison generated about **2,000 candidate names**, which reporters then resolved with dates of birth, specialty, graduation date, adjudicative records, and local reporting. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/bad-practice-how-we-built-a-database-of-delicensed-doctors)
  - An EU response supplied more than **17,000 anonymized IMI alert records**. Malta, Estonia, Greece, and Liechtenstein had filed no doctor alert from 2016 through the reporting period, while ten other countries filed fewer than ten. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/system-failure-banned-doctors-can-easily-move-across-europe-leaving-patients-vulnerable)
- **Finding type(s)**: **cross-border-sanction escape** — a professional barred in one jurisdiction retains authority in another; **warning-system non-use** — a mandatory safety-alert channel exists but is not filed, opened, or acted on; **disciplinary opacity** — licensing status is public while the reasons for restriction are hidden.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Professional licensing registers — public administrative data, scraped or manually queried**: reporters sought every licensed physician in 49 countries; some countries offered downloadable registers, while Spain and Austria required multiple regional scrapers. [Methods](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/bad-practice-how-we-built-a-database-of-delicensed-doctors)
  - **Discipline and revocation lists — public or request-obtained regulatory records**: only seven countries openly published inactive, suspended, or banned practitioners; reporters filed dozens of requests, with authorities in 17 countries supplying at least some licensing or discipline data. [Methods](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/bad-practice-how-we-built-a-database-of-delicensed-doctors)
  - **Tribunal decisions, judgments, and court records — public adjudicative records**: these established that a sanction reflected substantial patient harm or crime rather than an administrative technicality. British tribunal decisions were public; Scandinavian board decisions were obtained by individual requests. [Methods](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/bad-practice-how-we-built-a-database-of-delicensed-doctors)
  - **IMI alert history — European Commission response to a records request**: anonymized case numbers and sanction dates were date-matched to known decisions, then followed with national requests for identities. [Investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/system-failure-banned-doctors-can-easily-move-across-europe-leaving-patients-vulnerable)
  - **Active-practice checks — constructed verification**: partner reporters called employers, booked online appointments, or visited practices. Victim interviews and regulator/employer responses tested the human consequence and current status. [Investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/system-failure-banned-doctors-can-easily-move-across-europe-leaving-patients-vulnerable)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The lead generation and proof came from public registers, ordinary public-record requests, court and tribunal decisions, open media, and reproducible active-practice checks. No leak or Aleph Pro access was required. Coverage would vary sharply with each jurisdiction's transparency, but the method itself is available to an outside investigator today.
- **Detection signature**: **sanction-license cross-border anti-join** — disciplinary/revocation records joined to active-license registers on normalized name plus date of birth, specialty, graduation date, and sanction date revealed practitioners present in the banned ledger of jurisdiction A but the active ledger of jurisdiction B; IMI alert history compared with that result on country and sanction date revealed non-filing and non-response.
- **Corroboration structure**: The database generated leads rather than findings. Each published case required identity resolution across multiple biographical fields, the underlying tribunal or board decision, confirmation of an active foreign license, a current-work check, and questions to both regulators. Local-language reporters resolved naming conventions and manually reviewed ambiguous multi-part names.
- **Methodology notes**: OCCRP published a dedicated [methods feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/bad-practice/bad-practice-how-we-built-a-database-of-delicensed-doctors). It expressly describes the two target datasets, jurisdiction-specific scraping and requests, a common data dictionary, Unidecode transliteration, manual review of multi-part names, the 2,000-name candidate query, and matching on birth date, specialty, and graduation date. The feature identifies reordered multi-part names as a remaining weakness.
- **Official impact**: The European Commission said it would examine regulatory solutions; Norway reopened dozens of cases and notified **nine doctors** of immediate suspension or revocation, while the U.K. regulator said it would tighten registration checks. [OCCRP impact report](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/europe-reviewing-ways-to-better-protect-patients-in-response-to-occrp-doctors-investigation)
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] The detector transfers to lawyers, nurses, teachers, pilots, brokers, auditors, contractors, and care-home operators. Minimum viable logic is a cross-jurisdiction anti-join between a sanction ledger and an active authorization ledger, followed by high-confidence entity resolution and confirmation that the person is currently working.

### How Private Equity and an Ambitious Landlord Put Steward Health Care on Life Support (2024) — A hospital sale-leaseback enriched owners while locking the provider into unsustainable rent and debt

- **URL**: [OCCRP investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support); [Steward Files project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-steward-files)
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP-hosted investigation by Khadija Sharife, also published with *The Boston Globe*. The project says nearly 300,000 Steward documents were leaked to OCCRP and shared with *The Boston Globe* and *Times of Malta*; the Daphne Caruana Galizia Foundation also contributed. The project was OCCRP-coordinated, not ICIJ- or Forbidden Stories-led. No story-specific award was listed on the project or OCCRP awards pages checked.
- **What they found**:
  - Cerberus, CEO Ralph de la Torre, and associated interests extracted **more than $1.3 billion** while Steward became dependent on its landlord, Medical Properties Trust (MPT), and headed toward bankruptcy. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
  - In 2016 MPT paid **$1.2 billion** for nine Massachusetts hospital properties and leased them back. OCCRP calculated that the real estate price was almost nine times what Cerberus had paid for the properties with the hospital businesses; Carney Hospital alone went from a **$12.5 million** Steward purchase to a **$263 million** MPT real-estate valuation. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
  - The master lease could require rent for up to **30 years even if hospitals closed**. By 2019, Steward's $350 million annual rent supplied **42 percent of MPT's revenue** although Steward represented fewer than nine percent of the hospitals in MPT's portfolio. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
  - MPT reported a 9.9-percent Steward equity stake, just below a tax-sensitive REIT threshold, while internal emails used roughly 11 percent and Steward's auditor treated MPT as a related party. Some loans that kept Steward afloat were disclosed years late or, OCCRP found, not publicly disclosed. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
  - Steward entered bankruptcy with **more than $9 billion in liabilities**, including $6.6 billion in rent obligations and nearly $1 billion in unpaid vendor bills. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
- **Finding type(s)**: **care-asset stripping** — monetizing a provider's essential real estate and returning the proceeds to owners while the service inherits rent; **disclosure divergence** — private records describe a materially worse condition or relationship than public filings; **regulatory-threshold engineering** — economic interests are structured or described just below a rule-triggering percentage.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Internal Steward archive — leaked corporate records**: audited statements, operating agreements, ownership schedules, board presentations, loan and dividend correspondence, chats, and insolvency memoranda from the nearly 300,000-document trove. [Project provenance](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-steward-files)
  - **SEC filings and correspondence — public regulatory records**: MPT annual reports, earnings disclosures, declared ownership percentages, tenant exposure, rent revenue, and SEC questions supplied the public representation. [Investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
  - **Property transaction, lease, and valuation records — mixed public and leaked documents**: hospital acquisition prices, the $1.2 billion sale-leaseback, master-lease terms, and comparative valuations reconstructed the extraction mechanism. [Investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
  - **Massachusetts attorney-general review — public oversight report**: the state-approved conversion terms and a 2015 review documented promised versus delivered capital investment. [Investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
  - **Bankruptcy filings and congressional record — public legal/oversight records**: liability totals, compensation, hospital sales, and the subpoena conflict anchored the end state. [Investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(c) leak-dependent.** Public SEC filings, deeds, the Massachusetts oversight report, earnings calls, and the bankruptcy docket can expose high rent, tenant concentration, asset sales, dividends, and the collapse. They cannot reproduce the story's decisive proof of internal 11-percent ownership language, related-party treatment, deliberately discreet loans, private insolvency assessments, or the decision-makers' contemporaneous intent. The nearest public substitute is a sale-leaseback and solvency stress screen built from SEC filings, deeds, liens, and bankruptcy records; it loses hidden control, undisclosed financing, and inside knowledge.
- **Detection signature**: **sale-leaseback solvency-disclosure divergence** — hospital deed and acquisition values joined to sale-leaseback prices and rent obligations on facility and transaction date, then compared with public SEC ownership/health claims and private ownership, loan, and insolvency records on entity and reporting period, revealed value extraction, threshold engineering, and a publicly concealed dependence.
- **Corroboration structure**: The leak supplied the hidden side of each comparison; SEC filings, deeds, state conversion conditions, bankruptcy totals, and outside REIT/health-finance experts supplied independent anchors. The strongest propositions are diffs between documents created for different audiences, not a single insider's characterization.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone OCCRP methodology page was located. **[Inferred]** from the article's document links and source descriptions: reporters reconstructed a facility-level property and lease chronology, reconciled ownership units to stated percentages, compared internal financials with MPT's period-matched filings, and sought expert interpretations of REIT and related-party rules. The inference is limited to the analytic workflow; the source provenance and figures are stated in the article.
- **Official impact**: No discrete government or corporate action was identified as caused by this October 2024 story. Congressional, grand-jury, SEC, and bankruptcy scrutiny was already underway when it appeared. [Contemporaneous status](https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/how-private-equity-and-an-ambitious-landlord-put-steward-healthcare-on-life-support)
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] The pattern applies to nursing homes, schools, prisons, utilities, housing providers, and public infrastructure operators. A generic detector looks for a property sale followed by long non-cancellable leases, distributions or insider payments near the transaction, rising landlord concentration, vendor liens or arrears, and a later service-quality decline.

### ‘Robbing Peter to Pay Paul’: Inside Steward’s Crumbling Massachusetts Hospitals (2024) — Unpaid vendors and supply shortages placed patients in peril while owners took dividends

- **URL**: [OCCRP feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals); [Steward Files project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-steward-files)
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP feature by Brian Fitzpatrick with reporting contributed by Khadija Sharife and *The Boston Globe*. It is an OCCRP-hosted part of the OCCRP/*Boston Globe*/*Times of Malta* Steward Files collaboration, not an ICIJ or Forbidden Stories project. No story-specific award was listed on the checked OCCRP pages.
- **What they found**:
  - Internal emails showed vendors discontinuing services over unpaid invoices by January 2019 and an executive warning in April 2020 that suppliers might stop providing surgical materials. Steward shareholders later received a roughly **$111 million dividend**. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
  - Nurses and clinicians described missing IV tubing and medication dosages, broken elevators and equipment, personal credit-card purchases for supplies, and newborn remains placed in staff-bought cardboard boxes after a bereavement-box vendor went unpaid. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
  - *The Boston Globe* identified **at least 15 deaths** following unacceptable care tied to equipment or staffing problems and reported that federal regulators found **at least 2,000 people** had been placed in peril at Steward facilities; its count excluded ordinary medical-error cases unrelated to underinvestment. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
  - Carney and Nashoba Valley hospitals closed on August 31, 2024 after no qualifying bidder was found, while sales preserved six other Massachusetts hospitals. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
- **Finding type(s)**: **vendor-arrears service collapse** — nonpayment cuts off inputs needed for care; **extraction-harm contrast** — distributions and luxury expenditure continue while essential service inputs fail; **facility-level safety degradation** — staffing, equipment, and supply failures recur at the same facilities as regulator-documented peril.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Vendor and executive emails — leaked internal records**: dated warnings about invoice backlogs, credit holds, and surgical-supply interruptions established the upstream financial sequence. [Feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
  - **Federal regulator findings — public/request-obtained administrative records reported by the Globe**: facility inspections and patient-peril determinations supplied an external outcome ledger. [Feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
  - **Senate hearing — public sworn oversight record**: nurses described the care consequences under congressional scrutiny. [Feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
  - **Frontline interviews — constructed evidence**: long-tenured nurses and clinicians independently described shortages, workarounds, credit holds, staffing, and broken equipment by facility and period. [Feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
  - **Bankruptcy, closure, and sale records — public legal/administrative records**: they established the system's financial end state and which hospitals survived or closed. [Feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(b) re-anchoring.** The leaked emails exposed the early vendor-arrears chronology. The reusable public half is substantial: federal facility findings, the Senate hearing, bankruptcy and closure records, vendor litigation/liens, and on-record staff accounts independently verify service deprivation and patient peril. A platform without the leak can run a facility/date join between public inspections, lawsuits, liens, staffing events, closures, and dividends, but it loses the cleanest contemporaneous statement that unpaid bills were about to stop surgical supplies.
- **Detection signature**: **vendor-arrears-to-patient-harm timeline** — unpaid-invoice and credit-hold events joined to supply interruptions, staffing shortages, regulator peril findings, and adverse patient events on facility plus date, then compared with distributions and executive asset purchases in the same period, revealed that financial extraction preceded measurable care failure.
- **Corroboration structure**: Internal warnings establish mechanism; frontline witnesses describe the operational manifestation; regulator findings and the Globe's deliberately bounded fatality review establish outcomes; bankruptcy and closure records establish the terminal condition. No death was attributed solely from an email.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone OCCRP method page was located. **[Inferred]** from the story and linked Globe work: the fatality cohort was restricted to equipment/staffing-related unacceptable care rather than all malpractice, then placed on the vendor-arrears and staffing timeline. This inclusion rule matters because it limits causal overreach.
- **Official impact**: No action was identified as attributable specifically to this feature; the six hospital sales, two closures, bankruptcy, and Senate inquiry were contemporaneous events and evidence, not claimed effects of publication. [Feature](https://www.occrp.org/en/feature/robbing-peter-to-pay-paul-inside-stewards-crumbling-massachusetts-hospitals)
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] Applicable to any outsourced public service. A detector joins vendor suits, UCC liens, unpaid-bill complaints, staffing reductions, inspection deficiencies, emergency diversions, and adverse outcomes by operator/facility/month, then tests whether owners received dividends, fees, rent, or related-party payments during the deterioration.

### The Worker (2020) — More than 700 Balkan construction workers were trapped, underpaid, and abused on Azerbaijani prestige projects

- **URL**: [OCCRP story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker); [Slaves to Progress project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress); [ASTRA report hosted by OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/documents/slaves-to-progress/AstraReport.pdf)
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP-led project under Miranda Patrucić, written by Patrucić and Ilya Lozovsky with reporting across the Balkans and Azerbaijan. The page does not assign this story to an outside originating outlet or a named member center, and it is not ICIJ- or Forbidden Stories-led. The related OCCRP documentary *Building Baku* aired on Al Jazeera Balkans; no story-specific prize was listed on the project page. [OCCRP 2020 report](https://www.occrp.org/assets/annual-reports/annual-report-2020.pdf)
- **What they found**:
  - From 2006 to 2009, SerbAz recruited **more than 700** men from Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, and North Macedonia to build prominent Baku projects, at least three financed by the state. Many worked 12-hour days, had passports confiscated, lived with little food, and received sharply reduced wages or none. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker)
  - At least **two workers died**. Seudin Zoletić described a colleague dying in his arms while police who attended did not investigate the workers' broader condition or deficient immigration documents. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker)
  - Workers had been promised **$5–$8 per hour**, compared with roughly $200 a month in Bosnia, but were fined for minor behavior and required to sign statements disclaiming unpaid wages before receiving limited compensation and their passports. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker)
  - ASTRA collected the cohort's testimony; Bosnian prosecutors indicted 13 local SerbAz representatives for trafficking. Thirty-three workers later brought the Azerbaijani protection failure to Strasbourg. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker)
- **Finding type(s)**: **coercive-labor confinement** — passport retention, wage withholding, controlled housing, threats, and inability to leave turn nominal work into coercion; **victim-cohort reconstruction** — repeated testimony is organized as a group event rather than isolated anecdotes; **protection-agency indifference** — police encounter visible indicators but fail to trigger a trafficking investigation.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Victim interviews — constructed firsthand evidence**: Zoletić met a reporter three times over several months; multiple workers were separately interviewed about food, hours, beatings, passports, wages, and fines. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker)
  - **ASTRA anti-trafficking report — NGO case record built immediately after repatriation**: it compiled worker statements and helped initiate the Bosnian prosecution. [Report](https://www.occrp.org/documents/slaves-to-progress/AstraReport.pdf)
  - **Bosnian criminal and civil-case documents — public litigation records**: indictments, testimony, employer findings, and dispositions tested the workers' account and the employer's legal identity. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker)
  - **Azerbaijani proceedings and ECtHR case — public court records**: these documented the rejected domestic claim and eventual international protection claim. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker)
  - **Contemporaneous photographs and RFE/RL reporting — open corroborative media**: images of the compound, worksites, the body of a worker, and the SerbAz office supported location and condition reconstruction. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-worker)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The full human texture required new interviews, but the decisive forced-labor and state-protection case is now reproducible from the ASTRA report, domestic proceedings, contemporaneous media, and the public ECtHR judgment. An investigator today can obtain these without a leak or member-gated platform and can seek the surviving workers for additional verification.
- **Detection signature**: **victim-cohort-to-case-file reconstruction** — independently collected worker accounts joined to recruiter/employer documents, criminal and civil cases, site photographs, and repatriation records on worker, employer, worksite, and date revealed a common coercion system and the authorities' repeated failure to recognize it as trafficking.
- **Corroboration structure**: Repeated interviews with one central witness were checked against other workers; a near-contemporaneous NGO intake supplied a separate cohort record; court files tested employment and trafficking propositions; photographs and contemporaneous reporting fixed places and conditions. Dissenting experiences were retained: the story notes that some senior workers said they were not mistreated.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone methodology page was located. **[Inferred]** from the story's explicit sourcing: OCCRP used trauma-aware repeated interviewing, built a chronology at event rather than memory level, compared accounts across workers, and anchored allegations to ASTRA and court records. The basis for this inference is the disclosed three-meeting interview process, multi-worker sourcing, linked NGO report, and litigation history.
- **Official impact**: In 2021 the European Court of Human Rights found a violation of the procedural duty under Article 4 §2 to investigate possible forced labor and awarded **€5,000 to each of 33 applicants**. The judgment is an official validation of the investigation failure, not a finding that OCCRP caused the ruling. [ECtHR registry summary](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf/?filename=Judgments+and+decisions+of+07.10.2021.pdf&id=003-7145879-9686518&library=ECHR)
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] The same detector applies to migrant construction, agriculture, domestic work, fishing, logistics, and prison labor. Recognition cues are tourist rather than work visas, recruiter-controlled travel, passport retention, group housing, company-imposed fines, wage releases conditional on waivers, work on state-funded sites, and police encounters that never become trafficking referrals.

### The Minister (2020) — Public contracts and archived corporate records linked the labor-abuse contractor to Azerbaijan's sports minister's wife

- **URL**: [OCCRP story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-minister); [Slaves to Progress project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress)
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP-led investigation reported by Miranda Patrucić, Ilya Lozovsky, Madina Mammadova, and Kelly Bloss under the same OCCRP project team as *The Worker*. No outside coordinator, ICIJ, or Forbidden Stories role is credited. No story-specific award was listed on the checked project and awards pages.
- **What they found**:
  - Archived pages identified SerbAz as a subsidiary of ItalDizain. Scraped Azerbaijani records and Luxembourg ownership records then showed ItalDizain was ultimately owned by Zulfiya Rahimova, the wife of Youth and Sports Minister Azad Rahimov, and associate Elchin Zeynalov. Rahimov had directed ItalDizain before entering government. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-minister)
  - SerbAz's more than 700 laborers worked on at least six projects. Two ministry-financed examples were a **17-million-manat ($20.9 million)** restoration and a **32-million-manat ($38.8 million)** rowing center. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-minister)
  - A ministry report showed **$6.3 million** paid to SerbAz from May through October 2009, even though a meeting record said Rahimov knew of passport confiscation and abuse by May. The ministry paid another **$4.4 million** in November 2009 and **$10 million** in 2010 after SerbAz's work had been taken over. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/slaves-to-progress/the-minister)

Verification was public and reusable: archived company pages, a legitimately preserved pre-closure registry scrape, Luxembourg corporate records, common-address data, an official ministry payment report, the minister's letter, and court records. The leaked U.S. diplomatic cable was corroborative, not necessary to the ownership proof.

- **Corroboration structure**: The insider clue was never used alone. Two corporate pathways—archived self-description and registry ownership—converged on ItalDizain; an official payment ledger supplied the money; a meeting record supplied knowledge; worker/case evidence supplied the harm. The story distinguishes proven ownership, inferred spousal benefit, and uncertain control over cash.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone methodology page was located. **[Inferred]** from the story's linked evidence: reporters performed a successor-company expansion, recovered pre-secrecy registry history, treated an archived first-party organization chart as a relationship assertion, traced ownership through Luxembourg, and aligned payments to the official notice date. Every inferred step is visible in the story's documentary chain.
- **Official impact**: No official action tied specifically to the Rahimova ownership finding was located. The later ECtHR judgment found Azerbaijan failed to investigate the workers' credible forced-labor claim, but it did not adjudicate the beneficial-ownership allegation. [ECtHR summary](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf/?filename=Judgments+and+decisions+of+07.10.2021.pdf&id=003-7145879-9686518&library=ECHR)
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] This is a portable conflict detector for state construction, event venues, public hospitals, defense, and municipal outsourcing: enumerate contractors and successors, recover historic websites and registry snapshots, resolve directors/owners to officials' households and associates, then diff payment dates against complaint, inspection, suspension, or termination dates.

### Venezuelan Newborns Suffer as Mothers Struggle with Hunger (2018) — Leaked maternity data showed low-birth-weight rates rising as the government obscured the health crisis

- **URL**: [OCCRP story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger/venezuelan-newborns-suffer-as-mothers-struggle-with-hunger); [project page](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger)
- **Partner/awards**: Joint Efecto Cocuyo–OCCRP project. The story byline belongs to Edgar López, Ana Carolina Griffin, and Cristina González of Efecto Cocuyo; OCCRP's team provided the cross-border publication and production role shown on the project page. Efecto Cocuyo is not on OCCRP's current member-center roster, so it is recorded here as a publishing partner. No ICIJ or Forbidden Stories role and no story-specific award were found on the checked pages.
- **What they found**:
  - At Concepción Palacios Maternity Hospital, low-birth-weight births increased from **11.6 percent in 2015 to 16 percent in 2016**. At Santa Ana Maternity Hospital the rate rose from **11.2 percent in 2015 to 13.1 percent in 2016 and 13.9 percent in 2017**. These were leaked ward statistics at a time when national authorities minimized the crisis. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger/venezuelan-newborns-suffer-as-mothers-struggle-with-hunger)
  - Lisbeth Perez's daughter was born at **2.2 kilograms** and died ten days later; her death certificate recorded septic shock. The story carefully presented low birth weight as a possible contributing vulnerability rather than claiming it caused the child's congenital condition or death. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger/venezuelan-newborns-suffer-as-mothers-struggle-with-hunger)
  - Perez sought help at four major Caracas public-health centers; only one had a pediatric surgeon able to treat the baby after delivery. The eventual state payment was two million bolivars, then about **$12.60**, barely covering a short vigil and cremation. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger/venezuelan-newborns-suffer-as-mothers-struggle-with-hunger)
  - The broader project paired the data with the voices of **eight pregnant or recently pregnant women**, testing whether the statistical deterioration was visible in food access, prenatal care, and hospital experience. [Project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger)
- **Finding type(s)**: **hidden-health time-series deterioration** — suppressed facility data reveal a worsening outcome; **quantified-victim bridge** — a named case is checked against a cohort trend without treating the individual as proof of population causation; **service-capacity cascade** — nutrition scarcity, specialist absence, medication shortage, and delayed aid interact rather than appearing as one isolated medical failure.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Two maternity-hospital statistical series — leaked administrative data**: annual birth-weight aggregates from the two major Caracas maternity hospitals supplied the decisive trend. [Project provenance](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger)
  - **Death certificate and clinical history — family-held/medical records**: the certificate established cause of death; reported diagnosis and treatment history supplied the case chronology. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger/venezuelan-newborns-suffer-as-mothers-struggle-with-hunger)
  - **Mother and patient interviews — constructed firsthand evidence**: the Perez case and seven additional project voices documented food intake, prenatal access, and hospital navigation. [Project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger)
  - **WHO thresholds and expert interviews — open benchmark and interpretation**: the story compared the hospital trend with the WHO low-birth-weight threshold and 2025 reduction goal and sought maternal-fetal and nutrition expertise. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger/venezuelan-newborns-suffer-as-mothers-struggle-with-hunger)
  - **Hospital-director response and field observation — first-party and constructed checks**: reporters put the rising figures and missing micronutrients to officials and documented the care path. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/birth-and-death-in-venezuelas-time-of-hunger/venezuelan-newborns-suffer-as-mothers-struggle-with-hunger)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(c) leak-dependent.** The decisive two-hospital, year-by-year low-birth-weight series was non-public. The nearest public substitute is PAHO/WHO national mortality and nutrition reporting, demographic and household surveys, hospital-capacity reporting, and independently collected death certificates. Those substitutes can establish a national crisis but lose the contemporaneous facility-level trend, the comparison between two Caracas maternity hospitals, and the evidence that administrators possessed data the public could not see.
- **Detection signature**: **suppressed-ward time-series vs benchmark** — leaked low-birth-weight counts divided by annual hospital births and compared across year, facility, and WHO target revealed a synchronized deterioration; those rates joined to named mothers' prenatal-care paths and death/medical records on hospital and period showed how the aggregate appeared in lived service failure without asserting individual causation.
- **Corroboration structure**: Parallel movement in two independent hospital series reduced the chance of a one-facility artifact; WHO and specialist evidence interpreted risk; eight women's accounts tested mechanism and access; a death certificate fixed one outcome; hospital leadership received the figures and responded. The story explicitly preserved uncertainty around gastroschisis and causation.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone methods or about-the-data page was located. **[Inferred]** from the project and story: journalists calculated annual facility rates from leaked birth statistics, compared the two facilities and WHO goal, then used purposive victim interviews to test access pathways. Denominators, missing-data treatment, and leak-authentication procedures are not published, which limits independent replication.
- **Official impact**: No discrete official response attributable to this series was located on the OCCRP project or impact pages checked.
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] The pattern applies where governments suppress hospital infections, maternal deaths, prison mortality, heat deaths, school malnutrition, or disaster casualties. A detector needs facility-by-period numerator and denominator data, a stable clinical definition, an external benchmark, and a second verification layer of certificates or case files; it must avoid converting association into individual causation.

### Romania: Prosecutors Overwhelmed (2009) — Official caseload and case outcomes showed a justice system unable to carry major organized-crime cases through

- **URL**: [OCCRP story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed); [Battered Justice project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice)
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP-hosted and credited simply to OCCRP. The project page does not identify an outside coordinator or originating member center. It predates the consortium mega-project model and has no ICIJ or Forbidden Stories role. No story-specific award was listed on the checked OCCRP pages.
- **What they found**:
  - Romania's organized-crime prosecution directorate DIICOT reported more than **16,000 assigned cases in 2007**, about 90 per prosecutor, but only roughly **7,200 completed**, about 40 per prosecutor. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed)
  - Police seized about **84 kilograms of heroin worth more than €4 million** from an international ring, but judges released the suspects on unconditional bail; one was arrested again three months later with multiple false identity documents. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed)
  - In another transnational case, police seized **800,000 illegal cigarette packs** and arrested 16 people; one was held for 29 days and the other 15 were released. Investigators said a statutory definition made entry through a staffed border point a misdemeanor rather than smuggling. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed)
  - A prosecutor disclosed a protected witness's identity on television; colleagues briefly investigated but did not pursue the case, illustrating a protection system that exposed the witness while shielding the official. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed)
- **Finding type(s)**: **justice-throughput gap** — assignments substantially exceed completed cases; **high-harm release anomaly** — large seizures and organized-network indicators do not produce detention or timely adjudication; **protection inversion** — a witness-protection mechanism increases danger while the official breach produces no sanction.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **DIICOT annual report — open official performance data**: assigned and completed cases and prosecutor workload supplied the system denominator. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed)
  - **Seizure, arrest, bail, and trial records — law-enforcement and court records**: heroin, cocaine, cigarette, kidnapping, and corruption cases supplied transaction-level examples of attrition. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed)
  - **Statutory rules and ethics code — public legal texts**: surveillance limits, the border-point smuggling gap, and restrictions on magistrates criticizing colleagues supplied institutional explanations. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed)
  - **Police, prosecutor, anti-drug, and witness-protection interviews — constructed evidence**: mostly anonymous officials explained why cases stalled; named agency statistics and dispositions were used to test their claims. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/battered-justice/romania-prosecutors-overwhelmed)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive workload and disposition gaps are in official reports, statutes, court outcomes, and seizure/arrest records. Anonymous practitioners provided explanations but did not create the numeric finding. A contemporary outside investigator could rerun the method using annual prosecution statistics and docket outcomes; historical online availability may require archives or records requests, but no leak or closed platform is intrinsic to the design.
- **Detection signature**: **case-load-to-disposition attrition join** — annual cases assigned joined to cases completed on prosecutor and year revealed a throughput shortfall, while major seizure/arrest records joined to bail, detention length, trial delay, and final disposition on case and defendant revealed high-harm cases dropping out at successive justice stages.
- **Corroboration structure**: System-level workload establishes scale; several named transnational cases show the attrition mechanism; statutes test whether apparent leniency reflects a legal gap; officials from different roles explain operational failure. The story does not demonstrate that every release was corrupt and preserves prosecutors' evidence-quality limitations.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone methodology page was located. **[Inferred]** from the article: reporters treated the DIICOT report as a throughput baseline, then selected major cases to reconstruct stage-by-stage attrition and interviewed frontline officials about causal hypotheses. The story does not publish a complete case cohort, so it is an illustrative system audit rather than a statistically controlled disposition study.
- **Official impact**: No discrete official reform or case action attributable to this story was located on the OCCRP project or impact pages checked.
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] A generic justice-failure detector follows a cohort from referral through charge, detention, trial, sentence, and appeal, measuring loss at every stage and stratifying by offense value, defendant influence, and prosecutor workload. It also flags witness-identity breaches and statutes whose definitions exclude the dominant modus operandi.

### Europe's COVID-19 Spending Spree Unmasked (2020) — A 37-country contract corpus exposed direct awards, extreme unit-price dispersion, and transparency black holes

- **URL**: [OCCRP investigation and data notes](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked); [parent project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus)
- **Partner/awards**: OCCRP-coordinated data project by Adriana Homolova and Dada Lyndell with media partners in 37 countries. The contributor list includes current OCCRP member centers Hetq, Bivol, Investigace.cz, Atlatszo, IRPI/IrpiMedia, Re:Baltica, KRIK, and RISE Project; other contributors included *The Times*, *Irish Times*, NRK, YLE, DR, *Publico*, *Times of Malta*, and Finance Uncovered. It was not ICIJ- or Forbidden Stories-led. No story-specific award was found on the checked OCCRP awards page.
- **What they found**:
  - Partners assembled **more than 37,800** COVID-related tenders and contracts worth more than **€21 billion**, covering February through October 2020 and PPE, ventilators, tests, drugs, and temporary hospitals. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
  - In the collected data, respirator unit prices ranged from **€0.20 to €37**. Large hydroxychloroquine/chloroquine contracts rose 15-fold and dexamethasone contracts 36-fold from the comparison period. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
  - The most valuable one percent of records ranged from **€1 million to €1.5 billion**, and the vast majority were direct awards without open bidding. The largest national deal in the corpus was a **€181 million** no-bid Spanish PPE purchase. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
  - Portugal supplied roughly **15,000 contracts** and Russia 12,000, while Belgium, the Netherlands, and Denmark rejected data requests. OCCRP warned that record counts measured transparency, not comparative spending. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
- **Finding type(s)**: **emergency-procurement opacity** — ordinary competition and disclosure disappear during crisis; **unit-price dispersion** — like products carry implausibly different prices; **no-bid concentration** — high-value awards cluster in direct procedures; **transparency missingness** — country-level absence measures disclosure behavior rather than zero purchasing.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **EU Tenders Electronic Daily records — open supranational procurement data**: common notices supplied part of the cross-border backbone. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
  - **National and regional contract portals — open administrative data**: local partners collected and interpreted jurisdiction-specific records. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
  - **Public-record requests — request-obtained procurement data and refusal records**: partial Norwegian data and full refusals elsewhere made missingness an explicit result. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
  - **Published normalized tables — OCCRP-created open data**: a spending tracker, unit-price master list, top-one-percent table, and top-50 suppliers table made the analysis inspectable. [Downloads and descriptions](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
  - **Supplier identifiers and contract descriptions — public but frequently incomplete fields**: VAT numbers, product types, unit counts, values, dates, and award procedures supported matching and normalization; missing VAT numbers were a documented limitation. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive evidence came from open procurement portals, ordinary records requests, and published OCCRP tables. Local language and partner labor expanded coverage but did not introduce a secret source. The same design can be rerun now, subject to portal retention and the same missingness.
- **Detection signature**: **emergency-contract unit-price/competition diff** — tender and award records normalized to product class, unit, currency, date, buyer, supplier, and procedure, then like-for-like unit prices compared across buyer-country-month and high-value records grouped by competition method, revealed price outliers and direct-award concentration; country record counts compared with refusal logs revealed transparency black holes.
- **Corroboration structure**: A multinational corpus supplied breadth; unit-level subsets avoided comparing total contracts with different quantities; supplier and top-contract tables enabled case follow-up; explicit refusal and incompleteness notes prevented missing countries from being ranked as low spenders.
- **Methodology notes**: OCCRP put acquisition, scope, downloads, and limitations in the [investigation itself](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/europes-covid-19-spending-spree-unmasked), rather than a separate method page. It states the sources, period, countries, record count, published tables, and transparency caveat. **[Inferred]** only for the unreported implementation details: the cross-country schema necessarily required currency, product, and unit normalization, but no codebook or matching-error audit is linked.
- **Official impact**: No single official action attributable to this cross-border interactive was located. Its direct product was the downloadable public corpus and country-level leads.
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] The method applies to disaster relief, defense surges, refugee housing, reconstruction, energy shocks, and school or hospital emergency buying. A detector needs item description, quantity/unit, currency, award date, buyer, supplier, value, competition method, and amendments; it should model disclosure missingness separately from spending.

### China’s Oppressed Uighurs Made COVID-19 Protection Sold Throughout Europe (2020) — Public factory records and product tracing tied government PPE to coercion-risk labor

- **URL**: [OCCRP investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe); [parent project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus)

IRPI Media and Siena are current OCCRP member centers; Eesti Päevaleht is a publication of current member center Delfi Estonia. It was not led by ICIJ or Forbidden Stories. No story-specific award was listed on the checked OCCRP awards page.
- **What they found**:
  - Public records showed Hubei Haixin Protective Products had employed at least **130 transferred Uighur workers**. Its medical goods were distributed in Europe by McKesson subsidiaries and OneMed even after the coercion risk became public. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
  - Governments and health bodies in at least **five countries** bought goods from the implicated supply chains. OneMed's Norwegian shipments included at least **one million masks and 2.3 million gowns**; a 100,000-mask airlift containing Hubei Haixin products was publicly welcomed by Norway's prime minister. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
  - Reporters traced Hubei Haixin and Zhende Medical products through OneMed and McKesson operations in Sweden, Norway, Belgium, the Netherlands, Italy, Lithuania, Estonia, and Denmark. An Italian test purchase arrived with packaging identifying a U.K. McKesson sourcing subsidiary. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
  - The article did not claim it could prove coercion in every factory. Labor experts said the Xinjiang transfer system and lack of independent access made coercion risk impossible to exclude; OneMed said it had found no evidence of forced labor. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
- **Finding type(s)**: **coercion-risk supply chain** — a product chain intersects a state labor-transfer system where free consent cannot be reliably audited; **ethical-policy contradiction** — a distributor's public labor policy conflicts with traceable sourcing; **public-stockpile contamination** — state health inventories contain goods from a flagged upstream producer.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Chinese local-government reports, news, and videos — open first-party/public records**: they identified labor transfers, worker counts, housing, training, and factory locations. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
  - **ASPI and prior *New York Times* factory identification — open secondary leads**: these supplied an existing risk list that OCCRP extended into European distribution. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
  - **Company filings, websites, sourcing policies, and product catalogs — open corporate records**: these mapped factory, listed-company, subsidiary, distributor, and public ethical commitments. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
  - **Public procurement and stockpile records plus official shipment photographs — administrative and visual records**: they tied manufacturer products to government and hospital buyers. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
  - **Test purchase and package inspection — constructed physical verification**: the Italian order preserved manufacturer and importer markings that bridged the online catalog to the McKesson entity. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
  - **Distributor/factory correspondence and labor-expert interviews — obtained first-party and expert evidence**: these tested alternative explanations and the limits of proving coercion under restricted access. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/chinas-oppressed-uighurs-made-covid-19-protection-sold-throughout-europe)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(a) open-record-runnable.** The decisive chain can be rebuilt from public labor-transfer reports, factory and listed-company records, distributor catalogs, procurement/stockpile disclosures, event photographs, product packaging, and a test purchase. Obtained correspondence strengthened the chronology but was not necessary to identify the manufacturer-to-public-buyer path. No closed OCCRP platform was required.
- **Detection signature**: **labor-risk-to-government-purchase supply-chain join** — public labor-transfer and factory records joined to manufacturer and distributor product catalogs, procurement/stockpile records, and package importer markings on manufacturer, model/SKU, importer, supplier, and buyer revealed coercion-risk goods inside European public-health supply chains; sourcing dates compared with the public risk-notice date revealed continued purchasing after warning.
- **Corroboration structure**: Chinese sources identified the workers and factory; independent rights analysis supplied the coercion-risk interpretation; multiple countries' procurement and product traces established distribution; a physical purchase verified packaging; companies were asked to explain their due diligence. The story framed the result as possible forced labor rather than a proven condition for every worker.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone methodology page was located. **[Inferred]** from the article: reporters began with named risky manufacturers, enumerated distributors and subsidiaries, searched public purchases and stockpile events, and validated at least one chain physically by ordering the product. The exact SKU-matching rules and complete procurement universe are not published.
- **Official impact**: No discrete procurement suspension, investigation, or supplier action attributable to this story was located on OCCRP's project or impact pages checked.
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] The pattern transfers to prison labor, child labor, conflict minerals, illegal timber, sanctioned raw materials, and seafood. The detector joins a facility-level risk list to manufacturer identifiers, customs/product data, distributor subsidiaries, public awards, and packaging, then tests whether purchasing continued after a dated warning.

El Pulso is not listed on OCCRP's current member-center roster, so it is recorded as a Honduran publishing partner. The story was not ICIJ- or Forbidden Stories-led. No story-specific award was listed on the checked OCCRP awards page.
- **What they found**:
  - INVEST-H bought seven mobile hospitals for **more than $47 million**, paying the full amount in advance without a signed contract, performance guarantee, or late-delivery penalties to an unfamiliar company found online. The goal had been to add more than 450 beds. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
  - Intermediary Axel López sought underlying prices of **$4.45 million** for a 91-bed unit and **$3.175 million** for a 51-bed unit, then invoiced Honduras **$7.95 million** and **$5.75 million** respectively. OCCRP and El Pulso calculated a markup above 50 percent, more than **$16 million**; Honduras's anti-corruption council estimated 69 percent and more than $32.5 million. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
  - The first units arrived about four months after purchase, visibly used or damaged and without **61 ventilators** required for severe COVID-19 care. An INVEST-H team later recorded 57 technical issues in the remaining units. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
  - Public audits found no proper competition and noncompliance with INVEST-H's own emergency rules. The eventual supplier said López had used a false proposal and its product materials; inspectors concluded the delivered equipment could not fulfill the intended intensive-care function. [Source](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
- **Finding type(s)**: **emergency-middleman markup** — an intermediary with no demonstrated production capacity inserts a large spread between maker and government; **advance-payment control failure** — the buyer pays in full without contract, guarantee, or due diligence; **spec-to-delivery failure** — delivered assets are late, damaged, missing critical components, or incapable of the stated service.
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - **Confidential proposals, emails, purchase confirmations, and invoices — non-public transaction records**: these revealed order sequencing, confidentiality demands, claimed manufacturer, source price, and government invoice. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
  - **Bank-payment and INVEST-H purchase records — official financial records**: two payments on March 20 and April 2 established the $47 million outflow and lack of normal protections. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
  - **ASJ and National Anti-Corruption Council reports — public watchdog/audit records**: they tested procedure, obtained independent Turkish comparables, and estimated overpayment. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
  - **Public Ministry and official inspection/audit records — public enforcement and performance evidence**: physical condition, absent ventilators, installation failure, and technical deficiencies established nonperformance. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
  - **Florida corporate, address, website, social-media, and trade records — open corporate/OSINT evidence**: these tested Elmed's existence, capacity, history, representations, and López's political proximity. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
  - **Patient, physician, supplier, and official interviews — constructed/first-party evidence**: they established the bed shortage and intended clinical function and put the documentary contradictions to the participants. [Story](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/crime-corruption-and-coronavirus/mr-hispanopreneur-the-man-behind-honduras-47-million-coronavirus-disaster)
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY**: **(b) re-anchoring.** Confidential transaction correspondence revealed the precise hidden source-price and ordering sequence. The core is now independently reusable from public ASJ/CNA audits, INVEST-H payments, Public Ministry inspections, corporate/trade records, and the criminal prosecution: they verify the no-contract advance payment, deficient competition, overvaluation, missing equipment, and failed purpose. Without the confidential documents, the platform loses the clean invoice-versus-maker quote and proof that the intermediary had not secured a manufacturer when he billed Honduras.
- **Detection signature**: **middleman quote-invoice-delivery three-way diff** — upstream manufacturer quote compared with intermediary invoice on identical bed-count unit, then joined to payment date and delivery/inspection record on purchase order and unit, revealed the markup, payment before supplier commitment, delay, missing ventilators, and inability to perform the purchased clinical function.
- **Corroboration structure**: Confidential emails established the hidden transaction; two independent watchdogs generated comparison prices; bank/purchase records fixed payment; physical inspection and audit fixed delivery defects; later prosecution tested procurement-law violations. A named patient's experience supplied human context but was not used to prove the transaction.
- **Methodology notes**: No standalone methodology page was located. **[Inferred]** from the article's document sequence: reporters reconstructed the order chronologically, entity-checked the intermediary, diffed maker quote to invoice at matched bed capacity, compared against independent market quotes, and audited promised versus delivered specifications. The article shows the relevant source documents but does not publish a complete document set.
- **Official impact**: Former INVEST-H director Marco Bográn was sentenced in 2022 to nearly **11 years in prison** and ordered to return roughly **$60 million**; López was subject to an international warrant, and U.S. authorities had ordered seizure of about **$4 million** from his company's account. [OCCRP follow-up](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/honduras-sentences-ex-official-to-over-10-years-for-buying-useless-mobile-hospitals)
- **Generalization**: [Inferred] The detector transfers directly to emergency hospitals, PPE, ventilators, disaster shelters, generators, military aid, and reconstruction materials. Required fields are buyer, award authority, intermediary, actual maker, quote, invoice, quantity/specification, payment date, guarantee, shipment, acceptance test, and final inspection.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### A. Cross-Border Professional Sanction Escape

- **Mechanics**: Normalize every sanctioned professional and anti-join against active licenses in other jurisdictions; resolve candidates on name variants, birth date, education, specialty, license dates, and court/tribunal facts; separately measure safety-alert filing and regulator response.
- **Minimum data**: Active-license registers, sanction/revocation records with reasons and dates, professional identifiers or biographical fields, cross-border alert history, current employer/practice evidence.
- **Recognition cues**: Foreign-trained professionals, unexplained moves after sanction dates, blank disciplinary histories, active licenses concurrent with a foreign ban, jurisdictions that file zero alerts, and alerts that never produce a case.

#### B. Essential-Service Asset-Stripping Stress Test

- **Mechanics**: Build a facility-owner-operator-landlord timeline; diff purchase and resale values; calculate rent-to-revenue, related-party exposure, distributions, debt and vendor arrears; join the financial stress to staffing, inspection, diversion, closure, and harm events.
- **Minimum data**: Corporate ownership, deeds and property values, leases or rent disclosures, dividends/fees, debt and liens, facility identifiers, inspection and service outcomes.
- **Recognition cues**: Sale-leasebacks soon after privatization, master leases surviving closure, one tenant dominating landlord revenue, 9.9-percent-style threshold positioning, dividends followed by vendor credit holds, and adverse events clustered after underinvestment.

#### C. Hidden-Owner Public-Contract Harm Chain

- **Mechanics**: Expand every contractor through parents, successors, archived sites, and foreign owners; resolve beneficiaries to officials, households, and associates; align awards and payments to complaint/inspection/notice dates; attach worker or service harms only after separate verification.
- **Minimum data**: Award/payment ledger, corporate registry history, archived websites, official/household identifiers, notice and suspension dates, worker/court/inspection records.
- **Recognition cues**: Contractor renamed after scandal, offshore parent, official's relative at a common address, first-party archived subsidiary claims, payments after official notice, and investigation stopping at local managers.

#### D. Emergency Procurement Quote–Invoice–Delivery Diff

- **Mechanics**: Normalize identical units and compare source-maker quote, intermediary invoice, and peer prices; test competition and advance-payment controls; join to shipment, acceptance, inspection, and modification records.
- **Minimum data**: Item/SKU or functional specification, quantity, unit, currency, maker, intermediary, buyer, quote, invoice, payment, bid method, delivery, and inspection result.
- **Recognition cues**: Unknown intermediary found online, no demonstrated trade history, full advance payment, no contract or guarantee, direct award, confidentiality demand, rebranding of another maker's goods, extreme unit-price spread, missing critical components, or late unusable delivery.

#### E. Public-Service Outcome Suppression and Victim Bridge

- **Mechanics**: Construct facility-by-period outcome rates; compare with peer facilities, prior periods, and external standards; audit missingness and discontinued publication; verify a bounded set of cases with certificates, dockets, inspections, and trauma-aware interviews without inferring individual causation from aggregate association.
- **Minimum data**: Stable numerator and denominator, facility and period, outcome definition, benchmark, case-level record, and a coverage/missingness table.
- **Recognition cues**: Sudden disappearance of a report, implausible zeros, “unknown” dispositions, worsening parallel trends across facilities, official denial contradicted by internal series, and victim experiences matching the measured access failure.

The highest-value immediate build is a **U.S. federal emergency-contract + hidden-owner anomaly pipeline**: normalize FPDS/USASpending suppliers, resolve corporate parents and beneficial-owner proxies through the available registries, add sanctions/offshore exposure, score no-bid concentration and modifications, and flag intermediaries with no relevant operating history. In parallel, a **hospital sale-leaseback risk screen** can be built from deeds, registries, GLEIF, and bankruptcy/litigation records. Neither should claim human harm until facility-level inspection, staffing, mortality, or patient records are added.
