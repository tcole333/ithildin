# OCCRP evidence ontology: kleptocracy and asset tracing beyond the leak canon

**Research date:** 2026-07-29  
**Sampling rule:** Eleven investigations or project-level methods were selected only after traversing the six census-specified OCCRP project hubs: [Russian Asset Tracker](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker), [Dubai Unlocked](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked), [The Rotenberg Files](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-rotenberg-files), [The Great Gambia Heist](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-great-gambia-heist), [Dubai's Golden Sands](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands), and [YanukovychLeaks National Project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project). External sources below are used only to test reproducibility or document official action; they did not expand the story sample.

## Coding notes

Finding-type labels are observational, not imported from a taxonomy. They are defined at first use. A detection signature names the actual analytic operation, not the subject matter.

The input-dependency classes answer a present-tense question: could an outside investigator reproduce the decisive evidence today?

The **Partner/awards** fields distinguish project coordination from participation and from reuse of an earlier collaborative leak. None of the sampled story or project pages makes a story-specific award claim, so this report does not infer awards from unrelated OCCRP announcements.

- **(a) Open-record-runnable:** the decisive source set can be obtained today without privileged access.
- **(b) Re-anchoring:** a leak or insider clue supplied the discovery key, but the material finding can be rerun and verified on public records. The public and non-public halves are stated separately.
- **(c) Leak-dependent:** the decisive relationship, transaction, intent, or enumeration disappears without non-public material. “Leak” is used functionally here and includes rescued private files or a formerly public official register that is no longer publicly runnable.
- **(d) Closed-platform-dependent:** reproduction requires Aleph Pro, OCCRP ID, or another member-gated research platform.

### Beneficial-ownership access after the 2022 CJEU ruling

The beneficial-ownership leg is not generally reproducible on the same terms OCCRP enjoyed in 2022. The CJEU held on November 22, 2022 that indiscriminate general-public access to member-state beneficial-ownership registers was invalid under the EU Charter ([CJEU press release and judgment summary](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2022-11/cp220188en.pdf)). The latest Cyprus company-register operational guidance located for this review says general-public access remains suspended and limits access to competent authorities and obliged entities ([Cyprus Registrar guidance, February 2025](https://www.companies.gov.cy/assets/modules/wgp/articles/202103/1777/docs/guidance_final_solution_05022025.pdf)). Directive (EU) 2024/1640 now requires access for people with a legitimate interest and creates a presumption for journalists and civil-society organizations, with the relevant transposition deadline set at July 10, 2026 ([Directive 2024/1640, especially Articles 12 and 78](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ%3AL_202401640)). However, this review found no Cyprus company-register page demonstrating a live, automatable legitimate-interest route by the research date. The platform should therefore treat Cyprus UBO access as **manual and unverified**, not as a public API or reliably runnable open record.

That produces an awkward but important classification: the Austrian-hotel investigation below was open-record work at publication, but its decisive UBO record is classed **(c) today by present-access effect**. Ordinary corporate filings remain public substitutes, but they do not identify the same natural-person beneficial owners.

## Story-level ontology

### Newfound Assets of Russian Billionaire Besties: London Flats and Show Horses (2022) — Sanctions-list targets and their children led reporters to previously uncatalogued homes, horses, and aircraft

- **URL:** [OCCRP story](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/newfound-assets-of-russian-billionaire-besties-london-flats-and-show-horses); underlying [Russian Asset Tracker project](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker).
- **Partner/awards:** This was OCCRP reporting inside the OCCRP-coordinated Russian Asset Tracker. The project credits OCCRP ID and 25 media/research partners, including OCCRP member centers or network partners BIRD, Investigative Center of Ján Kuciak, Investigace.cz, IrpiMedia, MANS, Oštro, Re:Baltica, and Siena; ICIJ supplied access to earlier leak projects but did not coordinate this story ([project credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker)).
- **What they found:**
  - Eugene Shvidler controlled two London flats bought in 2010 for £20 million (then about $30.5 million), while his son Daniel owned a New York apartment bought for $2.7 million ([story](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/newfound-assets-of-russian-billionaire-besties-london-flats-and-show-horses), [London asset card](https://www.occrp.org/interactives/russian-asset-tracker/en/asset/608/two-london-flats), [New York asset card](https://www.occrp.org/interactives/russian-asset-tracker/asset/606/new-york-apartment)).
  - Roman Abramovich's daughter Sofia was linked to two elite show horses, while the oligarch's Airbus Eurocopter G-CLUL was valued at $4.9 million and held through BVI company Wenham Overseas ([story](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/newfound-assets-of-russian-billionaire-besties-london-flats-and-show-horses), [helicopter asset card](https://www.occrp.org/interactives/russian-asset-tracker/en/asset/590/airbus-eurocopter-g-clul)).
  - The helicopter was deregistered in Britain on February 8, 2022 and moved to the Russian register about a month before Abramovich was sanctioned by the U.K. and EU ([helicopter asset card](https://www.occrp.org/interactives/russian-asset-tracker/en/asset/590/airbus-eurocopter-g-clul)).
- **Finding type(s):** **Sanctions-edge asset surfacing** (using a sanctions subject and a narrow family/proxy perimeter to enumerate assets); **registry-flight** (an asset's deregistration or re-registration near an enforcement date); **kinship-held wealth** (an asset titled to a child or other relative but relevant to the subject's wealth network).
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Sanctions/designation records — public government records:** target names and effective dates supplied the person universe and timing axis; the article links the applicable U.K. and EU designations ([story](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/newfound-assets-of-russian-billionaire-besties-london-flats-and-show-horses)).
  - **Land/title records — public record or paid public lookup:** London and New York ownership and purchase prices anchored the residences ([London asset card](https://www.occrp.org/interactives/russian-asset-tracker/en/asset/608/two-london-flats), [New York asset card](https://www.occrp.org/interactives/russian-asset-tracker/asset/606/new-york-apartment)).
  - **Aircraft registry history — public registry:** registration, beneficial-owner confirmation, and the U.K.-to-Russia change established the helicopter's asset identity and movement ([helicopter asset card](https://www.occrp.org/interactives/russian-asset-tracker/en/asset/590/airbus-eurocopter-g-clul)).
  - **Equestrian competition and ownership records — specialist public sources:** the horses were linked to Sofia Abramovich through competition/ownership reporting cited by OCCRP ([story](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/newfound-assets-of-russian-billionaire-besties-london-flats-and-show-horses)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY:** **(a) open-record-runnable.** Sanctions lists, land records, aircraft-registration history, family relationships, and specialist horse records provide the decisive joins. The BVI company's ultimate owner required an official BVI confirmation reported by OCCRP, so that specific offshore leg may require a request or secondary citation, but the asset, corporate owner, registry movement, family perimeter, and sanctions timing are independently rerunnable ([helicopter asset card](https://www.occrp.org/interactives/russian-asset-tracker/en/asset/590/airbus-eurocopter-g-clul)).
- **Detection signature:** **Sanctions-date registry diff** — sanctions subjects and first-degree relatives joined to land, aircraft, and specialist asset registries on normalized name, corporate owner, address, and asset registration, then registry state before/after the sanctions date compared on asset ID, revealed unlisted property and a helicopter's pre-sanctions jurisdictional flight.
- **Corroboration structure:** A government designation establishes the risk event; an independent asset registry establishes the thing; land or company records establish title; kinship and company records connect nominal holder to subject. The different records corroborate different legs rather than duplicating one document ([tracker method](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker)).
- **Methodology notes:** **[stated]** OCCRP says the tracker began with the Anti-Corruption Foundation's list, searched land, mansions, companies, boats, and planes, and included assets tied to Putin's circle by “documentary evidence” ([project methodology](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker)). **[inferred]** This story applied a two-hop expansion—designated person → immediate family/company → asset identifier—then diffed the aircraft register against the enforcement calendar; that sequence follows the cited asset cards and dates.
- **Generalization:** Run against any sanctions, debarment, indictment, or politically exposed-person list. Expand one hop to relatives and controlled companies; normalize aircraft tail numbers, vessel IMO/MMSI numbers, parcels, and addresses; alert on high-value assets and registry changes within a configurable pre/post-event window.
- **Official impact:** The story notes that U.K. authorities were already holding two Shvidler aircraft; that enforcement predated publication and should not be counted as story-caused impact ([story](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/newfound-assets-of-russian-billionaire-besties-london-flats-and-show-horses)).

### Mysterious Group of Companies Tied to Bank Rossiya Unites Billions in Assets Connected to Vladimir Putin (2022) — A hidden shared email domain exposed a $4.5 billion cooperative-like asset network

- **URL:** [OCCRP investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin).
- **Partner/awards:** OCCRP coordinated the investigation with Meduza and a cross-border team including Le Monde, Der Spiegel, profil, Investigace.cz, Siena, The Guardian, Follow the Money, IrpiMedia, and Yle; ICIJ was not the coordinator and the story's discovery leak was not an ICIJ project ([story credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin), [tracker credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker)).
- **What they found:**
  - An email domain invisible on the public web, LLCInvest.ru, linked dozens of nominally separate companies holding at least $4.5 billion in cash and assets tied to Vladimir Putin's circle ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
  - The network included palaces, resorts, vineyards, aircraft, and yachts; companies appeared to transfer money and assets among themselves while relying on Bank Rossiya-linked infrastructure and personnel ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
  - Reporters valued the yacht *Shellest* at $23.1 million, *Nega* at about $10 million, and *Aldoga* at about $9 million, and traced aircraft operated for private rather than commercial use through Russian aviation records and tracking data ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
- **Finding type(s):** **Latent infrastructure consortium** (nominally separate entities behaving as one network because they share a non-public communications or administration layer); **registry-route co-occurrence** (common operational control inferred from repeated routes, bases, or service patterns across registered assets); **communal balance-sheet** (related entities shifting money or assets without ordinary arm's-length logic).
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Leaked email metadata — non-public discovery source:** Moskomsvyaz material exposed LLCInvest.ru in sender/recipient metadata and subject lines; OCCRP says reporters did not possess message bodies ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
  - **Corporate registries and company accounts — public records:** entity officers, owners, balance sheets, transactions, and common personnel established the public graph behind the domain ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
  - **Aircraft registry and flight tracking — public/specialist open data:** Russian aviation registrations and Flightradar records connected aircraft to private routes and LLCInvest entities ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
  - **Vessel registry, AIS, and customs records — public or commercially accessible records:** MarineTraffic and import/customs records established yacht identities, movement, winter storage, and valuation inputs ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
  - **U.S. Treasury designations — public government records:** designation narratives independently tied some assets and actors to Putin's circle ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY:** **(b) re-anchoring.** **Non-public half:** leaked metadata supplied the hidden LLCInvest.ru selector and showed that nominally unrelated entities communicated through it. **Public, reusable half:** company registers/accounts, aviation and vessel registries, flight/AIS histories, customs records, and sanctions records reproduce the entity-to-asset graph and much of its $4.5 billion valuation. Without the leak, a platform can still detect common officers, service providers, routes, addresses, and transfers, but it cannot fully enumerate the email-domain membership or prove the same shared communications infrastructure.
- **Detection signature:** **Hidden-domain multiplex join** — leaked sender/recipient metadata joined to corporate filings, company accounts, aircraft/vessel registries, and movement histories on email domain, legal entity, officer, registration number, and route revealed a cooperative-like $4.5 billion network behind formally separate owners.
- **Corroboration structure:** One weak but high-recall selector—the hidden domain—generated candidates. Independent legal identities, audited or filed accounts, government asset registrations, observable movements, customs records, and designation narratives then tested each candidate. The public records corroborated ownership and operation; they did not independently corroborate every communication ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).
- **Methodology notes:** **[stated]** OCCRP distinguishes the leak's metadata-only discovery from the public records used to trace companies and assets, and explains the valuation and movement sources in the body of the story ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)). **[inferred]** The reproducible method is selector expansion followed by heterogeneous asset joins and a related-party-flow test; the non-reproducible method is recovering the selector itself.
- **Generalization:** Search email domains, phone numbers, registered agents, bank-account fragments, office addresses, and nameservers across unrelated legal entities, then test whether their money and high-value assets co-move. A generic detector should score selector rarity, cross-entity reuse, asset-value concentration, non-commercial routes, and related-party transfers.
- **Official impact:** The story cites sanctions and asset designations already in force as corroboration; no project-specific post-publication official action is asserted on the story page ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/mysterious-group-of-companies-tied-to-bank-rossiya-unites-billions-of-dollars-in-assets-connected-to-vladimir-putin)).

### Sanctioned VTB Bank and Putin Ally Andrei Kostin Behind Luxury Austrian Hotel (2022) — Corporate and UBO records exposed a sanctions-era split designed to obscure control of an Alpine hotel

- **URL:** [OCCRP investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel).
- **Partner/awards:** Reported by OCCRP's Graham Stack and republished by iStories; it sits within the OCCRP-coordinated Russian Asset Tracker rather than an ICIJ or Forbidden Stories collaboration ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel), [tracker credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker)).
- **What they found:**
  - Sanctioned Russian state bank VTB secretly owned the five-star Hotel Tannenhof in St. Anton, Austria, through a Cyprus structure until 2015; the hotel was then moved into Cahulia Limited ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).
  - Around the introduction of Austrian beneficial-ownership disclosure, half of Cahulia was sold for a nominal €500 (then $584) to Aktien Enterprises, producing two equal shareholders and avoiding the threshold that would identify a single UBO ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).
  - A then-public Cyprus UBO record identified Canadian Eric Whyte behind Aktien and Rois Potamitis behind Cahulia; company accounts and related-party entries linked both sides back to the VTB/Kostin network ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).
- **Finding type(s):** **Threshold-engineered ownership** (splitting legal ownership at or below a disclosure/control threshold); **related-party control residue** (control remaining visible in filed loans, receivables, or transactions after title changes); **proxy-capacity mismatch** (a nominal owner whose occupation, means, or role does not fit the assets controlled).
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Cyprus beneficial-ownership register — official record, historically public:** natural-person UBOs supplied the decisive bridge between the two shareholder vehicles and the Kostin network ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).
  - **Cyprus and Austrian corporate filings/accounts — public official records:** ownership changes, consideration, loans, receivables, and related-party classifications preserved the restructuring chronology ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).
  - **Austrian land and business records — public or fee-access records:** tied the corporate chain to Hotel Tannenhof and its operating entities ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).
  - **Sanctions lists and press reporting — public government/secondary sources:** supplied the risk status and background on Kostin and VTB; they were corroborative, not proof of hotel ownership ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY:** **(c) leak-dependent by present-access effect; historically (a).** The decisive source was not a leak when OCCRP used it—it was an official, public Cyprus UBO entry. General-public access was later suspended after the CJEU ruling ([CJEU](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2022-11/cp220188en.pdf), [Cyprus guidance](https://www.companies.gov.cy/assets/modules/wgp/articles/202103/1777/docs/guidance_final_solution_05022025.pdf)). The nearest public substitute is ordinary Cyprus/Austrian corporate filings, filed accounts, land records, OpenSanctions, and historical web archives. It preserves the 50/50 split, nominal consideration, asset, and related-party residue but loses authoritative natural-person UBO identification. Directive 2024/1640 may support a journalist's manual legitimate-interest application, but it does not yet justify coding the source as reliably open or automatable ([EU directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ%3AL_202401640)).
- **Detection signature:** **Threshold-fragmentation ownership diff** — pre/post-disclosure corporate ownership snapshots compared on entity and effective date, then joined to the contemporaneous UBO entry and financial-statement related-party balances on shareholder and counterparty, revealed a nominal 50/50 split that obscured continuing VTB/Kostin-network control.
- **Corroboration structure:** The UBO record named natural persons; company filings timed the restructuring; land/business records tied the vehicle to the hotel; related-party accounting entries showed continuing economic links; sanctions records supplied relevance. No single source proved all legs ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).
- **Methodology notes:** **[inferred]** OCCRP provides no separate methodology page for this story. The source citations in the investigation show a longitudinal corporate diff followed by UBO and related-party joins; the post-2022 reproducibility assessment comes from the CJEU decision, Cyprus operational guidance, and the replacement EU directive ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel), [CJEU](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2022-11/cp220188en.pdf), [Cyprus guidance](https://www.companies.gov.cy/assets/modules/wgp/articles/202103/1777/docs/guidance_final_solution_05022025.pdf), [EU directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ%3AL_202401640)).
- **Generalization:** For every new ownership-disclosure or sanctions rule, diff ownership immediately before and after the effective date. Flag exact threshold splits, nominal consideration, transfers to employees/advisers, unchanged related-party balances, common lenders, and continued common management.
- **Official impact:** The relevant sanctions and ownership changes preceded publication; the investigation page does not claim a later official action caused by the story ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/russian-asset-tracker/sanctioned-vtb-bank-and-putin-ally-andrei-kostin-behind-luxury-austrian-hotel)).

### What Is “Dubai Unlocked”? Everything You Need to Know (2024) — The project converted leaked Dubai property and utility data into a globally verified ownership-risk index

- **URL:** [OCCRP methodology/FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know); [project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked).
- **Partner/awards:** C4ADS obtained the data; Norwegian outlet E24 and OCCRP coordinated the reporting with 75 outlets. ICIJ was one listed participant, not the coordinator. OCCRP member centers and network partners named by the project include BIC, BIRD, Context Romania, Direkt36, Frontstory.pl/Reporters Foundation, Investigative Center of Ján Kuciak, Investigace.cz, IrpiMedia, iStories, KRIK, Oštro, Re:Baltica, Siena, and Slidstvo.Info ([FAQ and full partner list](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
- **What they found:**
  - Multiple leaks, mostly from the Dubai Land Department and public utility companies, described hundreds of thousands of properties and their owners or users, largely in 2020 and 2022 ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
  - The data contained controlling parties plus dates of birth, passport numbers, and nationalities, though some records described renters rather than owners ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
  - Economists estimated foreign-held Dubai residential property at $160 billion in 2022; journalists selected about 200 public-interest subjects connected to more than 1,000 properties and independently verified their identities and holdings ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
- **Finding type(s):** **Property-owner risk overlay** (joining a bulk property-owner universe to sanctions, criminal, fugitive, and PEP universes); **identity-hardening** (resolving a name with DOB, passport, nationality, address, and independent records before publication); **tenure-state disambiguation** (separating owner, former owner, controlling party, and renter).
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Dubai Land Department and utility leaks — non-public bulk records:** supplied the property/person candidate universe, historical snapshots, and identifying fields; C4ADS obtained and shared them with E24 and OCCRP ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
  - **Official Dubai land registry — public but query-constrained government service:** reporters checked whether a named person remained an owner and, where necessary, matched passport information through a public government owner-check database ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
  - **Sanctions, court, government, and corporate records — public official records:** established public-interest status, legal allegations, company links, and cross-border identities; the FAQ describes these collectively as official records and open-source research ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
  - **Other leaked datasets — non-public corroboration:** passports and cross-border ownership traces strengthened identity resolution but did not substitute for independent confirmation ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY:** **(c) leak-dependent.** The decisive capability is bulk enumeration: without the leaked owner/user snapshots, an outsider cannot ask who among the world's sanctioned, accused, fugitive, or politically exposed people appears across Dubai's whole property market. The nearest public substitute is Dubai's query-by-known-person or property owner check, paired with public sanctions/court/corporate data. It can verify a pre-existing lead but loses the bulk owner universe, historical snapshots, renter/controller fields, passport linkage, and therefore discovery recall ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).
- **Detection signature:** **Bulk property-risk list join** — leaked Dubai property/controller records joined to sanctions, criminal-case, fugitive, and PEP lists on name plus DOB/passport/nationality, then compared to the current official land registry on person/property ID, revealed roughly 200 high-interest people connected to more than 1,000 properties.
- **Corroboration structure:** Leak record → multi-field identity match → public-interest record → current official ownership check. The leak generates the cohort; public records harden identity and status; the official registry resolves current versus historical tenure. Reporters excluded identities that could not be independently confirmed ([FAQ](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubai-unlocked/what-is-dubai-unlocked-everything-you-need-to-know)).

  - **Corporate records — public official records:** resolved major beneficiaries and their relationship to government or the petroleum sector ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-great-gambia-heist/the-state-company-that-fell-prey-to-the-president)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY:** **(c) leak-dependent.** The nearest public substitute is the Janneh Commission record, GNPC's statutory documents, public audit/budget material, and corporate registries. It can identify signatory capture and some major payments, but it loses the complete transaction population, purpose-field missingness, concentration calculation, exact authorization chain, and $55 million minimum.
- **Detection signature:** **Mandate-to-ledger anomaly join** — the GNPC transaction ledger compared to the company's statutory oil mandate and joined to directives, signatory records, and beneficiary companies on account/date/amount/payee revealed more than $55 million in diversions and a 40-percent purpose-record gap.
- **Corroboration structure:** The statement is the transaction fact; the mandate determines expected use; the directive attributes authorization; the company record identifies the recipient; commission testimony explains governance. The void-rate statistic comes from the full ledger and is not reproducible from selected testimony alone ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-great-gambia-heist/the-state-company-that-fell-prey-to-the-president)).
- **Methodology notes:** **[stated]** OCCRP identifies the underlying bank statements, directives, and commission evidence and reports the 8,000-transaction population ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-great-gambia-heist/the-state-company-that-fell-prey-to-the-president), [project “About the Data”](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-great-gambia-heist)). **[inferred]** The detector needs an explicit mandate classifier and a missing-purpose metric; merely sorting by largest payment would miss the control failure.
- **Generalization:** Apply to state-owned companies, sovereign funds, pension systems, public banks, and emergency funds. Compare every payee and purpose code to the entity's legal mandate, quantify blank or vague purpose fields, calculate beneficiary concentration, and alert when political officials become account signatories.
- **Official impact:** The Janneh Commission had already investigated Jammeh-era finances, and the later U.S. property action concerned his wider asset network; the story page does not claim a GNPC-specific post-publication recovery ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/the-great-gambia-heist/the-state-company-that-fell-prey-to-the-president), [U.S. action reported by OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/the-us-targets-gambian-ex-presidents-maryland-property)).

### Nigerian Oil and Dubai Land (2018) — An exact email match linked a disputed oil-payment stream to Dubai property owned by former minister Dan Etete

- **URL:** [OCCRP/Finance Uncovered investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land); [Dubai's Golden Sands hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands).
- **Partner/awards:** Reported by Finance Uncovered journalists Margot Gibbs, Ted Jeory, and Lionel Faull and published by OCCRP. The Golden Sands data came from C4ADS and the project formed part of the Global Anti-Corruption Consortium, an OCCRP–Transparency International partnership; it was not coordinated by ICIJ or Forbidden Stories ([story credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land), [project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands)).
- **What they found:**
  - Shell and Eni paid $1.3 billion for the OPL 245 oil license; Nigeria transferred about $1.1 billion onward, and Malabu Oil and Gas received $801.5 million ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
  - About $336.5 million moved through Rocky Top Resources; an FBI analysis cited by the story traced $54 million toward a private jet, $34.2 million to Dubai, and $21.5 million to Gunes General Trading ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
  - A Dubai-property contact email exactly matched an email on a payment authorization associated with former oil minister Dan Etete, linking the person in the oil-payment documents to two Dubai properties; a listed phone number supplied a second identity check ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
  - The property data placed the net value of one mansion at about $3.4 million ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
- **Finding type(s):** **Contact-identifier bridge** (an exact email or phone connecting records that lack a common legal name or entity ID); **proceeds-to-asset trace** (following a disputed payment through intermediaries into a high-value asset); **selector triangulation** (requiring more than one personal selector before resolving a cross-border identity).
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Leaked Dubai property/residency data — non-public records obtained by C4ADS:** supplied owner/user contacts, property descriptions, and estimated net value ([project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands), [investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
  - **OPL 245 payment authorizations, bank-flow records, and court/investigative documents — obtained or public legal records:** supplied the payment chain and the matching email address ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
  - **FBI financial analysis — government analysis cited in legal/investigative material:** supplied the jet and Dubai money-flow allocations; it is an official analytical assertion rather than independently published bank statements ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
  - **Phone and interview checks — direct verification:** a listed number and responses from relevant parties tested the Etete identity and alternative explanations ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY:** **(c) leak-dependent.** The nearest public substitute is OPL 245 court records in Italy, Nigeria, or the U.K.; corporate registries; ICIJ's Offshore Leaks Database; aircraft records; and property queries for known parcels or people. Those sources can reconstruct much of the oil-payment controversy and jet leg, but they lose the Dubai bulk ownership record, the exact property contact email/phone bridge, and portfolio/value completeness.
- **Detection signature:** **Exact-contact proceeds bridge** — leaked Dubai property data joined to OPL 245 payment authorizations and financial-flow records on an exact email, then corroborated on phone/person identity, revealed Etete's Dubai property at the destination side of a disputed oil-license payment chain.
- **Corroboration structure:** The email is the high-specificity bridge; the phone is an independent selector; transaction documents supply flow; property data supplies asset; corporate and court records supply legal context. A shared name alone would have been too weak in a cross-jurisdiction dataset ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).
- **Methodology notes:** **[stated]** OCCRP warns that Golden Sands data was compiled by private actors, sometimes reflected leases rather than title, and did not have the evidentiary quality of an official deed; reporters verified identities through addresses, phones, emails, interviews, and public records ([project methodology](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands)). **[inferred]** The exact email is the decisive identity key, while the phone and transaction chain prevent the match from resting on one potentially recycled selector.
- **Generalization:** Normalize emails, phones, passport numbers, and addresses across property, corporate, court, procurement, customs, and payment records. Rank exact rare-selector matches highly but require a second selector or independent document before asserting identity; then trace money chronologically to the asset.
- **Official impact:** The story reports ongoing and prior legal investigations into OPL 245 but does not assert a property seizure or other post-publication official action caused by this article ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/dubais-golden-sands/nigerian-oil-and-dubai-land)).

### Yanukovych's Real Estate (2014) — Rescued invoices and utility records showed presidential companies paying for homes nominally held by allies and officials

- **URL:** [OCCRP investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate); [YanukovychLeaks project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project).
- **Partner/awards:** This was a Ukrainian national collaborative project, not an OCCRP-originated leak or an ICIJ/Forbidden Stories collaboration. The story credits Lesya Ivanova of Nashi Groshi, Kateryna Kapliuk of OCCRP member center Slidstvo.Info, and Denys Bigus of ZIK; OCCRP hosted the project and its English-language publication ([story credits](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate), [project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project)).
- **What they found:**
  - After Viktor Yanukovych fled, divers recovered nearly 200 folders that departing staff had thrown into a lake at the Mezhyhirya residence; journalists and activists dried, organized, and investigated the records ([project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project)).
  - Documents contradicted a claimed $33 million apartment sale to Serhiy Klyuyev by showing that Tantalit still managed or paid for relevant property, and they exposed common managers and payment channels around nominally separate homes ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).
  - Tantalit-linked records showed approximately 3 million hryvnia ($375,000 at the article's conversion) spent on construction at a property associated with an official, while a judge's 10.3 million hryvnia ($1.25 million) contract covered an 835-square-meter house on state land ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).
  - Utility bills, construction contracts, blueprints, permits, and physical/site evidence showed who paid for and controlled properties even where title or public claims pointed elsewhere ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).
- **Finding type(s):** **Payer-over-title control** (inferring de facto control from who funds utilities, construction, security, or maintenance rather than from title alone); **rescued-record reconstruction** (reassembling a deliberately discarded private archive); **claim-document contradiction** (testing a public ownership or sale claim against contemporaneous operating records).
- **Evidence & sources:**
  - **Recovered Tantalit/Mezhyhirya files — rescued non-public records:** contracts, bills, plans, invoices, and correspondence supplied payer, manager, cost, and address links ([project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project), [investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).
  - **State architecture, permit, land, and technical-inventory records — public official records:** identified formal owners, construction status, parcel rights, and building specifications ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).
  - **Corporate and employment records — public official records:** connected payers, managers, officials, and Tantalit-linked entities ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).
  - **Site visits, neighbors, and photographs — direct observation/interviews:** checked whether the documentary address and physical property aligned ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).
- **INPUT DEPENDENCY:** **(c) leak-dependent.** The decisive archive was private material recovered from the lake. The original YanukovychLeaks archive was not confirmed as a currently searchable public corpus during this review; OCCRP's surviving project page offers an inquiry route for data access ([project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project)). The nearest public substitute is Ukrainian land, permit, architecture, corporate, court, and municipal records. It preserves formal title and construction facts but loses internal payers, utility/maintenance control, manager instructions, cost detail, and the contradiction to the claimed sale. If a complete public mirror of the rescued corpus becomes reliably available, this story should be recoded **(a)** for document-based reruns.
- **Detection signature:** **Utility-payer ownership override** — recovered utility and construction payments joined to public title, permit, company, and employment records on address, payer, contractor, and manager revealed de facto presidential-network control of properties nominally held or claimed by others.
- **Corroboration structure:** Private invoices establish payment; official permits/title establish the legal property; corporate/employment records connect the payer and manager to the political network; site visits verify the physical referent. A bill proves payment, not beneficial ownership, until repeated payer/manager patterns and independent records support control ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).
- **Methodology notes:** **[stated]** The project page describes volunteers and journalists recovering, drying, organizing, and investigating the discarded files ([project hub](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project)). **[inferred]** The story performs address normalization across damaged internal documents and official databases, then uses payer and manager recurrence to override misleading nominal title.
- **Generalization:** For proxy-held real estate, collect deeds, planning files, construction contracts, utilities, insurance, security, renovations, tax payments, and maintenance. Normalize addresses and parcels, then identify the recurring economic payer and operational manager. The same pattern applies to yachts, aircraft, and art when a third party consistently pays operating costs.
- **Official impact:** The story says Ukraine's Prosecutor General had received the documents and investigations had begun; this is an official response to the recovered archive, although the article does not quantify a recovery attributable to this particular real-estate analysis ([investigation](https://www.occrp.org/en/project/yanukovychleaks-national-project/yanukovychs-real-estate)).

### 1. Evidence-source types and frequency

Frequency counts the eleven entries above in which a source type materially contributes; one entry can contain several types.

|---|---:|---|

| Corporate registries, company accounts, or fund filings | 10 | Resolves legal entities, officers, ownership changes, related-party balances, and proxy vehicles. |
| Non-public leaked, obtained, or rescued records | 9 | Supplies the hidden selector, bulk property cohort, internal intent, private transaction ledger, or payer/control evidence. |
| Property title, cadastral, permit, utility, or technical-inventory records | 8 | Locates real estate, distinguishes title from use, confirms current tenure, and anchors cross-border valuations. |
| Interviews, site observations, or open-source identity checks | 7 | Tests identity, physical referent, occupation/capacity, and alternative explanations. |
| Bank statements, payment records, or private rental/loan records | 6 | Reconstructs flows, revenue, source of funds, accounting relabeling, or yield. |
| Cross-jurisdiction valuation inputs—purchase price, accounts, rent, market estimate, or partner valuation | 7 | Converts heterogeneous assets into comparable scale while preserving valuation date and method. |

| Beneficial-ownership register entry | 1 | Supplies an unusually direct natural-person bridge, but that exact Cyprus access path is no longer generally public and runnable. |

The cluster therefore does not support the claim that “asset tracing beyond the leak canon” means “without leaks.” Its reusable contribution is more precise: leaks often supply **enumeration, intent, or a rare selector**, while cadasters, registries, accounts, sanctions, courts, and direct observations supply **verification, temporal state, and asset identity**. The Russian Asset Tracker's simplest workflows are genuinely open-record-first; Dubai, Rotenberg, Gambia, Golden Sands, and YanukovychLeaks are mostly public-record-hardened leak investigations.

The recurring higher-order sequence is:

1. **Generate** candidates from a sanctions list, leak, rescued archive, or rare selector.
2. **Resolve** identity with DOB/passport/contact/address and canonical entity records.
3. **Anchor** the asset in title, cadastral, aircraft/vessel, company, or account records.
4. **Diff** state around a meaningful date.
5. **Test control** using financing, operating costs, related-party balances, movement, and proxy relationships.
6. **Value** at a stated date and method; do not mix purchase price, current estimate, and rental yield.

### 4. Transferable pattern candidates

#### Pattern candidate 1: Sanctions-Edge Ownership Diff

- **Mechanics:** Build person/company/asset graph snapshots before and after a sanctions, indictment, disclosure-rule, or freeze date. Expand one hop to close family, employees, advisers, and controlled entities. Flag title transfers, exact-threshold splits, registry migration, new fund wrappers, nominal consideration, and related-party balances that persist after legal ownership changes.
- **Minimum data:** event list with effective dates; historical company ownership/officers; asset registry histories; normalized people/companies; family/professional relationships. Financial statements materially improve control testing.
- **Recognition cues:** changes inside ±90 days; 49/51 or 50/50 splits; transfers to low-capacity proxies; same lender, manager, payer, address, or route after title changes; aircraft/vessel jurisdiction flight.
- **Evidence standard:** timing creates a lead, not intent. Confirm continuing control with financing, operating costs, instructions, related-party accounts, or authoritative UBO evidence.

#### Pattern candidate 2: Proxy Wealth Capacity and Control Residue

- **Mechanics:** For every nominal owner, compare asset value and financing to occupation, known business scale, relationship to the subject, and continuing economic ties. Search filed accounts for related-party loans, receivables, guarantees, or debt relabeling; search operational records for the same payer/manager.
- **Minimum data:** nominal owner; asset and valuation date/method; corporate/employment history; relationship graph; filed accounts or financing; sanctions/PEP context.
- **Recognition cues:** bodyguard, employee, romantic partner, relative, or adviser suddenly controlling a high-value asset; nominal-price transfer; liabilities nearly equal to asset cost; stable amount changing accounting category; owner lacks plausible capacity.
- **Evidence standard:** capacity mismatch is an anomaly, not proof. Require title plus a funding, operational, or related-party control trace.

#### Pattern candidate 3: Rare Selector Bridge

- **Mechanics:** Normalize emails, phones, passport numbers, addresses, account fragments, domains, and nameservers across corpora. Weight exact rare-selector matches; join the resulting identity to assets and transaction records; require an independent selector or record before publication.
- **Minimum data:** at least two datasets exposing a selector; normalization rules; selector-frequency statistics; one independent identity source.
- **Recognition cues:** non-public or obscure domain shared across nominally unrelated companies; exact email in both payment authorization and property file; recurring phone/address across proxy vehicles.
- **Evidence standard:** shared corporate-service-provider selectors can create false clusters. Distinguish a service-provider hub from subject-specific infrastructure with transactions, personnel, or asset co-movement.

#### Pattern candidate 4: Payer-Over-Title Control

- **Mechanics:** Join parcels or registered assets to utilities, permits, construction invoices, insurance, crew/security, maintenance, docking/hangar, taxes, and loan payments. Rank the person or entity repeatedly funding and managing the asset, then compare with legal title.
- **Minimum data:** stable asset identifier/address; title history; at least one operating-cost or improvement record; payer/contractor identities; company/person resolution.
- **Recognition cues:** one network pays utilities and renovations across several proxy-titled properties; nominal owner never appears in operating records; manager/contractor recurs across a ruler's or oligarch's portfolio.
- **Evidence standard:** a single paid invoice can reflect a legitimate service. Repeated payments, common managers, source-of-funds evidence, and owner behavior are needed to infer control.

#### Pattern candidate 5: Mandate-to-Ledger Diversion

- **Mechanics:** Normalize a state entity's full transaction ledger, classify each payee and purpose against the entity's statutory mandate, resolve beneficiary ownership, quantify blank/vague purposes and beneficiary concentration, and deduplicate internal transfers before calculating loss.
- **Minimum data:** transaction-level ledger; account and signatory history; statutory mandate/chart of accounts; company ownership; contracts or directives.
- **Recognition cues:** ruler or minister added as signatory; large off-mandate recipients; high blank-purpose rate; transfers to officials or related companies; a small group receiving most outflows; payments without matching contracts.
- **Evidence standard:** “off mandate” is a rules-based anomaly. A loss estimate requires transaction-level review, internal-transfer elimination, and documentary or testimonial support for illicit purpose.

### 5. What this platform can run today

#### Runnable now

- **Sanctions-Edge Ownership Diff:** OpenSanctions supplies subjects and dates; 20+ corporate registries, U.K. Companies House, OpenCorporates, and GLEIF supply legal entities; property/deed tools and the FAA registry supply assets; the ICIJ Offshore Leaks Database supplies public offshore associations. This supports class-(a) land/company/aircraft tracing and historical ownership alerts, subject to each registry's snapshot depth.
- **Proxy Wealth Capacity and Control Residue, partially:** corporate filings, Companies House accounts, OpenCorporates, GLEIF, property/deed records, OpenSanctions, and CourtListener can expose proxy relationships, loans, charges, litigation, and implausible asset control. The platform should score mismatch but withhold beneficial-control conclusions absent funding or operations evidence.
- **Rare Selector Bridge, partially:** local OpenAleph, the ICIJ Offshore Leaks Database, corporate registries, CourtListener exhibits, FAA records, and property documents can be normalized for emails, phones, addresses, directors, and registration numbers. A local corpus can reproduce the join when the selector exists in accessible material; it cannot recover LLCInvest's hidden domain or Golden Sands' property contact fields independently.
- **Payer-Over-Title Control, where local records expose payer data:** deed, mortgage, lien, permit, tax, and court tools can compare legal title with financing and improvements. CourtListener may expose invoices or declarations. The public record rarely includes ordinary utilities, security, maintenance, or private construction invoices.
- **Mandate-to-Ledger Diversion on public procurement rather than bank accounts:** USASpending/FPDS can be compared to agency mandate, award purpose, recipient ownership, debarment/sanctions, and CourtListener. This detects concentration, pass-through vendors, and off-mission contracting but does not reproduce Gambia's bank-account audit.
- **Public-chain variant:** Etherscan and Solscan can substitute a public blockchain ledger for otherwise unavailable bank statements. Entity attribution remains the hard part, but transaction reconstruction, concentration, event diffs, and proceeds-to-asset movement are natively runnable.

#### Material gaps

- **Bulk Dubai ownership and utility snapshots:** the decisive Dubai Unlocked/Golden Sands owner-user cohort, passport fields, historical snapshots, and contact identifiers are missing. A query-by-known-owner service is verification, not bulk discovery.
- **Stable European UBO access:** the platform needs jurisdiction-specific legitimate-interest workflows, approval tracking, and access-state monitoring after the CJEU ruling and Directive 2024/1640. OpenCorporates and ordinary filings are not substitutes for a natural-person UBO record.
- **Historical cadasters and ownership snapshots:** current deeds are insufficient for sanctions-event diffs when the asset moved before collection. Acquisition dates, prior owners, parcel lineage, and archived encumbrances are needed.
- **Private bank statements, wires, SARs, rental contracts, invoices, and government directives:** without these, the Gambia totals, Rotenberg intent, rent yields, and payer-over-title findings cannot be reproduced.
- **Global aircraft/vessel ownership and movement:** FAA covers one jurisdiction. The platform lacks a unified international aircraft registry, historical ADS-B, vessel beneficial ownership, long-horizon AIS, port/customs, and dependable valuation.
- **Leak-specific selectors and intent:** LLCInvest email metadata, Evocorp communications, Dubai property contacts, and rescued Mezhyhirya operations records have no public equivalent. Local OpenAleph helps only when equivalent documents have already been lawfully ingested.
- **Valuation normalization:** the platform needs dated valuation provenance distinguishing purchase price, assessed value, current market estimate, insured value, construction cost, and capitalized rent. Cross-jurisdiction currency conversion must preserve rate and date.
- **Portfolio-level identity resolution:** DOB/passport-strength matching is sparse in public records. Name-only joins across Russian, Arabic, Ukrainian, and Latin transliterations will otherwise overmerge people.

USPTO ownership and assignment data are not central to the sampled stories, but they can extend the same proxy-control and related-party-transfer patterns to intellectual property. CourtListener, USASpending/FPDS, and public chains are especially valuable substitutes because they expose transactions or sworn evidence—the evidence layer most often missing from ordinary registries.

## Assessment

The most transferable lesson is not a source list but a division of labor among sources. Sanctions lists generate event dates; leaks and rescued archives generate otherwise invisible cohorts, selectors, or intent; registries establish legal identity and state; cadasters establish the asset; movement and operating-cost records test real control; transaction records connect wealth source to destination; and valuations translate heterogeneous assets into comparable scale. A detector should preserve those roles instead of flattening every source into an undifferentiated “match.”

For this platform, the immediately productive implementation is a registry-first sanctions/event diff with proxy expansion, followed by rare-selector joins across local OpenAleph, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, corporate registries, property tools, CourtListener, FAA, and government awards. It will not recreate nine of the eleven stories, but it can generate evidence-qualified leads that name the missing proof: UBO, historical title, bank flow, operating payer, private instruction, or bulk property cohort.
