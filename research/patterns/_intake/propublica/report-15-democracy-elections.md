## 1. What Parler Saw During the Attack on the Capitol (2021) — a navigable, geolocated video archive of Jan. 6 built from a dead platform's exhaust

- **URL**: https://projects.propublica.org/parler-capitol-videos/ ; methodology essay: https://www.propublica.org/article/why-we-published-parler-users-videos-capitol-attack ; narrative companion: https://www.propublica.org/article/inside-the-capitol-riot-what-the-parler-videos-reveal
- **Partner/awards**: ProPublica original (Lena V. Groeger, Jeff Kao, Al Shaw, Moiz Syed, Maya Eliahou; essay by Scott Klein and Jeff Kao). No specific award verified for this project in this review. A companion series piece aggregates "Video Evidence Shown in the Capitol Insurrection Criminal Cases" (https://www.propublica.org/series/the-insurrection), i.e., the same footage class became court-exhibit material.
- **What they found**:
  - A "loose confederation of programmers" archived Parler's public content before AWS took the platform offline; one anonymous programmer downloaded "over 1 million videos, 'nearly all the videos ever uploaded to the service, according to the programmer.'"
  - Filtering by "technical data extracted from the videos by the programmer... when they were captured and their proximity to the building" yielded ~2,500 candidate riot videos; human review of every Jan. 6 video cut this to 500+ published, time-synchronized, placeable clips showing the crowd's movement into and through the Capitol.
  - The interactive presents rioters' own first-person recordings as a timeline/map experience — self-documented participation, since uploaders filmed themselves committing the acts.
- **Finding type(s)**: *evidentiary-archive-construction* (the finding is not a single fact but a curated, verifiable corpus that makes an event auditable by the public); *self-documented-participation* (subjects generated the evidence against themselves).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - Scraped platform archive (third-party civic archivist; obtained from the programmer, who cited safety concerns for anonymity) — the raw corpus of ~1M videos.
  - Embedded technical metadata (capture timestamps, location proximity — GPS-bearing video metadata Parler had retained) — extracted by the programmer, used as the filter key.
  - Staff frame-by-frame review — manual relevance/verification pass over every Jan. 6-dated candidate.
  - Legal analysis — General Counsel Jeremy Kutner: publishing the set "is not only undeniably in the public interest but... the essence of a transformative fair use."
- **Detection signature**: **geofence-timewindow metadata join** — video capture metadata (timestamp × location) joined to a target polygon (Capitol grounds) and a target window (Jan. 6) reduced 1M unverifiable clips to 2,500 candidates; human review then converted a data dump into an evidentiary archive. The platform's failure to strip metadata was the enabling defect.
- **Corroboration structure**: metadata filter → independent human review of every clip ("setting aside those that actually weren't taken near or in the Capitol") → cross-video mutual corroboration (multiple angles of the same moments) → alignment with the known public timeline of the breach.
- **Methodology notes**: explicit, stated: https://www.propublica.org/article/why-we-published-parler-users-videos-capitol-attack (quotes above). The essay does not detail the GPS-extraction tooling [that layer inferred from "technical data extracted... by the programmer"].
- **Generalization**: any dead or dying platform with an emergency archive (Parler, Gab dumps, Telegram channel exports) supports the same move: filter mass media by embedded metadata against an event geofence/timewindow, then human-verify. A generic detector looks for: platform shutdown/purge events + existence of a public archive + media formats known to retain EXIF/GPS. The same join works for protests, military strikes, disaster response audits, and police-conduct events.

## 2. Capitol Rioters Planned for Weeks in Plain Sight. The Police Weren't Ready. (2021) — the attack was pre-announced on public forums the authorities ignored

- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/capitol-rioters-planned-for-weeks-in-plain-sight-the-police-werent-ready (Jan. 7, 2021; Logan Jaffe, Lydia DePillis, Isaac Arnsdorf, J. David McSwane; co-published with FRONTLINE)
- **What they found**:
  - Explicit, public pre-announcements: Stop the Steal leaders posted Dec. 23 "We came up with the idea to occupy just outside the CAPITOL on Jan 6th"; Ali Alexander on Parler: "If D.C. escalates… so do we"; MyMilitia.com user (Dec. 12): "I advocate, Revolution and the rules of war."
  - The "Wild Protest" branding traced directly to Trump's "Will be wild" tweet — a public command-and-mobilization chain.
  - A 34-year Capitol Police veteran (Larry Schaefer) and security experts confirmed the preparation posture did not match the visible signal.
- **Finding type(s)**: *foreseeable-threat-ignored* (public open-source signal existed before the event; the institutional gap is the story); *open-forum mobilization record*.
- **Evidence & sources**: public social posts (Parler, MyMilitia, Twitter — "reviewed 'scores of social media posts' in the weeks preceding the attack," collected by reporters [inferred: manual monitoring, no systematic scraper described]); interviews (veteran officer, D.C. AG Karl Racine, academic intelligence expert).
- **Detection signature**: **pre-event signal vs. institutional-posture diff** — a corpus of dated public threats/planning posts compiled after the event and diffed against what the responsible agency did (staffing, intelligence bulletins). The finding is the delta: signal public and dated earlier than the response.
- **Corroboration structure**: screenshots/quotes of dated posts + insider interviews confirming the posture + official statements.
- **Methodology notes**: none published; [inferred] retrospective manual collection of forum posts within 24 hours of the event.
- **Generalization**: applicable wherever harm was preceded by public chatter — school threats, militia actions, coordinated harassment, financial bank-run rumors. Generic detector: archive candidate forums continuously; after any incident, query the archive for dated pre-announcements and diff against the responsible institution's contemporaneous actions.

## 3. Building the "Big Lie": Inside the Creation of Trump's Stolen Election Myth (2022) — each viral fraud claim traced to a named patient zero, and the campaign knew they were false within a day

- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/big-lie-trump-stolen-election-inside-creation (Apr. 26, 2022; Doug Bock Clark, Alexandra Berzon, Kirsten Berg)
- **Partner/awards**: co-published with FRONTLINE, with companion documentary ("Plot to Overturn the Election," per https://www.propublica.org/series/the-insurrection item "New Documentary by Frontline and ProPublica Reveals Origins of the Stolen Election Myth").
- **What they found**:
  - Individual origin attribution for the marquee fraud claims: the Venezuela/Smartmatic narrative (Dominion–Smartmatic ties actually terminated in 2012); Joe Oltmann's unproven "antifa conference call" claim about Eric Coomer (who sued for defamation); "Tore Says" (Terpsehore Maras) shown to have "created an entirely fake online persona"; Edward Solomon's "math proof" resting on "basic misunderstanding of how vote counts work"; Staci Burk's ballot-airplane story fabricated by a troll; Russell Ramsland's altered Antrim County report.
  - Trump campaign staffers internally vetted the claims and "took them less than a day to discover they were baseless" — the myth's promoters had access to the debunk and proceeded anyway.
  - Coalition mechanics documented via internal channels (e.g., the "Purple Unicorns" group chat) connecting Powell, Giuliani, Flynn, Byrne, Wood, Lindell.
- **Finding type(s)**: *disinformation-genealogy* (mapping a belief system to its individually named, dated sources); *knew-it-was-false record* (internal vetting predates public promotion — the scienter finding).
- **Evidence & sources**: a "trove of internal emails and other documentation" (leaked/provided by insiders [inferred]); private group chats; court filings and deposition transcripts from the defamation and election suits; recorded phone conversations; interviews with **eight sources with "firsthand knowledge of the coalition's efforts"** including participants (Byrne); a recantation on the record (Burk).
- **Detection signature**: **claim-genealogy reconstruction with internal-vetting diff** — every public claim was traced backward through communications to its first assertable source, then the date the promoters' own staff debunked it was compared to the dates of continued public promotion. "X (public claim timeline) joined to Y (internal vetting records) on key Z (claim identity) revealed W (promotion after known falsity)."
- **Corroboration structure**: internal documents ↔ sworn depositions ↔ participant interviews ↔ external factual checks (e.g., the 2012 Dominion–Smartmatic contract termination); recantations used as terminal verification.
- **Methodology notes**: no standalone methods page; sourcing characterized in-article (quotes above) [structure inferred from the article's own sourcing statements].
- **Generalization**: works on any viral false narrative with a discoverable insider layer: stock-promotion rumors, health scares, war atrocity fabrications. Generic detector: for each claim, build (first-assertion source, internal first-debunk date, continued-promotion dates); scienter = promotion postdating internal debunk. Litigation discovery (defamation suits) is the reliable declassifier of the internal layer.

## 4. Facebook Hosted Surge of Misinformation and Insurrection Threats in Months Leading Up to Jan. 6 (2022, w/ Washington Post) — 650,000 delegitimizing posts quantified from outside the platform

- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/facebook-hosted-surge-of-misinformation-and-insurrection-threats-in-months-leading-up-to-jan-6-attack-records-show (Jan. 4, 2022; Craig Silverman, Jeff Kao — ProPublica; Craig Timberg, Jeremy B. Merrill — Washington Post)
- **Partner/awards**: formal co-publication with The Washington Post.
- **What they found**:
  - "At least 650,000 posts attack[ed] Biden's election legitimacy across Facebook groups between Election Day and January 6, averaging 10,000+ posts daily" — a floor, since the data covers only public groups and excludes comments.
  - Over 27,000 U.S. political Facebook groups identified; roughly 2,500+ removed during the study window — quantifying both the surge and Facebook's partial, late enforcement.
  - Key accountability framing: "Only Facebook has access to all the data to calculate the true total — and it hasn't done so publicly."
- **Finding type(s)**: *platform-amplification-quantification* (converting "there was a lot of misinformation" into a counted, dated, floor-bounded number); *enforcement-gap measurement* (removals vs. volume).
- **Evidence & sources**:
  - Commercial monitoring dataset — CounterAction (disinformation research firm) tracked 100,000+ public groups, posts/metadata/images, Jan. 2020–June 2021; "CounterAction marked Facebook groups for tracking if group members had posted links to U.S. political websites."
  - ML classification — text models trained to identify political groups among the tracked set, "approximately 79% precision," yielding the 27,000-group frame.
  - Keyword lexicon — "about 60 terms that indicated delegitimization on their own, plus 86 more in two buckets" (e.g., "stop the steal," "fraud") applied to 18.7 million posts in the Election Day→Jan. 6 window; "Hand-checking achieved 64% precision."
- **Detection signature**: **lexicon census over a third-party platform shadow-archive** — because the platform withholds its own totals, an externally collected mirror (CounterAction) was classified (ML for population definition, lexicon for behavior) to produce a defensible minimum count with published precision rates. "X (18.7M mirrored posts) filtered by Y (validated delegitimization lexicon) within Z (political-group population defined by ML) revealed W (≥650K delegitimizing posts, ~10K/day)."
- **Corroboration structure**: precision measured by manual hand-checks and disclosed (79% / 64%); undercount direction stated explicitly (public groups only); platform given the chance to produce the true number.
- **Methodology notes**: methodology described in-article (all quotes above); explicitly quantified error rates — the most statistically disciplined piece in this cluster.
- **Generalization**: any platform-accountability question where the platform stonewalls: use commercial/academic shadow-archives (CrowdTangle exports, Pushshift, ad libraries), define the population with a classifier, count behavior with a validated lexicon, publish precision, and frame results as floors. Generic detector inputs: mirror corpus + labeled seed set + validated term list.

## 5. Redistricting dark-money arc: The Hidden Hands in Redistricting (2011) + How Dark Money Helped Republicans Hold the House and Hurt Voters (2012) — "citizen" map groups were fronts, and untraceable money bought the 2011 maps that outweighed 2012 votes

- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/hidden-hands-in-redistricting-corporations-special-interests (Sept. 23, 2011; Olga Pierce, Jeff Larson, Lois Beckett); https://www.propublica.org/article/how-dark-money-helped-republicans-hold-the-house-and-hurt-voters (Dec. 21, 2012; Olga Pierce, Justin Elliott, Theodoric Meyer). Series: https://www.propublica.org/series/redistricting
- **Partner/awards**: ProPublica originals. (Dark-money mechanics overlap sibling report; treated here only for the redistricting-specific joins.)
- **What they found**:
  - Nominally grassroots redistricting groups were "quietly bankrolled by corporations, unions and other special interests": Fair Districts Mass (chairman Jack Robinson, a three-time failed GOP candidate who re-registered Independent to look nonpartisan; unlimited undisclosed corporate money; proposals targeting Rep. John Tierney); Minnesotans for a Fair Redistricting (shared leadership and home address with the Koch-linked Freedom Foundation of Minnesota); Florida's Protect Your Vote (~$800,000 corporate, incl. Honeywell $25K and CSX $25K — both with business ties to figurehead Rep. Corrine Brown).
  - 2012: Democrats won ~1 million more House votes nationally yet Republicans held the chamber — an outcome seen "only three times in the past century" — because the RSLC's REDMAP raised $30M in 2010 (3x Democrats) to capture legislatures ahead of map-drawing; its 501(c)(4) arm (State Government Leadership Foundation) paid $166,000 to mapmaker Tom Hofeller's team; North Carolina went 7-6 D → 9-4 R while Democrats won 50.6% of the statewide House vote, via packing minority voters into three districts.
  - Smoking-gun funding intent: "The entirety of this effort will be paid for using non-federal dollars through our 501c(4) organization."
- **Finding type(s)**: *front-group-unmasking* (a "neutral civic" entity resolved to its funders/operators, whose interests contradict the stated mission); *money-in-maps* (dark-money flows tied to specific cartographic outcomes); *votes-seats asymmetry* (statistical outcome anomaly used as the hook that the money explains).
- **Evidence & sources**: IRS 990s and state nonprofit/business registrations (funding + officer identity); federal/state lobbying disclosures; campaign finance records; shared-address/shared-officer analysis; **litigation discovery** — NC lawsuit depositions and internal emails exposed the Hofeller payments and strategy; election-results analysis (statewide vote totals vs. seats); comparative district-map analysis against historical voting patterns; interviews.
- **Detection signature**: two chained moves. (a) **shared-infrastructure resolution**: candidate front groups joined to known interest groups on registered agent / office address / officers / domain registration → operator identity. (b) **outcome-vs-mandate asymmetry**: aggregate vote share compared to seat share per state; where they diverge, trace who financed and drew the lines (990 payments → consultants → specific maps).
- **Corroboration structure**: registry paper trail → funders' own strategy documents (obtained via litigation) → quantitative outcome data → subject interviews/denials.
- **Methodology notes**: no formal methods page for either ([inferred] from in-article sourcing); the 2012 piece's key documents credited to NC litigation records.
- **Generalization**: front-group resolution transfers to any "concerned citizens" campaign (zoning fights, referendum committees, patient-advocacy groups). Votes-seats asymmetry generalizes to any allocation system versus its input mandate (procurement scores vs. awards, grant rankings vs. funding). Generic detector: efficiency-gap-style outlier stats to select targets, then registry/990/address joins to identify who paid for the mechanism.

## 6. How Democrats Fooled California's Redistricting Commission (2011) — astroturf testimony, matched line-by-line to party strategy memos, steered an "independent" commission

- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/how-democrats-fooled-californias-redistricting-commission (Dec. 21, 2011; Olga Pierce, Jeff Larson); underlying document releases: "California Redistricting Commission Emails" and "...Meeting Transcripts" (Dec. 20, 2011, per https://www.propublica.org/series/redistricting)
- **Partner/awards**: ProPublica original; the commission published a formal response ("Statement from California Citizens Redistricting Commission Responding to Our Story," Dec. 23, 2011, series index), and California Republicans called for an official investigation (series index) — official impact.
- **What they found**:
  - House Democrats ran a covert operation against the new citizens commission: secret member meetings, consultant Paul Mitchell's fake grassroots group "OneSanJoaquin" (protecting Rep. Jerry McNerney), lobbyist Jennifer Wada presenting as an Asian community representative while actually being "from rural Idaho," and the Asian American Education Institute — a shell with no full-time employees whose **domain was registered to political consultant Bill Wong**.
  - Coordination hub: House aide Alexis Marks, documented in 100+ emails; memos set goals ("create a plan of action") and treated "communities of interest" as tactical cover.
  - Outcome diff: Democrats projected to gain 1–2 seats gained six to seven; internal memo: "Every member of the Northern California Democratic Caucus has a ticket back to DC." Genuine communities (South El Monte, Little Saigon) were split as collateral.
- **Finding type(s)**: *astroturf-testimony attribution* (public-comment records resolved to a coordinated sponsor); *process-capture of a neutral institution*; *stated-goal vs. achieved-map concordance* (internal targets matched final districts).
- **Evidence & sources**: leaked/obtained internal Democratic emails and strategy memos (100+); the commission's own record — "a torrent of advice — a total of 30,000 separate pieces of testimony and documents" — used as the public-side corpus; commission meeting transcripts; domain-registration (WHOIS) lookups; the final maps vs. internal seat projections; interviews with participants and commissioners.
- **Detection signature**: **testimony-to-memo join with persona verification** — the public testimony corpus was matched against internal strategy documents on district asks, phrasing, and personnel; individual witnesses were then background-verified (residence, employer, domain registrant), collapsing "community voices" into a party operation. Confirmed by outcome concordance: final lines matched the memos' targets, not the projections.
- **Corroboration structure**: internal documents → matched public testimony → persona checks (WHOIS, residency) → map-outcome comparison → participant interviews; full email/transcript sets published alongside.
- **Methodology notes**: no formal methods page [inferred from in-article description], but ProPublica published the underlying email and transcript collections as companion items — evidence-transparency as method.
- **Generalization**: applies to any notice-and-comment or public-hearing process: regulatory dockets (fake-comment scandals), zoning hearings, utility rate cases. Generic detector: cluster public submissions by template similarity/metadata; verify submitter identities (registries, WHOIS, address history); diff final decisions against any obtainable internal target lists.

## 7. The Real Bosses of New Jersey: $1.1 billion of $1.6 billion in Camden tax breaks traced to one unelected man's network (2019)

- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/george-norcross-democratic-donor-tax-breaks (May 1, 2019; Nancy Solomon — WNYC, Jeff Pillets — ProPublica); emails story: https://www.propublica.org/article/emails-show-how-much-pull-political-bosses-had-over-state-tax-breaks-new-jersey-norcross (May 21, 2019). Series: https://www.propublica.org/series/the-real-bosses-of-new-jersey
- **Partner/awards**: WNYC/ProPublica (Local Reporting Network); co-published with The Star-Ledger. No award verified in this review.
- **What they found**:
  - Of $1.6B in tax incentives to companies investing in Camden under the 2013 Economic Opportunity Act, "at least $1.1 billion went to Norcross' own insurance brokerage, his business partnerships and charitable affiliations, and clients of the law and lobbying firms of his brother Philip": Conner Strong & Buckelew $86.2M; Michaels Organization $79.4M; NFI $79.4M; American Water $164.2M (Philip's lobbying client); Holtec $260M (George on its board); 76ers $82M (Philip's law client); Cooper Health $39.9M (George chairman).
  - The enabling law was family-made: Philip Norcross's firm Parker McCay helped draft amendments; brother Donald (then state senator) co-sponsored; a lawmaker called it "George's baby." Camden got "more than four times as many tax breaks as the combined amounts of other cities designated as 'growth zones'" (EDA data). A state-commissioned Rutgers study put 13 "Camden alternatives" projects at $340,000 per job vs. ~$5,000 statewide; "Norcross has a link to at least nine of the 13."
  - The emails story: a **12,000-page email cache obtained through a public records request (Sept. 2013–Feb. 2015)** showed Parker McCay attorney Kevin Sheehan editing the tax-break legislation itself and shepherding client applications; Christie-administration counsel to Sheehan: "A copy of the bill incorporating all the changes we previously discussed would be very helpful."
- **Finding type(s)**: *capture-of-discretionary-benefit* (one network's share of a public program quantified); *legislation-authorship conflict* (beneficiary's agent drafted the rules); *cost-per-outcome outlier* (subsidy per job vs. baseline).
- **Evidence & sources**: NJ EDA applications, award ledgers, and employment certifications (program data); lobbying disclosures (client lists of Philip's firms); corporate/board records and charitable affiliations (relationship edges); the 12,000-page OPRA email production (drafting/influence record); state property records; litigation documents; Rutgers study (cost benchmark); interviews.
- **Detection signature**: **beneficiary-network overlay on an award ledger** — the full list of program awards was joined to a relationship graph centered on one person (ownership, board seats, partnerships, kin, and *clients of kin's firms*), then summed: $1.1B/$1.6B. Secondary signature: **rule-author identity check** — the legislative drafting record (emails) resolved the law's actual author to the beneficiaries' lawyer.
- **Corroboration structure**: program ledger (official) → relationship edges (registries, boards, lobbying rolls) → drafting emails (public records) → cost benchmarking (independent academic study) → on-record responses (Norcross's "King George" pushback, https://www.propublica.org/article/new-jersey-political-boss-defends-tax-breaks-denounces-king-george-critics).
- **Methodology notes**: no formal methods page; connection-mapping approach described in-article [inferred structure: award ledger × ownership/board/client joins]. Email provenance stated: public records request, 12,000 pages.
- **Official impact**: Gov. Murphy audit + task force under ex-prosecutor Jim Walden; AG inquiry into "whether any laws were broken"; $578M more in breaks put on hold (https://www.propublica.org/article/new-jersey-puts-578-million-in-controversial-tax-breaks-on-hold-after-investigation); the incentive program was allowed to lapse and be rebuilt. In June 2024 the NJ AG indicted Norcross and associates on racketeering charges centered on the same Camden waterfront conduct (https://gothamist.com/news/powerful-nj-political-boss-george-norcross-accused-of-racketeering-in-sweeping-indictment); a trial judge later dismissed the indictment and the state appealed [status beyond fetched pages; verify before reuse].
- **Generalization**: any discretionary benefit program (tax credits, opportunity zones, enterprise grants, COVID relief) supports the same overlay. Generic detector: compute each connected network's share of program dollars; flag networks whose share far exceeds peer share, especially when the program's enabling rules were authored or amended by the network's agents (check drafting correspondence, lobbying records, bill sponsor kinship).

## 8. A False Answer, a Big Political Connection and $260 Million (Holtec) + the Camden land assembly map (2019) — one sworn checkbox falsified against federal records; land bought ahead of the subsidy wave

- **URL**: Holtec: https://www.propublica.org/article/holtec-international-tax-break-application-false-answer-new-jersey-on-hold (June 4, 2019 follow-up; original May 23, 2019, per series index). Land interactive: https://projects.propublica.org/graphics/camden ("See How This Political Boss and His Associates Bought Up Valuable Land After A Tax Break Law," Dec. 9, 2019; Nancy Solomon — WNYC, Alex Mierjeski, Al Shaw — ProPublica). Related: https://www.propublica.org/article/how-a-politically-powerful-family-muscled-a-nonprofit-out-of-some-of-a-citys-most-valuable-land
- **Partner/awards**: WNYC/ProPublica.
- **What they found**:
  - Holtec CEO Kris Singh swore on the EDA application that the company "had never been barred from doing business with a state or federal agency" — but in 2010 the Tennessee Valley Authority suspended Holtec for two months and fined it $2M after Holtec paid a TVA employee $54,212 that the employee hid from disclosures. The false answer sat under a $260M award (largest in the program); Holtec had already collected a $26M installment. The state froze the break "while investigators examine details of its application."
  - The parcel map: Norcross, his business partners, and Parker McCay clients came to own most of the Camden waterfront redevelopment area. Timeline join: the 2013 law ("Norcross and his brothers helped push generous tax breaks for Camden through the Legislature") preceded acquisitions from 2015 on (Liberty Property Trust development rights 2015; Norcross's Ferry Terminal Building purchase 2016); land was bought from state agencies "for nominal sums (e.g., $134,191)" and resold higher; mapped breaks ranged $6M (Webimax tenant) to $245M (Triad 1828 tower).
- **Finding type(s)**: *false-sworn-certification* (an application answer contradicted by the referenced agency's own records); *insider-timed-asset-assembly* (acquisitions clustered after private knowledge of a value-creating law, before public value realization); *displacement-by-machine* (nonprofit muscled off valuable land).
- **Evidence & sources**: the sworn EDA certification (application document); federal TVA suspension/fine records (the external truth source); deed and property-tax records for every waterfront parcel; EDA award data per parcel/tenant; ownership-connection records (who is a partner/client of whom); attorney communications and later state grand-jury subpoenas (follow-on); interviews.
- **Detection signature**: two crisp moves. (a) **sworn-answer vs. external-registry diff** — every yes/no certification on a benefit application implies a checkable registry; Holtec's "never barred" answer was diffed against TVA's debarment/suspension record and failed. (b) **legislation-to-deed timeline join** — parcel transfer dates joined to the law's drafting/passage dates and to the buyers' relationship to the law's authors revealed purchase-ahead-of-subsidy behavior, rendered parcel-by-parcel on a before/after map (2011 vs. present).
- **Corroboration structure**: application text (primary) ↔ federal agency records (primary) ↔ company's own admission ("inadvertent mistake" via counsel); map layer: deeds ↔ EDA ledger ↔ relationship graph, visualized so readers can audit each parcel.
- **Methodology notes**: neither piece carries a formal methods page; the interactive lists its data sources (property records, deeds, EDA awards) in credits [inferred assembly: county deed pulls + EDA ledger join].
- **Official impact**: $260M break frozen; task force criminal referral(s); the waterfront conduct later formed part of the 2024 racketeering indictment (see entry 7).
- **Generalization**: (a) generalizes to every sworn application ecosystem — SAM.gov debarment vs. contract bids, SEC bad-actor questions, bar applications, grant certifications: enumerate the registries each question implies and diff. (b) generalizes to any legislated value event: join asset-transfer records (deeds, options, leases, stock purchases) to the nonpublic drafting window and to the drafters' network; clustering inside the window is the tell.

## 9. Big Jim (2019–2023, w/ Charleston Gazette-Mail / Mountain State Spotlight) — a sitting governor's companies profiled through 600+ lawsuits as a nonpayment business model he kept running while regulating himself

- **Partner/awards**: coalition "including Google News Lab, Univision, the USA Today Network, the CUNY Graduate School of Journalism, WNYC, First Draft and student journalists from 12 colleges and universities"; "more than 1,100 participating journalists." - **What they found / built**:
  - Infrastructure finding: a repeatable national monitoring system — "communicated with voters directly, checking in with more than 120,000 people"; intake via the 1-866-OUR-VOTE lawyer-staffed line (990 tips in 2016), SMS signup (3,111 texts), WhatsApp, web form, email, and trained students scouring social media; Google News Lab tracked voting-related search trends; everything flowed "into an internal system called Landslide"; "Professional journalists vetted" tips and routed "real-time leads to the national news desk and to local reporting partners" — a command center serving "250 reporters in 125 newsrooms" **while polls were still open**.
  - Substantive negative finding, repeated each cycle: "the Electionland coalition saw no evidence of widespread fraud" — the tip net that catches long lines and machine failures is also the systematic falsifier of fraud narratives.
  - Robocalls (2020): "according to data and call recordings provided by the firm TelTech, which owns the RoboKiller smartphone app," more than 3 million "stay safe and stay home" robocalls were placed on Election Day; at least 800,000 reached people in swing states; per-state call volumes included Texas 798K+, Florida 534K+, Pennsylvania 93K+, Michigan 89K+, North Carolina 60K+; 146,000+ spoofed originating numbers; TelTech traced the same campaign back to December 2019 with bursts tracking COVID spikes (and sightings in Canada/Australia) — complicating the suppression-intent read. FBI investigated; NY AG Letitia James: "Attempts to hinder voters from casting ballots by spreading misinformation is illegal."
- **Finding type(s)**: *real-time distributed-verification infrastructure* (the system is the finding); *negative-space finding* (absence of widespread fraud, established by the most sensitive detector available); *coordinated-suppression-signal quantification* (robocall counts from commercial telecom telemetry).
- **Evidence & sources**: crowdsourced voter reports (SMS/phone/WhatsApp/web); Election Protection call-center data (integrated coalition feed); trained-student social media monitoring (First Draft verification protocols); Google search-trend telemetry; commercial robocall-detection telemetry (TelTech/RoboKiller call data + recordings); state/county election-administration records for follow-ups; hundreds of local partner reporters as ground truth.
- **Detection signature**: **many-sensor triangulation with human verification gate** — independent sensor feeds (tips, hotline, social posts, search spikes) are clustered by place/time in a triage tool (Landslide); a report becomes a story only when feeds corroborate and a local reporter or official record confirms — inverted from normal journalism in that detection precedes assignment. For robocalls: **third-party telemetry census** — a spam-detection vendor's passive network data converted a rumor ("people got scary calls") into counted, per-state, time-series evidence including historical provenance (Dec. 2019 origin) that itself checked the intent narrative.
- **Corroboration structure**: tip → cross-feed clustering → journalist verification (First Draft protocols) → local-partner ground check → official response; robocall numbers corroborated by recordings, spoofing analysis, and multi-country sightings.
- **Methodology notes**: extensively documented across the launch post, "Monitoring the Vote," the ONA writeup, and partner postmortems (First Draft, Nieman Lab, CJR) — the best-documented methodology in this cluster.
- **Official impact**: FBI and NY AG action on the robocalls; same-day fixes as local partners confronted officials while polls were open [mechanism per the ONA/Nieman writeups].
- **Generalization**: the architecture transfers to any distributed, time-boxed event with dispersed witnesses: disaster response, census operations, vaccine rollouts, strike/picket monitoring. Minimum kit: multi-channel intake, a dedupe/cluster triage tool, a trained verification layer, and pre-committed local partners empowered to act in real time. The telemetry move transfers to any commercially-sensed abuse channel (robocalls, robotexts, spam email, ad networks): the anti-abuse vendor's passive dataset is an unexploited evidence store.

4. **Cross-jurisdiction accumulation on a resolved entity/person** — 3 (Big Jim dockets; GA challenge logs across 30 counties; certification refusals across 4 states). Aggregation itself produces the finding no single jurisdiction can see.

### 3. Transferable pattern candidates

**A. Single-Network Share-of-Program Test.** Take any discretionary public-benefit ledger (tax credits, grants, contracts, zoning variances) and a relationship graph around a hypothesized hub (ownership, board seats, kinship, and — the NJ innovation — *clients of kin's professional firms* counted as network edges). Compute the hub-network's share of total program value and compare to any peer network. Confirmed aggravator: the program's rules were drafted or amended by the network's own agents (get the drafting correspondence via records requests). Minimum data: complete award ledger; registry/board/lobbying edges; bill-drafting or rulemaking correspondence. Recognition cue in any domain: one city/entity/person receiving multiples of all comparables combined (Camden = 4x all other growth zones), plus a rules-origin story that keeps leading back to the beneficiaries.

**B. Sworn-Answer Registry Diff.** Every application containing yes/no certifications ("never debarred," "no prior denials," "no related-party interest") implicitly names the registry that could falsify it (debarment lists, suspension records, court indexes, prior filings). Systematically enumerate the implied registries and diff. One false sworn answer converts a policy story into a legal-exposure story and gives officials a nondiscretionary reason to act (NJ froze $260M on one checkbox). Minimum data: the application text (records request) + the external registry. Recognition cue: any high-value approval process where applications are certified but nobody re-checks the certifications — the diff has usually never been run.

**C. Law-to-Asset Clock.** Join dated asset transactions (deeds, options, leases, equity purchases) to the private-knowledge window of a value-creating government act (between drafting participation and public awareness/passage). Purchases clustered inside the window by parties connected to the drafters indicate trading on nonpublic legislative knowledge. Minimum data: transaction records with dates and parties; the act's drafting chronology (emails, lobbying records, sponsor identity); a relationship map to the drafters. Recognition cue: nominal-price acquisitions from public bodies shortly before subsidies/zoning make the same parcels valuable. Works identically for highway routes, base closures, rezonings, and tariff carve-outs.

**D. Distributed-Records Concentration Audit.** When a submission channel (challenges, complaints, comments, takedowns) is held in fragmented local logs, aggregate them centrally and compute submitter concentration and outcome rates; then reverse-engineer the top submitters' matching heuristics and audit their false positives with named victims. The two findings compound: the "movement" is six people, and their algorithm sweeps in lawful targets. Minimum data: per-jurisdiction logs with submitter identity and adjudication outcome (FOIA fan-out); a sample of underlying match keys. Recognition cue: template-identical submissions arriving in bulk, or public boasts of volume ("360,000 challenges") that no organic process could produce.

**E. Dead-Platform Metadata Harvest.** When a platform dies, purges, or leaks, its archive plus retained media metadata (GPS, timestamps, device info) supports geofence × timewindow reconstruction of physical events, with human review as the conversion step from data dump to evidentiary archive. Minimum data: the archive; metadata extraction capability; a defined event polygon/window; reviewer capacity (the Parler ratio: 1M → 2,500 machine-filtered → 500+ human-verified). Recognition cue: any event whose participants self-documented on a platform that subsequently lost control of its data. Publish the verified corpus itself — the archive as public evidence outlasts any single story.

**F. Enforcement-Gap Ledger** (bonus; combines signatures 4 and 7). Enumerate violations of a clear legal duty from primary records (refusals, recusal failures, open ethics cases), then join each to the accountability system's response and to elapsed time. The systemic story is the empty sanction column (10 refusals, majority unpunished; 1,200 bills/yr vs. ~4 recusals/yr; a 9-year-open "most egregious" case). Minimum data: an enumerable duty (certify, recuse, adjudicate) + event records + disposition records. Recognition cue: an oversight body that has never completed a disciplinary process — Surry County's was "the first completed... nationwide," which means the detector fires almost everywhere.
