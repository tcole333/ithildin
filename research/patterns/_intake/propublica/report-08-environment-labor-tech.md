## STRAND A — ENVIRONMENT

### Buried Secrets: Gas Drilling's Environmental Threat (2008–2011) — Fracking-linked water contamination is far more widespread than EPA asserted, and regulation was legislatively disabled
- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/buried-secrets-is-natural-gas-drilling-endangering-us-water-supplies-1113 (series index: https://www.propublica.org/series/fracking)
- **Partner/awards**: ProPublica original (some pieces w/ BusinessWeek). 2009 George Polk Award (environmental reporting); 2009 Sigma Delta Chi (Lustgarten, Sapien, Shankman); NPF Stokes Award ([awards](https://www.propublica.org/article/propublica-honored-with-two-awards-from-the-society-of-professional-journal)).
- **What they found**:
  - Sublette County, WY: benzene at 1,500x the safe level in a rural water well; contamination in 88 of 220 wells tested, plume extending 28 miles — the first federally verified fracking-area contamination case.
  - 1,000+ contamination cases documented across Colorado, New Mexico, Alabama, Ohio, Pennsylvania; seven sites examined in depth (incl. the 2007 Bainbridge, OH house explosion from methane-saturated water).
  - The 2005 Energy Policy Act ("Halliburton loophole") exempted hydraulic fracturing from the Safe Drinking Water Act; an EPA groundwater specialist: "That pretty much closed the door. So we absolutely do not look at fracking… under the Safe Drinking Water Act."
  - EPA's Denver office rated the Sublette development "unsatisfactory" and recommended halting it; BLM approved 4,400 new wells anyway (Sept 2008).
  - Follow-on series extended the pattern to Class II injection wells ("Injection Wells: The Poison Beneath Us," 2012 — https://www.propublica.org/article/injection-wells-the-poison-beneath-us).
- **Finding type(s)**: regulatory-capture (agency documented the risk, then was statutorily/administratively disabled); concentrated-harm-hotspot; institutional-coverup (industry non-disclosure of frac fluid composition). New tag: **statutory-exemption-as-evidence** (the carve-out itself is the finding).
- **Evidence & sources**: state water-test results (primary, request-gated); EPA emails and handwritten 2003 negotiation notes (request-gated); hazardous-material shipping manifests (request-gated); state inspection records & court documents (open/request-gated); resident/regulator/industry interviews (constructed-fieldwork); BLM approval records (open-public).
- **Access tier**: mixed — request-gated (EPA/state records) + open-public (federal registers, court dockets) + constructed (field interviews).
- **Acquisition path**: mixed — FOIA/state records requests + litigation-records + interviews + field-observation.
- **Detection signature**: case-accumulation-against-official-denial — state-by-state water test results and incident records aggregated across jurisdictions and compared to EPA's public position ("no confirmed cases") revealed the assertion was only true because the agency had stopped looking; joined to the legislative record showing *why* it stopped (2005 SDWA exemption).
- **Corroboration structure**: each contamination case anchored in a primary water test or inspection record; regulator admissions on record; the statutory exemption independently verifiable in the U.S. Code; industry rebuttals printed and tested against records.
- **Methodology notes**: No formal data-methodology page (pre-data-desk era). Method reconstructed from the articles and retrospectives ([Nieman Reports](https://niemanreports.org/reporting-time-and-resources-reveal-a-hidden-source-of-pollution/)) [inferred: multi-year records-request + shoe-leather accumulation].
- **Generalization**: any industry with a bespoke statutory exemption (crypto, PFAS, CAFO air emissions, private equity reporting). Generic detector: (1) find the carve-out in statute/rule; (2) collect incident records the exempted regulator no longer collects; (3) compare agency public claims of "no evidence" against the incident base. Absence of records after an exemption is the tell, not the refutation.

### Losing Ground (2014, with The Lens) — Louisiana loses a football field of coast every ~48 minutes; 2,000 sq mi gone since the 1930s, driven by oil-and-gas canals and levees
- **URL**: https://projects.propublica.org/louisiana/ (launch note: https://www.propublica.org/atpropublica/propublica-the-lens-launch-multimedia-project-on-coastal-loss-in-louisiana)
- **Partner/awards**: The Lens (New Orleans; text by Bob Marshall) + Knight-Mozilla OpenNews fellow Brian Jacobs; maps by Al Shaw. 2015 Edward R. Murrow Award (website audio) ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/atpropublica/losing-ground-wins-2015-edward-r.-murrow-award)); Gannett Award for Innovation in Watchdog Journalism ([The Lens](https://thelensnola.org/2015/04/03/lens-propublica-win-award-for-innovation-in-watchdog-journalism-for-losing-ground/)); OJA finalist ([OJA](https://awards.journalists.org/entries/losing-ground/)).
- **What they found**:
  - ~2,000 square miles of Louisiana coast lost since the mid-1930s; loss rate ≈16 sq mi/year — "a football field every 48 minutes" ([PRX summary](https://theworld.org/stories/2014/09/23/losing-ground-louisiana-2)).
  - Causes: Mississippi River levees starving the delta of sediment + ~10,000 miles of oil/gas canals and dredging accelerating saltwater intrusion (plus subsidence/sea rise).
  - Communities (e.g., Isle de Jean Charles region) shown disappearing within residents' lifetimes; projected future loss mapped against state master-plan scenarios.
- **Finding type(s)**: concentrated-harm-hotspot (slow-motion, geographic); externalized-cost-visualization. New tag: **remote-sensing-time-series** as finding-generator.
- **Evidence & sources**: NASA/USGS Landsat 8 raw band data (open-public bulk); historical USGS topographic maps and aerial photos back to a scanned 1922 survey map (open-public archival); state coastal master plan projections (open-public); resident interviews/audio (constructed-fieldwork).
- **Access tier**: open-public (satellite + archival cartography) with constructed processing; fieldwork for narrative.
- **Acquisition path**: bulk-public-data + field-observation + interviews.
- **Detection signature**: remote-sensing-time-series — 40 years of Landsat scenes (custom band math to sharpen land/water contrast) registered against 80-year-old survey maps on a common geographic grid revealed cumulative land loss invisible in any single year's imagery; the delta of the composites IS the finding.
- **Corroboration structure**: satellite-derived loss cross-checked against USGS published loss estimates and state master-plan science; human stories anchor specific mapped locations.
- **Methodology notes**: STATED — "How We Made Losing Ground," Source/OpenNews (Shaw & Jacobs): raw Landsat bundles (11 TIFF bands + QA), scenes selected Mar 31–May 15, 2014 for cloud-free coverage, direct control of wavelength bands to maximize land/water contrast ([Source](https://source.opennews.org/articles/how-we-made-losing-ground/)).
- **Generalization**: any slow environmental extraction: aquifer-driven subsidence, mining footprint growth, deforestation around concessions, coastal development in flood zones. Generic detector: pick a hard geo key (grid cell), build decadal composites from open EOS data, diff, then join the loss surface to permit/lease/ownership layers to assign responsibility.

### Killing the Colorado (2015) — The West's water crisis is man-made: law and subsidy, not just drought
- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/series/killing-the-colorado (summary: https://projects.propublica.org/killing-the-colorado/story/what-you-need-to-know)
- **Partner/awards**: ProPublica original (Lustgarten, Shaw, Larson, et al.); Discovery documentary 2016. 2016 Pulitzer finalist ([ProPublica Pulitzer page](https://www.propublica.org/about/pulitzer-prize-winners-and-finalists)); OJA entry ([OJA](https://awards.journalists.org/entries/killing-the-colorado/)).
- **What they found**:
  - Paper water exceeds wet water: the seven basin states allocate 5.4 trillion gallons/year — ~1.4 trillion more than the river has actually carried on average since 2001.
  - "Holy Crop": Congress paid >$1B in subsidies for Arizona cotton (and ~$3B for California cotton) since 1995, propping up one of the thirstiest crops in the desert.
  - "Use It or Lose It": prior-appropriation doctrine punishes conservation — farmers must waste water to preserve rights.
  - Lake Powell loses up to 292 billion gallons/year to evaporation and seepage (~6% of the river's flow); Navajo Generating Station, a top-emitting coal plant, exists largely to pump Colorado River water uphill to Arizona.
- **Finding type(s)**: policy-erosion / perverse-incentive-structure; regulatory-capture (water law serving incumbents); two-books-diff (allocated vs. actual flow). New tag: **subsidy-incentive-join**.
- **Evidence & sources**: USDA farm-subsidy payment data (open-public bulk); Bureau of Reclamation flow/allocation/evaporation records (open-public); state water-rights law and adjudications (open-public); utility/power records for NGS (open-public); farmer/official interviews (constructed-fieldwork).
- **Access tier**: open-public + constructed (fieldwork).
- **Acquisition path**: bulk-public-data + interviews + field-observation.
- **Detection signature**: subsidy-incentive-join — federal subsidy payment records joined to crop water-consumption coefficients and basin hydrology showed public money directly financing the over-consumption; separately, legal-allocation totals diffed against gauge-measured actual flow quantified the structural overdraft.
- **Corroboration structure**: every quantitative claim traceable to an agency dataset (USDA, USBR); legal claims to statute/doctrine; incentives confirmed by the affected farmers on record.
- **Methodology notes**: No single methods page; numbers sourced inline to USDA/USBR data [inferred: desk analysis of federal datasets + legal review]. Interactive: https://projects.propublica.org/killing-the-colorado/explore-the-river/.
- **Generalization**: any resource governed by rights regimes + subsidies: fisheries quotas, grazing permits, groundwater, carbon credits. Generic detector: (1) sum legal entitlements vs. measured resource (two-books-diff); (2) join subsidy flows to consumption intensity per recipient; (3) flag rules that penalize conservation.

### Fuel to the Fire (2018, with The New York Times Magazine) — U.S. biofuel law triggered Indonesian peatland destruction that out-emitted the carbon it was meant to save
- **URL**: https://features.propublica.org/palm-oil/palm-oil-biofuels-ethanol-indonesia-peatland
- **Partner/awards**: NYT Magazine co-publication; Overseas Press Club Whitman Bassow Award ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/atpropublica/propublica-wins-overseas-press-club-whitman-bassow-award)).
- **What they found**:
  - The 2007 Energy Independence and Security Act mandate (replace 35B gallons of petroleum with plant-based fuels) pushed global vegetable-oil demand into Indonesian palm oil; Kalimantan palm concessions more than tripled 2007–2014.
  - Draining/burning carbon-rich peatland: emissions equivalent to "opening 70 new, large coal-fired power plants"; NASA researchers called the 2015 fire season's contribution the largest single-year global carbon increase in two millennia; ~100,000 estimated premature deaths (Columbia/Harvard); 120,000+ NASA-detected fire hot spots in 2015.
  - Named chain: Bumitama Gunajaya Agro, Wilmar International, Salim Group/Indofood — with BlackRock/Vanguard as investors; EPA's own 2009–2010 land-use-change analysis had flagged the risk and was politically neutralized.
- **Finding type(s)**: policy-boomerang (mandate produces the harm it targets); supply-chain-harm-attribution; regulatory-capture (EPA lifecycle analysis sidelined). New tag: **cross-border-policy-externality-trace**.
- **Evidence & sources**: NASA satellite fire hot-spot data (open-public); concession maps/records (open/NGO data); EPA lifecycle rulemaking record (open-public); field reporting in Kalimantan incl. whistleblower Gusti Gelambong (constructed/privileged-insider); trade and production statistics (open-public).
- **Access tier**: mixed — open-public (satellite, rulemaking, trade data) + constructed (field) + privileged (insider accounts).
- **Acquisition path**: mixed — bulk-public-data + field-observation + interviews + insider testimony.
- **Detection signature**: cross-border-policy-externality-trace — U.S. mandate volumes (statute) correlated in time with Indonesian concession expansion and NASA fire/emissions data; the demand shock traced through commodity substitution (soy→palm displacement) to a measurable carbon outcome that inverts the policy's stated goal.
- **Corroboration structure**: policy timeline × satellite time series × concession records × on-the-ground verification of specific cleared villages; EPA's own suppressed analysis used as the internal-witness layer.
- **Methodology notes**: [inferred] from article sourcing (NASA hot-spot counts, EISA text, EPA docket); no standalone methods page.
- **Generalization**: EV-battery minerals, carbon offsets, "sustainable aviation fuel," conflict minerals. Generic detector: for any mandate/subsidy creating commodity demand, join mandated volumes to exporting-country land-use/emissions series and look for the displacement elasticity the policy's accounting ignored.

### The Great Climate Migration (2020, with NYT Magazine; Pulitzer Center support) — First modeled, geocoded projection of where climate change will move millions of people
- **URL**: https://features.propublica.org/climate-migration/model-how-climate-refugees-move-across-continents/ (US sequel: https://projects.propublica.org/climate-migration/ ; https://www.propublica.org/article/climate-change-will-force-a-new-american-migration)
- **Partner/awards**: NYT Magazine; funded by the Pulitzer Center ([methodology page](https://www.propublica.org/article/2020-climate-migration-part-1-methodology)).
- **What they found**:
  - Modeled migration from Central America/Mexico under coupled climate+development scenarios: food/water stress already reshaping movement toward the U.S. (opening case: Guatemalan farmer after ~5 years of failed rain).
  - U.S. sequel mapped county-level habitability shifts (heat, humid heat, wildfire, sea rise, crop yields) showing large parts of the South/Southwest degrading while Americans keep moving *toward* risk.
- **Finding type(s)**: predictive-harm-mapping. New tag: **model-commissioning** (constructing evidence by commissioning original scientific modeling rather than analyzing an agency's).
- **Evidence & sources**: commissioned migration model by geographer Bryan Jones (Baruch/CUNY), extending his World Bank "Groundswell" model (constructed); Rhodium Group county-level climate impact data (commercial-data); USFS wildfire projections (open-public); census/agronomic datasets (open-public); NCAR Cheyenne supercomputer runs — >10B data points, 4 days of compute; peer feedback from 8 outside scientists (constructed-review).
- **Access tier**: constructed (commissioned model) + commercial-data + open-public inputs.
- **Acquisition path**: mixed — commissioned modeling + commercial-data + bulk-public-data + field reporting.
- **Detection signature**: model-commissioning — where no agency dataset answers the question, build the instrument: extend a peer-lineage model (World Bank Groundswell) with journalistic scenario design, then report the scenario *spread* rather than a single prediction.
- **Corroboration structure**: external peer review panel (8 scientists) pre-publication; scenario bands published with explicit uncertainty; field reporting verifies model-implied dynamics at origin points.
- **Methodology notes**: STATED — "About Our Climate Migration Model" (https://www.propublica.org/article/2020-climate-migration-part-1-methodology; mirrored at [Pulitzer Center](https://pulitzercenter.org/stories/about-our-climate-migration-model)).
- **Generalization**: pension insolvency projections, insurance-retreat maps, sea-level muni-bond risk. Generic detector template: when the finding is about the *future*, commission/extend an existing peer-reviewed model, publish methods + uncertainty, and let field cases validate leading edges. High effort; reserve for systemic questions.

### Poison in the Air / Sacrifice Zones (2021) — First neighborhood-level national map of industrial cancer-causing air pollution, built from EPA's own dormant model; 1,000+ hotspots the agency never told anyone about
- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/toxmap-poison-in-the-air (map: https://projects.propublica.org/toxmap/ ; series: https://www.propublica.org/series/sacrifice-zones)
- **Partner/awards**: ProPublica original (Younes, Kofman, Shaw, Song; Laredo story co-published with The Texas Tribune). AHCJ Award for Excellence (public health) 2022 ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/awards/ahcj-award-for-excellence-in-health-care-journalism-public-health-2)); UF Investigative Data Journalism Award at 2022 OJAs ([UF](https://www.jou.ufl.edu/?p=466962)); Goldsmith honoree ([Goldsmith](https://goldsmithawards.org/honoree/sacrifice-zones/)).
- **What they found**:
  - 1,000+ hotspots of elevated cancer risk from industrial air toxics; ~256,000 people above EPA's 1-in-10,000 "acceptable" lifetime excess risk ceiling (~43,000 at triple that); ~74M above the 1-in-1M aspirational goal.
  - Predominantly Black census tracts face more than double the toxic industrial air pollution of majority-white tracts (~40% more in communities of color overall); a quarter of the 20 worst hotspots in Texas; Cancer Alley quantified; top corporate footprints: Dow, Huntsman, Eastman, BASF, LyondellBasell.
  - EPA regulates facility-by-facility and never assesses cumulative neighborhood risk — e.g., Baytown, TX resident's combined 1-in-46,000 risk from three co-located plants that each individually pass review; ethylene oxide is the dominant driver of excess industrial cancer risk nationally.
  - Sub-case (Laredo, TX, w/ Texas Tribune, Dec 2021): Midwest Sterilization — the nation's largest-emitting EtO sterilizer — put >37,000 schoolchildren in elevated-risk zones; one elementary school at ~1-in-3,700 estimated lifetime risk; TCEQ equipment read zero emissions for a decade (2007–2017) due to faulty equipment; Texas adopted an EtO standard 24,000x more permissive than EPA's ([story](https://www.propublica.org/article/a-plant-that-sterilizes-medical-equipment-spews-cancer-causing-pollution-on-tens-of-thousands-of-schoolchildren)).
- **Finding type(s)**: concentrated-harm-hotspot; disparate-impact-by-race-or-geography; regulatory-capture (agency holds the model but won't operationalize it); institutional-coverup (state non-warning). New tag: **dormant-public-model-operationalization**.
- **Evidence & sources**: EPA RSEI model output (open-public bulk: 810×810m grid, ~29M cells, 1.4B rows/year; 5-year average 2014–2018 ≈ 7B rows); Toxics Release Inventory self-reported emissions (open-public); facility confirmations — top 200 polluters contacted, 109 responded, 71% confirmed / 29% flagged reporting errors that were corrected (constructed validation); census demographics (open-public); resident/scientist interviews (constructed-fieldwork); school-location joins for Laredo (open-public).
- **Access tier**: open-public (agency model + self-reported emissions) with heavy constructed processing and validation.
- **Acquisition path**: bulk-public-data + interviews + field-observation.
- **Detection signature**: dormant-public-model-operationalization — EPA's own RSEI dispersion model, run at national scale and averaged over 5 years, summed *across facilities per grid cell* (the cumulative step the agency never performs), then joined to census race/income and school locations, revealed the hotspots and the disparate impact. The data always existed; the analytic move was cumulation + operationalization.
- **Corroboration structure**: (1) model output validated facility-by-facility with the emitters themselves; (2) corrected inputs re-run; (3) results cross-checked against known monitored hotspots; (4) full data + methodology released publicly ([data release](https://www.propublica.org/article/were-releasing-the-data-behind-our-toxic-air-analysis)).
- **Methodology notes**: STATED — "Visualizing Toxic Air" nerd post (Younes & Shaw; BigQuery pipeline: week-long jobs → minutes) https://www.propublica.org/nerds/visualizing-toxic-air ; data-release article above; overview at [Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/health/propublica-air-pollution-investigation/).
- **Impact (one line)**: EPA announced stepped-up monitoring/enforcement scrutiny of flagged communities; Clean Air Laredo Coalition formed; fed later EPA EtO/HON rulemakings ([follow-up](https://www.propublica.org/article/epa-finalizes-new-standards-for-cancer-causing-chemicals)).
- **Generalization**: any regulator with an internal risk/screening model it never publishes at grievance-relevant granularity: FRA rail-hazmat routing, FDA adverse events, OSHA site-specific targeting model, HUD inspection scores. Generic detector: inventory agency models (budget docs, contractor papers, FOIA logs mentioning "screening tool"); obtain outputs or reconstruct from inputs; aggregate to population-relevant units; join to demographics; diff against agency enforcement locations — the gap between modeled risk and enforcement is the story.

## STRAND B — LABOR

### Temp Land (2013) — The permatemp economy: temps do the most dangerous jobs, get hurt far more often, get sorted by race code words
- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/the-expendables-how-the-temps-who-power-corporate-giants-are-getting-crushe (raiteros: https://www.propublica.org/article/taken-for-a-ride-temp-agencies-and-raiteros-in-immigrant-chicago ; injuries: https://www.propublica.org/article/temporary-work-lasting-harm)
- **Partner/awards**: "Taken for a Ride" with APM's Marketplace ([APM](https://www.americanpublicmedia.org/blog/propublica-and-american-public-medias-marketplace-launch-exclusive-investigation-taken-for-a-ride-temp-agencies-and-raiteros-in-immigrant-america)). Gold Barlett & Steele Award 2014; Sidney Award ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/atpropublica/propublicas-temp-land-series-wins-top-barlett-steele-award)).
- **What they found**:
  - First multistate injury-rate analysis from workers' comp claims: temps had ~50% higher injury risk than non-temps in CA and FL; in Florida blue-collar workplaces, temps were ~6x as likely to be injured as permanent employees doing similar jobs; temps 2x as likely to suffer heat illness (CA), 3x chemical injuries (MN); "caught-in"/"struck-by" injuries concentrated among temps.
  - "Raiteros" labor-broker system in Chicago: brokers controlling access to work for household-name clients (Ty Inc., Sony, Frito-Lay, Smirnoff via agencies like Select Remedy), with van fees and check-cashing fees pushing effective pay below minimum wage.
  - Race/gender sorting via agency code words documented in federal enforcement data ("Number 2s," "Code 3," "Blue eyes," "Vanilla cupcakes"); Black workers 12.1% of workforce but 25.9% of temps.
- **Finding type(s)**: extraction-from-captive-population; disparate-impact-by-race-or-geography; algorithmic-or-systematic-denial (informal sorting system); **two-tier-labor-arbitrage** (new tag).
- **Evidence & sources**: state workers' comp claims microdata — >3.5M claims, 5 states (CA/FL/MA/OR microdata 2007–12; MN aggregate), from FROI/SROI filings (request-gated); OSHA/EEOC enforcement files; worker pay stubs (constructed-fieldwork); hiring-hall field observation (constructed); lawsuits (litigation-records).
- **Access tier**: mixed — request-gated (state claims databases) + constructed (fieldwork) + litigation-records.
- **Acquisition path**: mixed — state records requests (bulk claims data) + interviews + field-observation + litigation-records.
- **Detection signature**: claims-microdata-denominator — workers' comp claims classified temp-vs-non-temp via employer/agency codes, divided by employment denominators per sector, compared within similar occupations, revealed the injury-rate multiple no agency published. Secondary: fee-stacking arithmetic (van fees + check-cashing vs. hours) proved sub-minimum effective wages.
- **Corroboration structure**: five independent state datasets showing the same direction; individual case files matched to named workers; enforcement records corroborate the sorting; companies given itemized findings to rebut.
- **Methodology notes**: STATED — "How We Calculated Injury Rates for Temp and Non-Temp Workers": https://www.propublica.org/nerds/how-we-calculated-injury-rates-for-temp-and-non-temp-workers
- **Impact (one line)**: California enacted client-employer joint liability (AB 1897, 2014); OSHA launched a temp-worker initiative citing the disparity.
- **Generalization**: any two-tier labor structure — subcontracted janitors, gig workers vs. employees, H-2A/H-2B, prison labor, travel nurses. Generic detector: injury/claims/complaint microdata + an employer-type flag + an employment denominator; compute the rate ratio between the captive tier and the standard tier within matched occupations.

### Insult to Injury: The Demolition of Workers' Comp (2015, with NPR) — 33 states cut or restricted injured-worker benefits since 2003; the same arm is worth $48,840 in Alabama and $439,858 in Illinois
- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/the-demolition-of-workers-compensation (series: https://www.propublica.org/series/workers-compensation)
- **Partner/awards**: NPR (Howard Berkes) co-reporting ([Poynter on the project](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2016/how-npr-and-propublica-exposed-americas-unequal-workers-compensation-system/)).
- **What they found**:
  - Since 2003, 33 states cut workers' comp benefits or tightened eligibility while employer premiums hit multi-decade lows and insurers earned 18% returns (2013); ~$30B/yr in costs shifted to taxpayers per OSHA's echo report.
  - "How Much Is Your Arm Worth?": maximum compensation for identical amputations varies by an order of magnitude — arm: $48,840 (AL) vs. $193,950 (OH) vs. $439,858 (IL); big toe: $6,090 (CA) vs. $90,402 (OR); anchored by paired case study (Jeremy Lewis $45K vs. Josh Potter up to $740K for near-identical arm losses, 75 miles apart).
  - Opt-out laws (TX/OK model) let employers write their own benefit plans with severe limits.
- **Finding type(s)**: policy-erosion-across-jurisdictions (50-state race to the bottom); extraction-from-captive-population; **cost-shift-to-public** (new tag).
- **Evidence & sources**: 50-state + DC + FECA statutory benefit schedules, codified by ProPublica and verified with state officials/attorneys/judges (open-public statutes, constructed codification); insurance premium/profitability data (commercial/open); individual case files and medical records with worker consent; legislative histories (open-public); interviews incl. industry lobbyists (constructed).
- **Access tier**: mixed — open-public (statutes) with constructed codification + commercial-data (insurance rates) + consent-based personal records.
- **Acquisition path**: mixed — bulk-public-data (statute compilation) + interviews + records shared by subjects.
- **Detection signature**: cross-jurisdiction-comparison via **benefit-schedule-codification** — the same standardized injury (total loss of X body part) priced through every state's formula (impairment rating × weeks × wage cap) produced a single comparable table exposing both inter-state inequity (outliers) and intra-state temporal erosion (post-2003 amendment waves).

### The Tiger Mom Tax & Breaking the Black Box pricing experiments (2015–2016) — Princeton Review's ZIP-code pricing hit Asians with the top rate almost twice as often; Amazon's buy box steered buyers to pricier Amazon-linked offers
- **URL**: https://www.propublica.org/article/asians-nearly-twice-as-likely-to-get-higher-price-from-princeton-review ; https://www.propublica.org/article/amazon-says-it-puts-customers-first-but-its-pricing-algorithm-doesnt (series hub: https://www.propublica.org/article/breaking-the-black-box-when-algorithms-decide-what-you-pay)

- **What they found**:
  - Princeton Review (Sept 2015): identical Premier SAT tutoring priced $6,600–$8,400 by ZIP across four tiers; Asians 1.8x as likely to be quoted the higher tiers (8.49% of population in high-price ZIPs vs. 2.89% in low-price; 4.9% nationally) — disparate impact independent of income; seeded by Harvard undergrads' public code (Christian Haigh et al.).
  - Amazon (Sept 2016): across 250 frequently-bought products, ~75% of buy-box placements went to Amazon or Fulfilled-by-Amazon sellers despite cheaper rivals; default rankings omitted shipping costs only for Amazon/FBA offers; buying all 250 via the buy box cost ~$1,400 more (~20% premium; avg. $7.88/item).
- **Finding type(s)**: algorithmic-or-systematic-bias; disparate-impact-by-race-or-geography; platform-complicity-by-design (self-preferencing). New tags: **mystery-shopper-price-probe; self-preferencing-audit**.
- **Evidence & sources**: systematic price quotes harvested per ZIP from the target's own quote engine (constructed-scrape/mystery-shop); U.S. Census demographics per ZIP (open-public); Amazon listings scraped incl. logged-out/non-Prime simulation (constructed-scrape); company responses (constructed-interview).
- **Access tier**: constructed (scrape/mystery-shopper interaction with the subject's live system) + open-public (census).
- **Acquisition path**: platform-experiment + scrape + bulk-public-data.
- **Detection signature**: mystery-shopper-price-probe — query the seller's own pricing engine across the full ZIP space holding the product constant; join quoted tier to census demographics; disparate impact emerges without any access to the algorithm's internals. For Amazon: self-preferencing-audit — rank position vs. true landed cost (price+shipping) across sellers; systematic divergence in favor of the platform's own offers is the finding.
- **Corroboration structure**: repeated collection runs (July + late August) to show stability; tiers and method published; company explanations printed and tested against the data (income didn't explain the Asian disparity).
- **Methodology notes**: STATED in-article (tier mapping, census join, 250-product sample; Harvard students' code reviewed); series context in "When Big Data Becomes Bad Data" (https://www.propublica.org/article/when-big-data-becomes-bad-data).
- **Impact (one line)**: canonical citations in algorithmic-fairness literature; buy-box self-preferencing later central to antitrust scrutiny of Amazon [context, inferred].
- **Generalization**: insurance quotes, test-prep, delivery fees, dynamic pricing anywhere. Generic detector: enumerate a quote surface (ZIP, profile attributes), hold the product constant, collect quotes at scale, join price tier to protected-class geography; flag any tier boundary that tracks demographics better than cost drivers.

### 3. Transferable pattern candidates

**P1 — Dormant Public Model Operationalization.** Regulators frequently possess a risk model or screening dataset (RSEI; OSHA targeting; HUD inspection scores; FDA signal detection) built for internal triage and never operationalized at the granularity where harm is experienced. Obtain the model's outputs (or inputs plus published method), run it at full scale, perform the aggregation step the agency skips (cumulative, cross-facility, multi-year), and join the risk surface to population/demographic/institution layers. The finding is double: the harm map, and proof the agency could have produced it and didn't. Minimum data: agency model or self-reported microdata with geo keys; a population join layer; agency enforcement locations for the gap analysis. Agent detector: inventory the regulator's internal analytics (budget justifications, contractor deliverables, IG reports, FOIA logs mentioning "screening tool"/"risk model"); flag every model whose outputs aren't published at sub-county granularity.

**P2 — Constructed Interaction Audit.** When the system under investigation is a live commercial interface, evidence is *manufactured, not found*: buy the ad with the illegal exclusion; request the quote from many ZIPs; simulate the logged-out shopper; instrument consenting users' browsers. Controlled variation (paired probes differing in one protected attribute; identical product across geographies) converts the platform's responses into a dataset proving its decision rule from outside the black box. Two force multipliers: remediation-audit (re-run the identical probe after the fix is announced) and obstruction-as-confirmation (the subject breaking your instrument is itself evidence). Minimum data: none preexisting — an account, a small budget ($30 sufficed for one probe), a variant design matrix, rigorous logging (screenshots, receipts, timestamps, approval latencies). Agent note: for an autonomous platform this pattern requires explicit human sign-off (ethics/ToS/legal exposure) — emit as human_action, never auto-execute.

**P3 — 50-State Codification / Race-to-the-Bottom Table.** Take one standardized case (a lost arm; an unemployment claim; a wage-theft complaint) and price/process it through every jurisdiction's statutory formula, verifying each computation with local practitioners. The single comparable table exposes spatial outliers and, re-run over legislative history, temporal erosion waves that often trace to model-bill campaigns. Minimum data: statute/benefit-schedule texts (open); amendment histories with dates; optionally bill-text similarity for coordinated drafting. Agent detector: whenever a protection is state-administered with no federal floor, build the standardized-case table, sort, then cluster amendment dates/sponsors — synchronized erosion is a lobbying signature worth its own trace.

**P4 — Captive-Population Rate Ratio.** A subordinate class (temps, subcontractors, prisoners, gig workers, guest workers) shares a harm-reporting system with the standard class but no one publishes the comparison. Obtain claims/incident microdata (workers' comp FROI/SROI, OSHA severe-injury, 911 CAD), classify records into tiers via employer/agency/brand fields, construct exposure denominators (employment counts, store counts, hours), compute within-occupation or per-venue rate ratios. Temp Land's 6x Florida multiple and the dollar stores' per-store violence footprint are the same arithmetic. Minimum data: incident microdata with a tier-identifying field; a denominator source; occupation/venue matching to control composition. Agent detector: scan held datasets for incident tables containing an employer/intermediary field; where a two-tier or franchise structure exists, compute the ratio — an unpublished multiple >1.5x within matched work is story-grade.

**P5 — Algorithmic Middleman Cartel Detection.** A SaaS vendor sells "revenue management"/"benchmarking" to horizontal competitors, ingesting nonpublic transaction data and returning coordinated prices — hub-and-spoke collusion as a subscription. Detection: (1) enumerate the vendor's clients from job postings, testimonials, user-group rosters, conference sponsorships, earnings calls; (2) bound a micro-market and measure what share of supply the vendor's clients control (RealPage: 70% of one ZIP); (3) capture the vendor's own marketing claims of above-market outperformance and data pooling (intent, in their words); (4) diff price trajectories of users vs. non-users.
