# Access-Substitution Analysis: ICIJ and OCCRP

**Checked:** 2026-07-29  
**Scope:** the 33 ICIJ canon entries, 30 OCCRP canon entries, and methodology-only detection moves not already represented by a canon entry. No investigation-database writes were made.

## VERDICT

ICIJ is the higher-yield of the two, but neither should be the next full profiling target.

| Outlet | Extracted signatures | (a) Open-record-runnable | (b) Re-anchoring: public verification half | (c) Leak-dependent | (d) Closed-platform-dependent | Independently usable now, (a)+(b) | End-to-end public, (a) only |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ICIJ | 36 | 18 | 7 | 11 | 0 | **25/36 = 69.4%** | **18/36 = 50.0%** |
| OCCRP | 31 | 7 | 13 | 10 | 1 | **20/31 = 64.5%** | **7/31 = 22.6%** |

The `(a)+(b)` number is the fair answer to “can Ithildin reuse anything?” The `(a)` number is the fair answer to “can Ithildin reproduce the detector from public inputs?” A public verification half can authenticate, falsify, or enrich a lead; it cannot regenerate the population of leads that the inaccessible leak supplied.

**Recommendation:** profile **The Markup next**, not another leak-centered outlet. This is an editorial-priority inference, not a completed comparative profile: an outlet organized around published data, code, measurement, and open-record acquisition should yield more end-to-end cards per unit of extraction effort than either ICIJ or OCCRP. ICIJ remains worth a bounded follow-up because its public Offshore Leaks graph and selected releases support 18 complete moves. OCCRP is lower-yield than expected as a detector source: its strongest methods are ledger reconstruction and corpus-scale cross-reference, but the decisive ledgers and maintained corpus are usually unavailable. The public parts of OCCRP are still valuable as verification recipes and as a source-discovery catalogue.

### Counting and classification rule

- One count is assigned to each canon entry's expressly named **Detection signature**. A methodology move is added only if it emits a candidate, match, anomaly, or relationship and is not already represented by a canon signature. This adds three ICIJ classifiers and one OCCRP Aleph-wide cross-reference, yielding 36 and 31 signatures respectively.
- `(a)` means the core signal can be generated from data the platform can lawfully obtain from open, bulk, paid-public, or ordinary request-gated records. A privileged source may improve recall, but is not logically required.
- `(b)` means the inaccessible corpus supplies the seed or candidate population, while a named public-record half can be run independently once a candidate is known. The table states that half exactly.
- `(c)` means the signature's decisive relation, transaction, communication, or snapshot is absent without non-public source material.
- `(d)` means the move depends on Aleph Pro's maintained/member-tier corpus or equivalent closed infrastructure, rather than merely on the open-source Aleph/FtM software.
- “Public” is not treated as “complete.” Each runnable card still needs a source/vintage precondition and an explicit coverage statement.

### Detection-card discipline used below

| Code | Preconditions | Coverage statement | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** — identity join | At least one discriminator beyond a normalized name where collisions are plausible | Sources, jurisdictions, vintages, unmatched/ambiguous rows | Sample false matches; preserve rejected pairs |
| **G** — graph/path | Typed, evidence-backed edges and source IDs | Node/edge source coverage and missing relationship classes | Compare alternative paths; do not convert co-occurrence into control |
| **T** — timeline/diff | Defined event semantics, time zone, and window | Missing/coarse dates and snapshot periods | Placebo/time-shift windows; distinguish “before” from “known before” |
| **M** — ledger/mapping | Stable raw-row locator and parser version | Files/rows rejected, currencies, deduplication, and sampling frame | Reconcile totals to source; retain raw values |
| **C** — cohort/prevalence | Pre-registered cohort, denominator, outcome, and exclusions | Unknown membership and jurisdiction/source gaps | Matched comparator or defensible base rate |
| **S** — substance/rule test | Explicit legal/operational rule and measurement threshold | Registry population, missing accounts/staff/address fields | Legitimate peer group; alternative operational explanations |
| **ML** — classifier | Locked label definition and audited training/validation sample | Precision **and** recall/abstention/unknown share | Held-out test set and human adjudication |

### Beneficial-ownership access after the November 2022 CJEU ruling

The CJEU invalidated indiscriminate general-public access because of the interference with privacy and data-protection rights (checked 2026-07-29: [CJEU press release and judgment summary](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2022-11/cp220188en.pdf)). Directive (EU) 2024/1640 replaces that model with access for authorities, obliged entities, and persons with a legitimate interest; journalists and civil-society organizations working on AML are presumptively eligible, but national implementation and authentication still control actual access (checked 2026-07-29: [Directive 2024/1640](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/1640/oj)). Therefore “EU BO register” is not a reproducible anonymous-public input.

## OCCRP ID assessment

### Verdict

**Technically scrapeable, not officially machine-readable; high value as a source-catalogue seed, but obtain permission before recurring mirroring.**

Live inspection on 2026-07-29 found:

- The catalogue page is a server-rendered Jekyll-style HTML document containing **1,030 source cards** in a single roughly 1.08 MB response. Each card exposes a source name, outbound URL, description, `data-country`, `data-region`, and one or more `data-type` values. The page is live at [OCCRP ID databases](https://id.occrp.org/databases/) (checked 2026-07-29).
- The cards use three explicit access/quality badges: **Governmental** (772 occurrences), **Paid database** (97), and **Registration required** (152). Types include corporate, land, legal, gazette, government, IP, trade, transport, directory, social media, and combined types. These are useful access signals, but not a complete machine contract: free text also contains fee, subscription, login, request, and coverage warnings.
- Filtering is client-side over embedded HTML; no application JavaScript data API is required. Probes of `/databases.json`, `/api`, `/api/databases`, and `/databases.csv` returned the homepage HTML rather than JSON/CSV. `/robots.txt` and `/sitemap.xml` also returned homepage HTML, so no machine-readable crawl policy or sitemap was exposed at those paths (checked 2026-07-29: [catalogue](https://id.occrp.org/databases/), [homepage](https://id.occrp.org/)).
- OCCRP describes the catalogue as public access to more than 1,000 sources in more than 180 countries, while the staffed research-request service is for member centers, partners, and collaborators (checked 2026-07-29: [OCCRP ID homepage](https://id.occrp.org/)). The [ID terms](https://id.occrp.org/terms-of-use/) reserve rights over the site/information, discourage copying/re-selling, allow blocking use that stresses the service, and invite contact for uses beyond the stated terms (checked 2026-07-29).

### Concrete build assessment

1. **Acquisition:** one conditional GET of `/databases/`, no browser automation. Record ETag/Last-Modified when supplied, response hash, retrieval time, and the terms-page hash.
2. **Parser:** extract one row per `.country` card: `name`, `url`, `description`, `country`, `region`, normalized types, `governmental`, `paid`, `registration_required`, and the source-card ordinal. Do not infer “free” merely from the absence of a badge.
3. **Validation:** require approximately 1,000 cards, a valid country/type vocabulary, outbound HTTP(S) URLs, and sampled HTML-to-row fidelity. Diff removals, URL changes, description changes, and badge changes.
4. **Enrichment:** perform low-rate link health checks separately; classify access friction from both badges and description text; map sources to Ithildin modules and active investigation jurisdictions; never treat OCCRP's description as evidence from the linked registry.
5. **Gap ranking:** score an ID source higher when it is governmental, in an active/high-priority jurisdiction, fills a missing source class, and is not already covered. Lower it for paywall/login/manual-only access, stale/dead links, duplicative aggregators, or terms that prohibit automated use.
6. **Governance:** because the public page has no API/license for catalogue reuse and the terms restrict copying, ask OCCRP for permission before scheduling or redistributing a mirror. Until then, use a human-triggered snapshot for internal adapter triage, store minimal metadata and the originating URL, rate-limit to one page fetch, and do not republish descriptions.

**Build size [inferred]:** small parser and validator; medium editorial/permission task; ongoing link-maintenance cost. The catalogue can seed `adapter-gaps.md` and country coverage audits, but it cannot supply the underlying records, paid credentials, local-language judgment, or OCCRP's research desk.

## Bottom line

ICIJ's public release policy preserves a useful typed offshore graph and a handful of bounded datasets, making half of its extracted signatures fully runnable and another fifth reusable as public verification. OCCRP publishes one unusually useful payment ledger and excellent methods, but its signature portfolio is structurally more dependent on confidential ledgers, property snapshots, hacked/insider corpora, and Aleph Pro. Public substitutes usually preserve **identity, legal status, sanctions, filings, and endpoint assets**; they usually lose **transactions, communications, control instructions, exact timing, complete populations, and cross-jurisdiction freshness**. Those losses are not implementation gaps that another aggregator can erase.
