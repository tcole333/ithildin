# The Markup Story Index — Per-Story Evidence Base

The coded corpus behind the Markup pattern library's frequency and dependency claims. Distilled from the full extraction reports in `_intake/markup/` (reports 11–19), which carry complete entries with per-claim citations; this index compresses each coded story to its evidence skeleton. Report-10 (the census) is excluded because it is the sampling frame and contains no story entries.

**Entry fields** — URL; Partner/awards; Found (core finding); Types (the source report's exact finding-type tags); Evidence (typed sources with acquisition mode); Systems (specific named record systems used, for adapter-gap analysis); Signature (coined detection move and mechanics); Method (cited methodology and repository URLs vs. [inferred]); Impact (official consequences); Dependency (inline input class, present-day class-(a) status, and a short reason).

### Can You Get a Coronavirus Test? It May Depend on Where You Live (2020) — identical patient scenarios qualified for state-lab testing in some jurisdictions and not others
- URL: https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/16/can-you-get-a-coronavirus-test
- Partner/awards: The Markup original; no reporting partner or story-specific award located.
- Found: The Markup sent the same records request to all 50 states, New York City, and Washington, D.C. In the first published snapshot it had responses from 16 states, public guidance from four more jurisdictions, and denials or emergency postponements from four states and D.C. A symptomatic health-care worker with fever but no lower-respiratory symptoms would not have met Wisconsin's state-lab priority rule, while Massachusetts put health-care workers with fever or even mild respiratory symptoms in its highest priority category.
- Types: rule-fragmentation; snapshot-policy inequality

- Systems: DocumentCloud; state health-department COVID-19 testing-guidance records
- Signature: matched-hypothetical rule diff: state testing memos compared to five identical patient scenarios on keys symptoms, occupation, age, exposure, and congregate setting revealed geography-dependent eligibility.
- Method: https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2020/04/16/how-we-analyzed-states-coronavirus-testing-plans; https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-covid19-testing-guidelines
- Impact: No story-attributable official change located. The reporting did document that some governments had suspended or indefinitely postponed public-record access during the emergency.
- Dependency: (a) open-record-runnable; degraded today — historical artifacts; fresh acquisition degraded.

### Blind Users Struggle with State Coronavirus Websites (2020) — most state emergency sites contained machine-detectable barriers, while manual screen-reader use exposed additional failures
- URL: https://themarkup.org/coronavirus/2020/04/21/blind-users-struggle-with-state-coronavirus-websites
- Partner/awards: The Markup original. WebAIM conducted the WAVE survey for The Markup and supplied accessibility expertise; it was a testing collaborator, not a co-publisher.
- Found: Of 50 state COVID-19 homepages, 41 contained low-contrast text, 31 contained empty links or buttons, 15 lacked input labels, and 12 lacked alternative text. - Systems: WAVE accessibility scanner; NVDA screen reader; state COVID-19 websites
- Signature: automated-standard audit with human override: a 50-state public-site census tested against WAVE/WCAG error rules and compared to manual screen-reader behavior on key page controls and content objects revealed both widespread machine-detectable faults and high-impact false negatives.
- Method: [inferred]
- Impact: Iowa updated one COVID site after The Markup's email and cut its WAVE-detectable errors in half; Washington said it would update its site that week.

### Dollars to Megabits, You May Be Paying 400 Times As Much As Your Neighbor for Internet Service (2022) — four ISPs offered poorer and less-White neighborhoods slower service at the same base price
- URL: https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2022/10/19/dollars-to-megabits-you-may-be-paying-400-times-as-much-as-your-neighbor-for-internet-service
- Partner/awards: The Markup original, widely republished through its Associated Press partnership; local outlets used the data, but AP was distribution rather than joint reporting. Sigma impact note. Still Loading won a 2023 Sigma Award and first place in the 2024 Philip Meyer Journalism Award.
- Found: The socioeconomic analysis covered 848,618 address-level offers across 38 cities from AT&T, Verizon, EarthLink, and CenturyLink. The worst-deal neighborhoods were lower-income in 92% of cities and least-White in two-thirds of cities with sufficient variation. In every one of 22 analyzable cities with digitized historical maps, formerly redlined areas disproportionately received the worst deals.
- Types: geographic price-quality discrimination; legacy-infrastructure penalty; historical-disinvestment persistence
- Evidence: Constructed address-level offer collection — more than 12 million OpenAddresses/NYC Open Data addresses were geocoded; a random 10% sample within each Census block group was queried through ISP availability-tool endpoints; Instrumented public-product tests — four custom collectors made 1,606,964 delayed queries through residential IPs; 455,534 unserved results left 1,151,430 offers, from which the 848,618-offer socioeconomic subset was built; Public socioeconomic and historical geodata — 2019 five-year ACS income/race data, Census geography, FCC Form 477, and University of Richmond Mapping Inequality HOLC polygons; Company and resident corroboration — household cases confirmed practical consequences; providers acknowledged equal base prices across different speeds while disputing discrimination or the analysis; Released analysis package and reporting recipe — address-level offers, processed inputs/outputs, notebooks, localized tables/maps, methodology, and a step-by-step Story Recipe
- Systems: ISP availability-tool endpoints; OpenAddresses; NYC Open Data address records; ACS 2019 five-year estimates; FCC Form 477; Mapping Inequality HOLC polygons
- Signature: address-offer-demography spatial join: provider price/speed offers joined to ACS demographics and HOLC polygons on key address-to-block-group coordinates, then compared within provider-city pairs revealed equal-price/unequal-quality burdens concentrated in poorer, less-White, formerly redlined areas.
- Method: https://themarkup.org/show-your-work/2022/10/19/how-we-uncovered-disparities-in-internet-deals; https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-isp; https://themarkup.org/story-recipes/2022/10/19/journalists-investigate-which-neighborhoods-in-your-city-are-offered-the-worst-internet-deals
- Impact: Los Angeles later became the first U.S. city to ban digital discrimination locally; the council record and advocacy letter cited disparity studies including The Markup, and the city's complaint process requires demographic trend reporting. Impact story. The Markup also briefed local, state, and federal lawmakers and regulators, and at least seven local newsrooms used the data to localize findings. Sigma note.

### Introducing the United States Place Sampler (USPS) (2023) — the reporting team turned representative address selection into reusable public infrastructure
- URL: https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps
- Partner/awards: Built with Big Local News at Stanford; Joe Nudell is credited for Big Local News. Judges for the first-place 2024 Philip Meyer Journalism Award specifically praised the public bespoke mapping tool and partnership.
- Found: The team identified address sampling—not later statistical analysis—as a hidden bottleneck: public address files were scarce or incomplete, and selecting points by clicking Google Maps would create a biased sample. The beta indexed more than 200 million OpenAddresses records and could return a fixed number or percentage of random addresses by place, county, tract, or block group, with FIPS codes down to block-group level.
- Types: measurement-frame repair; method-as-public-infrastructure
- Evidence: Open address corpus — OpenAddresses records ingested nationwide during February 19–23, 2023; Official geographic boundaries and identifiers — 2022 Census TIGER shapefiles, TIGER Geocoder, and FIPS geography; Constructed sampling service — PostGIS spatial indexes align address coordinates to block groups, places, counties, and other designations before random selection; Open-source implementation — database, ingestion, application, and deployment materials in the Big Local News USPS repository
- Systems: OpenAddresses; Census TIGER/Line; TIGER Geocoder; FIPS geography; PostGIS
- Signature: representative-frame construction: OpenAddresses points spatially joined to Census TIGER boundaries on key coordinates and geographic IDs, then randomly sampled within declared places revealed a reproducible, less-biased address frame for downstream disparity measurements.
- Method: [inferred]; https://github.com/biglocalnews/usps; https://themarkup.org/still-loading/2023/03/02/introducing-the-united-states-place-sampler-usps
- Impact: The tool remains publicly usable and made address-frame construction available beyond The Markup's original 38-city corpus. Live USPS.
- Dependency: (a) open-record-runnable; runnable today — public inputs and live acquisition path.

### Suckers List: How Allstate's Secret Auto Insurance Algorithm Squeezes Big Spenders (2020) — Customer-level rate records exposed a retention algorithm that withheld deserved discounts and pushed the largest increases onto existing big spenders
- URL: https://themarkup.org/allstates-algorithm/2020/02/25/car-insurance-suckers-list
- Partner/awards: Joint investigation with Consumer Reports; its statisticians Andrew Cohen and Dina Haner are credited for data/statistical analysis in the story and methodology. No project-specific award was verified on The Markup's Awards page.
- Found: Allstate's withdrawn Maryland filing contained individual current, “indicated” risk-based, and proposed transition prices for 92,792 policies. After excluding 70 records without a Census match, the analysis covered 92,722 policies. Policyholders already paying roughly $1,900 or more per six months could receive increases up to 20 percent, while lower-paying customers with equally large indicated risk increases were generally capped at 5 percent. The large-increase group contained 1,831 policies, just 1.97 percent of the cohort (story; methodology).
- Types: personalized-price-extraction; proxy-concentrated-burden
- Evidence: Regulatory rate filing / customer microdata — withdrawn Maryland SERFF filing ALSE-129270805, including a 1,101-page customer table and Allstate's data dictionary; identified after reviewing hundreds of filings and then retrieved from Maryland's public SERFF database; Government demographic data — 2011–2015 ACS zip-code-tabulation-area race and income tables joined to Allstate territory/zip fields; Regulatory correspondence — Maryland's disapproval letter and Allstate letters to multiple states explaining Complementary Group Rating and retention models, linked in the methodology; Company and industry records — Allstate SEC/investor statements, NAIC price-optimization materials, and regulator interviews used to establish the cost-based-rating norm and cross-state deployment
- Systems: SERFF Filing Access; Maryland SERFF filing ALSE-129270805; ACS 2011–2015 ZCTA tables; SEC filings; NAIC price-optimization materials
- Signature: document-to-outcome reconstruction / risk-price residual segmentation: Allstate's current premium was compared with its own indicated risk premium and proposed selected premium on policy ID; residual plots exposed three discontinuous transition groups, and current price joined to the group label revealed that high existing spend—not merely the indicated risk change—predicted the 9.5–20 percent increase group.
- Method: https://github.com/the-markup/investigation-allstates-algorithm
- Impact: A Texas class action cited the investigation, and Michigan regulators later asked Allstate the same equal-risk/equal-price questions (Texas follow-up; Michigan follow-up).
