## Definition

Oversight Architecture Demolition is the systematic removal, defunding, or neutralization of conflict-of-interest and accountability mechanisms that would otherwise constrain government officials' ability to benefit their former employers, current investments, or future career prospects. Unlike Compliance Theater -- where oversight mechanisms exist in form but are configured to approve -- Demolition removes the mechanisms entirely, so that no approval is needed and no record is generated.

The distinction is operationally significant. Compliance Theater creates a paper trail (committee minutes, compliance clearances, risk assessments) that can later be examined by investigators or regulators. Demolition eliminates the paper trail at the source: if there is no ethics EO, there are no pledge violations to investigate. If OGE staffing is cut, there are fewer reviewers to flag conflicts. If IG positions remain vacant, there are fewer auditors to examine awards. The capture is not in the oversight process but in the *absence* of the oversight process.

In this investigation: The evidence documents a multi-front demolition:

**Ethics executive order**: Trump rescinded Biden ethics EO 13989 on Inauguration Day (Jan 20, 2025) with no replacement -- the first time since 1989 that no administration ethics pledge exists (F4686). This is not a weakening of the pledge or a narrowing of its scope; it is a removal. The effect is that appointees like Emil Michael are subject only to the bare-minimum statutory requirements of 18 USC 208, with no additional administration-specific restrictions on revolving-door lobbying, gift acceptance, or issue-specific recusal.

**Ethics waivers as substitute**: Where specific conflicts are too visible to ignore, OGE approved what a Washington University ethics expert described as "sham ethics waivers" for David Sacks, who retained 449 AI-connected investments while serving as AI/Crypto Czar (F4579). Senator Warren demanded Michael recuse from matters involving former clients and divest holdings, but his SASC APQ confirms only bare-minimum commitments: comply with 18 USC 208 with no specific divestiture list, no company-specific recusal, no Coatue-specific disclosure (F4739). The ethics agreement is a formality that constrains nothing.

**Inspector General vacancies**: IG positions across national security agencies remain unfilled, reducing the probability that contract awards, policy decisions, or financial flows will be audited.

**DOGE access without oversight**: DOGE personnel accessed financial systems and contract databases with advisory input and a 2-day review window over all DoD IT contracts above $10M and advisory contracts above $1M (F4696). Hegseth memos gave Feinberg approval authority over these contracts. The access is to systems that contain information necessary to direct awards; the oversight of that access is minimal.

**Structural consequence**: Each demolished oversight mechanism was designed to catch a specific type of conflict. The ethics EO catches revolving-door conflicts. OGE staffing catches disclosure deficiencies. IGs catch procurement irregularities. Ethics waivers, when substantive, force recusals. When all are simultaneously weakened, the system has no mechanism to detect or prevent the Acquisition Node Saturation and Convergent Policy Channeling patterns that the other frameworks in this family describe. The demolition is not an end in itself -- it is the enabling condition for the other patterns.

## Mechanism

1. **Formal removal** -- Oversight mechanisms that exist as executive orders, agency regulations, or administrative requirements are rescinded by the incoming administration. Because these are executive instruments (not statutory), they can be removed unilaterally. The removal is public but attracts limited attention because it is framed as "streamlining" or "reducing bureaucracy."

2. **Staffing reduction** -- Oversight bodies that cannot be formally eliminated (OGE, IGs, GAO) are weakened through hiring freezes, staffing cuts, or failure to nominate/confirm leadership. The body continues to exist on paper but lacks the personnel to function. Congressional oversight committees may not notice the capacity reduction until an audit backlog becomes visible months later.

3. **Waiver substitution** -- Where conflicts are too visible to ignore, formal ethics waivers are issued. But the waivers are drafted to be maximally permissive ("comply with 18 USC 208" without specifying what that means in practice) rather than constraining (specific divestiture lists, company-by-company recusal requirements). The waiver creates the appearance of ethics review without the substance.

4. **Access without clearance** -- Personnel affiliated with oversight-demolition entities (DOGE) are given access to financial, contract, and personnel systems that contain information relevant to acquisition decisions. The access is characterized as "advisory" rather than "decisional," which places it outside normal conflict-of-interest review.

5. **Cascade effect** -- Each demolished mechanism was designed to catch a different type of conflict. When all are weakened simultaneously, the system has no redundancy. A revolving-door conflict that the ethics EO would catch, that OGE review would flag, that an IG audit would discover, and that a substantive recusal would prevent -- now passes through all four weakened gates undetected.

## Detection Markers

- Executive order on ethics rescinded with no replacement (compare to historical practice: every administration since 1989 has had an ethics EO)
- Ethics waivers that impose no specific behavioral constraints: no named companies for recusal, no divestiture timeline, no asset-specific restrictions
- OGE or agency ethics office staffing levels declining relative to historical baselines
- IG positions vacant for 6+ months in agencies where major new spending programs are being created
- DOGE or equivalent oversight bodies whose personnel have access to procurement-relevant systems without standard ethics review or security clearance
- Congressional requests for ethics information (278e filings, waiver text, recusal agreements) that receive delayed or incomplete responses
- Post-government employment patterns: appointees returning to the same companies/funds they came from within months of departure, with no cooling-off period (because the EO that would have imposed one was rescinded)

## Why Existing Models Miss This

- **Compliance Theater** requires the existence of a compliance process -- one that goes through the motions of approval. Demolition removes the process entirely. There is no theater because there is no stage.
- **Defensible Ignorance Architecture** describes how oversight bodies record risk but never reach the conclusion of risk. Demolition is more radical: the oversight body is defunded, unfilled, or deauthorized, so it never records the risk in the first place.
- **Institutional Capture Lifecycle** describes the colonization of an oversight body by the industry it oversees. Demolition does not colonize the oversight body -- it removes it. The two patterns can operate in sequence (capture the DOJ through K&E alumni placements *and* demolish the ethics EO that would constrain those alumni), but they are analytically distinct.
- The existing frameworks in this investigation were developed in the context of the Epstein network, where oversight mechanisms existed but failed (Deutsche Bank compliance, Apollo Conflicts Committee). The tech-right investigation reveals a different failure mode: oversight mechanisms that are removed before they can fail.

## Transferability

The pattern appears whenever an incoming government systematically weakens the oversight infrastructure that constrains officials' personal financial interests. Historical precedents include: Harding-era removal of civil service protections before Teapot Dome; Reagan-era weakening of IG offices before defense procurement scandals; Bush-era undermining of CFPB before financial crisis. The tech-right variant is distinctive in its comprehensiveness (ethics EO + OGE staffing + IG vacancies + DOGE access + waiver quality, all simultaneously) and in its interaction with the Private Market Opacity Shield (the oversight that is demolished is the same oversight that would have needed to be enhanced to detect private market conflicts).

## Limitations

- Not all oversight reduction is demolition. Administrations legitimately streamline regulations, reduce redundant reporting, and eliminate ineffective programs. The framework applies when the *specific* oversight mechanisms removed are those that constrain the *specific* conflicts of interest held by the administration's appointees.
- The framework risks partisan application. Every incoming administration adjusts oversight mechanisms. The diagnostic question is not "did they change oversight?" (they always do) but "did the changes specifically remove the mechanisms that would have detected the appointees' own conflicts?"
- Statutory ethics requirements (18 USC 208, STOCK Act, Hatch Act) remain in place regardless of EO rescission. The demolished mechanisms are the *additional* administrative protections. The framework should not imply that no ethics rules apply -- it should identify the specific gap created by the removal of the administrative layer above the statutory floor.
- The framework requires counterfactual reasoning ("this conflict would have been caught if the EO existed"), which is inherently speculative. Strengthen arguments by identifying specific appointees whose specific conflicts would have triggered specific provisions of the rescinded EO.
