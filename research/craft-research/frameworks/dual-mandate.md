## Definition

Dual Mandate describes individuals or entities simultaneously occupying positions in private capital and public authority such that the distinction between private interest and public function dissolves. This is not a conflict of interest in the traditional sense -- a temporary collision between personal gain and public duty that can be resolved through recusal or divestiture. It is a structural feature: the dual mandate is the mechanism by which private capital and state power become operationally indistinguishable.

Trae Stephens as both Founders Fund managing partner and Anduril Executive Chairman. Clark Minor as HHS CIO overseeing Palantir contracts while coming from Palantir. Kenneth Howery as Ambassador to Denmark (controlling Greenland infrastructure decisions) while retaining Founders Fund carried interest, with Anduril excluded from his recusal obligations. In each case, the individual's public authority and private interest are not merely overlapping -- they are structurally inseparable. The public role creates decisions that affect the private portfolio; the private portfolio creates interests that shape the public role.

This is distinct from Bridge Tax (Tier 1), which describes power from occupying structural holes between networks and extracting rent from the crossing. Dual Mandate is not about taxing the boundary between public and private -- it's about *dissolving* the boundary. The individual doesn't stand at the crossing point; they *are* both sides simultaneously.

It is also distinct from the Enabler Gradient (Tier 1), which describes varying levels of awareness and complicity. Dual Mandate individuals are not unwitting participants at one end of a gradient -- they are structural nodes where private and public power converge by design.

## Mechanism

1. **Position acquisition** -- The individual acquires both a private capital position (VC partner, fund manager, board member, equity holder) and a public authority position (ambassador, agency head, procurement official, advisory role). The positions may be acquired sequentially (leave fund for government, but retain carried interest) or simultaneously (advisory role while maintaining fund position).
2. **Scope overlap** -- The public authority position involves decisions that affect the individual's private interests: procurement authority over portfolio companies, regulatory authority over industry sectors where the fund invests, diplomatic authority over regions where portfolio companies operate, data access relevant to investment decisions.
3. **Recusal inadequacy** -- Ethics frameworks attempt to manage the overlap through recusal: the individual recuses from specific decisions involving specific entities. But the dual mandate's power lies precisely in what recusal *doesn't* cover: industry-wide policy, procurement framework decisions, regulatory philosophy, personnel selection, information access. Recusal addresses the narrow conflict; the structural position generates influence beyond what recusal can reach.
4. **Information flow** -- Even with formal recusal, the dual mandate creates information advantages. An ambassador understands geopolitical dynamics that inform investment strategy. A CIO understands government technology needs that inform which startups to fund. An advisory role provides access to non-public government information. The information doesn't need to be actively misused -- the dual position itself creates an information architecture that private capital can exploit.
5. **Structural reproduction** -- Dual mandate individuals use their public authority to create conditions favorable to more dual mandates: relax ethics requirements (Sovereign Exception), expand OTA procurement (reducing transparency), appoint other dual-mandate individuals to related positions (Personnel Pipeline). The pattern reproduces itself.

## Detection Markers

- **Simultaneous positions**: Individual holding active private investment position (GP, LP, board seat, carried interest, equity stake) while exercising public authority. "Active" includes carried interest and unvested equity, not just current operational roles.
- **Scope mapping**: What decisions does the public role control? What entities does the private position have interest in? Map the overlap. The larger the overlap, the more powerful the dual mandate.
- **Recusal gap analysis**: What does the individual recuse from? What does the recusal *not* cover? The gap between specific recusal and general authority is where the dual mandate operates.
- **Portfolio impact**: Track stock price, contract awards, and regulatory outcomes for the individual's portfolio companies during their period of public authority. Correlation is not causation, but systematic favorable outcomes warrant investigation.
- **Ethics waivers and modifications**: Were ethics requirements waived, modified, or narrowly scoped to accommodate the dual mandate? This connects to Oversight Architecture Demolition.
- **Network effects**: Does the dual-mandate individual appoint, hire, or recommend other people from the same private ecosystem? This connects to Personnel Pipeline.

## Why Existing Models Miss This

- **Bridge Tax** describes power from connecting disconnected networks. Dual Mandate describes the collapse of the distinction between networks -- not bridging public and private but being both simultaneously.
- **Private Order** describes access-controlled networks within open societies. Dual Mandate is one mechanism by which the private order extends into state institutions, but Private Order doesn't describe the specific structural position of individuals who are both.
- **Enabler Gradient** places actors on a spectrum of awareness/complicity. Dual Mandate actors are not on a gradient -- they are structural nodes operating with full awareness of both sides of their position.
- **Infrastructure Lock-in** describes vendor dependency. Dual Mandate describes *individual* dual positioning, which may or may not involve the vendor relationships that create lock-in.

## Transferability

The pattern appears wherever the revolving door compresses to zero delay: individuals who hold government and private positions simultaneously or with minimal gap. Historically: Andrew Mellon (Treasury Secretary and major industrialist), Robert McNamara (Ford CEO → Defense Secretary retaining networks), Dick Cheney (Halliburton CEO → Vice President with blind trust but continued deferred compensation). The contemporary version is more extreme: the positions are held simultaneously, the financial interests are disclosed but not divested, and the recusal frameworks are being actively narrowed.

## Limitations

- Not every government official with prior private sector experience has a dual mandate. The model applies when the individual maintains *active* financial interests (carried interest, unvested equity, ongoing advisory relationships) in entities affected by their public authority. Past employment alone is not dual mandate.
- The model can imply corruption where legal structures may be functioning as intended. Some dual mandates are legally permissible under current ethics frameworks. The analytical question is not "is this illegal?" but "does this structural position create a channel between private capital and public authority that ethics frameworks fail to constrain?"
- Recusal can genuinely work in narrow cases. The model is most powerful when applied to the gap between what recusal addresses and what the structural position enables -- not when used to dismiss all recusal as inadequate.
- The model risks being applied to any wealthy person in government. Constrain to cases where: (a) active financial interests exist, (b) the public role has authority over relevant domains, and (c) the recusal framework doesn't cover the structural advantage.
