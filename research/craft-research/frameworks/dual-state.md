## Definition

Ernst Fraenkel's Dual State framework, developed from his analysis of Nazi Germany, describes how a regime can maintain two parallel legal systems simultaneously:

1. **The Normative State** — where regular law applies, especially for commercial activity: contract law, property rights, corporate governance, intellectual property. Markets need legal predictability to function. The normative state provides this, and its existence reassures business interests, foreign investors, and institutional actors that the system is fundamentally lawful.

2. **The Prerogative State** — where the regime acts without legal constraint, especially against political enemies: immigration enforcement, protest suppression, media intimidation, selective prosecution, agency gutting. In the prerogative state, the deciding factor is not law but the regime's political will.

The analytical power of Fraenkel's framework is that it explains how a system can appear simultaneously lawful and lawless — because it is both, applied to different populations and domains. The administration follows normal legal process when it's convenient (business contracts, property law, corporate transactions) and exercises raw prerogative when it wants something (immigration raids, agency purges, political prosecution).

This is directly applicable as a classification scheme for the investigation's findings. Each government action can be tagged: is this normative state (normal legal process operating as expected) or prerogative state (discretionary power exercised without legal constraint)? The pattern of which domains get which treatment reveals the political structure.

## Mechanism

1. **Domain sorting** — Certain state functions operate under normative rules (commercial law, property rights, financial regulation for allies) while others operate under prerogative discretion (immigration, political dissent, unfavored populations, agency independence). The sorting is not random — it reflects the coalition's interests.
2. **Legal performance** — The normative state is visibly legal: courts function, contracts are enforced, business proceeds predictably. This performance of legality legitimates the overall system and provides cover for the prerogative state.
3. **Prerogative exercise** — In designated domains, the state acts through discretion rather than law: executive orders, emergency declarations, selective enforcement, administrative action without process. The legal basis is asserted but functionally unreviewable.
4. **Boundary management** — The regime manages the boundary between normative and prerogative domains. When commercial interests are threatened by prerogative action (tariffs disrupting supply chains, immigration enforcement disrupting labor markets), the regime adjusts — not because of legal constraint but because the normative state serves the coalition's economic interests.
5. **Escalation ratchet** — Over time, the prerogative state expands and the normative state contracts. Domains that were previously under normal legal process are moved to prerogative discretion. Each expansion is justified by reference to Sovereign Exception (emergency) or Depoliticization (this is merely technical).

## Detection Markers

- **Domain classification**: For each government action in the investigation, ask: is this operating under normal legal process with meaningful judicial review, or under executive discretion with nominal legal cover?
- **Differential treatment**: Same legal framework applied differently to allies versus opponents. Business contracts enforced for coalition members; regulatory enforcement weaponized against opponents.
- **Selective enforcement**: Laws enforced against certain populations or entities while identical violations by allied populations are ignored.
- **Legal aesthetics without legal constraint**: The action has the *form* of legal process (executive order citing statutory authority, administrative procedure with comment period) but the *substance* of discretionary prerogative (outcome predetermined, process pro forma).
- **Commercial carve-outs**: When prerogative action threatens business interests, exceptions are created — indicating that the normative state serves a specific economic function rather than a universal legal principle.

## Analytical Application

For each finding or government action in the investigation, classify:

| Indicator | Normative State | Prerogative State |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------|
| Legal basis | Statute + judicial review | Executive discretion + nominal authority |
| Applies to | All similarly situated | Selectively to targets/populations |
| Process | Meaningful procedure | Pro forma or bypassed |
| Reversibility | Normal legal channels | Temporal arbitrage — acts before review |
| Beneficiaries | Coalition economic interests | Coalition political interests |

The ratio of normative to prerogative actions, and the trend over time, is itself an analytical finding.

## Limitations

- The normative/prerogative distinction is not always clean. Many government actions have elements of both. Use as a spectrum, not a binary.
- All governments exercise some discretion. The Dual State framework applies when discretion is *systematic* and *politically sorted* — not when individual actions involve executive judgment.
- Fraenkel developed this framework analyzing an authoritarian regime. Applying it to a democratic system requires care — the claim is not equivalence but structural analogy. The patterns are recognizable even if the degree and context differ.
- The framework can be used to delegitimate all executive action as "prerogative." Apply only when the pattern of selective application is documented across multiple cases, not inferred from individual instances.
