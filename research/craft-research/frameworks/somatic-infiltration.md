## Definition
Somatic infiltration is the practice of placing "human sensors"—assistants, researchers, students, or household staff—directly into a target's private environment under the guise of a professional favor or introduction. Unlike traditional espionage, the "sensor" may be unwitting or partially witting; their primary function is to provide the broker with a permanent, low-level window into the target's private life, schedule, moods, and indiscretions.

## Mechanism
1. **Identification of Gap**: The broker identifies a target's need for specialized or personal staff (e.g., a "science advisor," "house manager," or "personal assistant").
2. **The "Favor" Introduction**: The broker offers to fill the gap with a high-quality candidate they have "vetted" or "discovered."
3. **Placement**: The candidate is hired, often owing their position to the broker, creating an initial loyalty bond.
4. **Passive Collection**: The sensor provides the broker with regular updates on the target's activities, often framed as "checking in" or "seeking advice" on how to handle the target.
5. **Operational Window**: The broker uses this granular intelligence to time their own interventions, requests, or "rescues" (Manufactured Dependency).

## Detection Markers
- Staff members who were introduced to a target by the broker and have no prior professional connection to the target's industry.
- Individuals with "hybrid" roles (e.g., a "student" who acts as a travel coordinator).
- Recurring source locations for sensors (e.g., "graduates" of the same program or employees of the same former associate).
- Evidence of the broker receiving updates on the target's private health, family disputes, or "moods" from the staffer.
- The "Sensor" appearing in travel manifests alongside the target and the broker simultaneously.
