## Definition

Depoliticization is the framing of political action as technical, neutral, or apolitical to remove it from democratic contestation. The mechanism: take an action that redistributes power, resources, or access, and present it as a routine operational or technical decision that has no political valence. This prevents the action from being contested as a political choice, because political choices can be opposed through political means, but "technical" decisions can only be challenged on technical grounds -- and the actor controls the technical framing.

"We're just making government more efficient" (DOGE). "This is a technical procurement decision" (Golden Dome C2 contract). "We're just applying merit-based hiring" (while purging ideological opponents). "This is about national security" (while enriching portfolio companies). Each of these frames a political action -- one that redistributes power, resources, or access along ideological lines -- as though it were merely operational.

This is the inverse of Sovereign Exception. Where Sovereign Exception *politicizes* a situation to justify extraordinary power (declare emergency → claim authority), Depoliticization *de-politicizes* to avoid scrutiny while exercising power. They work as a sequential pair: declare an emergency to seize authority, then frame the specific exercise of that authority as merely technical. The emergency justifies the power grab; the technical framing shields the exercise of power from democratic opposition.

It is distinct from Compliance Theater (Tier 2), which describes institutional processes configured to produce approvals rather than scrutiny. Compliance Theater is about the *mechanics* of institutional oversight failure. Depoliticization is about the *rhetorical and epistemic framing* that prevents the action from being recognized as requiring oversight in the first place. CT operates within institutional process; DP operates on the framing that determines whether institutional process is triggered.

## Mechanism

1. **Technical framing** -- Present the action in operational/technical language that strips its political content. "Workforce optimization" instead of "mass firing of career civil servants." "Procurement modernization" instead of "awarding contracts to allies." "Data integration" instead of "centralizing surveillance capability." The language performs the depoliticization.
2. **Expertise claim** -- Assert that the decision requires technical expertise that political actors (Congress, courts, the public) lack. "You don't understand how government technology works." "This is a complex procurement matter best left to experts." "These are management decisions, not policy decisions." The expertise claim excludes democratic actors from contestation.
3. **Neutrality performance** -- Adopt the aesthetics of neutrality: data-driven decision-making, metrics, efficiency scores, performance reviews. The metrics themselves encode political choices (what counts as "efficient"? by whose measure? optimizing for what?), but the quantitative presentation makes them appear objective.
4. **Contestation foreclosure** -- Because the action is framed as technical, political opposition is delegitimized. Critics who object on political grounds are told they're "politicizing" a technical matter. This forecloses the normal democratic process of political contestation: debate, opposition, compromise, accountability.
5. **Accountability displacement** -- If the action is "just technical," then no one made a political decision that they can be held accountable for. "The algorithm decided." "The data showed." "The metrics indicated." Political responsibility is displaced onto technical processes that have no democratic accountability.

## Detection Markers

- **Language analysis**: "Efficiency," "streamline," "modernize," "optimize," "data-driven," "merit-based," "performance-based" — language that strips political content from distributional decisions
- **Who benefits from the "neutral" decision?** If a "technical" procurement decision consistently benefits companies connected to the decision-makers, the neutrality framing is doing work.
- **Expertise gatekeeping**: Claims that the decision is "too technical" for political oversight, congressional review, or public comment
- **Metric selection**: What is being measured and optimized? Efficiency metrics that ignore equity, access, or democratic accountability are encoding political choices as technical parameters.
- **Contestation response**: When the action is challenged on political grounds, does the actor respond by defending the politics or by accusing critics of "politicizing" a technical matter? The latter is Depoliticization in action.
- **Paired with Sovereign Exception**: Look for the sequence — political emergency declaration → technical operational response. The emergency justifies the authority; the technical frame shields the exercise.

## Why Existing Models Miss This

- **Compliance Theater** describes processes that produce approvals. Depoliticization prevents the question from reaching oversight processes in the first place.
- **Narrative Shield** controls what information environment people operate in. Depoliticization controls the *category* in which an action is understood -- not "what do people know about this?" but "do people recognize this as a political choice at all?"
- **Agnotology** produces unknowability. Depoliticization produces misrecognition -- the action is visible, but its political character is invisible.

## Transferability

The pattern appears wherever political decisions are rendered as technical ones: central bank interest rate decisions presented as purely economic (when they redistribute between creditors and debtors), algorithmic content moderation presented as neutral (when it encodes values about acceptable speech), zoning decisions presented as technical land-use planning (when they determine racial and economic segregation patterns), standardized testing presented as objective measurement (when it reflects and reinforces class advantage). The diagnostic: does the "technical" decision have distributional consequences? If yes, the technical framing is doing political work.

## Limitations

- Some government actions genuinely are technical. Not every efficiency initiative is Depoliticization. The model applies when a decision with clear distributional/political consequences is framed as though it has none -- not when genuinely operational matters are handled operationally.
- The model can become a tool for rejecting all expert decision-making as secretly political. It should be applied to decisions that *have* distributional consequences and *are being shielded* from democratic contestation, not to every instance of technical governance.
- Identifying Depoliticization requires knowing the distributional consequences of the "technical" decision. This is often not visible in real time and may require the kind of follow-the-money, follow-the-network analysis that the investigation does.
- The inverse problem: sometimes "politicizing" a genuinely technical matter is itself a power play. The model should be applied when the evidence shows distributional consequences being hidden, not assumed.
