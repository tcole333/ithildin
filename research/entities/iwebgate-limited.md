## Business Description

iWebgate provides "sophisticated network solutions to organizations of all sizes" focused on cyber and mobile security. Its core product is a **secure mobile container** — a technology that securely houses apps on phones/tablets while protecting data. Distributed through the **Telstra Marketplace** (Australia's largest telco). Claimed 800,000+ users as of February 2016.

---

## Relationship to Sprocket HK / Ubiquity, Inc.

### The Arrangement (February 2016)

On February 25, 2016, Ubiquity, Inc. (CIK 0001538329; OTCQB: UBIQ) filed an 8-K announcing:

> "its patented Sprocket software distribution through its licensee partnership with **iWebgate Limited** through Sprocket HK Ltd."

This was a sub-licensing arrangement: Ubiquity → licensed to Sprocket HK (Deayton, director) → sub-licensed to iWebgate (for distribution through iWebgate's mobile container platform).

The revenue model was per-user, monthly: **$1.00–$2.50 per user** depending on applications used, net of Telstra's service fee.

**Primary source**: `SEC:0001538329-16-007622` — 8-K (February 25, 2016) + EX-99.1

A second press release (~March 2016) announced iWebgate and Sprocket had **secured $8.575 million in funding** to accelerate growth — structured as a convertible note facility.

## The Tsiolis Bridge — Key Structural Finding

**James Tsiolis** is the critical connecting figure:

1. He is **Director and CEO of Strategic Capital Management Ltd** — which the original Sprocket HK launch press release (March 2015) names as an **investor in Sprocket Hong Kong Limited**.
2. He was **appointed Executive Chairman of iWebgate Limited** on **November 11, 2015** — approximately 8 months after Sprocket HK was formed and 3 months before the iWebgate/Sprocket sub-licensing deal was announced publicly.

This means Tsiolis simultaneously held:
- A **financial interest in Sprocket HK** (through his investment vehicle SCML)
- **Executive-level board control at iWebgate** (the sub-licensee)

The iWebgate/Sprocket sub-licensing deal was therefore arranged by someone who had financial skin in both sides of the transaction. This is a significant undisclosed conflict of interest in the context of a public company (iWebgate/ASX: IWG).

**Primary sources**: `prnewswire.com/news-releases/ubiquity-licenses-to-sprocket-hong-kong-300054837.html`; `delisted.com.au/company/iwebgate-limited/`

## Connection to Kenneth Raymond Deayton

**No direct connection found.**

Deayton's role is as Director of Sprocket HK Limited, which is the intermediary licensor between Ubiquity and iWebgate. Deayton is not named as an officer, director, or investor in iWebgate Limited in any source. The 6/F Wyndham Place address does not appear in any iWebgate record. The connection is structural (Deayton controls the entity that sub-licenses to iWebgate) but not direct.

The Tsiolis-SCML investment in Sprocket HK may have been the mechanism that brought iWebgate into the arrangement — Tsiolis knowing both Deayton (through the Sprocket HK investment) and the iWebgate management (through his chairman role).
