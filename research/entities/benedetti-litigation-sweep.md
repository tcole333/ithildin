### Benedetti v Sawiris — processing note (2026-07-03)

Both 2009 Patten J judgments were fetched from caselaw.nationalarchives (Akoma Ntoso XML; BAILII itself is behind an anti-bot wall) and archived at `datasets/bailii/benedetti-v-sawiris-2009-EWHC-1330-Ch.{xml,html,txt}` (+ the 1806 consequentials). What the judgment gives the caper investigation — a High-Court, findings-of-fact character assessment of Benedetti a decade before the smear:

- **Credibility (finding #11835)**: "highly defensive"; explanations "unconvincing and, to some extent, contradictory" [83]; "tiresome lack of accuracy" [84]; "evasive" [85]; account "changed materially" [85]; one explanation "simply invented" [180]; a drafting claim "clearly untrue" [253]; his claim "exaggerated" [91, 537].
- **Criminal history (finding #11836)**: Gefco liquidation 1988; Magnetofoni Castelli / Gruppo Ligresti (Antonio Ligresti jailed for bribery); Benedetti's own **false-accounting plea bargain + prison sentence**, which he tried to deny was a conviction ("it seems obvious to me that the opposite is the case" [75]). Distinct from the 2003 Turin Commertec matter (#11102). The Wind sale also drew an Italian Public Prosecutor **bribery** inquiry [84].
- **Opaque-payment MO (finding #11837)**: took a **€67M** fee through his controlled vehicle **ITM** while leading Sawiris/Abdou to believe it was for third parties [432] — a 2005 structural echo of the 2015 Barkmere channel.
- **Self-defeating over-reach (finding #11838)**: **won €75.1M but was ordered to pay the defendants' costs**, denied pre-judgment interest, because the litigation was "wholly avoidable" (he'd rejected an identical 2006 offer). The perfect illustration of the litigious-but-self-defeating fee-claimant.

## The Benedetti↔Misra fee dispute — status: NARRATIVE FACT, no filed case found

The WSJ reporting (Feb 2020, "SoftBank's Rajeev Misra Used Campaign of Sabotage to Hobble Internal Rivals") establishes the *motive* premise the objective is built on, but describes it as a business falling-out, **not** a lawsuit:

- Misra paid **$500,000** from his **Standard Chartered Bank** account to **Barkmere Group Ltd** (BVI, controlled by Benedetti) — characterized by sources as initial campaign expenses (Misra's spokesman: "investment in the oil industry").
- **Benedetti "expected a cut from Misra's earnings and to be named as the head of the Vision Fund in London in exchange for the favour, but this never happened."** This unfulfilled expectation is the reported source of the dispute — and the reported reason Benedetti's account leaked to the press.
- Named third parties in the op (all litigation-relevant if a fee case ever surfaces): **K2 Intelligence** (hired to investigate Arora/Sama), **Nicolas Giannakopoulos** (Swiss operative, distributed banking records/emails), **Powerscourt Group** (London PR, seeded journalists).

**No open source states that Benedetti actually filed suit against Misra, SoftBank, or a Misra entity — in any forum.** The dispute is reported as leverage/leak, not litigation.
