## Transaction History (Documented)

### 1. License Agreement Signed — March 13, 2015

Ubiquity, Inc. (Nevada; CIK 0001538329; OTCQB: UBIQ) filed an 8-K disclosing entry into a **Non-Exclusive Commercial Technology License Agreement** with Sprocket HK Limited.

**Terms as disclosed:**
- Ubiquity granted Sprocket HK a domestic and international non-exclusive license to make, use, copy, and distribute products and services based on Ubiquity's technology ("the Sprocket" platform — a software/media distribution product)
- Sprocket HK would negotiate sub-licenses with third parties
- **Consideration owed to Ubiquity:**
  - 51% non-dilutable stake in Sprocket HK (to be issued to Ubiquity)
  - $100,000 minimum initial cash payment
  - Board seat for CEO Christopher Carmichael
  - 51% of gross proceeds from sublicensing

**Signed by Deayton**: Kenneth Raymond Deayton signed the agreement as Director of Sprocket HK Limited. (Source: SEC Exhibit 10.1, accession 0001538329-15-017890. Direct fetch of exhibit returned HTTP 403; confirmed via subsequent SEC 10-Q disclosures that name Deayton in this role.)

**Primary sources**:
- `SEC:0001538329-15-017890` — 8-K (March 24, 2015) + Exhibit 10.1 (license agreement)
- `SEC:0001538329-15-005482` — 10-Q (November 2015)

### 2. iWebgate Limited Named as Sub-Distributor — February 25, 2016

Ubiquity filed an 8-K announcing "its patented Sprocket software distribution through its licensee partnership with **iWebgate Limited** through Sprocket HK Ltd."

This is the only public evidence of any operational activity: Sprocket HK appears to have been sub-licensing to iWebgate. **iWebgate Limited has not been investigated; its ownership and connection to Deayton are unconfirmed.**

**Primary source**: `SEC:0001538329-16-007622` — 8-K (February 25, 2016) + EX-99.1

### 3. Termination for Material Breach — June 28, 2016

Ubiquity terminated the license agreement citing three material breaches by Sprocket HK:
1. Non-payment of the $100,000 minimum initial cash payment
2. Failure to properly issue the 51% non-dilutable stake to Ubiquity
3. Failure to provide Christopher Carmichael a board seat

Ubiquity sent a cure notice; no remedy was made within the contractual 30-day window.

**Primary source**: `SEC:0001538329-17-008699` — 10-Q (August 2017, period June 30, 2016)

### 4. Sprocket HK Cancels Ubiquity's Shares — August 15, 2016

"A director of Sprocket HK sent a letter to the Company stating Ubiquity's shares are cancelled in the Sprocket HK entity, effectively confirming the termination of the arrangement."

Ubiquity's 10-Q explicitly states: "Although the Company never received its 51% stake of Sprocket HK, nor did Chris Carmichael receive the board seat..."

**Primary source**: `SEC:0001538329-17-008699` — 10-Q (August 2017)

## Outcome for Ubiquity

Ubiquity received no equity, no cash, and no board representation from Sprocket HK. Sprocket HK used the tech license for at least one sub-licensing arrangement (iWebgate) before the agreement was terminated.

The SEC revoked Ubiquity's stock registration on **August 22, 2017** for failure to file required reports. Ubiquity's interim CEO **Nicholas Mitsakos** subsequently pleaded guilty to securities and wire fraud and was sentenced to 30 months. The company's auditor was found non-PCAOB-registered.

Ubiquity appears in 14 SEC filings referencing Sprocket HK (2015–2017).
